The study of spatially resolved stellar populations represents a major paradigm shift in galactic archaeology, transitioning from the traditional [[integrated-light-method|Integrated light method]] to the two-dimensional mapping of a galaxy's internal properties. Historically, extragalactic studies treated galaxies as single, unresolved point sources. However, this integrated approach suffers from severe observational biases, such as the [[age-extinction-degeneracy|Age-extinction degeneracy]] and the tendency for luminous, young stars to outshine the older, underlying mass distribution, which can systematically underestimate total stellar mass by factors of two to five [Sorba et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.476.1532S/abstract). By utilizing [[galaxy-integral-field-spectroscopy-tool|Galaxy Integral-field Spectroscopy Tool]] (IFS) data cubes, pixel-by-pixel spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting, and the photometric analysis of resolved [[color-magnitude-diagram|Color-magnitude diagram]]s in the [[local-volume-and-nearby-galaxy-studies|Local Volume and Nearby Galaxy Studies]], researchers can now map kiloparsec-scale variations in [[star-formation-history|Star Formation History]], [[galaxy-stellar-age|Galaxy stellar age]], and [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] across distinct [[galaxy-subcomponents|Galaxy subcomponents]].

A cornerstone finding of this spatially resolved approach is that global galaxy scaling relations—such as the [[star-forming-main-sequence|Star-Forming Main Sequence]] and the mass-metallicity relation ([[z-l-relation|Z-L relation]])—are fundamentally the integrated sum of localized physical processes operating on kiloparsec scales [Sánchez et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ARA&A..58...99S/abstract). Large-scale IFS surveys like the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] and CALIFA [Sánchez et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...538A...8S/abstract) have robustly established the "inside-out" growth paradigm for massive [[late-type-galaxies-and-stellar-populations|Late-type Galaxies and Stellar Populations]]. This formation pathway is evidenced by ubiquitous [[negative-metallicity-gradients|Negative metallicity gradients]] and older central cores, indicating that massive galaxies typically assembled their centers rapidly before gradually accreting their extended disks. Spatially resolved mapping also provides crucial diagnostics for morphological transformation and quenching, allowing astronomers to distinguish between the "inside-out" cessation of star formation driven by central black hole feedback, and the "outside-in" environmental quenching caused by mechanisms like ram-pressure stripping in dense clusters. 

In recent years, the frontier of spatially resolved analysis has expanded to "Cosmic Noon" ($z \sim 2$) and beyond, driven by advanced grism spectroscopy and the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). High-resolution studies of [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]] are unveiling the earliest stages of mass assembly, revealing that the massive cores of early quiescent galaxies often already exhibit complex chemical signatures of rapid gas expulsion. Despite these observational leaps, significant open questions remain. Active debates center on whether observed [[redder-inside-disc-colour-gradients|Redder-inside disc colour gradients]] at high redshift are driven primarily by radially decreasing dust attenuation, strong [[abundance-gradients|Abundance gradients]], or by genuinely older stellar cores. Furthermore, resolving discrepancies between spectroscopic [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] and those derived from photometry hinges on overcoming systematic uncertainties in [[stellar-population-synthesis|Stellar Population Synthesis]] libraries and determining to what extent the stellar initial mass function (IMF) varies spatially within individual galaxies. Resolving these tensions ultimately points toward the next generation of extremely large telescopes, which aim to resolve individual stars and map precise stellar ages well beyond the Local Group.

## From Integrated Light to Spatially Resolved Methodologies

Historically, the study of extragalactic stellar populations relied heavily on the [[integrated-light-method|Integrated light method]] and broad-band photometry, which effectively treat a galaxy as a single, unresolved point source. A fundamental limitation of this approach is the age-metallicity degeneracy: as established by [[stellar-population-synthesis|Stellar Population Synthesis]] models, the composite spectrum of an older stellar population can be photometrically identical to that of a younger, more metal-rich population. Mathematically, maintaining constant broad-band colors requires a fractional change in age and [[metallicity|Metallicity]] $Z$ of $\Delta \ln(\text{age}) / \Delta \ln Z \approx -3/2$. To break this [[age-extinction-degeneracy|Age-extinction degeneracy]] more robustly for populations older than 300 Myr, observers turned to [[integrated-light-spectroscopy|Integrated light spectroscopy]] to measure specific absorption features, most notably the [[lick-indices|Lick indices]] (e.g., comparing Balmer lines like H$\beta$ against metal-sensitive indices like Mg$b$). Early spatial context utilized 1D long-slit spectroscopy, demonstrating that many [[high-mass-early-type-galaxies|High-mass early-type galaxies]] possess [[negative-metallicity-gradients|Negative metallicity gradients]] [Baum et al. (1990)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1990ASPC...10..119B/abstract), [Proctor et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.362..857P/abstract).

However, as spectral modeling improved, it became evident that unresolved measurements suffer from severe systematic biases. Because galaxies are spatially heterogeneous, integrating their light over large apertures causes young, luminous stars to "outshine" the older, more massive underlying populations. This effect, coupled with centralized dust obscuration, causes spatially unresolved analyses to systematically underestimate total galaxy stellar mass—often termed the "missing mass problem." While some unresolved photometric estimates are biased low by up to 40% [Zibetti et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IAUS..262...89Z/abstract), at higher specific star formation rates, unresolved spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting can underestimate mass by factors of 2 to 5 [Sorba et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.476.1532S/abstract). Furthermore, attempting to map masses via simple mass-to-light color relations (MLCRs) carries significant systematic uncertainties; depending on the choice of model priors, incorporating near-infrared data into these relations can introduce biases of up to 0.6 dex [Roediger et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.3209R/abstract).

To overcome these biases, modern galactic archaeology relies on spatially resolved methodologies that map kiloparsec-scale variations. In the Local Group and nearby universe, high-resolution imaging enables the photometric detection of individual stars via the [[color-magnitude-diagram|Color-magnitude diagram]] (CMD). Mapping stars in different evolutionary phases allows researchers to trace distinct epochs of star formation with age precisions of $\lesssim 1$ Gyr for older populations [Jennings et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv231116397J/abstract). Jointly fitting CMDs alongside external metallicity data significantly reduces classical degeneracies [Dolphin et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...825..153D/abstract). High-resolution imaging of nearby galaxies, such as the M31 [[phat-survey|PHAT survey]], also provides precise measurements of cluster formation efficiency ($\Gamma$), revealing that this efficiency varies significantly with mid-plane pressure and local gas depletion times [Johnson et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...827...33J/abstract). Spatially resolved analyses in the Andromeda Galaxy (M31) further demonstrate that variations in mass-to-light ratios across its disk strongly correlate with recent star formation history, while localized dust geometry significantly complicates color-M/L relations derived from optical data [Telford et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...891...32T/abstract).

For galaxies beyond the Local Volume, properties must be extracted from the integrated light of distinct spatial elements (spaxels) using Integral Field Spectroscopy (IFS). By applying [[full-spectrum-fitting|Full-spectrum fitting]] techniques, large-scale surveys such as the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] and CALIFA reconstruct 2D maps of [[mass-weighted-stellar-populations|Mass-weighted stellar populations]] and kinematics [Sánchez et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...538A...8S/abstract), . Furthermore, the field is increasingly utilizing data-driven spectroscopic methods, such as 'The Cannon', to derive high-precision stellar ages and abundances by placing multiple survey datasets onto a unified empirical scale [Ness et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018PASA...35....3N/abstract). When spectroscopic mapping is unavailable, researchers employ pixel-by-pixel SED fitting using advanced Bayesian codes like [[pixedfit|piXedfit]] to model multi-band emission across a galaxy's 2D footprint, breaking the [[age-extinction-degeneracy|Age-extinction degeneracy]] more effectively than integrated photometry [Abdurro'uf et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945..117A/abstract). Extensive datasets like the DESI value-added catalog further empower these methods, providing SED-derived physical properties for over 1.3 million galaxies and utilizing multi-wavelength resources like WISE data to effectively disentangle AGN contamination from stellar emission [Siudek et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.308S/abstract).

Despite these methodological advances, mitigating systematic uncertainties in theoretical stellar libraries remains a critical frontier, particularly because derived properties—and assumptions about the universality of the initial mass function—can conflict with resolved CMDs [Dolphin et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...825..153D/abstract), [Martins et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484.2388M/abstract), [Byrne et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.521.4995B/abstract).

## Radial Gradients and the Inside-Out Growth Paradigm

The spatially resolved properties of galaxies provide a fossil record of their chemical enrichment and [[stellar-mass-assembly-and-functions|Stellar Mass Assembly and Functions]]. A prevailing framework for galaxy evolution is the inside-out growth paradigm, which posits that massive galaxies typically assemble their central cores rapidly at early epochs, followed by the more extended, gradual accretion of their disks [González Delgado et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...581A.103G/abstract). This scenario leaves distinct observational signatures in the form of radial variations in stellar age (often visualized via an [[age-map|Age map]]) and metallicity.

Large integral-field spectroscopic surveys have mapped the stellar populations of thousands of galaxies in the local Universe. These observations robustly establish that [[negative-metallicity-gradients|Negative metallicity gradients]] are nearly universal features of massive disks [Roediger et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.416.1996R/abstract), [Sánchez et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ARA&A..58...99S/abstract), [Neumann et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.508.4844N/abstract). In [[late-type-galaxies-and-stellar-populations|Late-type Galaxies and Stellar Populations]], the inner regions are typically older, more metal-rich, and exhibit a higher stellar mass surface density than the outskirts, directly supporting an inside-out assembly history [González Delgado et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...581A.103G/abstract), [Goddard et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.466.4731G/abstract). The slope of these [[abundance-gradients|Abundance gradients]] correlates with the total stellar mass. For instance, in late-type galaxies, the metallicity gradient steepens with mass at a rate of $d(\nabla[Z/H])/d(\log M) \sim -0.2 \pm 0.05$, whereas [[high-mass-early-type-galaxies|High-mass early-type galaxies]] exhibit a much shallower mass dependence of $\sim -0.05 \pm 0.05$ [Goddard et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.466.4731G/abstract). However, this scaling is not perfectly linear; observations from surveys like the DESI Legacy Imaging Survey reveal an inflection point in the color gradient-mass relation at around $10^{10.5} M_\odot$, above which gradients become noticeably shallower in the most massive galaxies [Liao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.518.3999L/abstract).

Despite the success of the inside-out model for star-forming disks, the literature presents a more complex picture for spheroidal and quiescent systems. While textbook monolithic collapse models predict uniformly negative age and metallicity profiles, resolved observations of some massive spheroids reveal flat or even positive mass-weighted age gradients (i.e., older stars in the outskirts) of $\sim 0.09$ dex/$R_e$ [Goddard et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.466.4731G/abstract). Furthermore, recent studies highlight that a galaxy's total dynamical mass—which encompasses its extended dark matter halo—is directly linked to systematic variations in stellar population ages in its outermost regions [Scholz-Diaz et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260116019S/abstract). This inversion contradicts simple inside-out expectations. [[positive-age-gradients|Positive age gradients]] can arise through distinct mechanisms: they may be the signature of late-time "outside-in" growth driven by the accretion of older, lower-metallicity satellites building up the outskirts [Cheng et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.3604C/abstract), or they can result from minor mergers that trigger centrally concentrated starbursts, effectively lowering the average age of the core relative to the extended halo [Proctor et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.362..857P/abstract). Consequently, the relative dominance of the potential well depth (mass) versus Hubble type (morphology) in driving these gradients remains an active area of debate [González Delgado et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...581A.103G/abstract), [Neumann et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.508.4844N/abstract).

At higher redshifts ($z \gtrsim 1$), studies of [[passive-and-quiescent-galaxies|Passive and Quiescent Galaxies]] during "Cosmic Noon" provide a direct view of structural evolution and quenching. Recent observations using the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have revealed pronounced [[color-gradients|Color gradients]] in massive ($M_* > 10^{10} M_\odot$) galaxies. The physical drivers of these high-$z$ gradients are currently disputed. Some studies argue that radially decreasing dust attenuation primarily produces the observed redder cores [Miller et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...941L..37M/abstract). Detailed spectroscopic modeling highlights a diversity of formation histories. While some recent JWST observations of galaxies at $z \sim 2$ reveal older central cores indicative of inside-out quenching [Cheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912316C/abstract), other spectroscopic studies at $0.6 < z < 1.0$ find flat or even positive age gradients, demonstrating that redder central colors can be driven primarily by strong metallicity gradients rather than age [Cheng et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.3604C/abstract). Furthermore, the abundance ratios in these early cores reveal an evolving picture; while intermediate-redshift studies find flat $[\mathrm{Mg}/\mathrm{Fe}]$ gradients [Cheng et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.3604C/abstract), more recent JWST observations of galaxies at cosmic noon find Mg-deficient centers, suggesting rapid central gas expulsion [Cheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912316C/abstract).

Ultimately, extracting a single [[star-formation-history|Star Formation History]] from integrated light often obscures these rich internal variations. The transition to a [[spatially-resolved-galaxy-analysis|Spatially Resolved Galaxy Analysis]] has demonstrated that global scaling relations—and the apparent evolution of unresolved populations—are fundamentally the integrated sum of local, kiloparsec-scale physical processes [Sánchez et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ARA&A..58...99S/abstract).

## Resolved Scaling Relations: The Local Main Sequence and Mass-Metallicity Relation

The advent of large-scale [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|Resolved spectroscopic observations]] (e.g., the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]], CALIFA, and SAMI) has demonstrated that many global galaxy correlations are fundamentally the integrated manifestations of physical processes operating on kiloparsec scales [Sánchez et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ARA&A..58...99S/abstract). Chief among these local scaling laws are the resolved [[star-forming-main-sequence|Star-Forming Main Sequence]] (rSFMS) and the resolved mass-metallicity relation (rMZR), which link the local stellar mass surface density, $\Sigma_*$, to the star formation rate surface density, $\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$, and chemical abundances, respectively. 

The rSFMS reveals that star formation is locally regulated by the existing stellar mass surface density. However, the exact parametrization of the rSFMS is highly sensitive to the chosen statistical fitting technique and the physical scale probed. For instance, integral-field observations of galaxies at $z \sim 0.3$ yield a relatively shallow slope of $\sim 0.43$ when utilizing ordinary least squares (OLS) regression, but a significantly steeper slope of $\sim 1.16$ under orthogonal distance regression (ODR) [Koller et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A.315K/abstract). Furthermore, the relation breaks down on physical scales below $\sim 500$ pc, where star formation becomes stochastically driven by local molecular cloud dynamics rather than global disk properties [Hall et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865..154H/abstract). Below a critical stellar mass density of $\log(\Sigma_* / M_\odot\,\text{pc}^{-2}) \sim 7.5$, the rSFMS exhibits increased scatter and loses its predictive power [Hall et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865..154H/abstract). Extensive mapping of localized star formation has also been bolstered by surveys like the AMUSING++ compilation, which catalogs approximately 52,000 HII regions and confirms direct physical links between localized ionized gas properties and the ages of the underlying, spatially resolved stellar populations [Lugo-Aranda et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.528.6099L/abstract).

Parallel to the rSFMS, the rMZR demonstrates that local metallicity is primarily driven by the depth of the local potential well, effectively traced by $\Sigma_*$ [Neumann et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.508.4844N/abstract). This local enrichment drives the universally observed [[abundance-gradients|Abundance gradients]] across both early- and late-type galaxies, providing strong evidence for inside-out [[stellar-mass-assembly-and-functions|Stellar Mass Assembly and Functions]] [González Delgado et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...581A.103G/abstract). The steepness of these gradients correlates with total galaxy mass; late-type disks exhibit steeper gradients than early-types, with a characteristic slope scaling of $d(\nabla[Z/H])/d(\log M_*) \sim -0.2$ dex $R_e^{-1}$ per dex in mass [Goddard et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.466.4731G/abstract). Additionally, physical size plays a critical role in these relations; data from the SAMI Galaxy Survey demonstrate that galaxy size explains much of the residual scatter in the age-mass and metallicity-mass relations, with high-density (more compact) galaxies systematically appearing older and more metal-rich at a fixed mass [Scott et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.472.2833S/abstract). While the typical rMZR holds for most galaxies, extremely metal-poor galaxies (EMPGs) deviate significantly, exhibiting a distinct "metal-poor horizontal branch" in the rMZR that is likely fueled by metal-poor gas infall triggering episodic star formation [Nakajima et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241204541N/abstract). Tracing this evolution over cosmic time, studies matching "mass-metallicity twins" between the [[lega-c-survey|LEGA-C survey]] at $z \sim 0.7$ and the local MaNGA survey demonstrate that high-mass, low-metallicity galaxies evolve rapidly toward the present-day rMZR following the cessation of AGN activity [Zhou et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.1935Z/abstract). High-redshift studies show that the rMZR persists out to at least $z \sim 0.26$, though with an average metallicity $\sim 0.11$ dex lower than in the local Universe at fixed $\Sigma_*$ [Yao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661A.112Y/abstract).

Despite consensus on the rMZR, the existence of a resolved Fundamental Metallicity Relation (rFMR)—a secondary dependence of the local metallicity on $\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$—remains actively debated in the literature. While some studies find a significant rFMR at low stellar masses, where the inverse correlation between $\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$ and [[gas-phase-metallicity|Gas-Phase Metallicity]] tightens [Koller et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A.315K/abstract), others find no clear evidence that the FMR extends from the global to the resolved kiloparsec regime at $z \sim 0.26$ [Yao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661A.112Y/abstract).

## Quenching Mechanisms and Morphological Transformation

The transition of galaxies from the [[star-forming-main-sequence|Star-Forming Main Sequence]] to the [[red-sequence|Red sequence]] is driven by quenching mechanisms that operate on distinct spatial scales. Spatially resolved spectroscopy has revolutionized our understanding of these processes by allowing the construction of two-dimensional star formation rate (SFR) and specific SFR ($\rm sSFR$) maps. By leveraging [[star-formation-rate-tracers|Star formation rate tracers]] such as $\rm H\alpha$ emission corrected for dust attenuation, integral field surveys can trace where and how star formation ceases, distinguishing between internal and environmental drivers. To robustly interpret these emission lines, surveys like [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] have shown that [[galaxy-kinematics|Galaxy kinematics]] (specifically velocity dispersion) correlate strongly with traditional emission-line diagnostics. This kinematic information provides a critical secondary axis to help distinguish between pure HII regions, AGN, and LI(N)ER ionization zones [Law et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...35L/abstract).

**Internal (Inside-Out) Quenching**
Internal quenching mechanisms—often associated with active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, bulge growth, or the stabilization of the gas disk—typically operate from the inside out. Observations reveal that galaxies undergoing this mode of quenching exhibit a distinct radial dependence in their $\rm sSFR$, with central regions showing significant suppression while star formation continues in the outskirts [Jafariyazani et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...887..204J/abstract). Complex internal kinematics also play a role; for example, observations from the MUSE [[timer|TIMER]] survey of the barred galaxy NGC 613 reveal how gas inflows along bar dust lanes can trigger central star formation while simultaneously driving biconical outflows [Silva-Lima et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.540.2787S/abstract). Analysis of $\rm H\alpha$ maps from the SAMI Galaxy Survey supports a two-step "inside-out" quenching mechanism: halo mass first restricts the global gas supply, after which the remaining gas continues to form stars until the local stellar mass surface density ($\Sigma_{\star}$) stabilizes the fuel against further fragmentation, leading to a striking reduction of star formation in the nuclear regions [Medling et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.475.5194M/abstract). Supporting this, observations from the EDGE-CALIFA survey indicate that inside-out quenching in [[passive-and-quiescent-galaxies|Passive and Quiescent Galaxies]] fundamentally requires both low molecular gas fractions and a significantly reduced star formation efficiency (SFE) in the galactic centers [Colombo et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...699A.366C/abstract). This inside-out signature was already established at cosmic noon; recent JWST observations of massive quiescent galaxies at $1.2 \lesssim z \lesssim 2.2$ reveal older, Mg-deficient cores, suggesting early and rapid central gas expulsion as the primary quenching mechanism [Cheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912316C/abstract). 

**Environmental (Outside-In) Quenching**
Conversely, environmental quenching is predominantly an "outside-in" process. Galaxies residing in dense cluster or group environments are subjected to ram-pressure stripping and tidal interactions that preferentially remove or heat gas in the galactic outskirts. Spatially resolved maps demonstrate that galaxies in denser environments undergo a decrease in $\rm sSFR$ that begins at large galactocentric radii [Medling et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.475.5194M/abstract). These environmental effects are already established at high redshifts; cluster galaxies at $z \sim 1.6$ exhibit notably redder UV colors and a wider dispersion of outside-in color gradients compared to field galaxies, reinforcing the role of dense environments in early quenching [Cramer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240407355C/abstract). Passive spiral galaxies, which lack global emission lines despite retaining their disk morphologies, exhibit strong $\rm H\delta$ absorption in their outer regions. This indicates that star formation ceased recently in the outskirts—likely as a result of cluster infall and subsequent gas stripping [Ishigaki et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.382..270I/abstract).

## Resolved Populations at High Redshift and Cosmic Noon

The epoch of "Cosmic Noon" ($z \sim 2$) marks the peak of the cosmic [[star-formation-history|Star Formation History]] and a critical phase in mass assembly. Historically, studying galaxies at these distances relied on [[global-observables-of-galaxies|Global observables of galaxies]] and unresolved photometry. However, unresolved SED fitting systematically underestimates total stellar masses by factors of 2 to 5. This bias occurs because older, widely distributed stellar components are easily outshone by young, central starbursts and obscured by localized dust [Sorba et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.476.1532S/abstract). Advanced SED fitting codes, such as PROSPECT applied to the DEVILS survey, demonstrate that making physically motivated assumptions about metallicity evolution can shift inferred stellar masses by up to 0.2 dex [Thorne et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.505..540T/abstract). Consequently, spatially resolved analyses are essential to accurately reconstruct [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]].

Advancements using Hubble Space Telescope (HST) 2D grism spectroscopy and deep ground-based campaigns like the [[lega-c-survey|LEGA-C survey]] have extended resolved stellar population studies to $0.6 < z < 1.0$. The second data release (DR2) of LEGA-C mapped the intricate relationship between dynamical and stellar population properties, revealing that star-forming galaxies in this epoch typically possess stellar velocity dispersions of $\sim 150$ km/s alongside strong $\rm H\delta$ absorption [Straatman et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJS..239...27S/abstract). Its third and final data release provided over 3000 high-quality spectra of K-selected galaxies to benchmark stellar population studies [van der Wel et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJS..256...44V/abstract). Observations of massive [[passive-and-quiescent-galaxies|Passive and Quiescent Galaxies]] in this regime reveal predominantly flat age and $[\alpha/\mathrm{Fe}]$ gradients, but negative $[\mathrm{Fe}/\mathrm{H}]$ gradients (iron-rich cores). This indicates that the negative color gradients observed in mature high-redshift systems are often driven by metallicity rather than age [Cheng et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.3604C/abstract). At $z \sim 1.5$, significant negative color gradients have been detected in roughly 70% of early-type galaxies, often driven by underlying radial age variations [Gargiulo et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.425.2698G/abstract).

At $z \sim 2$, spatially resolved imaging from CANDELS has shown that internal color dispersion (ICD) peaks specifically in disk-dominated galaxies with masses between $10^{10}$ and $10^{11} M_\odot$. This high color dispersion indicates the presence of prominent star-forming clumps that formed before substantive bulge assembly stabilized the disk [Boada et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...803..104B/abstract). Complementary HST grism observations of strongly lensed quiescent galaxies have mapped stellar populations on sub-kiloparsec scales, uncovering flat age gradients in their inner cores that support early, rapid formation scenarios [Akhshik et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...900..184A/abstract). Parallel efforts mapping gas-phase metallicity at $z \sim 2{-}3$ using the JWST-PASSAGE survey suggest that lower-mass galaxies exhibit more effective feedback-driven metal transport, contributing to their diverse chemical profiles [Acharyya et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250805335A/abstract).

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has fundamentally transformed the study of resolved populations at Cosmic Noon and beyond. Using high-resolution NIRCam imaging of galaxies selected from the [[3d-hst-survey|3D-HST survey]], spatially resolved rest-frame $U-V$ and $V-J$ color profiles at $1.7 < z < 2.3$ show that most star-forming galaxies possess negative color gradients (redder inside, bluer outside). These gradients are predominantly driven by radially decreasing dust attenuation rather than true age variations [Miller et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...941L..37M/abstract). Conversely, JWST NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy of massive quiescent galaxies at $1.2 \lesssim z \lesssim 2.2$ reveals negative age gradients (older cores) accompanied by Mg-deficient centers (positive $[\mathrm{Mg}/\mathrm{H}]$ and $[\mathrm{Mg}/\mathrm{Fe}]$ gradients). These abundance and age profiles provide direct spectroscopic evidence for inside-out quenching, potentially triggered by rapid central gas expulsion [Cheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912316C/abstract). 

At even earlier epochs ($z \sim 7{-}11$), JWST NIRCam medium-band photometry has proven essential. By $5 < z < 9$, JWST demonstrates that early mass assembly is highly heterogeneous. Spatially resolved [[stellar-population-synthesis|Stellar Population Synthesis]] reveals that integrated galaxy light is often dominated by intensely star-forming clumps with extreme emission line equivalent widths ($\mathrm{EW}([\mathrm{O~III}]+\mathrm{H}\beta) \sim 3000{-}4000$ Å). Resolving these systems unveils underlying older stellar populations that are otherwise hidden, correcting stellar mass estimates upward by $0.5{-}1.0$ dex [Giménez-Arteaga et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948..126G/abstract). In highly magnified systems such as the $z=6.2$ "Cosmic Spear", JWST resolves extremely dense [[star-clusters|Star clusters]] with effective radii $R_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim 5$ pc and extreme stellar mass surface densities $\Sigma_* > 10^4 \, \mathrm{M}_\odot \, \mathrm{pc}^{-2}$.

## Chemical Enrichment and Alpha-Enhancement Mapping

Spatially resolved mapping of [[stellar-chemical-abundances|Stellar Chemical Abundances]]—specifically iron ([Fe/H]) and alpha-elements ([$\alpha$/Fe], often approximated observationally by [Mg/Fe])—provides a direct fossil record of a galaxy's star formation history. Because $\alpha$-elements are produced rapidly by core-collapse supernovae while iron-peak elements are delayed by $\sim 1$ Gyr from Type Ia supernovae, the local [$\alpha$/Fe] ratio encodes the quenching timescale of galactic sub-components.

In the local Universe, most massive galaxies exhibit [[negative-metallicity-gradients|Negative metallicity gradients]]. Traditional monolithic collapse models and early observations of early-type galaxies suggest a canonical metallicity gradient of $\Delta [{\rm Fe}/{\rm H}] / \Delta \log r = -0.2 \pm 0.1$. Resolved integral field unit (IFU) surveys have quantified this further, demonstrating that the steepness of the gradient scales with total stellar mass. For [[late-type-galaxies-and-stellar-populations|Late-type Galaxies and Stellar Populations]], this mass dependence is relatively steep, with a slope of $d(\nabla [Z/{\rm H}])/d(\log M) \sim -0.2 \pm 0.05$, whereas early-type galaxies exhibit a much shallower dependence of $-0.05 \pm 0.05$ [Goddard et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.466.4731G/abstract). Conversely, [$\alpha$/Fe] gradients in local early-type galaxies are frequently flat up to half of the effective radius, indicating that their inner regions underwent a uniformly rapid, high-efficiency starburst phase prior to inside-out quenching [Rosa et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018Ap&SS.363..131R/abstract). 

However, the literature reveals significant heterogeneity, particularly among the most massive systems. Measurements of [[mass-weighted-stellar-populations|Mass-weighted stellar populations]] in brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) show highly uniform central ages and velocity dispersions, with essentially flat or insignificant [$\alpha$/Fe] gradients across the sample. For instance, spatially resolved studies of fossil group BCGs have found conflicting $\alpha$-element profiles: some exhibit positive gradients ($\Delta [{\rm E}/{\rm H}]/\Delta R \sim 0.1$), hinting at recent central starbursts, while others show negative gradients ($\Delta [{\rm E}/{\rm H}]/\Delta R \sim -0.1$) consistent with classical inside-out formation [Proctor et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.439.2281P/abstract). 

The mapping of these abundances is currently being pushed to higher redshifts, where tensions with local observations are emerging. In intermediate-redshift ($0.6 \lesssim z \lesssim 1.0$) quiescent galaxies, studies generally find flat [Mg/Fe] gradients paired with negative [Fe/H] gradients, indicative of iron-rich cores [Cheng et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.3604C/abstract). Yet, recent JWST NIRSpec observations of massive quiescent galaxies at cosmic noon ($1.2 \lesssim z \lesssim 2.2$) reveal structurally different populations: they feature *positive* [Mg/Fe] gradients alongside flat [Fe/H] and negative age gradients [Cheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912316C/abstract). This points to Mg-deficient cores driven by extraordinarily rapid central gas expulsion, a quenching scenario that sharply contrasts with the flat $\alpha$-gradients commonly observed at lower redshifts.

Beyond probing global inside-out growth, 2D [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] are increasingly utilized to identify otherwise hidden galactic substructures. These advanced mapping techniques explicitly link local [[metallicity-distributions-and-scaling-relations|Metallicity Distributions and Scaling Relations]] to the hierarchical assembly of galaxies.

## Open Questions and Future Frontiers

Despite the immense success of [[spatially-resolved-galaxy-analysis|Spatially Resolved Galaxy Analysis]], extracting accurate physical parameters from integrated light remains fundamentally limited by systematic uncertainties in [[stellar-populations|Stellar Populations]] synthesis models. A primary bottleneck is the fidelity of stellar libraries. Empirical libraries often lack comprehensive coverage in the ultraviolet (UV) regime, which is increasingly critical for interpreting the high-redshift universe [Byrne et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.521.4995B/abstract). While theoretical libraries can sometimes outperform empirical ones over broad wavelength ranges, predictions for specific spectral features—such as [[lick-indices|Lick indices]]—can still vary by up to a factor of two, occasionally yielding unphysical ages and metallicities during [[full-spectrum-fitting|Full-spectrum fitting]] [Martins et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484.2388M/abstract), [Byrne et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.521.4995B/abstract). 

These template uncertainties feed directly into active conflicts in the literature, most notably regarding the spatial variability of the stellar initial mass function (IMF). A key line of evidence for a non-universal stellar initial mass function (IMF) comes from empirical star formation histories derived by the [[atlas3d-survey|Atlas3D survey]] [McDermid et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013IAUS..295..290M/abstract). Subsequent spatially resolved studies further indicate that the high-mass IMF slope is non-universal, becoming significantly flatter in regions of high star formation rate surface density ($\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$) [Salvador et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250108510S/abstract), [Martín-Navarro et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...654A..59M/abstract). 

Another major open question concerns the physical drivers of radial color gradients during [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]]. Observations of massive galaxies at $z \sim 2$ frequently reveal reder-inside color gradients, but the literature is sharply divided on their cause. Breaking this [[age-extinction-degeneracy|Age-extinction degeneracy]] requires true sub-kpc resolution spectroscopy. Early JWST observations are beginning to map these gradients on sub-kiloparsec scales, with recent NIRSpec data favoring older, Mg-deficient cores over purely dust-driven gradients in quiescent systems [Cheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912316C/abstract), though a consensus has yet to emerge across diverse environments.

Looking forward, the ultimate test of integrated light models lies in resolving individual stars to construct precise color-magnitude diagrams. Currently, this "galactic archeology" is largely restricted to the Local Group and nearby dwarf galaxies. However, early release observations from Euclid's Nearby Galaxy Showcase have already mapped deep anatomies of nearby galaxies (such as NGC 6744 and NGC 2403), resolving approximately 1.3 million individual stars to finely constrain their star-formation histories [Hunt et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240513499H/abstract). Furthermore, the Euclid mission's high spatial resolution will enable the generation of deep, spatially resolved stellar mass maps for galaxies in the local Universe out to $z \sim 0.1$ [Euclid Collaboration et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...702A..72E/abstract). Looking further ahead, future extremely large telescopes (ELTs) equipped with advanced adaptive optics—such as the E-ELT with the HARMONI and EAGLE instruments—will enable the extraction of robust chemical abundances and kinematics for individual stars well beyond the Local Group [Evans et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010aoel.confE1004E/abstract), [Matsunaga et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018arXiv181001022M/abstract), [Barrientos Acevedo et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524..907B/abstract).
