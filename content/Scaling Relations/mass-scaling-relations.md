In astrophysics, **mass scaling relations** are empirical and theoretical power-law correlations that link the [[total-gravitating-mass|Total gravitating mass]] or component mass of celestial structures—such as [[galaxy-properties|galaxies]], [[dark-matter-halo-properties-and-statistics|dark matter halos]], and [[galaxy-cluster-properties-and-scaling-relations|galaxy clusters]]—to directly measurable [[mass-proxy|proxies]]. Rooted fundamentally in the [[virial-theorem-and-parameters|Virial Theorem and Parameters]] and models of self-similar gravitational collapse, these relations provide a crucial framework for estimating the mass of astrophysical objects where direct dynamical measurements are unfeasible. By mapping observables like [[galaxy-dm-velocity-dispersions|stellar velocity dispersion]], [[x-ray-scaling-relations|X-ray luminosity]], or the integrated Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) signal to the underlying [[dark-matter-mass|Dark matter mass]], scaling relations serve as the primary tool for translating observational surveys into constraints on the $\Lambda$CDM [[hierarchical-merger-paradigm|Hierarchical merger paradigm]] [White et al. (1997)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1997gsr..proc....3W/abstract). 

On galactic scales, [[fundamental-scaling-relations|Fundamental scaling relations]] trace the deep physical connection between a galaxy's luminous [[stellar-component|Stellar component]] and its host dark matter halo. Classical empirical laws, such as the [[tully-fisher-relation|Tully-Fisher relation]] for rotation-supported disks [Courteau et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003astro.ph.10440C/abstract) and the Fundamental Plane for pressure-supported ellipticals, reveal tightly constrained correlations between intrinsic luminosity, physical size, and kinematic velocities. Modern frameworks have increasingly unified these into morphology-independent metrics, heavily exploring the stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR) to quantify star-formation efficiency, as well as the [[radial-acceleration-relation|Radial Acceleration Relation]] to probe the tight dynamic coupling between [[baryonic-matter-and-evolution|baryons]] and dark matter. Furthermore, structural correlations like the Kormendy relation characterize the distinct physical origins of bright and faint ellipticals [Mosenkov et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.1066M/abstract), while distinct empirical laws like the $M_{\rm BH}-\sigma$ relation highlight the complex co-evolution of [[galaxy-structure|galactic bulges]] and their central supermassive black holes across cosmic time.

Moving to macroscopic regimes, [[galaxy-cluster-properties-and-scaling-relations|Galaxy Cluster Properties and Scaling Relations]] describe the most massive virialized structures in the Universe. In these systems, properties of the hot intracluster medium act as highly robust, low-scatter [[mass-proxy|Mass proxy]] observables [Giodini et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013SSRv..177..247G/abstract). While massive clusters generally adhere well to gravity-driven self-similar predictions outside their cores, the calibration of these relations against weak lensing often reveals a systematic [[mass-discrepancy|Mass discrepancy]]. This offset is typically induced by line-of-sight projection effects, cluster triaxiality, and the breakdown of hydrostatic equilibrium assumptions. Consequently, characterizing the [[intrinsic-scatter|Intrinsic scatter]] and modeling observational selection biases within these relations is paramount for minimizing [[mass-calibration-uncertainty|Mass calibration uncertainty]] and utilizing galaxy clusters as precision [[cosmological-probes|Cosmological probes]] [Mantz et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010MNRAS.406.1759M/abstract), [Mantz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.3582M/abstract).

This article details the theoretical foundations, historical development, and contemporary applications of [[astrophysical-mass-scaling-relations|Astrophysical Mass Scaling Relations]] across various regimes. It reviews the dominant physical processes—such as active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, supernova winds, and radiative cooling—that cause observable deviations from pure self-similarity. Finally, it explores several critical open questions driving current research. These include the physical origin of the 'missing baryon' problem in low-mass halos, persistent tensions between [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] and observed galaxy quenching, and recent discoveries from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) that reveal stark deviations in fundamental scaling laws during the epoch of cosmic dawn.

## Theoretical Foundation: The Virial Theorem and Self-Similarity

The structural and dynamical properties of both galaxies and galaxy clusters are deeply rooted in the [[virial-theorem-and-parameters|Virial Theorem and Parameters]]. For a self-gravitating system in dynamical equilibrium, the virial theorem relates the kinetic energy $K$ to the potential energy $U$ via $2K + U = 0$. This implies that the [[total-gravitating-mass|Total gravitating mass]] $M$ scales with a characteristic [[galaxy-dm-velocity-dispersions|velocity dispersion]] $\sigma$ (or rotation velocity $V$) and a spatial scale $R$ according to:
$$ M \propto \frac{\sigma^2 R}{G} $$
where $G$ is the gravitational constant. This physical principle provides the theoretical baseline for the [[logarithmic-slope|Logarithmic slope]] of empirical [[galaxy-scaling-relations|Galaxy Scaling Relations]], such as the [[tully-fisher-relation|Tully-Fisher relation]] for disk galaxies and the Fundamental Plane for elliptical galaxies [Zaritsky et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ISRAA2012E..12Z/abstract). The theoretical foundation within the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm posits that scaling laws ultimately arise from tight, non-linear relations connecting both galaxy and halo masses, as well as their characteristic radii [Navarro et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ASSP...56..103N/abstract). Robust interpretation of these scaling relations requires a comprehensive understanding of [[mass-estimators|Mass estimators]] for stellar, gas, and dark matter components, as well as an assessment of the systematic uncertainties inherent to different techniques, such as the varying reliability of [[mass-modeling|dynamical modeling]] versus weak lensing calibrations [Courteau et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014RvMP...86...47C/abstract). Global models investigating [[galaxy-properties|disk galaxies]] further suggest a structural partition reflecting this balance: disk galaxies are typically baryon-dominated at their centers and heavily dark-matter-dominated in their outskirts [Courteau et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...801L..20C/abstract). Meanwhile, across different morphological types, equilibrated structures—including dark matter halos and the remnants of disk galaxy mergers—have been shown to exhibit a universal relation linking their radial density slope to [[anisotropic-velocity-dispersion|velocity anisotropy]] [Hansen et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006NewA...11..333H/abstract). 

While pure virial scaling dictates how mass should relate to velocity and radius, observational relations map luminosity or stellar mass rather than total mass, leading to a noticeable "tilt" in the observed scaling laws relative to pure virial predictions. This tilt is a systematic departure driven primarily by variations in the [[galaxy-mass-to-light-ratio|Galaxy mass-to-light ratio]] tied to stellar population age, [[metallicity-distributions-and-scaling-relations|metallicity]], and the [[initial-mass-function-imf|Initial Mass Function (IMF)]] [Cappellari et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015IAUS..311...20C/abstract). Independent frameworks and theoretical models also explore the varying fractions of [[dark-matter-mass|Dark matter mass]] enclosed within the luminous radius as an alternative driver of this tilt [Pahre et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001Ap&SS.276..983P/abstract). Alternative theoretical frameworks even propose that the [[baryonic-tully-fisher-relation|Baryonic Tully-Fisher relation]] might fundamentally act as an emergent consequence of the scalar virial theorem, representing a preferred energetic and dynamical configuration rather than requiring intricate fine-tuning [La Fortune et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230703975L/abstract). Recent theoretical models argue that scaling relations for both [[intrinsic-galaxy-and-stellar-properties|early- and late-type galaxies]] can be successfully unified under a single structural framework. In this view, self-similar cold dark matter halo profiles and tight stellar-to-halo mass relations naturally generate the observed correlations without requiring separate physical origins for different morphologies [Ferrero et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...648A.124F/abstract).

On larger scales, [[galaxy-cluster-properties-and-scaling-relations|Galaxy Cluster Properties and Scaling Relations]] are frequently framed using the self-similar model. In a purely gravity-driven spherical collapse scenario, dark matter halos form as scaled versions of one another. Cosmological simulations reveal that the underlying dark matter hosts themselves define precise scaling relations akin to those seen in galaxy clusters, though they exhibit distinct slopes driven by non-linear cosmological collapse and systematic trends in their mass-to-light ratios [Lanzoni et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005mmgf.conf..408L/abstract). For a cluster of mass $M$ and radius $R$ defined at a given overdensity, the mass scales as $M \propto R^3$. Coupling this definition with the virial theorem yields the prediction that the average gas temperature $T$ scales with mass as $T \propto M^{2/3}$, which can be rearranged to define the mass-temperature relation:
$$ M \propto T^{3/2} $$
Similar gravity-driven power-law indices can be analytically derived for other observables, such as [[x-ray-scaling-relations|X-ray luminosity]] and the integrated Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) signal [Ettori et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.420.2058E/abstract). In fact, analytical studies demonstrate that the X-ray temperature function of galaxy clusters can be derived directly from the statistics of gravitational potential perturbations, effectively bypassing the need for poorly defined mass indicators entirely [Angrick et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009A&A...494..461A/abstract). Outside of cluster cores, observational measurements of the intracluster medium generally align well with these self-similar predictions [Mantz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.3582M/abstract). The mapping between these regimes is increasingly detailed; for example, the mean dark matter density evaluated within the optical extent of galaxies has emerged as a powerful tracer to reconstruct broader cluster assembly histories and infall redshifts [Taylor et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.488.1111T/abstract).

Despite the success of the self-similar model at large radii, significant deviations are observed, particularly in low-mass groups and cluster cores. Energetic feedback from active galactic nuclei (AGN) and radiative cooling selectively expel or heat gas in shallower potential wells, steepening [[x-ray-scaling-relations|X-ray scaling relations]] relative to self-similar predictions [Maughan et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.437.1171M/abstract). Furthermore, estimating cluster masses using [[equilibrium-models|Equilibrium models]] based on the assumption of hydrostatic equilibrium introduces systematic [[mass-discrepancies|Mass discrepancies]] when compared to weak lensing mass calibrations. These observational tensions are frequently attributed to unthermalized bulk motions, line-of-sight projection effects, or cluster triaxiality [Marrone et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...754..119M/abstract). Consequently, care must be taken during statistical analysis; applying fully parametric models to the intracluster medium can implicitly bias the derived scaling relation slopes toward the theoretical self-similar expectation, potentially masking the true physical impact of baryonic feedback [Mantz et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011arXiv1106.4052M/abstract).

## Historical Development and Classical Scaling Laws

The empirical foundation of [[galaxy-scaling-relations|Galaxy Scaling Relations]] began with the discovery of tight correlations linking the intrinsic luminosities of galaxies to their kinematic properties. For rotation-supported late-type galaxies, this is codified in the [[tully-fisher-relation|Tully-Fisher relation]], typically parameterized as
$$L = A V_{\rm max}^\alpha$$
where $L$ is the total luminosity and $V_{\rm max}$ is the maximum [[universal-rotation-curves|rotation velocity]]. Early formulations tracing dark matter in late-type galaxies revealed key structural dependencies, such as the finding that less luminous systems typically possess smaller halo core radii and higher central dark matter densities [Kormendy et al. (1990)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1990ASPC...10...33K/abstract). Observations constrain the logarithmic slope $\alpha$ to lie between 2.5 and 4, with the relation steepening in redder photometric bands. At a fixed $V_{\rm max}$, the intrinsic scatter in luminosity is relatively small, roughly 20%. While the slope of the relation is widely conserved across different types of disk galaxies, the zero-point $A$ is dependent on galaxy color and morphology; redder, early-type disks rotate faster than bluer, later-type disks of equivalent luminosity [Courteau et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003astro.ph.10440C/abstract). 

For pressure-supported early-type galaxies, the historical analogue is the Faber-Jackson relation, which connects total luminosity to the central stellar velocity dispersion, $\sigma$ [White et al. (1997)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1997gsr..proc....3W/abstract), [Trippe et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ZNatA..69..173T/abstract). This one-dimensional scaling law was later expanded into a more precise two-dimensional surface known as the [[fundamental-scaling-relations|Fundamental Plane]] [Zaritsky et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ISRAA2012E..12Z/abstract). The advent of major wide-field programs heavily accelerated this mapping. Early results from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), for instance, enabled vastly improved measurements of the local [[galaxy-luminosity-function|Galaxy luminosity function]] while simultaneously mapping extended dark matter distributions through pioneering weak lensing observations [Weinberg et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ASPC..283....3W/abstract). Utilizing SDSS weak lensing, [[cosmological-probes|astronomers]] successfully demonstrated a rigorous linear scaling relation between total galaxy mass and red-band luminosity that held remarkably consistent across differing environments [McKay et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001astro.ph..8013M/abstract). Over time, massive datasets from large surveys have systematically established the global distributions of stellar mass, luminosity, and [[mass-distribution-and-functions|gas mass functions]] in the nearby universe [Blanton et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ARA&A..47..159B/abstract).

The physical basis for the Fundamental Plane is the [[virial-theorem-and-parameters|Virial Theorem and Parameters]], which naturally relates structural and dynamical quantities [Dantas et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000ApJ...528L...5D/abstract), [Cappellari et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015IAUS..311...20C/abstract), [D'Onofrio et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...675A.186D/abstract). By applying a concentration-based correction, researchers have demonstrated the existence of a "generalized Faber-Jackson relation" that brings both rotation-supported disk galaxies and pressure-supported spheroidal galaxies onto a single morphology-independent dynamical sequence [Catinella et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.420.1959C/abstract). However, the observed plane exhibits a prominent "tilt" relative to simple virial predictions. Detailed dynamical modeling suggests that the extraordinarily low scatter in these structural scaling relations ultimately arises from a physical requirement for a constant product between the scaling radius and central density [Zhang et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.455.1364Z/abstract). Furthermore, targeted analyses within dense environments, such as the Virgo cluster, reveal that the tilt of the Fundamental Plane correlates strongly with the ratio of dynamical-to-stellar mass enclosed within galaxies' [[half-mass-radius|half-light radii]] [Ouellette et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...843...74O/abstract). The physical origin of this tilt remains debated: it is either primarily driven by systematic variations in stellar populations [Cappellari et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015IAUS..311...20C/abstract), or it results from structural non-homology and variations in the [[dark-matter-fraction|Dark matter fraction]] enclosed within the luminous radius [Pahre et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001Ap&SS.276..983P/abstract), [Ferrero et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...648A.124F/abstract). Notably, the position of [[dwarf-galaxy-populations|dwarf galaxies]] on the Stellar Mass Fundamental Plane has been shown to be strictly consistent between dense cluster environments and the Local Group, suggesting the physical processes setting these coordinates are largely environment-independent [Eftekhari et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.4714E/abstract).

In addition to kinematic relations, pure photometric correlations such as the Kormendy relation characterize the structural properties of [[intrinsic-galaxy-and-stellar-properties|early-type]] systems [Mosenkov et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.1066M/abstract). Modern studies caution that contemporary structural scaling laws involving sizes and concentrations are frequently observed to be curved rather than linear; additionally, some high-redshift compact elliptical galaxies are now understood as representing the isolated bulges of modern disk galaxies [Graham et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013pss6.book...91G/abstract). Alternatively, ongoing observations indicate that compact dwarf galaxies undergoing high rates of star formation and merging also serve as potential progenitors for the [[galaxy-structure|compact elliptical galaxy]] population [Nidhi Chhatkuli et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250409801N/abstract). The complex assembly histories required to generate these objects have been rigorously validated; theoretical models mapping multiple dry mergers successfully replicate both the slopes and the intrinsic scatters observed in structural scaling relations across cosmic time [D'Onofrio et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250110047D/abstract). At macroscopic scales, the observed scaling relations of [[galaxy-cluster-properties-and-scaling-relations|galaxy clusters]] can be broadly explained by an overarching, systematic trend in their overall mass-to-light ratio, mirroring the behavior classically seen in individual elliptical galaxies [Lanzoni et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...600..640L/abstract).

Modern multi-wavelength surveys have increasingly sought to unify distinct empirical laws into a single theoretical framework. By replacing luminosity with stellar mass, researchers have constructed a morphology-independent Mass Fundamental Plane. Detailed dynamical modeling of the Coma cluster provides a precise benchmark, revealing a Mass Plane that aligns closely with pure virial theorem expectations and provides stringent constraints on the stellar initial mass function [Shetty et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.494.5619S/abstract). Remarkably, massive star-forming and quiescent galaxies out to redshift $z \sim 0.7$ lie on nearly the identical Mass Fundamental Plane, exhibiting an intrinsic scatter as low as $0.055$ dex locally [Bezanson et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...799..148B/abstract). This striking convergence supports the paradigm that such [[fundamental-scaling-relations|Fundamental scaling relations]] are minimally susceptible to progenitor bias and emerge naturally from the underlying [[hierarchical-merger-paradigm|hierarchical assembly]] of cold dark matter halos [White et al. (1997)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1997gsr..proc....3W/abstract), [Ferrero et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...648A.124F/abstract).

## The Stellar-to-Halo Mass Relation (SHMR)

The Stellar-to-Halo Mass Relation (SHMR) parametrizes the fundamental link between a galaxy's luminous [[stellar-component|Stellar component]] and the mass of its host [[dark-matter-halo-properties-and-statistics|dark matter halo]]. If galaxies traced dark matter perfectly, the predicted luminosity function would vastly overpredict observed galaxy counts on both large and small scales, unless the [[high-redshift-cosmic-star-formation-efficiencies|star-formation efficiency]] $\epsilon_{\rm SF}$ or the [[galaxy-mass-to-light-ratio|Galaxy mass-to-light ratio]] $\Upsilon_\star$ vary significantly as a function of halo mass. High-resolution simulations of Milky Way satellite analogues further confirm the primacy of localized baryon physics, showing that robust scaling relations for stellar mass, physical size, and metallicity are already set in place long before these objects are ultimately accreted into larger dark matter halos [Macciò et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.472.2356M/abstract).

To account for this, the SHMR is conventionally modeled as a [[broken-power-law|Broken power law]], characterized by a [[pivot-mass|Pivot mass]] where the baryon conversion efficiency reaches its maximum. In the local universe, this peak efficiency occurs at a halo mass of $M_{\rm halo} \sim 10^{12} M_\odot$. Below this threshold, energetic feedback from supernovae and photoionization heating from reionization suppress star formation; above it, active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback and long gas cooling times prevent further stellar mass growth. The stellar [[pivot-mass|Pivot mass]] $M_p$ is not static but evolves with redshift, increasing from $\log(M_p/M_\odot) \approx 10.2$ at $z=0.4$ to $\log(M_p/M_\odot) \approx 10.9$ at $z=1.7-3$ [Mowla et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872L..13M/abstract). Notably, this evolving pivot mass closely coincides with the threshold where the star-forming galaxy fraction is 50%, reflecting a transition from accretion-dominated growth to depletion-dominated growth around $z \sim 1.5$ [Mowla et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872L..13M/abstract), [Pan et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...885L..14P/abstract). Furthermore, scaling relations extend beyond the stellar component; observations of isolated disk galaxies reveal a universal [[h-i-mass-to-halo-mass-relation|H I-to-dark halo mass ratio]] that holds remarkably constant across four orders of magnitude in stellar mass, suggesting a mass-independent self-regulation mechanism governs their formation [Korsaga et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...952L..41K/abstract).

At the high-mass end of the SHMR, the exact power-law slope describing the decline in star formation efficiency remains a subject of debate. While historical parameterizations often adopted a shallow high-mass slope of $\beta \sim 0.2$, observations of [[massive-galaxies|Massive galaxies]] suggest a significantly steeper slope of $\beta \sim 0.35-0.70$ that persists up to $z \sim 1$ [Shankar et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...797L..27S/abstract).

The [[intrinsic-scatter|Intrinsic scatter]] of the SHMR at $z=0$ is remarkably tight, measured to be $\sim 0.16-0.2$ dex for massive halos ($M_{\rm halo} > 10^{12} M_\odot$) [Gu et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...833....2G/abstract), [Shankar et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...797L..27S/abstract). This approximately constant scatter is interpreted as a coincidence of two physical regimes: mass assembly is driven primarily by in situ star formation at lower masses, whereas it is governed by the [[hierarchical-merger-paradigm|Hierarchical merger paradigm]] at higher masses [Gu et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...833....2G/abstract). For [[satellite-stellar-mass-functions|satellite galaxies]], variations in the SHMR are driven primarily by orbital histories and compactness rather than the bulk properties of the host cluster [Niemiec et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.512.6021N/abstract). In the regime of [[low-mass-galaxies|Low-mass galaxies]], theoretical semi-empirical models predict that the scatter should rise significantly—reaching $\sim 0.5$ dex at $M_{\rm halo} \sim 10^9 M_\odot$—due to the stochastic quenching effects of reionization, while the underlying dark matter halos continue to grow [Kim et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240815214K/abstract).

Recent literature highlights a significant structural bifurcation in the SHMR depending on a galaxy's evolutionary state: at a fixed stellar mass, quiescent central galaxies reside in systematically more massive dark matter halos than their star-forming counterparts [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240807743W/abstract), [Wang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...980..233W/abstract). This indicates that star-forming centrals are more efficient at converting baryons into stars. Reproducing this dichotomy poses a major challenge for modern theoretical frameworks. While semi-analytical models (such as the [[munich-galaxy-formation-model|Munich galaxy formation model]], L-Galaxies) successfully reproduce this split, state-of-the-art [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] (including IllustrisTNG and EAGLE) currently struggle to replicate the observed difference, often predicting incorrectly similar conversion efficiencies for both populations [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240807743W/abstract), [Wang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...980..233W/abstract).

## Galaxy Cluster Scaling Relations and Mass Proxies

Galaxy clusters are the largest virialized structures in the Universe, making them ideal laboratories for probing cosmology and structure formation. Their global integrated observables—such as [[x-ray-scaling-relations|X-ray luminosity]] ($L_X$), intracluster medium (ICM) temperature ($T_X$), [[gas-fraction|gas mass]] ($M_{\rm gas}$), and the integrated Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) signal ($Y$)—are strongly correlated with their [[total-mass-astrophysics|Total mass (astrophysics)]], $M_{\rm tot}$. Assuming pure gravitational collapse, the self-similar model predicts specific power-law scalings between these parameters, such as $M_{\rm tot} \propto T_X^{3/2}$ and $M_{\rm tot} \propto Y^{3/5}$. Observations using X-ray and microwave facilities confirm that these self-similar predictions generally hold remarkably well outside of cluster cores (e.g., at radii $r > 0.2 r_{500}$) [Zhang et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007A&A...467..437Z/abstract), [Mantz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.3582M/abstract). Specifically, observations derived from instruments like the AMiBA project robustly support the self-similar predictions that the integrated Compton-y parameter closely scales with both cluster gas temperature and total mass [Huang et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...716..758H/abstract). 

Despite the success of the self-similar framework, notable departures exist, particularly in low-mass clusters and groups. The $L_X-T_X$ and $L_X-M_{\rm tot}$ relations are typically steeper than gravity-only predictions. This steepening is primarily attributed to [[baryonic-matter-and-evolution|baryonic physics]], specifically AGN feedback, which expels low-entropy gas from shallower potential wells and heats the remaining ICM [Maughan et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.437.1171M/abstract). Cosmological hydrodynamical simulations indicate that simple power-law models fail to capture the curvature in the [[gas-fraction|Gas fraction]] scaling relation at masses below $\sim 10^{14} M_\odot$, where baryon redistribution is most pronounced [Miller et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250420159M/abstract), [Rasia et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250521624R/abstract). Furthermore, the IllustrisTNG simulations show that across the vast mass range spanning from individual galaxies to massive clusters, local universe scaling relations exhibit a prominent break, indicating they are better described by a broken power law rather than a single continuous slope [Pop et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv220511528P/abstract). Additionally, cool-core phenomena drive significant [[intrinsic-scatter|Intrinsic scatter]] in the $L_X$ and $T_X$ scaling relations [O'Hara et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ApJ...639...64O/abstract).

Because X-ray luminosity is highly sensitive to the dense gas in cluster cores, modern mass calibration heavily relies on the integrated SZ Compton parameter ($Y_{\rm SZ}$) and $M_{\rm gas}$, which act as the most robust, low-scatter [[mass-proxy|Mass proxy]] observables. Theoretical models of structure formation consistently highlight the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect as a highly promising tool to obtain precision cluster mass measurements in high-redshift samples, independent of distance-related dimming [Norman et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005bmri.conf....1N/abstract). Multi-wavelength analyses show that the fractional intrinsic scatter of $M_{\rm gas}$ at fixed halo mass is as low as $\sim 0.16$ [Mulroy et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484...60M/abstract). Similarly, the $Y_{\rm SZ}-M_{\rm tot}$ relation is tightly correlated, though calibrations against weak lensing (WL) reveal an intrinsic scatter in $M_{\rm WL}$ at fixed $Y$ of $\sim 20\%$ [Marrone et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...754..119M/abstract). Increasingly precise calibrations draw from surveys like the Subaru Hyper Suprime-Cam (HSC), whose high-quality [[3x2pt-analysis|weak lensing]] measurements provide rigorous constraints on the physical scaling relations connecting galaxy group mass with total luminosity and velocity dispersion [Rana et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.510.5408R/abstract). 

Stellar observables also play a central role. Using mid-infrared photometry from the Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer (WISE), observers have derived precise empirical scaling relations to estimate robust [[galaxy-mass-to-light-ratio|stellar masses]] across highly diverse galaxy and group types [Jarrett et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...946...95J/abstract). When applied to large local datasets, such as the Northern Sky Optical Cluster Survey (NoSOCS) within SDSS, cluster richness and optical luminosity have been rigorously validated as highly reliable mass proxies. Crucially, these optical mass indicators exhibit intrinsic scatters entirely comparable to precision X-ray mass tracers [Lopes et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009MNRAS.399.2201L/abstract). Modern validations of total cluster stellar mass ($\mu_\star$) confirm this utility, exhibiting a mass-conditioned scatter of approximately $0.26$ in natural logarithm ($\sim 0.11$ dex) [Palmese et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.493.4591P/abstract). Additionally, researchers have developed novel analytical mass profiles that construct direct structural scaling relations, systematically connecting directly observable properties in cluster interiors with total gravitational masses at the outer [[cosmological-turnaround-and-mass-scales|turnaround scale]] [Korkidis et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240218634K/abstract).

The calibration of these scaling relations often exposes a [[mass-discrepancy|Mass discrepancy]] between dynamical or hydrostatic equilibrium (HSE) masses and the true gravitational mass measured via weak lensing. Alternatively, modern analyses leverage the [[cosmological-turnaround-and-mass-scales|splashback radius]] to extract dynamical cluster mass measurements directly from photometric surveys without the need for spectroscopic velocity information [Contigiani et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.518.2640C/abstract). [[hierarchical-merger-paradigm|Major mergers]] and complex substructure drive asymmetric scatter in the scaling laws, generally biasing inferred [[equilibrium-models|HSE masses]] lower than their true values [Krause et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.419.1766K/abstract). Clusters with significant substructure also tend to be cooler at a fixed halo mass compared to their relaxed counterparts [Ventimiglia et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...685..118V/abstract). Furthermore, line-of-sight projection effects and cluster triaxiality heavily influence lensing-derived masses, leading to morphology-dependent biases in the derived relations [Marrone et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...754..119M/abstract). These systematic uncertainties manifest prominently in precision cosmology; for example, the $Y$-mass scaling relation measured directly from weak lensing exhibits a strong departure from the relation assumed in the Planck cosmology results [Mantz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.3582M/abstract).

## Supermassive Black Hole Co-evolution

The correlation between the mass of a central supermassive black hole ($M_{\rm BH}$) and the large-scale properties of its host galaxy is a cornerstone of [[galaxy-and-universe-evolution|Galaxy and Universe evolution]] models [Combes et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230212917C/abstract). The tightest and most well-studied of these empirical laws is the $M-\sigma$ relation, which links the black hole mass to the stellar velocity dispersion ($\sigma$) of the host's bulge. Local kinematic constraints also provide essential boundary conditions; for example, precision kinematic measurements of [[intrinsic-galaxy-and-stellar-properties|stellar systems]] in our own local neighborhood, such as the Large Magellanic Cloud, place stringent upper mass limits on central black holes that perfectly align with established galactic scaling models [Boyce et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...846...14B/abstract). Interestingly, these physical correlations extend beyond the global properties of the galaxy; studies demonstrate that tight scaling relations between velocity dispersion, stellar age, and metallicity persist even at the [[galaxy-structure|nuclear and circumnuclear scales]] (spanning tens of parsecs) within nearby galaxies [da Silva et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...702A..94D/abstract). While correlations with total stellar mass and bulge mass are also well-established, investigations show that nuclear star clusters—despite also being central massive objects—exhibit significantly shallower mass scaling relations than SMBHs (e.g., $M_{\rm NC} \propto M_{\rm Gal, dyn}^{0.55 \pm 0.15}$). This indicates that nuclear star clusters and supermassive black holes do not form a single evolutionary family governed by identical scaling laws [Scott et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...763...76S/abstract).

Recent analyses reveal that [[observable-mass-scaling-relations|Observable-mass scaling relations]] for SMBHs are highly dependent on galaxy morphology, reflecting distinct evolutionary pathways for [[asymmetric-galaxy-morphologies-and-kinematics|barred]] versus unbarred, and early-type versus late-type systems [Sahu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022hypa.confE..24S/abstract). To minimize the intrinsic scatter of these empirical laws, high-dimensional scaling relations have been constructed using symbolic regression. For instance, a relation combining the central velocity dispersion, the bulge-to-total ratio, and the density at the black hole sphere of influence yields an intrinsic scatter as low as $0.083$ dex. This is a significant improvement over the typical $\sim 0.3$ dex scatter of the traditional $M-\sigma$ relation [Jin et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv231019406J/abstract).

The physical origin of these tight correlations is generally attributed to the self-regulating nature of AGN feedback. As the black hole accretes, it injects energy into the surrounding medium, driving outflows that expel gas and subsequently quench both star formation and further black hole accretion [Combes et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230212917C/abstract). Both negative and positive AGN feedback modes are required in advanced semi-analytic models to simultaneously match the observed $M_{\rm BH}$ scaling relations and the broader [[star-forming-main-sequence|Star-Forming Main Sequence]] [Contini et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240510544C/abstract). Observational support for this energetic coupling is found in the X-ray regime, where the temperature of circumgalactic hot gas is observed to be higher in galaxies hosting more massive black holes at a fixed stellar velocity dispersion [Martín-Navarro et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.491.1311M/abstract).

Despite theoretical successes, accurately reproducing the slopes and zero-points of both the $M_{\rm BH}-\sigma$ and $M_{\rm BH}-M_*$ relations simultaneously remains a challenge. A major source of tension is the presence of observational selection effects: the requirement to spatially resolve the black hole sphere of influence systematically biases dynamically measured SMBH samples toward higher masses [Barausse et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.468.4782B/abstract). Robust, high-resolution measurements—such as recent JWST stellar dynamics observations—serve as critical anchor points to recalibrate these biases [Nguyen et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260117439N/abstract). Furthermore, these relations are not static; state-of-the-art semi-analytic models predict significant [[mass-evolution|Mass evolution]] across cosmic time. At a fixed stellar mass, SMBH masses may have been up to $\sim 1$ dex higher at $z=9$ compared to the local universe, with the scatter around the scaling relation increasing by a factor of $2-5$ toward $z=0$ [Bravo et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250603354B/abstract). Observations showing a pronounced doubling in the characteristic sizes of massive galaxies since $z=1$ correspondingly imply that deep-time black hole merger rates are significantly higher than previously estimated, potentially generating detectable stochastic backgrounds of gravitational waves [McWilliams et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012arXiv1211.4590M/abstract). Characterizing this precise evolutionary timeline—specifically determining whether black holes or host galaxies systematically grow faster out to $z \sim 1.5$ and identifying their ultimate maximum mass limits—was formally prioritized as a key observational goal for next-generation telescope facilities in the Astro2020 Decadal Survey [Gültekin et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019arXiv190401447G/abstract).

## Chemical Abundances and the Mass-Metallicity Relation

The mass-metallicity relation (MZR) establishes that galaxies with higher stellar mass ($M_\star$) systematically exhibit higher gas-phase and stellar metallicities ($Z$). This correlation is a fundamental feature of [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] in the universe and offers direct insight into the baryonic lifecycle of galaxies [Maiolino et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&ARv..27....3M/abstract). At a fixed stellar mass, observations reveal a secondary anticorrelation between metallicity and the star formation rate (SFR), defining a tighter 3D surface known as the [[metallicity-distributions-and-scaling-relations|Fundamental Metallicity Relation]] (FMR). Critically, scaling correlations mapping properties such as total metallicity and star formation rate have been shown to be highly sensitive to their broader geometric environment, meaning that a galaxy's specific placement within the filaments and voids of the cosmic web influences its chemical makeup [Nandi et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...02..012N/abstract). Furthermore, investigations into [[h-i-mass-function|neutral hydrogen (HI)]] scaling relations reveal an analogous dependency; galaxies residing in high-density environments are typically significantly more HI-deficient than field galaxies of similar stellar mass [Dénes et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.444..667D/abstract).

The physical origin of the MZR and FMR is best understood through the framework of **baryon cycling**—a dynamic equilibrium between the accretion of pristine intergalactic gas, the conversion of gas into stars along the [[star-forming-main-sequence|Star-Forming Main Sequence]], and the expulsion of metal-enriched material via galactic outflows [Mitra et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1184M/abstract), [Chen et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.1899C/abstract). Collectively, the chemical abundances and precise spatial distribution of synthesized metals provide an integrated, observable record mapping out this fundamental [[baryonic-matter-and-evolution|baryon cycle]] across cosmic history [Curti et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250408933C/abstract). The retention of these synthesized metals is primarily regulated by the depth of a galaxy's gravitational potential well ($\Phi \propto M_\star / R_e$), which dictates the local escape velocity required for supernova-driven winds to permanently eject enriched gas from the halo [Barone et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...898...62B/abstract). Consequently, low-mass dwarf galaxies ($M_\star \lesssim 10^9 M_\odot$) suffer from highly efficient metal ejection. This characteristic mass scale corresponds to an observed slope change in both the MZR and the [[h-i-mass-function|atomic gas sequence]], marking a critical transition in galaxy evolution where feedback-driven gas loss dominates over retention [Scholte et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240803996S/abstract).

Analytical models demonstrate that the intrinsic scatter of the FMR is ultimately driven by stochastic variations in the gas accretion rate, while the variation in the outflow mass-loading factor at a fixed mass remains remarkably small ($\lesssim 0.1$ dex) [Forbes et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.443..168F/abstract). This self-regulated balance maintains the shape of the stellar MZR across long stretches of cosmic time, though the overall metallicity normalization increases significantly from $z \sim 2$ to $z \sim 1$ as galaxies continually enrich their interstellar mediums [Taylor et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.2465T/abstract). Interestingly, while distant high-redshift galaxies generally possess much higher total molecular gas fractions, comparative studies indicate that the global integrated abundance of atomic hydrogen evolves very weakly with cosmic time [Lagos et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012arXiv1210.4974L/abstract).

While the FMR successfully unifies the [[star-formation-history|Star Formation History]] and chemical enrichment of galaxies over the latter half of the universe's history, its universality breaks down at very high redshifts. Recent JWST observations of galaxies at $z=7-10$ reveal that they deviate significantly from the established FMR, possessing only about one-fourth of the chemical abundance expected for their mass and SFR [Heintz et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023NatAs...7.1517H/abstract).

## Angular Momentum and Structural Scaling

The spatial extent and rotational support of a galaxy are intrinsically linked to the mass and assembly history of its host dark matter halo. These structural scaling relations—specifically the [[size-mass-relation|Size-mass relation]] (or Mass-Radius relation) and the Fall relation—provide crucial constraints on the partition of baryons and dark matter during disk formation and morphological evolution.

The [[size-mass-relation|Size-mass relation]] characterizes how galaxy size scales with stellar mass, typically exhibiting a morphology- and mass-dependent broken power-law behavior. For early-type galaxies, the [[half-mass-radius|half-light radius]] scales steeply at high masses ($R_{1/2} \propto M_*^{0.54}$) but follows a shallower slope ($R_{1/2} \propto M_*^{0.3}$) for dwarf systems [Chiosi et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012arXiv1206.2532C/abstract). Extending this to higher redshifts ($z \sim 3$) using the radius containing 80% of the light ($r_{80}$), the scaling reveals a distinct pivot mass. Below this pivot mass, sizes grow slowly with mass ($r_{80} \propto M_*^{0.15}$), whereas above it the relation steepens significantly ($r_{80} \propto M_*^{0.6}$) [Mowla et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872L..13M/abstract). This pivot mass evolves from approximately $10^{10.2} M_\odot$ at $z=0.4$ to $10^{10.9} M_\odot$ at $z \sim 2$, closely tracking the mass scale where the star-forming fraction is 50%. This suggests the break in the [[size-mass-relation|Size-mass relation]] reflects a transition from dissipational to dissipationless galaxy growth [Mowla et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872L..13M/abstract). Furthermore, the stellar surface mass density, $\Sigma \propto M/R_e^2$, is a primary structural driver of stellar population age, indicating that compact galaxies assembled their [[stellar-component|Stellar component]] earlier in the universe when gas fractions were higher [Barone et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...898...62B/abstract). Additional structural nuances have been revealed by rigorously applying dust- and inclination-corrections to observed profiles, which demonstrate that star-forming disks are, on average, systematically more physically extended than the underlying stellar continuum emission would immediately suggest [Pastrav et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.52711167P/abstract).

The rotational support of a galaxy is quantified by the Fall relation, which links the [[specific-angular-momentum|Specific Angular Momentum]] ($j$) of the baryonic or stellar component to its total mass. To ensure robust foundational theories, [[hierarchical-merger-paradigm|hierarchical models]] of galactic disk formation have been rigorously benchmarked against detailed structural scaling relations charting rotation velocity, galaxy size, and total luminosity in spiral systems [Dutton et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ASPC..396..463D/abstract). Late-type and early-type galaxies form parallel but distinct sequences in the $j-M$ plane. Late-type disk galaxies generally retain a specific angular momentum consistent with the full conservation of the initial angular momentum generated by cosmological tidal torques. In contrast, early-type galaxies typically retain only $\sim 30\%$ of this initial angular momentum, often due to abrupt losses during major mergers [Genel et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804L..40G/abstract). The underlying structural connection remains strong across observational methodologies; for instance, modeling the [[universal-rotation-curves|rotation curves]] of nearby galaxies (such as those in the SPARC dataset) using generalized [[einasto-profile|Einasto profiles]] reveals a direct positive correlation between the extended dark matter core size and the stellar disk scale-length [Ghari et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...623A.123G/abstract).

Observations and [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] demonstrate that the scatter in the stellar Fall relation is heavily influenced by the neutral gas content. At a fixed stellar mass, [[h-i-rich-galaxies|H I-rich galaxies]] possess systematically higher specific angular momentum than their gas-poor counterparts [Elson et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023NewA...9901964E/abstract). Advanced computational models, such as the Simba cosmological simulations, demonstrate that the extremely tight empirical correlation forming the HI mass-size relation is highly robust even against broad systematic changes in modeled feedback physics, reflecting an inherent structural self-similarity dictating atomic gas distributions globally [Rabyang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251204582R/abstract). Theoretical models suggest that these macroscopic gas and stellar scaling relations are fundamentally driven by disk gravitational instability and the remarkable uniformity of the Toomre $Q$ parameter across varying galaxy morphologies [Romeo et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.491.4843R/abstract). Replicating the slope, zero-point, and small intrinsic scatter of these relations necessitates carefully tuned subgrid physics. Simulations such as Illustris show that energetic galactic winds with high mass-loading factors are required to prevent catastrophic angular momentum loss and reproduce the extended disks of late-type galaxies, whereas active galactic nucleus feedback operates in the opposite direction [Genel et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804L..40G/abstract).

Additionally, the remarkable tightness of the [[size-mass-relation|Size-mass relation]] and the Fall relation poses a theoretical challenge, given that the distribution of dark matter halo spin parameters ($\lambda \approx 0.035$) and concentrations should introduce substantial intrinsic scatter. A proposed theoretical solution is that an inherent anti-correlation between halo spin and concentration in cold dark matter models may act to suppress the expected covariance in the residuals of these observable scaling laws, maintaining their tightness across a wide range of masses. However, the authors caution that this suppression mechanism is only effective within a relatively small portion of the explored theoretical parameter space, meaning it may not provide a universal explanation for the observed tightness [Posti et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...644A..76P/abstract).

## Redshift Evolution and Scaling at Cosmic Dawn

While several [[galaxy-scaling-relations|Galaxy Scaling Relations]], such as the [[size-mass-relation|Size-mass relation]] and the baryonic Tully-Fisher relation, are observed to be remarkably stable or evolve only mildly out to $z \sim 2-3$ [Taylor et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.2465T/abstract), [Bell et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ASPC..396..389B/abstract), pushing observations into the epoch of reionization and cosmic dawn ($z > 6$) reveals substantial shifts in the fundamental coupling between baryons and dark matter. The empirical [[redshift-evolution-and-distribution|redshift evolution]] of foundational metrics—such as the Tully-Fisher and surface-brightness scaling relations measured reliably out to $z=3$—provides a highly sought-after potential discriminator for resolving competing models of broad galaxy formation [Buchalter et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001MNRAS.328..520B/abstract). Alternatively, sweeping gravity-based theoretical paradigms attempt to unify both galaxy and massive cluster scaling laws by proposing that their apparent mass-velocity offsets simply arise naturally as an emergent consequence of cosmic time evolution across a shared gravitational continuum [Marongwe et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251120188M/abstract). 

Recent JWST observations, combined with sub-millimeter data from ALMA, have established that a well-defined [[star-forming-main-sequence|Star-Forming Main Sequence]] exists as early as $z < 10$ [Di Cesare et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.4632D/abstract). The galaxies in this epoch exhibit rapid mass assembly and high specific star formation rates that are sustained by efficient cold gas accretion. Notably, JWST has identified unexpectedly massive galaxies at $z \simeq 8-10$ that challenge standard $\Lambda$CDM predictions. Historically, theoretical frameworks have projected that exceptionally massive objects existing at high redshifts ($z \geq 4$) would likely lie upon intrinsic dynamical scaling planes from their very inception, despite exhibiting a systematic structural tilt relative to fully relaxed virial planes observed locally [Oñorbe et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...732L..32O/abstract). These massive early systems are systematically underpredicted by the traditional [[sheth-tormen-mass-function|Sheth-Tormen mass function]], requiring either extremely high star formation efficiencies or more realistic non-linear collapse models for their host dark matter halos to reconcile theory with observations [Fakhry et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250723742F/abstract). Furthermore, populations of low-mass galaxies ($10^8 < M_\star/M_\odot < 10^9$) at $z \le 6-7$ are found to exceed some current estimates of the stellar mass function, acting as a primary source of [[intrinsic-scatter|Intrinsic scatter]] in the high-redshift stellar-to-halo mass relation [Di Cesare et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.4632D/abstract). The stochasticity and intrinsic scatter present within scaling relations describing these discrete, high-redshift galaxy populations are formally predicted to introduce highly significant shot noise complications when mapping the cosmological 21cm power spectrum during the epoch of reionization [Davies et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250417254D/abstract). 

Chemical scaling relations also undergo a pronounced transition during cosmic dawn. While lower-redshift star-forming galaxies tightly adhere to a universal fundamental metallicity relation, JWST spectroscopic observations of galaxies at $z = 7-10$ demonstrate a clear deviation. Galaxies observed approximately 600 Myr after the Big Bang possess chemical abundances roughly one-fourth of the expected value for their stellar mass and star formation rate [Heintz et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023NatAs...7.1517H/abstract).

Finally, redshift evolution extensively impacts the co-evolution of galaxies, clusters, and supermassive black holes (SMBHs). On macro scales, meticulously tracing the hierarchical evolution of massive red galaxies inside galaxy clusters utilizing dark matter halo merger trees demonstrates periods of highly significant stellar growth occurring in brightest cluster galaxies directly between $z=1.0$ and $z=0.3$ [Lin et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250313592L/abstract). In galaxy centers, theoretical models tuned to match high-redshift scaling relations suggest significant temporal evolution in the SMBH-stellar mass plane. Simulations indicate that the SMBH mass at a fixed stellar mass may be up to $\sim 1$ dex higher at $z=9$ compared to $z=0$ [Bravo et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250603354B/abstract). This implies that early SMBH growth outpaced the stellar mass assembly of the host galaxy, a dynamic that later reverses as feedback and quenching mechanisms regulate [[galaxy-and-universe-evolution|Galaxy and Universe evolution]] in the lower-redshift universe.

## Statistical Modeling: Intrinsic Scatter and Selection Bias

Accurately characterizing the [[intrinsic-scatter|Intrinsic scatter]] of mass-observable relations requires separating underlying physical variations from measurement uncertainties. Classical first-order estimators systematically underestimate true intrinsic scatter by approximately $25\%$, necessitating the use of [[hierarchical-bayesian-inference|Hierarchical Bayesian inference]] frameworks that properly account for non-linear error propagation and covariant uncertainties [Stone et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...912...41S/abstract). In multivariate scaling frameworks, the explicit treatment of property covariance is essential; omitting the intrinsic covariance between observables such as X-ray luminosity and temperature can severely bias the inferred slopes of cluster scaling laws [Rozo et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.438...62R/abstract), [Mulroy et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484...60M/abstract). Furthermore, extracting rigorous statistics at varying scales requires nuanced techniques. For example, for massive, highly biased cosmic tracers, theoretical treatments show that the one-halo terms corresponding to both the power spectrum and trispectrum rigidly obey an exceptionally steep, predictive scaling relation expressed purely in terms of bulk galaxy bias [Abramo et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.454.2844A/abstract). Furthermore, the mass correlation function can be computed strictly analytically utilizing the dynamical evolution of the pair-conservation equation, providing a versatile physical tool to precisely fit N-body simulations spanning from purely linear down to highly non-linear clustering regimes [Caldwell et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001ApJ...547L..93C/abstract).

The magnitude and symmetry of intrinsic scatter provide direct constraints on the physical drivers of galaxy and cluster evolution. At the galaxy cluster scale, integrated Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) signals and gas mass serve as highly robust proxies, exhibiting a fractional intrinsic scatter of $\sim 0.16$ at fixed [[total-mass-astrophysics|Total mass (astrophysics)]] [Mulroy et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484...60M/abstract). However, major mergers induce asymmetric scatter, biasing the inferred [[dynamical-mass|Dynamical mass]] of disturbed systems toward lower values [Krause et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.419.1766K/abstract). Broad statistical frameworks leverage scaling properties linking total galaxy mass and intrinsic star formation histories directly to retroactively reconstruct an individual galaxy's assembly path, yielding powerful phenomenological tools to systematically constrain parameters like the expected delay time distribution of binary neutron star mergers within the local universe [Safarzadeh et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...878L..14S/abstract). On galactic scales, the scatter in the stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR) is remarkably small at $z=0$ ($\sim 0.2$ dex). Cosmological simulations indicate this scatter is driven by in situ growth histories at low masses and by hierarchical assembly variance ($\approx 0.16$ dex) in massive halos [Gu et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...833....2G/abstract). Incorporating simulation-informed scatter models into weak lensing analyses significantly tightens constraints on these scaling laws [Li et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241103152L/abstract), [Li et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...700A.202L/abstract). Similarly, modeling conditional luminosity functions—which explicitly define the broad distribution of available galaxies as a strict function of total underlying halo mass—can be robustly anchored and predicted entirely by extrapolating localized central galaxy scaling relations across mass populations [Cooray et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.363..337C/abstract).

Observational selection biases can severely distort the inferred parameters of [[scaling-relation-models|Scaling relation models]]. In X-ray cluster surveys, flux-limited selection effects can easily mimic redshift evolution if not rigorously modeled alongside the underlying halo [[mass-distribution-and-functions|mass function]] [Nord et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.383L..10N/abstract), [Mantz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.3582M/abstract). Additionally, the choice of parameterization introduces implicit priors; fully parametric models of the intracluster medium inherently favor self-similar slopes, whereas non-parametric methods avoid this bias [Mantz et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011arXiv1106.4052M/abstract). For instance, photometric stellar mass estimates derived from spectral energy distribution (SED) modeling generally show systematic agreement with dynamical estimates in nearby galaxies, yielding statistically indistinguishable scatter when applied to dynamical scaling laws like the Tully-Fisher and Fall relations. In contrast, alternative mass estimation methods derived purely under the assumption that galaxy discs are only marginally stable introduce the most substantial scatter into these relations [Marasco et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...695L..23M/abstract). Selection biases also plague scaling relations for supermassive black holes (SMBHs). The observational requirement to resolve the black hole's sphere of influence biases the $M_{\bullet}-\sigma$ relation high; semi-analytic models align with observational data only when this selection effect is explicitly applied [Barausse et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.468.4782B/abstract).

The exact measurement of intrinsic scatter remains a point of active debate, particularly in evaluating modified gravity versus cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) paradigms. Studies of the [[radial-acceleration-relation|Radial Acceleration Relation]] (RAR) have yielded conflicting results regarding its intrinsic scatter. Some analyses report an exceptionally tight relation with scatter as low as $0.045$ dex; rather than challenging dark matter, this low variance is utilized to precisely map the distribution of dark matter in galaxies, as well as to constrain models of modified dynamics [Vărăşteanu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.541.2366V/abstract). Conversely, detailed Monte Carlo models of observational errors estimate it slightly higher at $0.11 \pm 0.02$ dex [Stone et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...882....6S/abstract), a value more consistent with the $0.06 - 0.08$ dex scatter predicted by $\Lambda$CDM simulations incorporating baryonic feedback. Innovative high-level approaches continue to seek independent clarity; for instance, third-order galaxy [[halo-correlation-function|correlation functions]] mathematically derived from core principles of statistical mechanics provide completely independent, high-precision thermodynamic constraints on foundational cosmological scaling parameters such as $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_m$ [Choudhary et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250623829C/abstract).

## Testing Gravity and Cosmological Isotropy

Mass scaling relations are sensitive probes of the underlying gravitational framework and the large-scale geometry of the Universe. Because these empirical laws connect observable baryonic matter to the total dynamical mass, they provide rigorous test beds for the standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology against alternative gravity theories and assumptions of cosmic isotropy. One fundamental benchmark repeatedly emphasized in the literature is that for modified gravity paradigms to remain physically viable, they must consistently pass a key observational test: the inferred localized excess dynamical mass mapped must explicitly and strongly correlate with the independently observable visible baryonic mass distribution [Sellwood et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ASPC..273..243S/abstract). 

**The Radial Acceleration Relation and Modified Gravity**

The [[radial-acceleration-relation|Radial Acceleration Relation]] (RAR) and the [[baryonic-tully-fisher-relation|Baryonic Tully-Fisher relation]] define a remarkably tight coupling between the gravitational acceleration inferred from dynamics ($g_{\rm obs}$) and the Newtonian acceleration generated by the baryonic mass distribution ($g_{\rm bar}$). This empirical correlation spans approximately 16 orders of magnitude in baryonic acceleration [Lelli et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241102499L/abstract). The inherent structural simplicity driving galactic structural scaling relations—specifically the undeniable existence of a uniform, global [[universal-characteristic-acceleration-scale|characteristic acceleration]]—presents a profound ongoing challenge to $\Lambda$CDM formulations that heavily rely on notoriously complex stochastic baryonic feedback to shape structural formation [Walker et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ConPh..55..198W/abstract). Independent empirical confirmation strategies have developed to better contextualize this phenomenon. For instance, high-precision astrometry measuring distinct stellar kinematic orbits within the Milky Way directly maps escape velocity profiles, successfully providing an independent physical basis for directly contextualizing and interpreting the RAR without relying upon extragalactic generalized proxies [La Fortune et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020arXiv200810445L/abstract). The intrinsic scatter of the RAR is extremely low; while some studies utilizing spatially resolved stellar mass-to-light ratios report values as small as $0.045 \pm 0.022$ dex [Vărăşteanu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.541.2366V/abstract), detailed Monte Carlo models of observational errors estimate it slightly higher at $0.11 \pm 0.02$ dex [Stone et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...882....6S/abstract).

The physical origin of this tight coupling remains a subject of active debate. Proponents of Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) argue that the low scatter and predictive power of the RAR are manifestations of a fundamental change in the law of gravity at a universal characteristic acceleration scale, eliminating the need for dark matter [Trippe et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ZNatA..69..173T/abstract), [Lelli et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241102499L/abstract). Link "Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND)" to [[milgrom-relation|Milgrom relation]]. Parallel advanced models, such as the scalar-vector-tensor modified gravity (MOG) theory, similarly aim to construct testable cosmological alternatives independent of both MOND and the standard cold dark matter paradigms [Moffat et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011arXiv1112.4386M/abstract). Conversely, other frameworks interpret the RAR as an emergent phenomenon driven either by complex baryonic feedback processes within the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm, or through novel interactions such as Baryon-Interacting Dark Matter [Famaey et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020JCAP...06..025F/abstract). Notably, alternative theories such as Superfluid Dark Matter—which replicate MOND-like phenomenology on galaxy scales—are challenged by local Milky Way kinematics, where vertical accelerations strongly prefer standard cold dark matter models [Lisanti et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023PDU....3901140L/abstract). Furthermore, while generalized theoretical mass-velocity relations hold tightly on galactic scales, rigorous observational tests utilizing galaxy clusters find little robust evidence supporting the existence of an identical, universal acceleration constant persisting across massive cluster scales [La Fortune et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021arXiv211002684L/abstract).

Scaling relations also place upper limits on the local effective cosmological constant from galactic rotation curves, yielding bounds on the vacuum energy density of $\rho_{(<\Lambda)} \sim 10^{-25}$ kg/m$^3$ [Benisty et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240516650B/abstract). These fundamental limits placed explicitly upon the local effective cosmological constant can be consistently and independently constrained across wide mass regimes by meticulously studying scaling relations applied simultaneously to massive super-spiral galaxies and nearby galaxy clusters [Amekhyan et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021RAA....21..309A/abstract). On cluster scales, modified gravity models such as chameleon $f(R)$ gravity have been tested against observable-mass relations, requiring precise analytical corrections to map their predictions to $\Lambda$CDM equivalents [Mitchell et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502.6101M/abstract).

**Cosmic Isotropy and Bulk Flows**

On sub-gigaparsec scales, [[galaxy-cluster-properties-and-scaling-relations|Galaxy Cluster Properties and Scaling Relations]] serve as independent tracers of the Hubble expansion and cosmic isotropy. Because the mapping between X-ray or thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) observables and cluster mass depends heavily on cosmological parameters, spatial variations in these scaling relations can reveal anisotropies in the local Universe. Recent observational analyses of cluster scaling relations have reported highly significant ($\sim 5.4\sigma$) spatial anisotropies in the Hubble constant ($H_0$), potentially indicative of large-scale bulk flows extending beyond $500$ Mpc [Migkas et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240601752M/abstract).

However, the interpretation of these anisotropies as a breakdown of $\Lambda$CDM is contested. Analyses using large-volume [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] (e.g., the FLAMINGO suite) show that while similar apparent anisotropies have a low probability ($0.12\%$, or $3.2\sigma$) of randomly emerging in an isotropic $\Lambda$CDM universe, over $80\%$ of the observed amplitude can be attributed to statistical noise and intrinsic scatter rather than coherent large-scale peculiar velocities [He et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250401745H/abstract). Although a moderate tension between the cluster data and $\Lambda$CDM persists, accurately constraining cosmic isotropy hinges critically on reducing [[mass-calibration-uncertainty|Mass calibration uncertainty]] and understanding the [[intrinsic-scatter|Intrinsic scatter]] in these relations.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

Despite the remarkable success of the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm in establishing [[astrophysical-mass-scaling-relations|Astrophysical Mass Scaling Relations]], several critical tensions remain at the intersection of dark matter dynamics and baryonic physics. Reconciling theoretical predictions with multi-wavelength observations continues to drive the development of [[next-generation-cosmological-simulations|Next-generation cosmological simulations]] and semi-analytic models. Potential long-standing disagreements between standard dark matter theory and observed dynamical scaling relations, particularly centered precisely within dense galaxy centers and regarding angular momentum, are hypothesized to be ultimately resolvable through a vastly more precise, detailed understanding of localized gas cooling efficiency coupled directly with AGN feedback processes [Primack et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005NewAR..49...25P/abstract). Resolving the notoriously complicated, intertwined micro-physics surrounding turbulent star formation directly paired with feedback from supernovae and active nuclei remains the absolutely critical boundary to cleanly reconciling apparent mass disagreements between theory and bulk scaling observations [Primack et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007NuPhS.173....1P/abstract).

**The Missing Baryon Problem**
At the scale of galaxy groups and low-mass clusters, scaling relations often deviate from purely gravity-driven self-similar predictions. A major open question is the physical cause of the 'missing baryon' problem in low-mass haloes. Comparisons between Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) surveys and hydrodynamical models suggest that $\sim 13.8\% - 14.1\%$ of the expected cosmic baryon fraction is "missing" from standard observational phases. This discrepancy is largely attributed to baryons redistributing into the warm-hot intergalactic medium (WHIM) for haloes below a breakpoint of $\log_{10}(M_{500c}/M_{\odot}) \sim 14$, as well as taking the form of elusive intracluster light across all mass scales [Miller et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250420159M/abstract). For instance, while state-of-the-art hydrodynamical simulations effectively replicate the intrinsic richness and overall SZ signals of massive galaxy groups, they repeatedly systematically overpredict bulk X-ray luminosities derived specifically from optically selected, lower-mass observational group samples [Jakobs et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.480.3338J/abstract).

**Tensions in Sub-grid Physics**
Replicating the exact slope and [[intrinsic-scatter|Intrinsic scatter]] of scaling relations relies heavily on sub-grid physics, particularly the feedback from active galactic nuclei (AGN) and supernovae. The literature currently shows significant conflict regarding how well models capture these processes. For example, while semi-analytic frameworks like L-Galaxies successfully reproduce the observed dichotomy in the stellar-to-halo mass relation (SHMR) between star-forming and quiescent galaxies, state-of-the-art [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] (e.g., IllustrisTNG, EAGLE) struggle to replicate this difference, predicting similar star formation efficiencies for both populations at a given halo mass [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240807743W/abstract), [Wang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...980..233W/abstract). Furthermore, simulations systematically underpredict the number density of quenched galaxies at $z \geq 1.5$ [Vani et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240800824V/abstract). This challenge is compounded by profound degeneracies between baryonic feedback effects and small-scale cosmological variations—such as warm dark matter (WDM)—which can produce nearly identical perturbations to radial density profiles and scaling laws [Stafford et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.497.3809S/abstract), [Silvestrini et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260107543S/abstract). The intersection of complex core mechanics directly affects modeling stability; uniquely derived theoretical scaling relations anchored in the intrinsic concentration-mass correlation heavily predict discrete, observable thermodynamic signatures highlighting the critical cusp-to-core transition naturally operating within dark matter halos [Kaneda et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240703614K/abstract). Novel approaches to calibrating sub-grid physics are continually being developed. For example, the [[magneticum-simulations|Magneticum]] Pathfinder simulation suite has successfully matched several empirical scaling relations—such as the Magorrian and mass-size relations—by explicitly tuning their feedback prescriptions to match the hot gas content of clusters, rather than the more conventional approach of tuning to the local stellar mass function [Dolag et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250401061D/abstract).

**High-Redshift Deviations**
The advent of JWST has introduced new challenges to established scaling relations in the early Universe. Observations of galaxies at $z \sim 7-10$ reveal strong deviations from the universal fundamental-metallicity relation, with chemical abundances measuring only $\sim 1/4$ of the expected values, suggesting rapid dilution by pristine gas infall [Heintz et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023NatAs...7.1517H/abstract). Moreover, the detection of unexpectedly [[massive-galaxies|Massive galaxies]] at these epochs challenges standard halo mass functions, requiring either highly elevated star formation efficiencies or more realistic halo collapse physics to reconcile theory with observation [Fakhry et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250723742F/abstract).

**Mass Calibration and Intrinsic Scatter**
Finally, reducing [[mass-calibration-uncertainty|Mass calibration uncertainty]] remains a paramount objective if cluster scaling relations are to be utilized as precision [[cosmological-probes|Cosmological probes]]. Significant tensions exist between total masses derived under hydrostatic equilibrium assumptions and those measured via weak lensing. These discrepancies are often driven by line-of-sight projection effects, halo triaxiality, and merger-induced asymmetric scatter, which can bias inferred masses low [Marrone et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...754..119M/abstract), [Krause et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.419.1766K/abstract). The community remains divided on whether the dominant source of intrinsic scatter in mass-observable relations is the dynamic state and substructure of the cluster [Krause et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.419.1766K/abstract), [Ventimiglia et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...685..118V/abstract), or intrinsic halo properties such as spin and concentration [Krause et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.419.1766K/abstract), [Posti et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...644A..76P/abstract).
