## Article

In galactic dynamics, **gas-star misalignments** (often referred to interchangeably as [[stellar-gas-misalignments|Stellar-gas misalignments]]) occur when the angular momentum vector of a galaxy's stellar component is kinematically offset from that of its gaseous interstellar medium. Observationally, these offsets are characterized using spatially resolved velocity maps from integral field spectroscopy (IFS). A galaxy is typically classified as misaligned if the difference between the kinematic position angles of its gas and stars exceeds $30^\circ$. Because collisional gas naturally dissipates energy and settles into the principal equatorial plane of a host galaxy's gravitational potential, substantial kinematic offsets indicate that the stellar body and the gas do not share a secular origin. Instead, these misalignments serve as critical signatures of external galaxy assembly processes, such as smooth cold gas accretion from the cosmic web, cooling from the hot circumgalactic halo, or past merger events. 

The systematic study of these kinematic offsets was revolutionized by early large-scale IFS surveys, which transitioned the field from identifying isolated peculiar galaxies to establishing robust demographic statistics [Cappellari et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.379..418C/abstract), [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract). These foundational studies revealed a stark morphological dichotomy. In late-type galaxies (LTGs), misalignments are exceptionally rare, occurring in only $\sim 5\%$ of the population. This indicates that gas in undisturbed disk galaxies strictly follows the established stellar potential, and that historical accretion was preferentially aligned. Conversely, approximately 40-45% of early-type galaxies (ETGs) host kinematically offset gas. In massive, slow-rotating ETGs characterized by triaxial potentials, accreted gas can settle into stable, strongly decoupled orbits—such as minor-axis polar rings—forming complex [[multi-spin-galaxies|Multi-spin galaxies]]. Notably, when multi-phase gas (molecular, atomic, and ionized) is present in these decoupled systems, the phases are almost always kinematically aligned with one another, cementing the consensus of a shared, external origin for the gas reservoir [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract).

Despite comprehensive statistical mappings across modern massive surveys, the dynamical evolution of misaligned gas remains an active area of theoretical and observational research. When externally acquired gas interacts with a non-spherical stellar potential, it experiences gravitational torques and viscous drag that drive it toward a stable equilibrium plane. A major open question in the literature has been the exact timescale of this dynamical settling. Standard analytic models predict rapid relaxation on timescales of $\sim 0.1 \text{ Gyr}$, yet reconciling these models with the high observed frequency of local misalignments requires the structures to persist for up to 2 Gyr, a longevity supported by continuous gas accretion in modern cosmological simulations. Furthermore, as the misaligned gas eventually settles, it sheds substantial orbital angular momentum. This dissipation drives powerful radial mass inflows toward the galactic center, linking gas-star misalignments to the efficient fueling of supermassive black holes (SMBHs). Understanding these transient kinematic states is therefore not only vital for tracking galaxy assembly, but also provides a critical diagnostic tool for tracking galactic structure in the era of multi-messenger astrophysics.

## Definitions and Observational Characterization

A [[stellar-gas-misalignments|gas-star kinematic misalignment]] occurs when there is a significant spatial offset between the angular momentum vector of a galaxy's stellar component and that of its interstellar medium. In observational practice, this offset is quantified by comparing the kinematic position angle (PA) of the two components. The kinematic PA is defined as the axis of maximum rotation projected on the sky, typically extracted from two-dimensional velocity maps. The misalignment angle is expressed as:
$$ \Delta\text{PA} = |\text{PA}_{\text{gas}} - \text{PA}_{\text{stars}}| $$
By convention in the literature, a galaxy is broadly classified as "misaligned" if $\Delta\text{PA} > 30^\circ$ [Lagos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448.1271L/abstract), [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract), with values approaching $180^\circ$ indicating complete counter-rotation [Li et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.501...14L/abstract).

The systematic characterization of these offsets relies heavily on IFS. Spatially resolved velocity maps from large-scale IFS surveys—such as ATLAS3D, SAMI, MaNGA, and CALIFA—allow for the precise extraction of both stellar and gas kinematics across the optical extent of a galaxy. A crucial observational finding from these spatially resolved maps is that while the gas may be completely decoupled from the stars, the different phases of the interstellar medium (ionized, molecular, and atomic gas) are almost always kinematically aligned with one another. This multi-phase coherence suggests a shared physical origin for the decoupled gas, independent of the stellar backbone [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract).

IFS surveys have established a stark demographic dichotomy in the prevalence of Stellar-gas misalignments. Across the general galaxy population, approximately $11\% - 12\%$ of galaxies exhibit significant kinematic offsets [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract), [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract). However, this fraction is highly dependent on morphology. Studies of non-interacting spirals demonstrate that $\sim 90\%$ of isolated disks exhibit tight morpho-kinematic alignment ($\Delta\text{PA} \lesssim 16^\circ$), indicating that the gas in undisturbed systems strictly follows the stellar gravitational potential [Barrera-Ballesteros et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..70B/abstract). In LTGs broadly, severe misalignments ($\Delta\text{PA} > 30^\circ$) occur in only $\sim 5\%$ of the population [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract). 

In contrast, early-type galaxies host a much higher fraction of misalignments, with $\sim 40\% - 45\%$ of ETGs showing $\Delta\text{PA} > 30^\circ$ [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract), [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract). The incidence of misalignments is further correlated with the underlying stellar kinematic state of the ETG. Among fast-rotating ETGs, over a third exhibit misaligned ionized gas, a feature widely interpreted as a signature of external gas acquisition [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract). Conversely, massive slow rotators frequently exhibit a broad distribution of gas-star misalignment angles [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract). It is important to distinguish these gas-star offsets from *stellar* kinematic misalignments, which measure the offset between a galaxy's stellar rotation axis and its own stellar photometric axis. Because massive, slow-rotating systems typically possess triaxial potentials rather than simple oblate axisymmetry, they permit complex stellar orbits that deviate strongly from the photometric major axis, including minor-axis (prolate) stellar rotation [Ene et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.479.2810E/abstract), [Cappellari et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.379..418C/abstract).

## Historical Context: From Individual Decoupled Cores to Large Surveys

Before the widespread adoption of large-scale IFS, observations of [[stellar-gas-misalignments|decoupled kinematics]] were limited to long-slit spectroscopic studies of individual, peculiar galaxies. In these early case studies, orthogonal or counter-rotating gas was generally interpreted as the footprint of a discrete acquisition event. For example, observations of the Sa spiral NGC 2855 revealed ionized gas rotating perpendicularly to the stellar disc, firmly establishing that such [[multi-spin-galaxies|Kinematically Distinct Components (KDCs)]] were markers of external gas accretion or past galaxy mergers [Corsini et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002A&A...382..488C/abstract). Further developments required advanced modeling to detect subtle kinematic signatures even in local prototypes; for instance, the SPLASH Survey noted that rotation has been detected in the inner spheroids of local disk galaxies like Andromeda (M31), where Markov chain Monte Carlo modeling of resolved stars is required to disentangle the spheroid rotation from the dominant disk [Dorman et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...752..147D/abstract).

The transition from studying isolated decoupled cores to systematic demographic analyses was initiated by the SAURON Project. By providing 2D kinematic maps for a representative sample of local galaxies, SAURON demonstrated that ETGs subdivide kinematically into fast and slow rotators [Cappellari et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.379..418C/abstract). This work revealed that slow rotators are typically moderately triaxial systems that exhibit varying degrees of stellar kinematic misalignments, while a number of fast rotators show evidence for containing Kinematically Distinct Components (KDCs) [Cappellari et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.379..418C/abstract). 

The subsequent ATLAS3D survey massively expanded this IFS approach, establishing robust baseline statistics for Stellar-gas misalignments. ATLAS3D found that $36 \pm 5\%$ of fast-rotating ETGs host ionized gas kinematically offset from the stellar component [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract). Because internal stellar mass loss should naturally co-rotate with the stars, this high frequency provided a strong lower limit on the importance of externally acquired gas. By contrast, the CALIFA survey analyzed non-interacting galaxies and found tight alignment in the vast majority of isolated systems [Barrera-Ballesteros et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..70B/abstract). This cemented the consensus that substantial misalignments are direct evidence of external intervention.

In the current era of massive IFS campaigns, surveys encompassing thousands of galaxies have quantified the structural and environmental dependencies of these offsets. The SAMI Galaxy Survey firmly quantified the morphological dichotomy, demonstrating that the global $11-12\%$ misalignment average obscures a severe split between prevalent decoupling in ETGs and rarity in LTGs [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract), [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract). Concurrently, the MaNGA Survey mapped stellar and gas kinematics across $\sim 2300$ systems. This comprehensive mapping unveiled a clear bimodality in the specific angular momentum-ellipticity diagram and detected a secondary peak at $90^\circ$ in the kinematic-photometric misalignment distribution for massive slow rotators, highlighting distinct evolutionary pathways for central hosts depending on their kinematic state [Graham et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.477.4711G/abstract).

As sample sizes have grown, new tensions have emerged in the literature regarding the dominant physical drivers of these offsets. While it is broadly agreed that misaligned gas is of external origin, there is active debate over the delivery mechanism. Some authors note that discrete major or minor mergers are one contributing process to kinematic decoupling [Li et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.501...14L/abstract), whereas others argue that smooth gas accretion from the cosmic web or cooling from hot halos is mathematically required to explain the high occurrence rate in ETGs [Lagos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448.1271L/abstract). Furthermore, the role of the local environment remains actively debated, with surveys differing on whether local environmental density or internal morphology is the ultimate dictator of misalignment probabilities [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract), [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract). 

Ultimately, these large surveys have transformed the study of Multi-spin galaxies from catalogs of individual oddities into a rigorous statistical framework for testing cosmological models of galaxy assembly.

## Primary Physical Drivers: Accretion vs. Mergers

The high incidence of [[stellar-gas-misalignments|Stellar-gas misalignments]] in ETGs strongly indicates an external origin for their interstellar medium. In non-interacting late-type galaxies, the gas and stellar kinematic position angles are overwhelmingly aligned, reflecting a shared secular history in a flattened gravitational potential [Barrera-Ballesteros et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..70B/abstract). Conversely, kinematic misalignments in ETGs imply that their cold gas reservoirs were acquired after the main stellar body had already assembled [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract). The physical mechanisms supplying this gas are broadly divided into discrete merger events and smooth external accretion.

Galaxy mergers, encompassing major interactions and minor mergers, are a confirmed channel for generating misaligned kinematics. Observational samples demonstrate that the merging fraction is significantly elevated in misaligned galaxies relative to their co-rotating counterparts [Li et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.501...14L/abstract). In the most massive slow-rotating ETGs, structural and kinematic complexities are frequently attributed to a sequence of gas-poor mergers that randomize the stellar orbits and introduce stellar kinematic misalignments [Ene et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.479.2810E/abstract), [Ene et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...891...65E/abstract). Observationally distinguishing between ongoing misaligned gas accretion and past merger events can be complex. However, results from the SOSIMPLE survey for NGC 7135 illustrate how spatially resolved kinematics and chemical distributions can successfully distinguish between ongoing gas-star misalignments and much older major merger events occurring $6\text{--}10$ Gyr ago [Davison et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502.2296D/abstract). Despite this, theoretical models and cosmological simulations demonstrate that discrete mergers are insufficient to explain the full demographic. Semi-analytic models suggest that if mergers were the sole driver, only $2-5\%$ of early-type galaxies would exhibit misalignments [Lagos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448.1271L/abstract), while hydrodynamical simulations like EAGLE attribute only $\sim 17\%$ of unstable misalignments directly to this channel [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract). 

To bridge the gap between merger-driven events and the $\sim 40-45\%$ misalignment fraction observed in ETG populations, smooth external accretion must dominate. This encompasses both direct streams of cold gas from the cosmic web and the continuous cooling of gas from the galaxy's hot halo. Because this gas is acquired independently of the established stellar mass, its angular momentum vector is dynamically decoupled from the stars, frequently leading to initial offsets of $>30^{\circ}$ [Lagos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448.1271L/abstract), [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract). 

Finally, while external inflow is the primary driver, researchers caution that up to $\sim 8\%$ of observed kinematic misalignments may actually trace outflowing gas driven by active galactic nuclei or centralized starbursts, masquerading as accreted disks [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract).

## Internal Galaxy Properties and Dynamics

The persistence and dynamic evolution of [[stellar-gas-misalignments|Stellar-gas misalignments]] are fundamentally governed by the internal structural properties of the host galaxy. As a population, misaligned galaxies exhibit higher Sérsic indices, lower stellar spin, and lower specific star formation rates compared to their aligned counterparts [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract). In centrally concentrated, gas-poor systems, newly accreted misaligned gas experiences smaller differential gravitational torques and reduced viscous drag from pre-existing gas, allowing the kinematic offset to persist over longer timescales [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract).

The global shape of the host galaxy's gravitational potential dictates the stable orbits available for gas. Massive ETGs (typically with stellar masses $M_* > 10^{11.5}\, M_\odot$) that are classified as slow, dispersion-dominated rotators are often mildly triaxial [Cappellari et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.379..418C/abstract). Triaxial potentials are inefficient at torquing star-forming gas into the primary stellar equatorial plane [Casanueva et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2031C/abstract). Consequently, while fast-rotating galaxies show a strong preference for coplanar alignment (or exact counter-rotation), slow rotators display a broader distribution of misalignment angles and complex orbital geometries. 

In contrast, the dominant potential of a galactic disc strongly enforces coplanarity. Internal secular structures such as bars do not generate large-scale kinematic misalignments. Integral field surveys demonstrate that even in the presence of strong bars, both the stellar and ionized gas components trace the underlying gravitational potential of the disc [Barrera-Ballesteros et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..70B/abstract). Therefore, significant kinematic offsets in strongly barred or disc-dominated systems are typically markers of external intervention rather than internal secular instabilities. Complex systems with sustained misalignments across multiple baryonic components, such as [[multi-spin-galaxies|Multi-spin galaxies]], are rare in pure disc potentials without an external supply of misaligned material.

Extending beyond the central regions of massive galaxies, wide-field surveys such as SLUGGS have traced kinematic transitions and misalignments out to large radii (up to $2.6$ effective radii), confirming that outer angular momentum properties can differ significantly from central regions due to a galaxy's specific accretion history [Foster et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.457..147F/abstract). At the lower end of the mass spectrum, investigations into dwarf galaxy kinematics reveal that star-forming dwarfs follow a "dwarf fundamental plane" connecting their gravitational potential, line width, and surface brightness, suggesting their gas motions are a consequence of gravity rather than driven by turbulence [McCall et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...540A..49M/abstract).

## Relaxation Timescales and Dynamical Settling

While [[stellar-gas-misalignments|Stellar-gas misalignments]] are fundamentally transient phenomena, the duration over which a galaxy can maintain a kinematically distinct gas disc is governed by a complex interplay of gravitational torques and dissipative processes. When gas accretes at an angle offset from the host galaxy's principal axes, it is subjected to gravitational torques from the non-spherical stellar potential. These torques act to precess the gas and, coupled with viscous drag, drive the material toward a stable equilibrium plane—typically the equatorial plane or, in triaxial systems, the polar plane. 

There has been an active tension in the literature regarding the exact timescale of this dynamical settling. Standard theoretical models of dissipative settling predict rapid relaxation, often estimating alignment timescales on the order of $t_{\rm relax} \sim 0.1$ Gyr [Baker et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250701894B/abstract). However, reconciling these short timescales with the high observed frequency of misalignments in large-scale surveys requires the misaligned states to survive much longer. High-resolution cosmological simulations, such as those from the EAGLE and FIRE projects, indicate that gas can persist in a misaligned state for $0.5$ to $2$ Gyr [van de Voort et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.3269V/abstract), [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract). In the EAGLE simulation, the median relaxation lifetime is found to be $\sim 0.5$ Gyr, with approximately $20\%$ of unstable misalignments persisting for more than three torquing times ($\gtrsim 1 \text{ Gyr}$) [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract).

Several physical factors modulate this alignment timescale and contribute to the longevity of the decoupled state:
*   **Ongoing Gas Accretion:** The relaxation time is substantially lengthened if a galaxy experiences continuous, high-rate gas accretion. During this phase, the constant influx of fresh angular momentum from the infalling gas overwhelms the restorative stellar torques, stalling the alignment until the accretion rate drops [van de Voort et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.3269V/abstract), [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract).
*   **Host Galaxy Morphology:** The structural properties of the stellar body heavily dictate torquing efficiency. Centrally concentrated galaxies (those with high Sérsic indices) and dispersion-dominated, triaxial systems exert weaker aligning torques on extended gas discs, rendering them significantly less efficient at realigning the star-forming gas [Casanueva et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2031C/abstract), [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract). 
*   **Viscous Drag:** In early-type, gas-poor systems, newly accreted gas experiences reduced viscous drag forces due to the lack of a pre-existing massive gas disc, further extending the survival time of the misalignment [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract).

During the dynamical settling phase, the gas disc undergoes significant structural and kinematic evolution. Because the inner regions of the galaxy possess shorter dynamical times, the center of the gas disc aligns more rapidly than the outskirts, frequently generating a strongly warped disc [van de Voort et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.3269V/abstract). Furthermore, as the gas in these systems interacts with the stellar potential and experiences internal dissipation, it efficiently sheds angular momentum, driving strong radial inflows of gas toward the galactic center.

## Environmental Influence on Gas Kinematics

The likelihood of observing [[stellar-gas-misalignments|kinematic offsets]] is intricately linked to both the host galaxy's internal structure and its local environment. A pronounced environmental dichotomy exists between isolated field galaxies and those residing in dense cluster centers. Observations from the ATLAS$^{\rm 3D}$ survey demonstrate that fast-rotating ETGs in dense groups and the Virgo Cluster almost exclusively possess kinematically aligned molecular and ionized gas [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract). This alignment strongly suggests a purely internal origin for the gas, such as stellar mass loss, because external gas supplies in these regions are largely suppressed by the hot intra-cluster medium and ram-pressure stripping. In stark contrast, field galaxies exhibit a high rate of kinematic decoupling; kinematic misalignments indicate that at least $42 \pm 5\%$ of fast-rotating field ETGs have their gas supplied from external sources [Davis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..882D/abstract). 

However, the literature remains in debate regarding whether the local environmental density is the *primary* physical driver of this dichotomy, or if internal galaxy properties ultimately govern the observable rates of Stellar-gas misalignments. While early studies heavily emphasized the availability of external gas supplies in the field, subsequent results from the SAMI survey argue that local environmental density is secondary to morphology and internal mass distributions [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract), [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract). Simulations suggest a more nuanced picture: while the broader environment provides the necessary gas, internal factors—such as a high Sérsic index, triaxiality, and dispersion-dominated stellar kinematics—dictate whether the gas quickly realigns or persists in an unstable misaligned state [Casanueva et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2031C/abstract). 

Furthermore, environmental interactions leave distinct signatures on the galaxy's orbital structure. In simulated populations, misaligned galaxies frequently reside in regions with higher tidal fields and possess a greater fraction of *ex situ* accreted stars compared to their aligned counterparts [Casanueva et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2031C/abstract). In dense cluster environments where external accretion is limited, the rare misalignments that do occur are often driven by specific cluster-gravitational processes or outflowing AGN gas rather than standard environmental infall [Bryant et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483..458B/abstract), [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract).

## Misalignments as Drivers of SMBH Growth and AGN Activity

The fueling of a Supermassive Black Hole (SMBH) requires efficient angular momentum transfer to transport gas from galactic scales down to the central parsecs. In systems exhibiting [[stellar-gas-misalignments|Stellar-gas misalignments]], gravitational torques exerted by the stellar potential onto the misaligned gas drive rapid dissipation of angular momentum. As the gas relaxes toward a stable plane, this loss of angular momentum funnels material inward, providing a robust reservoir for black hole accretion and triggering Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) activity [Baker et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250701894B/abstract). 

Observational surveys provide strong empirical evidence linking kinematic misalignments to non-starbursting activity. In the SAMI Galaxy Survey, an analysis of ionized gas in misaligned galaxies reveals that star formation is the primary ionizing source in only $22\%$ of such systems. Instead, $17\%$ are classified as Seyfert galaxies, and $61\%$ exhibit Low-Ionization Nuclear Emission-line Region (LINER) properties [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract). This distribution indicates that the inflow of kinematically decoupled gas frequently accompanies alternative ionization mechanisms in ETGs that are distinct from standard star formation.

Cosmological simulations support this physical picture, demonstrating that the kinematic state of the gas governs central accretion efficiency. In the EAGLE simulation, galaxies experiencing unstable, non-coplanar misalignments show systematically enhanced SMBH growth over typical relaxation timescales (evaluated over $\sim 0.5$ Gyr windows) compared to aligned control samples [Baker et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250701894B/abstract). Consequently, galaxies at $z \approx 0.1$ that spent a significant fraction of their recent history (the preceding $\approx 2$ Gyr) in an unstable misaligned state are significantly more likely to host overmassive black holes [Baker et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250701894B/abstract). Interestingly, stable counter-rotating gas configurations—often found in [[multi-spin-galaxies|Multi-spin galaxies]]—do not exhibit this enhanced black hole growth. This indicates that it is the active torquing and ensuing dynamical instability of the gas, rather than mere retrograde rotation, that drives the radial mass inflow [Baker et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250701894B/abstract).

While gas-star misalignments point to accretion, *stellar* kinematic misalignments (the offset between stellar rotation and stellar photometry) serve as critical dynamical signatures for locating the host galaxies of massive black hole binaries and mergers. Cosmological simulations indicate that host galaxies of SMBH mergers with high chirp masses ($> 10^{8.2} M_\odot$) and high mass ratios ($> 0.5$) are uniquely characterized by slow stellar rotation and prominent stellar kinematic misalignments [Bardati et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..265B/abstract). Utilizing these structural and stellar kinematic signatures in integral field unit (IFU) spectroscopy allows for the identification of these host galaxies with accuracies exceeding $85\%$. Consequently, these stellar misalignments are expected to serve as a vital diagnostic tool for electromagnetic follow-up campaigns of future gravitational wave detections by observatories like the Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) [Bardati et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240714061B/abstract), [Bardati et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..265B/abstract). The environments directly surrounding these extreme black holes also exhibit distinct dynamical signatures; for instance, dynamical measurements of massive SMBHs in Brightest Cluster Galaxies (BCGs) like NGC 4889 reveal tangentially biased stellar orbits on scales comparable to the photometric core [McConnell et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...756..179M/abstract).

## Cosmological Simulations and Theoretical Modeling

Modern numerical modeling, particularly through large-volume and zoom-in cosmological hydrodynamical simulations, has become essential for understanding the lifecycle of [[stellar-gas-misalignments|Stellar-gas misalignments]]. While early theoretical models frequently assumed that discrete galaxy mergers were the sole driver of kinematic offsets, recent simulations reveal a more complex and continuous picture of gas acquisition. In the EAGLE simulation, for instance, mergers account for only $\sim 17\%$ of unstable misalignments in massive galaxies ($M_{*} \geq 10^{9.5} M_\odot$). Instead, the diverse formation pathways observed in EAGLE suggest that smooth accretion from the cosmic web and hot halo cooling are the dominant mechanisms for supplying misaligned gas to early-type galaxies [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract).

As detailed in earlier sections, a major theoretical breakthrough provided by these simulations is resolving the survival timescale of these misaligned gas discs. While standard analytic models predicted rapid realignment on timescales of $\sim 0.1 \text{ Gyr}$, high-resolution simulations from the FIRE and EAGLE projects demonstrate that continuous gas accretion and inefficient torques in triaxial halos allow misalignments to persist for up to $\sim 0.5{-}2 \text{ Gyr}$ [van de Voort et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.3269V/abstract), [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract). This provides a crucial theoretical match to the high incidence of misalignments observed in local integral field surveys, and naturally produces the structural complexity akin to [[multi-spin-galaxies|Multi-spin galaxies]] seen in dispersion-dominated hosts [Casanueva et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2031C/abstract). 

Furthermore, simulations have uncovered a vital link between gas relaxation and the fueling of central SMBHs. As unstable gas discs reorient, enhanced dissipation and angular momentum loss drive gas inflows toward the galactic center. EAGLE demonstrates that galaxies experiencing unstable misalignments exhibit systematically enhanced SMBH growth, frequently leaving behind overmassive black holes at $z \approx 0.1$ as a fossil record of past misalignment events [Baker et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250701894B/abstract). 

Numerical modeling also indicates that extreme kinematic geometries may serve as reliable signposts for identifying the host galaxies of massive black hole (MBH) binaries and mergers. Mock IFU observations generated from the Romulus25 simulation demonstrate that host galaxies of high-mass (chirp mass $\gtrsim 10^{8.2} M_\odot$) and high-mass-ratio ($\gtrsim 0.5$) MBH mergers uniquely exhibit slow rotation combined with pronounced stellar kinematic misalignments. Using a combination of these kinematic parameters, models predict that such merger hosts can be identified with $>85\%$ accuracy, providing a crucial diagnostic tool for future gravitational-wave follow-up campaigns [Bardati et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240714061B/abstract), [Bardati et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..265B/abstract).

## High-Redshift Observations and Galaxy Evolution

Observations at $z > 1$ capture the peak epoch of galaxy assembly, providing a crucial baseline for understanding the origin of [[stellar-gas-misalignments|kinematic decoupling]] seen in the local universe. At these high redshifts, resolving the internal kinematics of both gas and stars is observationally challenging. Consequently, large campaigns like the MOSDEF survey have been critical in mapping ionized gas kinematics for star-forming galaxies at $z \sim 1.4\text{--}3.8$ [Price et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...894...91P/abstract). By statistically modeling unresolved kinematics and explicitly accounting for observational galaxy-slit misalignments, such surveys extract global structural and dynamical parameters to trace the kinematic state of galaxies over time.

In the high-redshift MOSDEF sample, the ratio of intrinsic ordered to disordered motion, $V/\sigma_{V,0}$, is found to increase with increasing stellar mass and decreasing specific star formation rate. This trend suggests that early star-forming systems are characterized by marginally stable, gas-rich thick disks rather than the dynamically cold, thin disks typical of local LTGs [Price et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...894...91P/abstract). A notable tension arises in the dynamical mass modeling of these early galaxies: for systems at $z \gtrsim 2$, the inferred baryon fractions, $f_{\mathrm{bar}}$, within the effective radius frequently exceed $100\%$ [Price et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...894...91P/abstract). This discrepancy indicates a significant tension in standard dynamical mass modeling or baryonic mass estimation during the era of peak assembly.

The kinematic conditions at high redshift deeply inform the overall galaxy assembly history and the dichotomy of Stellar-gas misalignments observed locally. In the nearby universe, LTGs show high degrees of gas-star kinematic alignment. This implies that the sustained gas accretion feeding these disk galaxies at higher redshifts was preferentially aligned with their developing stellar angular momentum vectors [Ristea et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.2677R/abstract). Such smooth, aligned accretion appears necessary to explain the persistence of giant, bulgeless pure-disk galaxies, whose high frequency directly challenges traditional hierarchical clustering models in which frequent major mergers would otherwise destroy pristine disks and introduce widespread kinematic decoupling [Kormendy et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...723...54K/abstract).

Conversely, the high prevalence of misalignments in local ETGs points to a fundamentally different evolutionary path. Cosmological models suggest that galaxies which accreted most of their cold gas prior to their main stellar mass assembly tend to remain kinematically aligned. In contrast, massive ETGs typically formed the bulk of their stellar bodies early; the misalignments we observe in them today are largely the result of late-time, misaligned smooth gas accretion or cooling from the hot halo [Lagos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448.1271L/abstract). Thus, the $z \sim 2$ kinematics represent a crucial phase where the foundational angular momentum distributions of today's normal disks and future [[multi-spin-galaxies|Multi-spin galaxies]] were established.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

Although large-scale integral field surveys have robustly quantified the demographics of [[stellar-gas-misalignments|Stellar-gas misalignments]], fundamental questions regarding their physical drivers and role in galactic evolution remain unresolved. 

One of the most prominent ongoing debates is the exact physical origin of the stark morphological dichotomy in alignment geometries. While it is broadly understood that late-type galaxies (LTGs) predominantly exhibit aligned accretion and early-type galaxies (ETGs) frequently result in misaligned or counter-rotating systems, the precise cosmological or environmental mechanisms dictating this divergence at high redshift are not fully constrained [Lagos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448.1271L/abstract). Furthermore, while major mergers have been observed to be one contributing process to kinematic offsets [Li et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.501...14L/abstract), modern models increasingly favor smooth gas accretion from the cosmic web or the cooling of hot halos as the primary sources [Lagos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448.1271L/abstract), [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241203707B/abstract). The relative weighting of these channels across different mass bins and cosmic epochs remains to be definitively mapped observationally.

Looking forward, galaxy kinematics are expected to serve as vital probes of SMBH fueling and binary mergers. Exploiting kinematic markers—such as the correlation between slow stellar rotation, stellar kinematic offsets, and binary hosts—via IFU spectroscopy will be a crucial strategy for identifying the electromagnetic counterparts to massive black hole mergers detected by upcoming gravitational wave observatories like LISA [Bardati et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240714061B/abstract), [Bardati et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..265B/abstract).

Finally, understanding these phenomena across a broader range of galaxy masses and redshifts will require next-generation observations and modeling. High-resolution simulations incorporating sophisticated multiphase physics—including magnetic fields, cosmic rays, and radiative transfer—predict that episodic starbursts can induce transient misalignments even in dwarf galaxies [Martin-Alvarez et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.3806M/abstract). Instruments like JWST will be instrumental in probing the multiphase interstellar medium of these systems, providing the data necessary to test whether [[multi-spin-galaxies|Multi-spin galaxies]] and kinematic misalignments are a ubiquitous feature of galaxy assembly across cosmic time and mass scales.
