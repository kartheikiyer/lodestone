**Super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies** are a rare, extreme class of [[high-redshift-active-galactic-nuclei|High-redshift active galactic nuclei]] ($z \approx 2-4.6$) characterized by the simultaneous presence of hyper-efficient black hole accretion, powerful relativistic jets, and profound circumnuclear dust obscuration. These systems host central supermassive black holes accreting at or above the Eddington limit—the theoretical threshold where outward radiation pressure balances the inward gravitational force. Because the [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|active nucleus]] is deeply embedded within a dense, gas-rich interstellar medium, its immense ultraviolet and optical output is almost entirely absorbed and thermally reprocessed by surrounding dust. This produces extreme mid-to-far infrared emission, frequently classifying these objects as hyperluminous [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOG (Hot Dust-obscured Galaxy)]]s [Dey et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...677..943D/abstract), [Assef et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...27A/abstract). The resulting X-ray spectra are typically reflection-dominated and Compton-thick, confirming the presence of an extraordinarily dense obscuring cocoon [Piconcelli et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...574L...9P/abstract).

The existence of these sources challenges classical models of [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]]. Traditionally, the majority of radio galaxies were thought to be [[low-power-radio-galaxies|Low-power radio galaxies]] residing almost exclusively in gas-poor, massive elliptical galaxies fueled by radiatively inefficient hot gas accretion, a population known as [[low-excitation-radio-galaxies|Low-excitation radio galaxies]] [Hardcastle et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.376.1849H/abstract), [Janssen et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...541A..62J/abstract). In contrast, super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies demonstrate that highly collimated jets can be successfully launched even in the gas-rich, highly dissipative environments of [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]]. This is part of a broader realization that massive ellipticals are not strictly required for jet production, as evidenced by the discovery of powerful jets launched from bulgeless spiral hosts even at low Eddington rates [Bagchi et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788..174B/abstract). Astrophysicists now generally view these super-Eddington objects as a brief, maximal-growth transitional phase in a broader evolutionary sequence. Triggered by major galaxy mergers or dense protocluster environments, the coeval starburst and central engine undergo rapid growth until the black hole initiates severe multiphase feedback. The interplay between wide-aperture, radiation-pressure-driven winds and jet-ISM interactions drives massive molecular outflows, which are hypothesized to eventually clear the obscuring dust and reveal an unobscured optical quasar [Wu et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...852...96W/abstract).

Recent advancements in millimeter and infrared interferometry in the JWST and ALMA eras have mapped the host galaxy morphologies of these systems on kiloparsec scales, revealing complex tidal bridges, companion galaxies, and extreme localized dust temperatures. However, several critical open questions remain. An ongoing debate concerns the true magnitude of their accretion rates; recent mid-infrared analyses suggest that once severe dust extinction is properly accounted for, some candidate super-Eddington sources may actually be accreting at mildly super-Eddington rates rather than at the extreme levels initially reported. Furthermore, it remains unknown whether this heavily obscured, radio-loud phase is a universal bottleneck for all massive galaxies or a rare evolutionary offshoot. Ongoing research aims to determine the exact coupling efficiency of their jet-driven feedback, their role in early-universe black hole seed formation, and their potential multi-messenger contribution to the cosmic neutrino background via [[super-eddington-accretion-and-feedback|Super-Eddington Accretion and Feedback]].

## Definition and Fundamental Properties

Super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies represent a rare, extreme phase of galaxy evolution typically observed at Cosmic Noon ($z \approx 2-4.6$). They are characterized by a combination of hyper-efficient black hole accretion, relativistic jets, and deeply embedded central engines enshrouded by dense circumnuclear dust cocoons. This population intersects the broader class of [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOG (Hot Dust-obscured Galaxy)]]s, which are hyperluminous infrared sources ($L_{\rm IR} > 10^{13}-10^{14} L_\odot$) first identified via their extremely red mid-infrared colors in WISE surveys [Dey et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...677..943D/abstract), [Assef et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...27A/abstract). While primarily observed at high redshifts, recent studies have also identified low-redshift ($z < 0.5$) Hot DOG counterparts; although these local systems exhibit lower overall bolometric luminosities, they maintain the defining characteristic of near-Eddington or super-Eddington accretion rates [Li et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...981..104L/abstract). Another related population involves infrared-faint radio sources (IFRS), which remain undetected at far-infrared wavelengths and frequently exhibit extreme radio-to-infrared flux ratios; these objects are considered potential lower-luminosity siblings or more distant counterparts to [[high-redshift-radio-galaxies|High-redshift radio galaxies]] [Herzog et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...580A...7H/abstract).

The fundamental property of these systems is their profound dust obscuration coupled with immense bolometric luminosity. The central supermassive black hole is buried under column densities that are often Compton-thick ($N_{\rm H} \sim 10^{24}$ cm$^{-2}$), resulting in reflection-dominated X-ray spectra [Piconcelli et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...574L...9P/abstract). For example, the prototypical hyperluminous source W2246-0526 exhibits an extreme visual extinction of $A_V \sim 300$ mag and gas densities of $n_{\rm H} \sim 3 \times 10^3$ cm$^{-3}$ [Fernández Aranda et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...682A.166F/abstract). The dust is heated to unusually high temperatures ($T > 60$ K) by the obscured [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|active galactic nucleus]], yielding bolometric luminosities that can exceed $3 \times 10^{14} L_\odot$ [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract). These immense luminosities are hosted by exceptionally massive galaxies; for instance, the derived stellar mass for W2246-0526 is $4.3 \times 10^{11} M_\odot$, highlighting it as one of the most massive systems known at $z > 4.5$ [Fan et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...854..157F/abstract). These complex gas kinematics and dense environments are often reflected in neutral gas tracers; for instance, there is a high detection rate (approximately $38\%$) of [[extragalactic-h-i-absorption-disks|H I 21-cm absorption]] in radio-loud AGN exhibiting double-peaked [O III] emission lines, which are signatures frequently associated with galaxy mergers [Dutta et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.516.4338D/abstract).

These galaxies are defined by their extreme accretion physics. They typically host massive black holes ($M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^9 M_\odot$) that are accreting at or above the Eddington limit. Optical and near-infrared measurements of sources like W2246-0526 yield an Eddington ratio of $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 2.8$ [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract), while the radio-loud dust-obscured galaxy J1406+0102 at lower redshift ($z \approx 0.24$) shows a similarly extreme $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 3$ [Fukuchi et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230305605F/abstract), [Fukuchi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...987...66F/abstract). However, the literature presents some active debate regarding the precise magnitude of these accretion rates. Recent JWST/NIRSpec observations suggest that after applying rigorous, mid-infrared-based corrections for severe dust extinction, the intrinsic luminosities of some "super-Eddington" candidates may place them at mildly super-Eddington rates ($\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 2$) rather than firmly in the highly super-Eddington regime [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...55K/abstract).

The radio-loudness of these highly accreting, gas-rich systems challenges classical models of [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]]. In the local universe, [[low-power-radio-galaxies|low-power radio jets]] are predominantly hosted by gas-poor, massive elliptical galaxies undergoing radiatively inefficient hot-gas accretion, commonly classified as [[low-excitation-radio-galaxies|Low-excitation radio galaxies]]. Conversely, super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies launch powerful relativistic jets despite residing in dense, gas-rich, [[star-forming-galaxies|star-forming environments]] [Kotilainen et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...832..157K/abstract), [Fukuchi et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230305605F/abstract). The coexistence of extreme radiative efficiency, profound dust obscuration, and powerful synchrotron radio emission suggests these objects represent a brief, transitional phase in which an over-massive black hole begins to clear its cocoon via coupled radiative and jet-driven feedback [Wu et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...852...96W/abstract), [Fukuchi et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230305605F/abstract).

## Historical Development and the Evolutionary Sequence

Historically, the study of extragalactic radio sources relied on a strict morphological and phenomenological dichotomy. [[low-power-radio-galaxies|Low-power radio jets]] were thought to reside almost exclusively in massive, passively evolving elliptical galaxies experiencing radiatively inefficient, sub-Eddington accretion from hot gas halos, a population often classified as [[low-excitation-radio-galaxies|Low-excitation radio galaxies]] [Hardcastle et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.376.1849H/abstract), [Janssen et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...541A..62J/abstract). More powerful radio sources (e.g., [[fanaroff-riley-radio-galaxies|FR II sources]]) required cold gas to fuel radiatively efficient accretion, but were still predominantly associated with massive elliptical hosts [Smith et al. (1986)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1986ApJ...306...64S/abstract). Classical radio galaxies were often studied via their HI properties, but they contrast sharply with compact young radio sources, which are frequently surrounded by enormous disks of neutral gas reflecting their gas-rich origins [Emonts et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010MNRAS.406..987E/abstract). In contrast, intense starburst activity was generally associated with gas-rich [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] lacking powerful radio-loud AGN. This paradigm treated starbursts and quasars as largely distinct entities with separate evolutionary tracks.

This view was upended by the discovery of hyperluminous, heavily obscured systems, prompting a conceptual shift toward an integrated *starburst-to-quasar hypothesis*. This conceptual shift was predated by the early discovery that about a quarter of [[luminous-infrared-galaxies-lirgs|extremely luminous infrared galaxies]] harbor compact, Seyfert-like radio cores [Norris et al. (1988)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1988MNRAS.234P..51N/abstract). In the modern evolutionary sequence, a gas-rich major merger triggers rapid gas inflow, fueling both a massive nuclear starburst and a central supermassive black hole. As the accretion rate peaks, the system enters a brief, heavily dust-obscured *transition phase* bridging the gap between submillimeter-selected starbursts and unobscured optical quasars [Dey et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...677..943D/abstract), [Ishibashi et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.1291I/abstract). A primary example of this rapid coeval growth is the Spiderweb Galaxy (MRC 1138-262) at $z=2.156$, which exhibits a star formation rate of $\sim 1400\ M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ alongside an active nucleus accreting at $20\%$ of the Eddington limit [Seymour et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...755..146S/abstract). A crucial observational link in this sequence was identified with the discovery of GNz7q at $z=7.1899$, a heavily dust-obscured compact object that directly bridges the gap between early starbursts and unobscured quasars at Cosmic Dawn [Fujimoto et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022Natur.604..261F/abstract). Looking beyond their immediate evolutionary paths, these high-redshift radio sources also offer immense utility as background beacons, providing a potential method to probe the 21 cm forest and neutral minihalos before the epoch of reionization [Furlanetto et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...579....1F/abstract).

The *WISE All-Sky Survey* was instrumental in identifying the apex of this evolutionary sequence, uncovering a rare population of [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOG (Hot Dust-obscured Galaxy)]] sources at $z \sim 2-4.6$ with extreme infrared luminosities ($L_{\rm IR} > 10^{13}-10^{14} L_{\odot}$) [Assef et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...27A/abstract), [Wu et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...852...96W/abstract). These sources consistently exhibit Eddington ratios ($\lambda_{\rm Edd}$) close to or exceeding unity, confirming they are in an earlier, higher-growth stage than unobscured quasars [Wu et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...852...96W/abstract), [Kim et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240803324K/abstract). For instance, the hyperluminous Hot DOG W2246-0526 was initially estimated to have $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 2.8$ [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract). However, it should be noted that the precise magnitude of these accretion rates remains an active area of investigation; recent JWST observations suggest that after rigorous correction for mid-infrared dust extinction, some extreme super-Eddington candidates may actually be accreting closer to a mildly super-Eddington rate ($\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 2$) [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...55K/abstract). This population traces a crucial epoch of cosmic history; while [[dust-obscured-star-formation-and-galaxies|dust-obscured star formation]] dominates the cosmic star formation rate density at cosmic noon, it becomes less prominent at higher redshifts, contributing less than $25\%$ by $z \sim 6$ [Koprowski et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912922K/abstract).

The geometry and triggering of these extreme systems are intimately linked to *merging progenitors* and dense environments. Observations demonstrate that super-Eddington, radio-loud active galaxies frequently reside in protoclusters and regions of significantly higher galaxy density than matched radio-quiet samples [Hatch et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..280H/abstract). High-resolution ALMA imaging of W2246-0526 has confirmed it as a multiple merger system, actively accreting from at least three companion galaxies via tidal dusty bridges [Díaz-Santos et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018Sci...362.1034D/abstract), [Fernández Aranda et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250210503F/abstract).

To complete the evolutionary sequence and emerge as an optical quasar, the system must undergo a "blowout" phase to shed its obscuring cocoon. Both radiative feedback, driven by radiation pressure on dust [Ishibashi et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.1291I/abstract), [Ishibashi et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.469.1496I/abstract), and jet-driven multiphase outflows [Vayner et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...923...59V/abstract) are invoked to expel the interstellar medium. The literature remains conflicting on which mechanism dominates the quenching of star formation: some models strongly favor jet-driven shocks capable of removing cold gas even in starbursting DOGs [Zhong et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.4531Z/abstract), while other observations of high-redshift radio-loud AGN find surprisingly weak jet-ISM coupling, suggesting radiative "quasar-mode" feedback is the primary driver of the transition [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...683A.169W/abstract). Furthermore, it remains an unresolved debate in [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] whether this extreme, radio-loud Hot DOG phase represents a universal evolutionary bottleneck for all massive galaxies or a specialized, rare evolutionary path.

## Accretion Physics and the Super-Eddington Limit

At the heart of [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOG (Hot Dust-obscured Galaxy)]]s and related hyperluminous dust-obscured quasars are supermassive black holes accreting at exceptional rates. Observations of their broad emission lines yield typical black hole masses on the order of $M_{\rm BH} \sim 10^9 M_\odot$, with bolometric luminosities pushing the Eddington ratio close to or above unity [Wu et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...852...96W/abstract), [Kim et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JKAS...57...95K/abstract). The Eddington ratio is defined as:
$$ \lambda_{\rm Edd} = \frac{L_{\rm bol}}{L_{\rm Edd}} $$
where $L_{\rm bol}$ is the bolometric luminosity and $L_{\rm Edd}$ is the Eddington luminosity. For instance, multiwavelength modeling of the hyperluminous source W2246-0526 reveals an Eddington ratio of $\lambda_{\rm Edd} = 2.8$ [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract). The extraordinary energetic output of this accretion profoundly affects the surrounding interstellar medium; for instance, W2246-0526 exhibits the most highly excited CO ladder ever reported (peaking at $J=10-12$), driven by extreme molecular gas densities and kinetic temperatures where the kinetic-to-dust temperature ratio reaches $T_k/T_d \sim 3.9$ [Harrington et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250417639H/abstract). Furthermore, recent simulation results suggest that these transient, highly super-Eddington accretion phases are intimately linked to the early growth of heavily obscured, JWST-identified "Little Red Dots", acting as their rapid-assembly progenitors [Quadri et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250505556Q/abstract).

At such high accretion rates, the standard geometrically thin, radiatively efficient accretion disk is expected to puff up into a radiation-pressure-dominated slim accretion disk [Mei et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...653A.100M/abstract). A defining feature of these flows, central to the physics of [[super-eddington-accretion-and-feedback|Super-Eddington Accretion and Feedback]], is photon trapping. Because the inward advection time-scale of the accreting gas becomes shorter than the outward photon diffusion time-scale, radiation is dragged across the event horizon rather than being emitted. This effect can saturate the observed bolometric luminosity, meaning that the intrinsic mass accretion rate ($\dot{M}$) and the corresponding black hole growth rate may significantly exceed what is inferred from the observed radiation [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract). On a local, smaller scale, Ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs) provide a critical analogy for this extreme physics, allowing astronomers to study super-Eddington accretion disks and photon trapping in stellar-mass systems [Fabrika et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016arXiv160105971F/abstract).

The extreme radiation environments in these central engines also profoundly affect their X-ray emission and coronal properties. Highly accreting systems, such as [[radio-loud-narrow-line-seyfert-1|Radio-loud Narrow-line Seyfert 1]] galaxies, frequently exhibit a prominent soft X-ray excess. In the context of [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]], this feature is often modeled as arising from an optically thick, low-temperature plasma, known as a [[warm-corona|Warm corona]], which Comptonizes disk photons [Ghosh et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.456..554G/abstract), [Ghosh et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.479.2464G/abstract). The standard hot, optically thin corona can also undergo dramatic changes in these environments. For example, X-ray flares in super-Eddington AGN have been observed to cause rapid coronal cooling (dropping from $kT_e \sim 200$ keV to $\sim 15$ keV) due to intense pair production, coinciding with the launching of powerful, high-velocity winds [Ding et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...931...77D/abstract). However, directly observing the intrinsic X-ray continuum in hot dust-obscured galaxies is challenging, as they are typically heavily obscured. Their X-ray spectra often appear anomalously flat (photon index $\Gamma \sim 0.8$) and reflection-dominated, implying Compton-thick obscuration ($N_{\rm H} > 10^{24}$ cm$^{-2}$) where the observed X-ray luminosity is merely a small fraction of the intrinsic output [Piconcelli et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...574L...9P/abstract).

Despite the theoretical appeal of sustained super-Eddington accretion, the literature presents some tension regarding the true magnitude of these accretion rates. Estimates of $L_{\rm bol}$ and $M_{\rm BH}$ relying on ultraviolet and optical broad lines are highly sensitive to dust extinction corrections. Recent JWST analyses using mid-infrared estimators, which are largely immune to dust obscuration, suggest that some sources previously reported to be accreting at extreme super-Eddington rates (e.g., $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \sim 40$) are actually accreting at mildly super-Eddington rates, with $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 2$ [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...55K/abstract). Nevertheless, even when these robust mid-infrared estimators are applied, heavily dust-obscured quasars consistently exhibit systematically higher Eddington ratios than their unobscured counterparts [Kim et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240803324K/abstract). This consensus reinforces the paradigm that the dust-obscured phase corresponds to a transient, maximal-growth epoch in early supermassive black hole assembly.

## Relativistic Jet Launching in High-Accretion Regimes

The traditional paradigm of radio galaxy evolution asserts that relativistic jets are predominantly hosted by massive elliptical galaxies. In this framework, [[low-power-radio-galaxies|low-power jets]] (such as Fanaroff-Riley Class I sources) are typically associated with radiatively inefficient states (e.g., [[low-excitation-radio-galaxies|Low-excitation radio galaxies]]), where the supermassive black hole is fed via the Bondi accretion of hot halo gas [Hardcastle et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.376.1849H/abstract). In contrast, more powerful radio galaxies (e.g., [[fanaroff-riley-radio-galaxies|FR II sources]]) are fueled by radiatively efficient cold gas accretion, yet still predominantly reside in massive ellipticals [Smith et al. (1986)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1986ApJ...306...64S/abstract). The discovery of powerful radio outflows in gas-rich, rapidly accreting spiral and merging systems has expanded this classical triggering model.

Notable counterexamples include [[radio-loud-narrow-line-seyfert-1|Radio-loud Narrow-line Seyfert 1]] (NLS1) galaxies, which frequently reside in spiral or pseudobulge hosts and accrete near the Eddington limit [Kotilainen et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...832..157K/abstract), [D'Ammando et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019Galax...7...87D/abstract). For instance, the peculiar source 1H 0323+342 possesses dual characteristics, exhibiting features of both a flat-spectrum radio quasar and a rapidly accreting NLS1 galaxy [Paliya et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...789..143P/abstract). At higher redshifts and higher luminosities, heavily dust-obscured systems demonstrate that relativistic jets can be launched even during maximal, super-Eddington growth phases. For example, the dust-obscured galaxy J1406+0102 operates at an extreme Eddington ratio of $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \simeq 3$ while hosting bright radio emission [Fukuchi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...987...66F/abstract). Similarly, the $z=1.92$ starburst merger MRC 0152-209 features a powerful jet with a kinetic luminosity of $L_{\rm jet} \gtrsim 2 \times 10^{46}$ erg s$^{-1}$ that is inferred to be launched from a super-Eddington accretion disk [Zhong et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.4531Z/abstract). Furthermore, the discovery of a 1.6 Mpc radio jet emerging from the bulgeless spiral galaxy J2345-0449 proves that massive ellipticals are not a strict morphological prerequisite for the formation of highly collimated, megaparsec-scale jets [Bagchi et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788..174B/abstract).

The theoretical explanation for jet launching in these high-accretion environments relies on the Blandford-Znajek mechanism, which extracts rotational energy from a spinning black hole via threading magnetic fields. Within the context of [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]], the Magnetically Arrested Disk (MAD) scenario is frequently invoked to explain how such systems become radio-loud [Rusinek-Abarca et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...922..202R/abstract). In the MAD paradigm, the magnetic flux accumulated at the black hole horizon—rather than black hole spin or $\lambda_{\rm Edd}$ alone—is the dominant factor governing jet power [Sikora et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...764L..24S/abstract). It is hypothesized that large-scale magnetic flux is advected to the center during gas-rich major mergers or prior hot-accretion phases. When the accumulated magnetic pressure halts the inward flow of gas, powerful jets can be launched even if the accretion flow is geometrically thick and radiatively efficient due to super-Eddington radiation pressure. Nonetheless, the literature remains divided on whether magnetic flux advection or high black hole spin combined with specific accretion modes is the ultimate determinant of radio loudness in these systems [Sikora et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...764L..24S/abstract), [Kozieł-Wierzbowska et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017FrASS...4...39K/abstract).

Once launched, jets in super-Eddington, dust-obscured galaxies must propagate through an exceptionally dense and turbulent interstellar medium. These early evolutionary stages are often observationally classified as gigahertz-peaked spectrum (GPS) or compact [[steep-spectrum-radio-source|Steep-spectrum radio source]]s (CSS) [Glowacki et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.489.4926G/abstract), [Yao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.533.1281Y/abstract). Even in classical early-type galaxies, such young, compact radio sources can drive significant feedback; for example, jet-ISM interactions in B2 0258+35 induce prominent neutral gas turbulence with velocity dispersions of $\sim 90\text{ km s}^{-1}$ [Murthy et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...629A..58M/abstract). Recent surveys using LOFAR have also identified a population of "galaxy-scale jets" (GSJs) representing an early stage of jet expansion where the radio emission is still contained entirely within, or is directly influencing, the host galaxy's evolution before breaking out into intergalactic space [Webster et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.500.4921W/abstract). In such environments, the young or confined jets couple strongly with the ambient cold gas, generating diffuse shocks and driving massive multiphase outflows [Allison et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021AN....342.1062A/abstract). The continuous interaction between the relativistic plasma and the dense molecular cocoon ultimately dictates whether the jet can successfully break out to form large-scale radio lobes, or if it remains permanently frustrated by its host's extreme high-accretion environment.

## Thermal Reprocessing and Dust Obscuration Mechanisms

The spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies are defined by the extreme thermal reprocessing of their central [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]]. In these systems, the intrinsic ultraviolet and optical emission from the accretion disk is almost entirely absorbed by a thick, obscuring cocoon of dust and re-radiated at longer wavelengths. This process generates a profound mid-infrared excess, placing their total infrared luminosities in the hyperluminous regime ($L_{\mathrm{IR}} > 10^{13}-10^{14} L_{\odot}$) [Dey et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...677..943D/abstract), [Assef et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...27A/abstract). 

A defining characteristic of this population—distinguishing them from typical [[luminous-infrared-galaxies-lirgs|Luminous Infrared Galaxies (LIRGs)]] or [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]]—is the unusually high temperature of the obscuring medium. SED modeling reveals that the bulk of the dust is heated to $T > 60\text{ K}$, an effect primarily driven by the intense radiation field of the central quasar rather than distributed star formation [Assef et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...27A/abstract). High-resolution ALMA observations of the archetypal hyperluminous source W2246-0526 demonstrate that dust temperatures can peak at $T \sim 110\text{ K}$ in the nuclear region before dropping to $\sim 40\text{ K}$ at radii of 8 kpc [Fernández Aranda et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250210503F/abstract). The presence of such hot, dense material produces a steep mid-infrared continuum alongside deep silicate absorption features, which act as hallmark signatures of deeply embedded, rapid black hole growth. Despite this profound obscuration, a sub-population of "blue excess" Hot DOGs has been identified, where scattered reflection or co-eval starbursts provide unextincted ultraviolet light that manages to leak through the surrounding dust cocoon [Assef et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...819..111A/abstract).

The immense dust columns required to reprocess the quasar's bolometric output imply extreme gas and dust densities in the host's nuclear regions. Radiative transfer modeling indicates local hydrogen densities on the order of $n_{\mathrm{H}} \sim 3 \times 10^3\text{ cm}^{-3}$ and visual extinctions reaching $A_V \sim 300\text{ mag}$ [Fernández Aranda et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...682A.166F/abstract). At X-ray wavelengths, these enormous column densities manifest as Compton-thick obscuration ($N_{\mathrm{H}} \gtrsim 10^{24}\text{ cm}^{-2}$) [Parlanti et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...684A..24P/abstract). X-ray spectra of these sources often exhibit a distinctly flat continuum ($\Gamma \sim 0.8$) accompanied by strong Fe K$\alpha$ emission, indicating that the direct X-ray continuum is heavily suppressed and the observed flux is almost entirely reflection-dominated [Piconcelli et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...574L...9P/abstract). In such dense environments, the dust-to-gas ratio plays a critical dynamical role; increased dust opacity couples efficiently with the quasar's radiation field to boost the effective Eddington ratio, driving powerful radiative outflows that eventually clear the obscuring cocoon [Ishibashi et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.1291I/abstract). The large-scale geometry of this obscuring material may sometimes take the form of "[[supermassive-disks|superdisks]]"—gigantic structures of thermal gas and dust oriented perpendicular to the radio jets—which have been proposed to explain radio lobe asymmetries and localized Ly$\alpha$ absorption dips in high-redshift sources [Gopal-Krishna et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000ApJ...529..189G/abstract).

However, this severe dust obscuration complicates precise measurements of the intrinsic bolometric luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{bol}}$) and black hole mass ($M_{\mathrm{BH}}$). Consequently, there is an active debate in the literature regarding the exact accretion state of these objects. Initial studies relying on optical or rest-frame UV lines often inferred extreme super-Eddington accretion rates (e.g., $\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd} } \approx 3$) due to the inherent uncertainties in correcting for such massive extinction [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract). Recent analyses utilizing mid-infrared continuum estimators—which are substantially less sensitive to dust extinction—suggest that while these quasars are accreting very rapidly, some may be closer to a mildly super-Eddington regime ($\lambda_{\mathrm{Edd}} \approx 2$) rather than vastly exceeding it [Kim et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240803324K/abstract), [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...55K/abstract). Although the literature remains conflicting on the precise prevalence of true super-Eddington accretion versus Eddington-limited growth, it is universally recognized that this [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOG (Hot Dust-obscured Galaxy)]] phase represents a transient period of maximal, heavily obscured assembly for both the black hole and its host galaxy.

## Multiphase Feedback: Radiative Winds and Jet-ISM Interaction

The interplay between high-accretion-rate central engines and host galaxy environments in super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies manifests through powerful multiphase outflows. These systems exhibit a complex feedback mechanism where both wide-aperture, radiation-pressure-driven winds and collimated relativistic jets act in concert to deplete the host's gas reservoir and inject significant turbulence into the interstellar medium (ISM). This process, known as negative feedback, is thought to be the primary mechanism by which a [[heavily-dust-obscured-starbursts|dust-enshrouded starburst]] transitions into an unobscured quasar [Ishibashi et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.1291I/abstract).

**Radiative Winds and Ultra-fast Outflows**
During the super-Eddington accretion phase, immense radiation pressure acts directly on the high dust column surrounding the nucleus. The coupling between the radiation field and the dusty ISM launches wide-aperture winds that are capable of reaching extreme velocities. For instance, early results from JWST Early Release Science programs targeting $z \sim 3$ [[luminous-reddened-quasars|extremely red quasars]] have detected prominent shock-excited regions orthogonal to their ionization cones, likely representing massive, wide-angle outflows impacting the host galaxy [Vayner et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...955...92V/abstract). Observations with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have also revealed dusty outflows blueshifted by $5250\text{ km s}^{-1}$ in a $z=4.6$ [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOG (Hot Dust-obscured Galaxy)]] [Vayner et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251009870V/abstract). At the innermost scales, X-ray spectroscopy has detected ultra-fast outflows propagating at $0.2-0.3c$ in the extreme local accretor PDS 456, carrying a kinetic power that can exceed the Eddington luminosity [Xrism Collaboration et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025Natur.641.1132X/abstract).

Furthermore, the intense radiation from the central engine can exert profound localized negative feedback on neighboring systems. Observations of a $z \approx 2.7$ quasar demonstrate that its radiation directly disrupts and compacts the molecular gas in an interacting companion galaxy, effectively inhibiting star formation within the companion [Balashev et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025Natur.641.1137B/abstract).

**Jet-ISM Interaction and Molecular Outflows**
In addition to radiative winds, the propagation of radio jets through a dense, clumpy ISM drives energy-conserving shocks that efficiently sweep up surrounding material [Vayner et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...923...59V/abstract). This jet-driven feedback is highly effective at launching massive molecular outflows. In the $z=1.92$ radio galaxy MRC 0152-209, Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) observations resolved a compact ($\sim 500\text{ pc}$), massive molecular outflow with a mass outflow rate of $1200-2600\ M_{\odot}\text{ yr}^{-1}$ propagating perpendicular to the jet axis [Zhong et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.4531Z/abstract). In such multiphase outflows, the bulk of the expelled mass resides in the cold molecular phase, whereas the majority of the kinetic luminosity and momentum flux is carried by the ionized gas [Vayner et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...923...59V/abstract). The injection of jet-driven shocks into the host galaxy's ISM drastically increases turbulence, disrupting molecular clouds and yielding gas depletion timescales on the order of just a few megayears. 

**Kinetic Efficiency and Feedback Constraints**
Despite the immense energies involved, the literature presents a conflicting picture regarding the overall kinetic efficiency—defined as the ratio of outflow kinetic power to the bolometric luminosity of the [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|AGN]]. Studies of some high-redshift radio-loud quasars find coupling efficiencies of $0.1\% - 1\%$, consistent with the requirements of theoretical models for effective negative feedback [Vayner et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...923...59V/abstract). The momentum boost factors in these systems (ranging from 3 to 24) support a radiative-mode AGN feedback scenario that is augmented by episodic jet clearing [Zhong et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.4531Z/abstract). 

However, the transfer of energy to galactic scales is not uniformly efficient. In the powerful $z \approx 3.5$ radio-loud AGN 4C+19.71, JWST observations revealed a highly inefficient coupling ($\lesssim 10^{-4}$), with only a minor fraction of the feedback processes happening on scales $< 25\text{ kpc}$ [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...683A.169W/abstract). The discovery of highly inhomogeneous, clumpy wind structures by the XRISM observatory further suggests that the energy and momentum from nuclear winds may not transfer efficiently to galaxy-scale outflows in all systems due to the clumpiness of the wind and the surrounding ISM [Xrism Collaboration et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025Natur.641.1132X/abstract). Thus, while the combination of jet-driven shocks and radiative winds is universally observed to drive massive molecular outflows, the global efficiency of this multiphase feedback in fully quenching [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] remains an active subject of debate.

## Host Galaxy Morphologies and Environmental Triggering

Classical models of [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] assert that extended radio jets are predominantly hosted by massive elliptical galaxies [Smith et al. (1986)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1986ApJ...306...64S/abstract). This morphological preference is underpinned by the classic finding in the local universe that the fraction of radio-loud AGN rises steeply with stellar mass, scaling proportionally to $M_*^{2.5}$ [Best et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.362...25B/abstract). However, super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies, alongside related populations such as [[radio-loud-narrow-line-seyfert-1|Radio-loud Narrow-line Seyfert 1]] galaxies, challenge this morphological dichotomy. Observations have revealed powerful megaparsec-scale relativistic jets launching from [[luminous-gas-rich-disc-galaxies|gas-rich spiral galaxies]] and pseudobulges [Bagchi et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788..174B/abstract), [Kotilainen et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...832..157K/abstract). In the high-redshift Universe, these radio-loud systems are often deeply embedded in massive, highly active [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]].

These extreme systems are preferentially located in exceptionally dense environments. [[high-redshift-active-galactic-nuclei|High-redshift radio-loud AGN]] and [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot Dust-Obscured Galaxies (Hot DOGs)]] reside in regions significantly denser than their radio-quiet counterparts, often marking the nodes of assembling protoclusters [Hatch et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..280H/abstract), [Assef et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...27A/abstract). Some of these early radio-loud AGN are embedded within "[[giant-ly-nebulae|enormous Lyα nebulae]]" (ELAN) at $z \approx 2$, which have been found to host massive molecular gas reservoirs comparable in mass to the ionized gas within the nebulae themselves [Decarli et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...645L...3D/abstract). Structures surrounding $z > 5$ radio-loud quasars represent some of the most overdense regions known in the early Universe, providing a continuous supply of cold gas to fuel both starbursts and rapid black hole growth [Overzier et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...926..114O/abstract). 

Major galaxy mergers are frequently implicated as the primary triggering mechanism, gravitationally funneling gas into the nuclear regions to feed super-Eddington accretion. Recent high-resolution Hubble Space Telescope (HST) WFC3/IR imaging strongly supports this paradigm, revealing that powerful radio-loud quasars at $z \sim 1-2$ overwhelmingly reside in ongoing or recent major mergers compared to matched radio-quiet control samples [Breiding et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...91B/abstract). A spectacular example is the hyperluminous Hot DOG W2246-0526 at $z=4.60$. ALMA observations resolve this object as a multiple-merger system where the central galaxy is actively stripping gas from at least three companions, connected by dusty tidal bridges extending over $50 \text{ kpc}$ [Díaz-Santos et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018Sci...362.1034D/abstract), [Fernández Aranda et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...695L..15F/abstract). In extreme cases, the chaotic dynamics of these massive mergers can result in gravitational wave recoil following the coalescence of binary supermassive black holes. A notable example is the radio-loud quasar 3C 186, considered a strong candidate for a gravitational wave recoiling black hole; it exhibits a substantial spatial offset of $11\text{ kpc}$ and a spectral velocity shift of $2100\text{ km s}^{-1}$ relative to its host galaxy's center [Chiaberge et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...600A..57C/abstract), [Castignani et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661L...2C/abstract). Similarly, massive molecular outflows and super-Eddington accretion ($\lambda_{\rm Edd} \sim 0.07-4$) have been observed in radio-loud starburst mergers at $z \sim 1.9$ [Zhong et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.4531Z/abstract).

However, the literature is conflicting regarding whether major mergers are universally required to trigger this phase. While systems like W2246-0526 are violently interacting, other hyperluminous Hot DOGs exhibit undisturbed kinematics. For instance, W0410-0913 at $z=3.63$ hosts a fast-rotating, rotationally supported molecular disk with no clear kinematic signatures of a recent major merger, despite being surrounded by at least 24 companion galaxies [Ginolfi et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022NatCo..13.4574G/abstract). This suggests that secular gas accretion from a dense environment or a series of minor mergers may also be sufficient to sustain extreme, super-Eddington accretion without a catastrophic major merger.

During this heavily obscured phase, rapid stellar mass assembly occurs coevally with black hole growth. The host galaxies of Hot DOGs and [[high-redshift-dusty-star-forming-galaxies|High-redshift dusty star-forming galaxies]] typically build up massive stellar populations of $M_* \sim 10^{11}-10^{12} M_\odot$ [Assef et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...27A/abstract). Driven by star formation rates frequently exceeding $1000 M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$, these systems undergo an intense epoch of population growth and are predicted to be the direct progenitors of the massive, quiescent elliptical galaxies observed at lower redshifts [Long et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...953...11L/abstract).

## Recent Breakthroughs: The JWST and ALMA Era

The advent of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and the Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) has revolutionized the study of super-Eddington radio-loud [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOGs]] and related heavily obscured quasars. The unprecedented sensitivity and angular resolution of these observatories have enabled the characterization of multiphase interstellar media, the discovery of close companion galaxies, and the measurement of spatially resolved dust properties on kiloparsec scales.

The synergy between millimeter and infrared observatories has also been critical in correctly identifying the true nature of extreme cosmic sources. For example, NOEMA millimeter continuum observations have revealed that many "red excess" galaxy candidates initially identified by JWST as residing at $z > 10$ are, in fact, heavily dust-obscured massive galaxies at intermediate redshifts ($z \sim 2-4$), mimicking the photometric signatures of ultra-high-redshift objects [Meyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681L...3M/abstract).

Recent millimeter interferometry has mapped the thermal and chemical structures of extreme starbursting environments. In the hyperluminous $z=4.6$ Hot DOG W2246-0526, ALMA observations spanning seven frequency bands—including the challenging ALMA Band 10—have constrained the fine-structure line emission and continuum across a $\sim 50$ kpc region [Fernández Aranda et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...682A.166F/abstract), [Fernández Aranda et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250210503F/abstract). These data reveal spatially resolved dust temperatures that peak at $\sim 110$ K in the quasar-heated nucleus but drop to $30-40$ K in the extended tidal streams. This indicates that the dust in the interacting halo is primarily heated by in-situ star formation rather than the central [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|active galactic nucleus]] [Fernández Aranda et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250210503F/abstract). The detection of extreme ionization states ($\log(U) = -0.5$) and high gas densities ($n_H \sim 3 \times 10^3$ cm$^{-3}$) further confirms the presence of an X-ray-dominated region in the core [Fernández Aranda et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...682A.166F/abstract).

The combination of JWST and ALMA has also provided direct evidence that these massive, [[high-redshift-active-galactic-nuclei|high-redshift AGNs]] reside in exceptionally dense environments. Observations of luminous $z \approx 3.5$ radio-loud AGNs have ubiquitously detected minor companion systems within projected distances of $\lesssim 18$ kpc, traced by [C II] $158\ \mu$m emission and peculiar [O III] kinematics [Wang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..88W/abstract). In a remarkable $z=5.63$ interacting system, ALMA has identified [C II] bridges spanning $\sim 10$ proper kpc between a bright quasar and a compact dusty companion. This structural connectivity strongly supports the paradigm that major mergers simultaneously trigger intense starbursts and fuel rapid black hole growth [Zhu et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024RNAAS...8..284Z/abstract). Furthermore, recent ALMA Band 7 observations of the host galaxies of radio-loud quasars at $z > 5$ suggest a potential mild suppression of [C II] emission relative to their infrared luminosities, hinting at early-stage jet feedback impacting the cool gas reservoirs [Mazzucchelli et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...694A.171M/abstract).

In the near-infrared, JWST NIRSpec spectroscopy has permitted the robust characterization of host galaxy kinematics and black hole accretion rates, free from the severe extinction that plagues optical studies. High-resolution integral field spectroscopy of heavily obscured $z \sim 4.7$ [[high-redshift-dusty-star-forming-galaxies|dusty star-forming galaxies]], such as ALESS073.1, has uncovered extremely broad H$\alpha$ emission ($>9000$ km s$^{-1}$), confirming the presence of buried supermassive black holes. Furthermore, while warm ionized outflows are observed to inject turbulence into the central regions, they appear insufficient to disrupt the cold molecular disks or completely quench star formation [Parlanti et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...684A..24P/abstract). 

Finally, JWST has prompted a critical re-evaluation of the most extreme accretion rates reported in the literature, resolving tensions regarding the physical plausibility of sustained hyper-Eddington accretion. For the $z=3.965$ [[dust-obscured-star-formation-and-galaxies|dust-obscured quasar]] LID-568, which was previously thought to be accreting at $40$ times the Eddington limit, an extinction-free mid-infrared analysis yields an intrinsic bolometric luminosity of $L_{\rm bol} = 10^{46.83 \pm 0.07}$ erg s$^{-1}$ and a revised Eddington ratio of $\lambda_{\rm Edd} = 1.97 \pm 0.88$ [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...55K/abstract). This suggests that after proper correction for dust attenuation, some objects originally classified as vastly super-Eddington may simply be accreting near the Eddington limit during their peak evolutionary phase.

## Open Questions and Future Research Directions

Despite rapid observational progress, super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies remain enigmatic, presenting several unresolved questions regarding their accretion physics, feedback efficiency, and cosmological impact. 

A primary tension in the literature concerns the true magnitude of their accretion rates. While many studies report extreme super-Eddington ratios (e.g., $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 3$), recent analyses using JWST/NIRSpec mid-infrared estimators suggest that heavy dust extinction can severely bias bolometric luminosity calculations. When properly corrected, some candidates previously thought to be highly super-Eddington may actually be accreting at mildly super-Eddington rates ($\lambda_{\rm Edd} \approx 2$) [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...55K/abstract). 

Furthermore, the physical mechanism governing their radio loudness is an active subject of debate within [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]]. It remains unclear whether the launching of powerful relativistic jets in these high-accretion systems is primarily determined by high black hole spin, or if it is governed by magnetic flux accumulation at the event horizon, as described by the Magnetically Arrested Disk (MAD) paradigm [Sikora et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...764L..24S/abstract). 

The evolutionary context of these sources also presents open questions. It is currently debated whether the [[hot-dog-hot-dust-obscured-galaxy|Hot DOG (Hot Dust-obscured Galaxy)]] phase is a universal, albeit brief, stage in the evolution of all massive galaxies or a rare evolutionary pathway. Additionally, characterizing the redshift evolution of these sources is critical to determining whether high-redshift "Little Red Dots" and Hot DOGs represent distinct populations or simply different temporal snapshots of the same evolutionary sequence transitioning from heavily dust-enshrouded starbursts to unobscured quasars.

These systems also have profound implications for early-universe cosmology. The exceptionally dense environments surrounding some high-redshift radio-loud quasars, such as SDSS J0836+0054 at $z=5.8$, qualitatively resemble the conditions theoretically required to spur the formation of direct collapse seeds for massive black holes [Overzier et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...926..114O/abstract). Determining whether [[super-eddington-accretion-and-feedback|Super-Eddington Accretion and Feedback]] is a necessary phase for the rapid assembly of all $z > 6$ supermassive black holes remains a critical research frontier.

Finally, super-Eddington radio-loud dust-obscured galaxies are emerging as potentially important sources in multi-messenger astrophysics. Driven by massive, jet-driven multiphase outflows and extreme radiation fields, objects such as the $z=0.236$ system J1406+0102 indicate that these radio-loud systems may provide a significant contribution to the high-energy ($\gtrsim 100\mathrm{\,TeV}$) cosmic neutrino background [Fukuchi et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230305605F/abstract), [Fukuchi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...987...66F/abstract). Future observations constraining the exact coupling efficiency of jet-driven feedback in these dusty environments will be vital for understanding both their role in generating the cosmic neutrino flux and their impact on global galaxy quenching.
