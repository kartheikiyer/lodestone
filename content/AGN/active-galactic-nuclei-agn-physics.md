## Article

**Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics** is the study of the extreme energetic processes occurring at the centers of a fraction of galaxies, where matter accreting onto [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] powers bolometric luminosities that can outshine the entire host galaxy. The foundational paradigm of the field establishes that this immense, broadband radiative output is driven by the highly efficient conversion of gravitational potential energy into radiation via various [[accretion-processes|Accretion Processes]] [Blandford et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992AIPC..254....3B/abstract), [Shields et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999PASP..111..661S/abstract). Depending on the accretion rate relative to the [[eddington-ratio|Eddington limit]], these central engines manifest in different physical states: as radiatively efficient, geometrically thin accretion disks—characteristic of luminous [[quasars|Quasars]] and [[seyfert-galaxies|Seyfert galaxies]]—or as a [[radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow|Radiatively inefficient accretion flow]] (RIAF) typical of [[low-luminosity-active-galactic-nuclei|Low-Luminosity Active Galactic Nuclei]] [Ho et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999ApJ...516..672H/abstract). 

Historically, the diverse observational taxonomy of active galaxies—ranging from heavily obscured sources to highly variable [[blazars|Blazars]]—was synthesized through [[agn-classification-and-unified-models|AGN Classification and Unified Models]], which attribute spectral differences primarily to the observer's viewing angle through a dusty circumnuclear torus. However, modern AGN physics recognizes that intrinsic physical properties, such as black hole spin and accretion geometry, are fundamental to driving complex phenomena like the launching of relativistic jets and the resulting radio-loud dichotomy [Sikora et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...658..815S/abstract). These jets, along with radiation-driven ultra-fast [[galaxy-outflows|Galaxy outflows]], generate profound [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]] that actively regulate the evolution of their [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]]. This mechanical and radiative energy injection is an essential component of [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]], acting to offset cooling flows in massive galaxy clusters and establishing tight co-evolutionary scaling relations between black holes and their host spheroids [Marconi et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002astro.ph..1504M/abstract), [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract), [King et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ARA&A..53..115K/abstract).

The activation of these extraordinary central engines is intricately linked to both secular gas dynamics and [[galaxy-merger-physics|Galaxy Merger Physics]], the latter of which funnels massive amounts of gas into the nuclear region and frequently produces closely interacting [[dual-active-galactic-nuclei|Dual active galactic nuclei]]. This article explores the comprehensive physics of AGN, from the high-energy X-ray plasmas of the innermost corona to the large-scale thermodynamic impact of jet-inflated cavities. Finally, it surveys the current open questions and future frontiers of the field. These include the precise origin of the ultra-high-energy cosmic ray and neutrino backgrounds, the ongoing debate over the physical mechanisms that dictate the radio-loud dichotomy, and the nature of heavily obscured [[high-redshift-active-galactic-nuclei|High-redshift active galactic nuclei]] recently unveiled by next-generation infrared observatories.

## Historical Development and the SMBH Paradigm

The recognition of Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) as a distinct class of astrophysical objects evolved significantly over the twentieth century. Early optical observations identified a subset of galaxies exhibiting unusually broad, high-excitation emission lines in their central regions. Although Carl Seyfert conducted a systematic study of these peculiar [[seyfert-galaxies|Seyfert galaxies]] in 1943, the phenomenon remained a niche curiosity until the advent of radio astronomy in the 1950s [Shields et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999PASP..111..661S/abstract). Radio surveys revealed a novel population of highly energetic, compact extragalactic sources. The watershed moment occurred in 1963 with the measurement of the cosmological redshifts for the radio sources 3C 273 and 3C 48 [Shields et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999PASP..111..661S/abstract). These observations demonstrated that [[quasars|Quasars]] possessed extraordinary bolometric luminosities—often outshining their entire [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]] by factors of up to a thousand—emanating from spatially unresolved nuclear regions.

The extreme luminosities combined with rapid temporal variability (often on timescales of days) dictated that the emission must originate from regions only light-days across. Furthermore, the radiation produced a powerful, broad [[non-stellar-continuum|Non-stellar continuum]] spanning from radio to X-ray wavelengths, strongly indicating a non-thermal or accretion-driven origin rather than a collection of stars. Because the required energy density could not be sustained by stellar nucleosynthesis, theorists sought an alternative physical mechanism.

To resolve this energetics problem, the paradigm of accretion onto [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] (SMBHs) was advanced. Within a year of the quasar redshift discoveries, it was independently proposed by Salpeter (1964), Zel'dovich & Novikov (1964), and Lynden-Bell (1969) that the immense radiative output was powered by the release of gravitational potential energy from matter accreting into a deep gravitational potential well. This theoretical framework established the modern concept of AGN [[central-engines|Central engines]]: a central SMBH, with a mass typically in the range of $10^6 - 10^9 M_\odot$, fed by a surrounding accretion flow.

This accretion process is exceptionally efficient, capable of converting a significant fraction of the infalling rest-mass energy into radiation according to $L_{\rm bol} = \eta \dot{M} c^2$, where the radiative efficiency $\eta$ can exceed the efficiency of nuclear fusion by more than an order of magnitude. The canonical "standard model" defines these engines as comprising the massive black hole surrounded by a geometrically thin, optically thick accretion disk, which produces the bulk of the ultraviolet and optical continuum [Blandford et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992AIPC..254....3B/abstract). Subsequent high-resolution observations have consistently verified that the primary non-thermal emission emanates from scales smaller than a few parsecs. Today, the foundational consensus in extragalactic astrophysics asserts that the gravitational potential energy of gas accreting onto a central SMBH drives the high luminosities observed across the electromagnetic spectrum [Krolik et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999agnf.book.....K/abstract), [Marconi et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002astro.ph..1504M/abstract), [Beckmann et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012agn..book.....B/abstract). This realization marked a profound shift from empirical, emission-line-based taxonomy to a unified physical understanding governed by black hole accretion physics.

## Accretion Dynamics and Disk Physics

The structure, radiative output, and overall spectral energy distribution (SED) of AGN [[central-engines|Central engines]] are primarily dictated by the black hole mass ($M_{\rm BH}$), spin ($a_*$), and the dimensionless accretion rate $\dot{m} \equiv \dot{M}_{\rm acc}/\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}$, where $\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}$ is the Eddington mass accretion rate [Blandford et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992AIPC..254....3B/abstract), [Marconi et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002astro.ph..1504M/abstract), [Capellupo et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.446.3427C/abstract), [Su et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250110793S/abstract). The variation in $\dot{m}$ drives distinct phase transitions in the accretion flow, resulting in several diverse observable accretion modes.

In the moderately high [[eddington-ratio|Eddington ratio]] regime ($0.01 \lesssim \dot{m} \lesssim 1$), [[accretion-processes|Accretion Processes]] are well-described by the standard geometrically thin, optically thick accretion disk model. This disk is highly radiatively efficient and produces a characteristic "[[non-stellar-continuum|Big Blue Bump]]" peaking in the ultraviolet. Although earlier multi-wavelength discrepancies cast doubt on the universality of thin disks, comprehensive SED modeling that accounts for host galaxy reddening and intrinsic variability has confirmed their presence in standard luminous AGN [Capellupo et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.446.3427C/abstract). 

At low accretion rates ($\dot{m} \ll 0.01$), the accretion disk undergoes a structural transition to a [[radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow|Radiatively inefficient accretion flow]] (RIAF), frequently modeled as an advection-dominated accretion flow (ADAF) [Yuan et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006JPhCS..54..427Y/abstract). Multi-wavelength observations of [[low-luminosity-active-galactic-nuclei|Low-Luminosity Active Galactic Nuclei]] (LLAGNs) robustly demonstrate the absence of the Big Blue Bump, indicating that the inner thin disk has either truncated or transitioned into a hot, optically thin, and geometrically thick state [Ho et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999ApJ...516..672H/abstract). This radiatively inefficient mode is closely associated with [[low-excitation-radio-galaxies|Low-excitation radio galaxies]], which typically reside in massive ellipticals with depleted cold gas reservoirs [Smolčić et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...730...64S/abstract). Notably, radio-loudness is strongly anti-correlated with the [[eddington-ratio|Eddington ratio]] across different AGN populations [Sikora et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...658..815S/abstract). However, the literature remains divided on the physical origin of this dichotomy: it is actively debated whether the transition between accretion states alone dictates jet production, or if black hole spin acts as the dominant parameter driving radio-loudness (the "spin paradigm") [Sikora et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...658..815S/abstract), [Sikora et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008NewAR..51..891S/abstract).

In the extreme regime of $\dot{m} \gtrsim 1$, the flow transitions into a "slim disk" characterized by strong radial advection and photon trapping. These systems, linked to [[super-eddington-accretion-and-feedback|Super-Eddington Accretion and Feedback]], feature a self-gravitating, non-Keplerian outer disk and an optically thick inner flow. Spectral modeling of extreme sources, such as the narrow-line Seyfert 1 galaxy [[ton-s180|Ton S180]], supports this multi-component slim disk structure at near- or super-Eddington rates, where the emission wavelength of the self-gravitating radius ($\lambda_{\rm sg}$) can reach $\approx 4000$ Å depending on the viscosity parameter [Kawaguchi et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004A&A...415...47K/abstract). 

Across all these accretion modes, the innermost region of the accretion flow—closest to the event horizon—can be dynamically probed through high-energy observations. X-ray variability on timescales of minutes to hours, along with coronal continuum shifts, provides direct measurements of the underlying accretion physics and disk-corona interactions [Kara et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250322791K/abstract). The combination of these X-ray constraints with broadband SEDs reveals that radiative efficiency is primarily governed by $\dot{m}$, while total bolometric luminosity relies comparably on both $\dot{m}$ and $M_{\rm BH}$ [Su et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250110793S/abstract).

## The Unified Model and Multi-wavelength Emission

The foundational framework of [[agn-classification-and-unified-models|AGN Classification and Unified Models]] posits that the observed diversity of active galaxies—such as [[seyfert-galaxies|Seyfert galaxies]], [[quasars|Quasars]], and [[blazars|Blazars]]—primarily arises from the observer's viewing angle relative to an anisotropic, dusty torus. In this "pure orientation" scheme, [[optically-classified-type-1-active-galactic-nuclei|Type 1 AGNs]] are viewed face-on, allowing direct observation of the [[central-engines|Central engines]] and the dense, high-velocity Broad Line Region (BLR). Conversely, in Type 2 AGNs, the line of sight is obstructed by the torus, restricting observations to the more extended, slower-moving [[narrow-line-regions-and-seyferts|Narrow Line Region]] (NLR). Interestingly, the Close AGN Reference Survey (CARS) has identified a correlation between the maximum projected size of this [[extended-narrow-line-regions|extended narrow-line region]] and black hole mass, potentially serving as a timescale indicator for radiative accretion episodes [Husemann et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...659A.124H/abstract).

The physical nature of this obscuring material within the [[circumnuclear-environment|Circumnuclear Environment]] has been heavily investigated, with models increasingly favoring [[clumpy-torus-models|Clumpy torus models]] over smooth, homogeneous gas distributions. Spectroscopic studies of silicate emission features reveal that AGN dust differs significantly from the standard Galactic interstellar medium; the grains are primarily micron-sized ($\sim 1.5\,\mu\mathrm{m}$), producing a relatively flat or "gray" extinction law [Xie et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJS..228....6X/abstract). Deep mid-infrared and hard X-ray surveys have demonstrated that this obscuration is ubiquitous, uncovering large populations of heavily obscured and [[compton-thick-agn|Compton-thick AGN]] that escape optical detection [Ballantyne et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...736...56B/abstract). The overall fraction of obscured AGNs is strongly dependent on intrinsic luminosity and redshift, peaking at bolometric luminosities of $L_{\mathrm{bol}} \sim 10^{10}-10^{11} L_\odot$ and systematically increasing out to $z \sim 4$ [Lyu et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966..229L/abstract).

A complete unified picture must also account for the multi-wavelength spectral energy distribution (SED), which is fundamentally dictated by the accretion rate and black hole mass. In standard, highly luminous AGNs, the SED is dominated by a "Big Blue Bump" peaking in the ultraviolet, which is the thermal signature of a geometrically thin, optically thick accretion disk [[accretion-processes|Accretion Processes]]. Conversely, [[low-luminosity-active-galactic-nuclei|Low-Luminosity Active Galactic Nuclei]] lack this characteristic UV excess. Their SEDs are instead successfully modeled by radiatively inefficient, advection-dominated accretion flows (ADAFs) where the accreting plasma is optically thin and geometrically thick [Ho et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999ApJ...516..672H/abstract), [Yuan et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006JPhCS..54..427Y/abstract). 

Beyond the thermal disk, multi-wavelength emission traces powerful non-thermal processes. High-energy X-ray continuum emission typically originates from inverse Compton scattering in a hot, compact [[warm-corona|Warm corona]] near the inner accretion disk [Zhang et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...863...71Z/abstract). When the central engine launches relativistic jets, the emission spans from the radio regime to $\gamma$-rays. If the jet is aligned closely with the observer's line of sight, relativistic beaming amplifies the [[non-stellar-agn-continuum|Non-stellar AGN continuum]], resulting in the extreme variability and high luminosities characteristic of [[blazars|Blazars]] [Dermer et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016CRPhy..17..594D/abstract).

Recent observations have begun to challenge the strict pure orientation hypothesis, prompting a paradigm shift toward a multi-parameter unified model. Comparative studies indicate that Type 1 and Type 2 AGNs often reside in [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]] with distinct intrinsic properties. For example, Type 2 hosts show significantly higher supernova rates and differing stellar ages compared to Type 1 hosts [Villarroel et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...837..110V/abstract). Furthermore, when matched in mid-infrared luminosity, Type 1 AGNs exhibit intrinsic [O III] $\lambda 5007$ luminosities up to a factor of ten higher than their Type 2 counterparts. These findings imply that intrinsic luminosity, accretion efficiency, and the evolutionary stage of the host galaxy—not just viewing angle—are necessary to explain the full AGN dichotomy [Villarroel et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...837..110V/abstract).

## Relativistic Jets and Radio-Loud Populations

Relativistic jets are highly collimated outflows of magnetized plasma launched from the immediate vicinity of [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]]. While all active galactic nuclei (AGN) accrete matter, only $\sim 10-20\%$ of the AGN population exhibit powerful large-scale jets, creating a fundamental "radio-loud" dichotomy [Ballantyne et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MPLA...22.2397B/abstract). The exact physical trigger for jet launching remains an active debate. The "spin paradigm" posits that rapid black hole spin—which is particularly prevalent in [[high-mass-galaxies|giant elliptical galaxies]] following major mergers—is the primary driver of jet production [Sikora et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...658..815S/abstract), [Sikora et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008NewAR..51..891S/abstract). However, spin alone is insufficient; jet formation also appears to require a specific accretion geometry, most commonly associated with a large scale-height, [[radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow|Radiatively inefficient accretion flow]] (ADAF) [Ballantyne et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MPLA...22.2397B/abstract). Consequently, across different populations, radio loudness is observed to strongly anti-correlate with the [[eddington-ratio|Eddington ratio]], $\lambda = L_{\rm bol}/L_{\rm Edd}$, increasing markedly as the normalized accretion rate drops [Sikora et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...658..815S/abstract).

The radio-loud AGN population is broadly divided into two distinct accretion modes, which are reflected in their optical emission lines and [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]]: [[high-excitation-radio-galaxies-herg|High-excitation radio galaxies (HERG)]] and [[low-excitation-radio-galaxies-lerg|Low-excitation radio galaxies (LERG)]]. This dichotomy suggests that HERGs and LERGs are not merely the result of orientation effects, but represent fundamentally different stages of massive galaxy buildup [Smolčić et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...699L..43S/abstract), [Smolčić et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...730...64S/abstract). 

LERGs represent the dominant radio AGN population in the local universe. Cosmological hydrodynamic simulations project their number densities to be roughly an order of magnitude higher than HERGs ($\sim 8.5 \text{ Mpc}^{-3}$) [Thomas et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.503.3492T/abstract). LERGs are typically hosted by massive, red-sequence elliptical galaxies and are powered by continuous, radiatively inefficient accretion from a [[hot-circumgalactic-medium|Hot circumgalactic medium]] at very low Eddington ratios [Smolčić et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...699L..43S/abstract), [Thomas et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.503.3492T/abstract). Conversely, HERGs are typically found in lower-mass, bluer "green valley" hosts featuring younger stellar populations and larger cold molecular gas reservoirs [Smolčić et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...730...64S/abstract). They accrete efficiently via standard, optically thick, geometrically thin disks at much higher Eddington ratios [Turner et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...806...59T/abstract). 

The distinct physical drivers of these populations shape the overall [[radio-luminosity-function|Radio luminosity function]] (RLF) across cosmic time. Quiescent galaxies with AGN-dominated radio flux dominate the $1.4 \text{ GHz}$ RLF at high radio powers ($P_{1.4} \gtrsim 10^{22} - 10^{23} \text{ W Hz}^{-1}$), whereas star-forming galaxies begin to dominate the space density at lower radio powers [Thomas et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.503.3492T/abstract). 

The mechanical energy transported by relativistic jets is a critical component of "maintenance-mode" [[mechanical-feedback|Mechanical feedback]]. By excavating relativistic cavities (or [[galactic-bubbles|Galactic bubbles]]) in the intra-cluster and intra-group medium, low-accretion rate radio AGNs inject substantial kinetic power into their environments. In the central galaxies of clusters, this kinetic injection is often $\sim 10^{45} \text{ erg s}^{-1}$, which is entirely sufficient to offset the radiative cooling of the hot halo gas, thereby maintaining a long-term heating-cooling balance and suppressing runaway star formation [Turner et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...806...59T/abstract).

## Mechanical and Radiative Feedback Mechanisms

Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) play a critical role in host galaxy evolution through the injection of energy and momentum into the interstellar and circumgalactic media, a process broadly termed [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]]. This feedback is generally partitioned into two primary regimes: the radiative (or quasar) mode, dominated by radiation pressure and wide-angle winds, and the kinetic (or radio) mode, characterized by highly collimated relativistic jets. Together, these processes act as a [[global-star-formation-suppression-mechanism|Global star formation suppression mechanism]], preventing the over-production of massive galaxies and establishing fundamental scaling relations such as the $M_{\text{BH}}-\sigma$ relation.

Radiative feedback operates predominantly in high-[[eddington-ratio|Eddington ratio]] systems. The intense radiation field from the accretion disk couples to the surrounding gas, driving multi-phase winds. At the smallest scales, X-ray spectra reveal ultra-fast [[galaxy-outflows|Galaxy outflows]] (UFOs) with velocities of $v \sim 0.1-0.2c$ [King et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ARA&A..53..115K/abstract). As these winds propagate, they sweep up the host galaxy's interstellar medium. Observational constraints on the [[ionised-mass-outflow-rate|Ionised Mass Outflow Rate]] highlight the efficiency of this mass-loading; for instance, the outflow in the narrow-line region of the Seyfert galaxy NGC 4151 reaches $\dot{M}_{\text{out}} \approx 3.0\ M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ at a distance of 70 pc, exceeding the nuclear mass accretion rate by a factor of $\sim 230$ [Crenshaw et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...799...83C/abstract). However, the immediate impact of radiative feedback on galaxy-wide star formation remains debated. High-resolution radiative transfer simulations indicate that while long-term gas removal is significant, instantaneous AGN heating and photoionization may only reduce the star formation rate (SFR) by a few percent, as the densest star-forming clouds ($n > 10^2 \text{ cm}^{-3}$) remain resilient to the quasar's radiation [Roos et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...800...19R/abstract).

Conversely, [[mechanical-feedback|Mechanical feedback]] dominates in massive elliptical galaxies and cluster environments, typically associated with low-excitation radio galaxies operating at lower accretion rates. In this "maintenance mode," radio jets inflate large-scale cavities in the hot X-ray-emitting atmospheres, injecting kinetic powers on the order of:
$$P_{\text{kin}} \sim 10^{45} \text{ erg s}^{-1}$$
This energy injection is sufficient to offset the radiative cooling of the intracluster medium and suppress late-time star formation. [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] demonstrate that long-range, high-velocity jet feedback is essential not only for quenching massive galaxies but also for matching the column density distribution of the low-redshift Ly$\alpha$ forest, a feat not achieved by isotropic thermal feedback models [Tillman et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945L..17T/abstract). Note that feedback efficiency in simulations is highly sensitive to subgrid prescriptions, with virtual particle feedback producing more complex structures compared to standard isotropic thermal energy injection [Zubovas et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.457..496Z/abstract). Recent multi-channel FIRE simulations further confirm that mechanical outflows and cosmic rays are required to simultaneously match the local size-mass and Faber-Jackson relations [Byrne et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973..149B/abstract), [Goyal et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251211062G/abstract).

While AGN feedback is primarily invoked as a negative feedback mechanism, there is growing observational and theoretical support for positive feedback, wherein outflows or jets compress cold gas and trigger starbursts. Observations of the Chandra Deep Field South reveal that radio-selected AGN with pronounced jets exhibit significantly higher SFRs than purely [[x-ray-detected-galaxies|X-ray-selected]] AGN at fixed luminosities, strongly suggesting jet-induced star formation [Zinn et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...774...66Z/abstract). Modern semi-analytic models increasingly incorporate both negative and positive modes, allowing star formation quenching and localized AGN-triggered starbursts to coexist within the same evolutionary framework [Contini et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240510544C/abstract).

## X-ray Physics and the Corona

High-energy X-ray emission is a fundamental probe of the [[central-engines|Central engines]] of active galaxies, providing direct insight into the innermost regions of [[accretion-processes|Accretion Processes]]. The primary X-ray continuum in a typical [[x-ray-agn|X-ray AGN]] is generated within a compact, hot, and potentially [[warm-corona|Warm corona]] situated above the accretion disk. Here, UV and optical seed photons from the disk are up-scattered to X-ray energies via inverse Compton scattering. Because this emission exhibits rapid variability on timescales ranging from minutes to hours, the coronal plasma must be highly compact, localized to within a few gravitational radii of the event horizon [Kara et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250322791K/abstract).

The resulting X-ray spectrum is generally characterized by a [[non-stellar-agn-continuum|non-thermal power-law continuum]] that terminates at a high-energy cutoff, $E_c$, which is dictated by the electron temperature, $kT_e$, of the plasma. Hard X-ray observations from facilities like NuSTAR indicate that these coronae are radiatively compact, with temperatures strongly regulated by the physical limits of electron-positron pair production [Reynolds et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016AN....337..404R/abstract). Variations in the high-energy cutoff serve as a unique diagnostic of coronal physics. For example, spectral ratio techniques applied to NuSTAR observations reveal that several local AGN exhibit a "hotter-when-brighter" trend. This correlation between an increased coronal temperature and an overall spectral softening implies that the physical geometry or structure of the corona dynamically changes with the accretion state [Zhang et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...863...71Z/abstract).

Beyond the primary continuum, X-ray spectra are shaped by reflection off the accretion disk and absorption by the [[circumnuclear-environment|Circumnuclear Environment]]. High-resolution X-ray spectroscopy frequently detects "warm absorbers"—multiphasic, outflowing ionized gas. While slower outflow components ($v \sim 100-300 \text{ km s}^{-1}$) can be successfully reproduced by radiation-driven fountain models, the presence of much faster [[warm-absorber-outflows|Warm absorber outflows]] ($v \gtrsim 1000 \text{ km s}^{-1}$) indicates that significant quantities of highly ionized gas are launched from sub-parsec scales well inside the obscuring dusty torus [Ogawa et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...925...55O/abstract).

On cosmological scales, X-ray surveys provide a robust census of accretion activity, largely immune to the obscuration that affects optical wavelengths. Measurements of the [[x-ray-luminosity-function|X-ray luminosity function]] reveal a phenomenon known as "cosmic downsizing," where the comoving number density of high-luminosity quasars peaks at $z \sim 2$, whereas the population of [[low-luminosity-active-galactic-nuclei|Low-Luminosity Active Galactic Nuclei]] peaks later, at $z < 1$. This evolution is primarily driven by a systematic decline in the average accretion rate onto [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] over cosmic time, rather than a shift toward lower-mass black holes accreting at the [[eddington-ratio|Eddington limit]] [Babić et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007A&A...474..755B/abstract). Furthermore, hard X-ray surveys mapping the $14-195 \text{ keV}$ band demonstrate that heavily obscured (Type 2) sources typically exhibit both lower bolometric luminosities and lower values of the Eddington ratio compared to their unobscured (Type 1) counterparts [Winter et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...710..503W/abstract).

## Triggering, Mergers, and Dual AGNs

The activation of a supermassive black hole requires the efficient transport of cold gas into the deep central potential well, a process that relies heavily on [[galactic-gas-inflow-and-infall|Galactic Gas Inflow and Infall]] mechanisms to shed angular momentum. The triggering of this accretion spans a spectrum of mechanisms, from internal secular instabilities to massive galaxy collisions.

The dynamics of [[galaxy-merger-physics|Galaxy Merger Physics]] dictate that tidal interactions in major mergers are highly efficient at compressing and channeling gas to the nucleus. This major merger paradigm is particularly relevant for the most luminous ($\log(L_{\text{IR}}/L_{\odot}) \sim 12.5$) and heavily obscured Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) [Chang et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJS..233...19C/abstract). The role of external interactions in fueling is further supported by the elevated AGN fraction ($\sim 20\%$) observed in galaxies exhibiting significant ($>45^{\circ}$) kinematic misalignment between their gas and stellar disks, compared to a $\sim 6\%$ fraction in aligned systems [Raimundo et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250621749R/abstract). Minor mergers are also capable of activating primary black holes to moderate [[eddington-ratio|Eddington ratios]] ($f_{\text{Edd}} > 0.01$), sustaining bolometric luminosities of $10^{43}$--$10^{44}\text{ erg s}^{-1}$ over typical durations of $0.5$ to $2.7\text{ Gyr}$ [Yang et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019RAA....19..177Y/abstract). 

Conversely, at lower luminosities, the merger fraction drops significantly. For many Seyfert galaxies and LINERs, nuclear activity is predominantly triggered by internal secular processes, such as [[gaseous-nuclear-bars|galactic bars]], large-scale disk instabilities, and nuclear rings [Chang et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJS..233...19C/abstract), [Haan et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008AJ....135..232H/abstract). 

The progression of a gas-rich galaxy merger naturally leads to the formation of [[dual-active-galactic-nuclei|Dual active galactic nuclei]] and [[offset-nuclei|Offset nuclei]] as the two supermassive black holes spiral inward. High-resolution hydrodynamical simulations indicate that strong dual AGN activity primarily occurs during the late stages of a merger at projected separations of $<1$--$10\text{ kpc}$ [Van Wassenhove et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...748L...7V/abstract). However, because gas accretion onto the two black holes is rarely simultaneous, the observable fraction of actively accreting dual AGNs in the local universe is limited to a few percent [Van Wassenhove et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...748L...7V/abstract). 

Recent high-resolution observations with JWST have revealed that the dual and offset AGN fraction scales strongly with redshift, increasing from a few percent at $z \sim 0.5$ to $\sim 23\%$ at $z \sim 4.5$ [Li et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240514980L/abstract). This fraction is especially elevated among moderately luminous ($L_{\text{bol}} \sim 10^{43}$--$10^{46}\text{ erg s}^{-1}$) obscured populations compared to unobscured quasar pairs [Li et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240514980L/abstract). In extreme cases, the correlation between millimeter continuum and hard X-ray luminosity provides a novel diagnostic to identify hidden dual AGNs in heavily obscured, merging (U)LIRGs where traditional tracers are blocked [Droguett-Callejas et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260115186D/abstract). Striking individual examples of high-redshift pairs include a confirmed accreting black hole of $\log(M_{\bullet}/M_{\odot}) \sim 7.7$ at $z = 7.15$ with a spatial offset of $620\text{ pc}$ and a velocity offset of $\sim 40\text{ km s}^{-1}$ from the centroid of its host's optical emission, indicating a late-stage merger [Übler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531..355U/abstract).

Finally, apparent offset AGNs can also be produced by post-merger gravitational recoil rather than an incoming dual system. Following the coalescence of a supermassive black hole binary, anisotropic emission of gravitational waves can impart kick velocities up to several $1000\text{ km s}^{-1}$ to the remnant black hole. This recoil can lead to long-lived kinematic oscillations within the core or even total ejection of the accreting black hole from the host galaxy [Komossa et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012AdAst2012E..14K/abstract).

## AGN Feedback in Galaxy Groups and Clusters

In the dense environments of massive galaxies, galaxy groups, and clusters, AGN feedback primarily operates in a "kinetic" or "maintenance" mode. [[central-galaxy-properties-and-evolution|Central]] [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] in these systems typically accrete hot gas via a [[radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow|Radiatively inefficient accretion flow]] at low Eddington ratios, characteristic of [[low-excitation-radio-galaxies-lerg|Low-excitation radio galaxies (LERG)]] [Turner et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...806...59T/abstract), [Thomas et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.503.3492T/abstract). Rather than emitting intense radiation, these systems channel accretion power into highly collimated, relativistic radio jets. As these jets propagate into the [[intracluster-and-intragroup-phenomena|intracluster or intragroup medium]] (ICM/IGM), they inflate lobes of relativistic cosmic-ray plasma. These expanding lobes displace the thermal X-ray emitting gas, carving out observable cavities or [[galactic-bubbles|Galactic bubbles]] [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract).

The mechanical energy deposited by these jets provides a crucial source of [[non-gravitational-heating|Non-gravitational heating]] that prevents the runaway radiative cooling of [[gaseous-halos|Gaseous halos]]. Without this energy injection, the central cooling times of many clusters would be significantly shorter than the Hubble time, leading to massive cooling flows and extreme starbursts. Instead, observations and cosmological simulations confirm a long-term thermodynamic balance. The typical kinetic power of a central cluster AGN is $\sim 10^{45} \text{ erg s}^{-1}$, which is sufficient to offset the cooling luminosity of the surrounding gas [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract). In simulated cluster environments such as TNG-Cluster, approximately 39% of central regions host such cavities ranging from a few to tens of kiloparsecs in diameter, with roughly 25% of these structures driving weak shocks (Mach number $\mathcal{M} \approx 1.5$) into the surrounding medium [Prunier et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241021366P/abstract).

While AGN feedback is prominent in massive clusters, its impact is proportionally more dramatic in galaxy groups due to their shallower gravitational potential wells [Eckert et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021Univ....7..142E/abstract). In some systems, the energy supplied by the AGN can exceed the total gravitational binding energy of the halo gas. For instance, X-ray and low-frequency radio observations of the Nest200047 galaxy group reveal multiple generations of radio lobes injecting an excess energy of $(5-6.5) \times 10^{60} \text{ erg}$ within the central $400 \text{ kpc}$. This extreme heating drives gas out of the system entirely, reducing the baryon mass fraction to just $\sim 4\%$ within $r_{500}$ and severely suppressing future star formation [Majumder et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250611312M/abstract). Similar feedback-driven cavities and multi-generational outbursts are observed in other nearby group-dominant ellipticals like NGC 4472 in the Virgo Cluster [Spasic et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240600781S/abstract) and the cool-core group NGC 5044 [Schellenberger et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976..246S/abstract).

Over time, these relativistic bubbles detach from the central AGN and rise buoyantly through the stratified atmosphere of the halo. Hydrodynamic models and low-frequency radio observations (e.g., $<200 \text{ MHz}$) indicate that pressure gradients reshape these bubbles into toroidal or "mushroom cloud" structures [Brienza et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5.1261B/abstract). The dissipation of this buoyant energy efficiently heats the [[gaseous-halos|Gaseous halos]], but the precise microphysics of the thermalization remains an active area of research. Current observational evidence suggests that the cosmic-ray plasma within the [[galactic-bubbles|Galactic bubbles]] does not thoroughly mix with the thermal gas even after hundreds of millions of years, likely due to the stabilizing presence of magnetic fields draping the bubble interfaces [Brienza et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5.1261B/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Frontiers

The study of active galactic nuclei is currently transitioning from phenomenological classification toward a detailed physical understanding of multiphase accretion and feedback, driven by next-generation observatories and multi-messenger astrophysics.

**Obscured Growth in the Early Universe**
The advent of JWST has revolutionized the study of [[high-redshift-active-galactic-nuclei|High-redshift active galactic nuclei]]. JWST is uniquely poised to uncover 'elusive' AGNs that are typically missed by standard X-ray or optical surveys due to heavy obscuration or contamination by host galaxy star formation [Satyapal et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...906...35S/abstract). Mid-infrared and spectroscopic observations with JWST/MIRI and NIRSpec are uncovering a vast population of obscured AGN that were previously hidden from X-ray and optical surveys [Lyu et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966..229L/abstract). Among these discoveries are "Little Red Dots"—compact, heavily reddened sources characterized by high Eddington ratios (and possibly super-Eddington accretion) that contribute significantly to early [[galaxy-black-hole-assembly|supermassive black hole assembly]] [Volonteri et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240812854V/abstract). Furthermore, high-resolution JWST imaging has revealed a surprisingly high fraction of offset and [[dual-active-galactic-nuclei|Dual active galactic nuclei]] in the early universe, including systems at $z > 7$ with projected separations as small as $\sim 620 \text{ pc}$ [Übler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531..355U/abstract). However, the literature remains conflicted on whether dual AGN are as common as theoretical models predict, as observational sensitivities at late-stage mergers often struggle to resolve small separations.

**Time-Domain and Transient Phenomena**
High-cadence monitoring is providing new insights into the innermost accretion flows of AGN. X-ray variability on timescales of minutes to hours allows researchers to trace gas dynamics near the event horizon [Kara et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250322791K/abstract). Additionally, the discovery of [[changing-look-active-galactic-nuclei|Changing-look active galactic nuclei]]—which undergo rapid shifts in their Eddington ratios—suggests that standard accretion disk structures may be subject to extreme, short-lived instabilities. Recent studies demonstrate that these rapidly shifting sources actually cluster in the low-accretion regime, offering critical clarification for the apparent overabundance of massive high-redshift black holes observed by JWST [Panda et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251001486P/abstract).

**Multi-messenger Astrophysics**
AGN are prime candidate sources for ultra-high-energy [[galactic-cosmic-rays|cosmic rays]] (UHECRs) and high-energy neutrinos. While blazars and radio galaxies are established $\gamma$-ray emitters, recent theoretical models suggest that ultra-fast outflows (UFOs) and wind termination shocks can efficiently accelerate protons to EeV scales, generating high-energy neutrinos up to hundreds of PeV [Peretti et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526..181P/abstract). Concurrently, turbulent AGN coronae have been proposed as the primary source of the $1-100 \text{ TeV}$ diffuse neutrino flux observed by the IceCube observatory [Fiorillo et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...989..215F/abstract). A major unresolved challenge in multi-messenger astrophysics is correlating current UHECR detections with specific AGN; due to the $\sim 10^6$ year propagation delays of charged particles and intrinsic AGN variability, linking contemporary cosmic ray events to present-day AGN activity remains highly ambiguous [Fargion et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018uhec.confa1010F/abstract).

**Open Questions**
Despite advances in observational capabilities and [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] (e.g., SIMBA, FIRE), several fundamental questions remain unresolved:
*   Does black hole mass uniquely determine AGN lifetime via a universal scaling relation, or do local environmental factors dominate?
*   To what extent does black hole spin, rather than the accretion state or host galaxy morphology, drive the radio-loudness dichotomy?
*   What is the precise fractional contribution of ultra-fast outflows versus relativistic jets to the high-energy cosmic neutrino background?
*   Are simultaneous dual AGN frequent in late-stage major mergers, or do physical mechanisms effectively suppress simultaneous accretion?
*   Can binary black hole mergers occurring within AGN disks serve as reliable 'standard sirens' to constrain the Hubble constant via gravitational waves, without requiring unique host galaxy identification [Bom et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230701330B/abstract)?
*   How can modern hybrid feedback models—which are now incorporating transitions between thin, thick, and slim disk states alongside black hole spin evolution—be optimized to more accurately simulate jet directionality and feedback efficiency in a cosmological context [Huško et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250905179H/abstract)?
