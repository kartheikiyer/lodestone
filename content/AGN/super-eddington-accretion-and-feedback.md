## Article

**Super-Eddington Accretion and Feedback** describes the physical regime in which a compact object, typically a [[supermassive-black-holes|black hole]], [[accretion-processes|accretes matter]] at a rate exceeding the classical [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]]. The Eddington limit traditionally defines the maximum continuous luminosity a spherically symmetric body can achieve before outward radiation pressure overwhelms inward gravitational pull. However, in realistic astrophysical environments such as [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]], breaking spherical symmetry and reaching a high [[dimensionless-accretion-rate|Dimensionless accretion rate]] allows the system to bypass this theoretical threshold. In this extreme regime, standard thin [[shakura-sunyaev-disks|Shakura-Sunyaev disks]] transition into geometrically thick, advection-dominated "slim disks" ([[various-black-hole-accretion-models|Various black hole accretion models]]) [Wallinder et al. (1991)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991MNRAS.253..184W/abstract). Due to the extreme optical depth of the inner flow, these disks are characterized by "photon trapping," a process where viscously generated radiation is advected radially across the event horizon before it can diffuse outward. This results in a [[radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow|Radiatively inefficient accretion flow]] where the observed luminosity saturates near the Eddington limit, even as the true [[net-accretion-rates|mass accretion rate]] climbs orders of magnitude higher [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract), [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract).

Because the highly accreting system cannot efficiently radiate away its liberated gravitational energy, the excess thermal and magnetic pressure drives intense [[mechanical-feedback|Mechanical feedback]]. While textbook models originally assumed these super-Eddington flows were fragile and strictly radiation-pressure dominated, modern simulations reveal they are often stabilized by strong toroidal magnetic fields, engaging in highly efficient [[magnetic-disk-accretion|Magnetic disk accretion]] [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract). The trapped accretion energy is funneled into powerful relativistic jets and broad, sub-relativistic winds that inject vast amounts of [[wind-mechanical-energy|kinetic energy]] into the host galaxy's interstellar medium [Ciotti et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...717..708C/abstract), [King et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014SSRv..183..427K/abstract). This [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]] plays a dual, often contradictory role in galaxy co-evolution: it can drive massive outflows that clear the [[nuclear-regions-of-galaxies|nuclear gas supply]] and quench [[clumpy-star-formation|star formation]]—maintaining "red and dead" massive ellipticals [Springel et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.361..776S/abstract), [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract)—or, paradoxically, it can shock-compress dense interstellar clouds to trigger bursty [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]] [Silk et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.364.1337S/abstract). 

Cosmologically, super-Eddington accretion is the primary theoretical candidate for explaining [[high-redshift-black-hole-growth|High-redshift black hole growth]]. The observation of billion-solar-mass [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] existing within the first billion years of the Universe ($z \sim 6-7$) requires either massive initial seeds or sustained periods of hyper-accretion that violate standard Eddington-limited $e$-folding timescales. Recent James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) discoveries of anomalously bright, high-redshift "Little Red Dots" have intensified focus on this regime. The seemingly over-massive black holes in these compact objects may either reflect genuine early-universe super-Eddington growth or be observational artifacts caused by applying standard virial scaling relations to the saturated, photon-trapped outflows of slim disks [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract). In the local universe, similar physics can be studied in transient episodic events like [[fallback-accretion|tidal disruption events]] (TDEs) and persistently accreting Ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs).

This article provides a comprehensive overview of the physical principles governing super-Eddington accretion and its multi-scale impact on the cosmos. It traces the theoretical evolution from classical [[accretion-disk-theory|Accretion Disk Theory]] to state-of-the-art radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) models of the inner disk, and outlines the primary radiative, kinetic, and mechanical feedback channels. Finally, it reviews current observational signatures and details the most pressing open questions in the field, including the long-term sustainability of super-Eddington episodes, the precise mechanical efficiency of wind-ISM coupling, and whether these [[violent-disk-instability|violent growth phases]] ultimately quench or rejuvenate their host galaxies.

## Fundamental Principles and the Eddington Limit

The classical [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]] represents the theoretical maximum luminosity a gravitationally bound celestial body can achieve while maintaining hydrostatic equilibrium. It is derived by balancing the inward gravitational force on a proton with the outward radiation pressure exerted on an accompanying electron via Thomson scattering, assuming a fully ionized hydrogen plasma and strictly spherical accretion. The Eddington luminosity is given by:

$$ L_{\rm Edd} = \frac{4\pi G M m_p c}{\sigma_T} $$

where $G$ is the gravitational constant, $M$ is the mass of the central object, $m_p$ is the proton mass, $c$ is the speed of light, and $\sigma_T$ is the Thomson scattering cross-section. 

To relate this luminosity threshold to a [[net-accretion-rates|mass accretion rate]], one assumes a characteristic radiative efficiency $\epsilon_r \sim 0.1$, defined by the gravitational potential depth of the innermost stable circular orbit. The canonical Eddington accretion rate is thus defined as:

$$ \dot{M}_{\rm Edd} = \frac{L_{\rm Edd}}{\epsilon_r c^2} $$

The state of an accreting system is frequently parameterized by two related quantities: the [[eddington-ratio|Eddington ratio]], $\lambda_{\rm Edd} = L_{\rm bol} / L_{\rm Edd}$, and the [[dimensionless-accretion-rate|Dimensionless accretion rate]], $\dot{m} = \dot{M} / \dot{M}_{\rm Edd}$. In standard [[shakura-sunyaev-disks|Shakura-Sunyaev disks]], accretion is strictly sub-Eddington ($\dot{m} \lesssim 1$ and $\lambda_{\rm Edd} \lesssim 1$). 

Exceeding the Eddington limit requires violating the assumptions of the classical spherical model. In realistic astrophysical environments, such as [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]], accretion flows are highly non-spherical. Anisotropy allows matter to accrete rapidly along the equatorial plane of a thick disk, while radiation escapes preferentially along the path of least optical depth at the polar axes. Furthermore, in the outer regions, disk self-gravity becomes important and can lead to fragmentation, forming bound [[clumpy-disks|clumps]] or stars [Lodato et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007NCimR..30..293L/abstract). Additionally, when the inner disk is misaligned with the black hole's spin, Lense-Thirring precession can cause "disk tearing," breaking the flow into discrete rings that drive rapid variability [Raj et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...909...82R/abstract).

When the mass accretion rate exceeds $\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}$, the local viscous heating timescale becomes shorter than the radiative diffusion timescale. In this regime, photons are unable to diffuse out of the disk before being advected inward with the bulk flow and swallowed by the event horizon. This "photon trapping" effect drastically lowers the radiative efficiency of the flow ($\epsilon_r \ll 0.1$), permitting the black hole to accrete mass at highly super-Eddington rates ($\dot{m} \gg 1$) while the observed luminosity saturates at or near $L_{\rm Edd}$ [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract), [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract). Classically, these advection-dominated, radiation-pressure supported structures are modeled as "slim disks" ([[various-black-hole-accretion-models|Various black hole accretion models]]). 

Recent theoretical advancements have superseded purely radiation-dominated models of super-Eddington flows. High-resolution radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) simulations demonstrate that super-Eddington disks can become strongly magnetically dominated, characterized by a plasma $\beta \ll 1$ in the midplane. The intense toroidal magnetic fields stabilize the disk against fragmentation and permit immense, sustained hyper-accretion rates reaching tens to hundreds of solar masses per year [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract).

Because the system cannot radiate the locally liberated energy efficiently, the excess thermal and magnetic pressure drives powerful mechanical feedback. In cases where the accreting gas lacks sufficient angular momentum to form a standard disk, such as in tidal disruption events, the flow inflates into a weakly bound, quasi-spherical envelope, and the excess energy is funneled outward through the polar regions, launching highly collimated jets and broad, sub-relativistic winds [Coughlin et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...781...82C/abstract). Thus, the physics of super-Eddington accretion intrinsically links hyper-accretion to profound mechanical feedback, self-regulating the black hole's growth environment.

## Evolution of Accretion Theory: From Shakura-Sunyaev to Slim Disks

The classical paradigm of [[accretion-disk-theory|Accretion Disk Theory]] was established by the standard [[shakura-sunyaev-disks|Shakura-Sunyaev disks]] model. In this regime, the accretion flow is geometrically thin, optically thick, and viscous heating is entirely balanced by local radiative cooling. This yields a high radiative efficiency of $\epsilon_r \approx 0.1$. However, this analytical framework strictly applies only to sub-Eddington accretion. As the mass [[net-accretion-rates|accretion rate]] $\dot{M}$ approaches and exceeds the [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]], the fundamental assumptions of the thin-disk model break down due to extreme radiation pressure and increasing optical depth.

To address the super-Eddington regime, theorists developed the "slim disk" model [Wallinder et al. (1991)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991MNRAS.253..184W/abstract). In a slim disk, the vertical diffusion timescale of photons through the dense flow exceeds the radial accretion timescale. This results in "photon trapping," wherein the radiation is advected radially inward and swallowed by the black hole before it can escape to infinity. Consequently, the disk becomes a [[radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow|Radiatively inefficient accretion flow]] despite its high optical depth. Because the emerging luminosity saturates near $L_{\rm Edd}$ even as the accretion rate climbs, the observed luminosity of super-Eddington sources can severely underestimate the true black hole growth rate [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract), [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract). These extreme accretion conditions are not limited to steady gas inflows; theoretical models also predict that accretion rates will naturally exceed the Eddington limit during the final stages of [[galaxy-merger-physics|supermassive black hole binary coalescence]] [Armitage et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...567L...9A/abstract). Furthermore, observational evidence points towards [[chaotic-cold-accretion|cold, clumpy molecular accretion flows]], which depart from traditional [[hot-mode-accretion|'hot mode']] spherical inflows by delivering gas to the black hole via [[stochastic-accretion|stochastic]], thermal instabilities [Tremblay et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016Natur.534..218T/abstract).

The underlying physics of photon trapping is a form of advection, generalizing the concept of advection-dominated accretion flows (ADAFs). While ADAFs are most famously applied to the optically thin, [[low-accretion-rate-objects|very low accretion rate regime]] (such as the hot accretion flow around Sagittarius A*) [Narayan et al. (1994)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1994astro.ph.11060N/abstract), [Yuan et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ARA&A..52..529Y/abstract), the super-Eddington slim disk represents the optically thick branch of advection-dominated solutions. In both branches, viscous heat is retained in the gas rather than radiated away, [[puffing-astrophysics|puffing up]] the disk into a geometrically thick structure with $H/R \sim 1$.

Recently, the historical picture of radiation-pressure dominated slim disks has undergone a paradigm shift driven by high-resolution radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) simulations. Classical radiation-dominated models are theoretically susceptible to the [[radiation-pressure-instability|Radiation-pressure instability]], which threatens to fragment the disk. However, modern [[gas-rich-galaxy-interactions|cosmological zoom-in simulations]] reveal that super-Eddington flows naturally evolve into hyper-magnetized states rather than pure radiation-supported states. These disks are dominated by toroidal magnetic fields, reaching an extreme plasma parameter of $\beta \sim 10^{-4}$ even in the disk midplane [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract). This form of [[magnetic-disk-accretion|Magnetic disk accretion]] stabilizes the flow against fragmentation and enables sustained accretion rates as high as $10-100 \, M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ into the inner disk [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..18H/abstract).

Furthermore, these hyper-magnetized, super-Eddington states are inherently linked to violent [[episodic-accretion|feedback mechanisms]]. When vertical magnetic flux accumulates near the event horizon, it triggers magnetic state transitions that launch relativistic jets and powerful sub-relativistic winds. For rapidly spinning black holes, the extraction of rotational energy via these jets can be so efficient that it spins down the black hole within 5-10 Myr [Ricarte et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...954L..22R/abstract), [Kaaz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..248K/abstract). The recognition of magnetic dominance in the super-Eddington regime thus bridges the gap between idealized analytical slim disk models and the complex, highly variable reality of black hole fueling in the early universe.

## Physics of the Super-Eddington Regime

When the [[dimensionless-accretion-rate|Dimensionless accretion rate]] exceeds unity (${\dot{M} \gtrsim \dot{M}_{\rm Edd}}$), the accretion flow departs significantly from standard, geometrically thin [[shakura-sunyaev-disks|Shakura-Sunyaev disks]]. The flow inflates into a geometrically thick ($H/R \sim 1$) "slim disk" ([[various-black-hole-accretion-models|Various black hole accretion models]]) [Wallinder et al. (1991)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991MNRAS.253..184W/abstract). The defining thermodynamic feature of this regime is radiation trapping (or photon trapping). Because of the extreme optical depth in the inner disk, the characteristic timescale for photons to diffuse outward becomes longer than the inward advection timescale of the gas. Consequently, the flow advects viscously generated heat directly across the event horizon rather than radiating it away [Wallinder et al. (1991)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991MNRAS.253..184W/abstract), [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract). This causes the observed radiative output to saturate near the [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]], even as the physical [[net-accretion-rates|mass accretion rate]] continues to grow. As a result, applying standard bolometric corrections to highly accreting [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] can lead to severe underestimations of their true mass [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract), [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract).

Historically, textbook models posited that radiation-pressure-dominated disks would rapidly succumb to the [[radiation-pressure-instability|Radiation-pressure instability]] and fragment. However, high-resolution radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) simulations have revised this picture, revealing that super-Eddington quasar accretion disks can be stabilized by intense [[magnetic-disk-accretion|magnetic fields]]. In these states, the disk pressure is overwhelmingly dominated by toroidal magnetic fields, yielding a plasma parameter $\beta \ll 1$ even at the midplane [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract). This hyper-magnetized structure allows the disk to resist fragmentation and sustain continuous hyper-accretion rates of $10-100\, M_\odot\, \text{yr}^{-1}$ down to sub-parsec scales [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..18H/abstract), [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract). In dilute, radiatively inefficient phases, the magnetoviscous-thermal instability also acts as a candidate mechanism for transporting angular momentum [Islam et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...746....8I/abstract). Moreover, the extreme tidal and magnetic environments of super-Eddington disks can trigger star formation within the accretion flow itself. Simulations indicate that stars forming in these disks exhibit a uniquely top-heavy [[stellar-mass-function|initial mass function]] (IMF), which is highly sensitive to protostellar disk dynamics that decouple from the surrounding gas on very short timescales [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..71H/abstract).

The energy that cannot be radiated away efficiently drives powerful mechanical outflows. The morphology and reach of these outflows are governed by the Bernoulli parameter of the gas. In extreme transient scenarios, such as the super-Eddington phase of [[fallback-accretion|tidal disruption events]], the trapped accretion energy inflates the debris into a weakly bound, quasi-spherical envelope characterized by a near-zero Bernoulli parameter, with excess energy escaping primarily as polar jets [Coughlin et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...781...82C/abstract). For sustained accretion flows, thermal winds originating from the hot inner radii can propagate to vast galactic distances, as the rotational energy and enthalpy of the gas compensate for the climb out of the gravitational potential well [Cui et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...890...80C/abstract). 

If the highly magnetized flow reaches a magnetically arrested disk (MAD) state, the black hole can launch highly efficient, relativistic jets. These jets extract tremendous amounts of angular momentum from the accreting black hole, imposing a strong spin-down torque that rapidly drives the dimensionless spin parameter toward zero [Ricarte et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...954L..22R/abstract), [Kaaz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..248K/abstract).

Despite consensus on the internal physics of the disk, the long-term sustainability of the super-Eddington regime remains a subject of active debate. Some simulations suggest that the powerful jets and winds launched during this phase self-regulate the flow, quenching rapid growth within $\sim 1$ Myr and maintaining the time-averaged accretion rate at merely 2-3 times the Eddington limit [Massonneau et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...670A.180M/abstract). Conversely, other studies argue that in the low-angular momentum, gas-rich environments of the early Universe, [[episodic-accretion|super-Eddington accretion]] can persist or recur episodically long enough to naturally produce the over-massive black holes recently observed at [[high-redshift-black-hole-growth|high redshift]] [Volonteri et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804..148V/abstract), [Zana et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821114Z/abstract).

## Feedback Channels: Radiative, Kinetic, and Mechanical

Accreting [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] inject vast amounts of energy and momentum into their host galaxies via two primary feedback channels: the radiative ("quasar") mode and the kinetic or mechanical ("radio") mode [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract), [Weinberger et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3291W/abstract). The dominance of these channels is strongly governed by the accretion rate, particularly as the system crosses the [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]].

**Radiative Feedback**
At near-Eddington accretion rates, a fraction of the accreted rest-mass energy couples to the surrounding gas via photoionization, Compton heating, and radiation pressure, driving wide-angle winds [Ciotti et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...717..708C/abstract). However, as the mass supply enters the super-Eddington regime, the accretion flow becomes optically thick and advection-dominated. In these "slim disks", photon trapping severely limits the escape of radiation, causing the observed luminosity to saturate and reducing the radiative efficiency, $\epsilon_r$ [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract), [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract). Consequently, while the absolute radiative output remains high, the fractional energy available for classical radiative feedback drops, and momentum-driven mechanical outflows take over the primary feedback budget [King et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014SSRv..183..427K/abstract).

**Kinetic and Mechanical Feedback**
Kinetic feedback is traditionally associated with low-luminosity, [[radiatively-inefficient-accretion-flow|Radiatively inefficient accretion flow]] states where energy is channeled into highly collimated relativistic jets [Yuan et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ARA&A..52..529Y/abstract). However, recent general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) simulations demonstrate that [[mechanical-feedback|Mechanical feedback]] is also remarkably potent during super-Eddington accretion. Immensely powerful jets and sub-relativistic outflows can be launched from diverse highly magnetized states, even at accretion rates vastly exceeding the Eddington limit. This includes Magnetically Arrested Disks (MADs), which are supported by strong poloidal magnetic flux [Ricarte et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...954L..22R/abstract), as well as qualitatively distinct, hyper-magnetized disks that are overwhelmingly dominated by intense toroidal fields (reaching $\beta \ll 1$) [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract). A global inventory of these feedback processes reveals that while [[massive-star-physics|massive stars]] dominate the global momentum injection within galaxies, the kinetic energy explicitly driven by supermassive black hole jets is an order of magnitude higher than that from their associated winds [Heckman et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Galax..11...21H/abstract).

In episodic super-Eddington events, such as tidal disruption events (TDEs), the infalling gas often lacks the angular momentum to form a standard thin disk. Instead, the trapped accretion energy inflates the gas into a weakly bound, quasi-spherical envelope. Excess energy is then expelled through polar jets and radially distributed winds, driving brief but intense mechanical feedback [Coughlin et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...781...82C/abstract).

The exact efficiency of mechanical feedback, parametrized as $\epsilon_w = \dot{E}_w / (\dot{M}_{\rm acc} c^2)$, has historically been a point of tension in the literature. While early cosmological simulations often assumed high mechanical efficiencies ($\epsilon_w \sim 10^{-2}$), reproducing both the X-ray luminosity of hot galactic halos and central black hole masses strictly requires a much lower efficiency, typically $\epsilon_w \sim 10^{-4} - 10^{-3}$ [Ciotti et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...717..708C/abstract), [Ostriker et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...722..642O/abstract). The injected kinetic energy from these outflows thermalizes via shocks in the surrounding medium, providing distributed heating that suppresses large-scale cooling flows [Weinberger et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3291W/abstract). More broadly across galaxy populations, the wind mass-loading efficiency scales inversely with the galaxy's circular velocity ($V_c$). In low-density [[dwarf-galaxy-physical-processes|dwarf galaxies]], outflows are typically dominated by shock-heated supernova feedback rather than AGN-driven mechanical modes.

**Impact on Black Hole Spin**
In the super-Eddington regime, mechanical feedback not only disrupts the host galaxy but also profoundly alters the black hole itself. The launching of powerful electromagnetic jets extracts significant angular momentum from the black hole. For rapidly spinning black holes accreting at super-Eddington rates, this severe angular momentum loss can spin the black hole down to near-zero spin on timescales as short as $5-10$ Myr [Ricarte et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...954L..22R/abstract), [Kaaz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..248K/abstract). This rapid spin-down limits the long-term efficiency of jet production, acting as an internal self-regulation mechanism for kinetic feedback during rapid growth phases.

## High-Redshift Growth and the Early Universe

The existence of billion-solar-mass [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] ($\sim 10^9 M_\odot$) at redshifts $z > 6$ presents a fundamental challenge to models of [[high-redshift-black-hole-growth|High-redshift black hole growth]]. If a black hole accretes at the classical [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]] with a standard radiative efficiency ($\epsilon_r \sim 0.1$), the $e$-folding timescale for mass growth is insufficient to grow these objects from stellar-mass remnants within the first billion years of the Universe. Consequently, rapid early assembly must either proceed from heavy initial seeds—such as [[direct-collapse-black-holes|Direct collapse black holes]]—or invoke sustained periods of super-Eddington accretion, where the luminosity exceeds the Eddington luminosity ($L \gg L_{\rm Edd}$) while the radiative efficiency drops significantly. Global census constraints on the AGN population imply a high average radiative efficiency ($\eta \sim 0.3 - 0.34$), which suggests that growing supermassive black holes are, on average, spinning rapidly and possess significant angular momentum [Ananna et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...903...85A/abstract). However, their overall contribution to cosmic reionization remains subdominant ($\sim 23\%$), roughly four times lower than the contribution from galaxies [Ananna et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...903...85A/abstract). Furthermore, the radiative efficiency of black hole accretion experiences strong cosmological evolution. Models of [[episodic-accretion|episodic]], random accretion episodes suggest that the average efficiency decreases significantly over cosmic time, dropping from $\eta \approx 0.3$ at $z \approx 2$ down to $\eta \approx 0.03$ in the local universe at $z \approx 0$ [Wang et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...697L.141W/abstract). Extreme super-Eddington mass supplies (reaching $50-100$ times the Eddington rate) can tightly collimate the emergent emission; this profound geometrical obscuration explains why many high-redshift black holes appear Compton-thick and lack detectable X-ray signatures [King et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.536L...1K/abstract). Direct observations of the early universe support the presence of such violent mechanical feedback; for instance, the $z \sim 6.6$ LoBAL quasar J0923+0402 exhibits powerful, multiphase (ionized and atomic) outflows where the [[wind-mechanical-energy|kinetic power]] reaches a significant fraction of the quasar's total luminosity, powering a bright [C II] halo [Bischetti et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...970....9B/abstract).

In the super-Eddington regime, the accretion flow is frequently modeled as a geometrically thick "slim disk" ([[various-black-hole-accretion-models|Various black hole accretion models]]) characterized by strong photon trapping and advection. In these environments, highly magnetized disks can theoretically sustain mass accretion rates of $10 - 100 M_\odot \, \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract). However, the duration of these super-Eddington episodes remains a subject of active debate in the literature. High-resolution hydrodynamical simulations suggest that super-Eddington feedback, particularly via relativistic jets and winds, can efficiently evacuate gas from the galactic nucleus and quench accretion within $\sim 1$ Myr [Sassano et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.1837S/abstract), [Massonneau et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...670A.180M/abstract). Conversely, other models argue that gas-rich, low-angular momentum environments in protogalaxies can foster episodic accretion bursts capable of producing over-massive black holes despite strong local feedback [Zana et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821114Z/abstract), [Piana et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.1732P/abstract). Additionally, hyperaccretion resulting from [[fallback-accretion|tidal disruption events]] has been proposed as an alternative, highly episodic mechanism to dominate the growth of light seeds ($< 10^6 M_\odot$) in the early Universe [Pfister et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.500.3944P/abstract).

Recent observations by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) have brought renewed attention to this theoretical tension with the discovery of "Little Red Dots" (LRDs). These compact, high-redshift sources host active galactic nuclei with inferred black hole masses that appear anomalously high, often rivaling a significant fraction of their host galaxy's stellar mass. The physical nature of LRDs is currently unresolved. Some studies interpret them as heavily dust-obscured, red-quenched [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|AGN]] [Quadri et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250505556Q/abstract). Alternatively, LRDs may represent [[super-eddington-radio-loud-dust-obscured-galaxies|Black Hole Stars]]—dense, gas-enshrouded supermassive black holes lacking a prominent host galaxy light contribution [Naidu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250316596N/abstract). 

Crucially, if the black holes in LRDs are undergoing super-Eddington accretion, their intrinsic luminosity will saturate due to photon trapping. Applying local virial scaling relations to such radiation-trapped, super-Eddington outflows may result in overestimating the true black hole masses by up to an order of magnitude [Tsai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...868...15T/abstract), [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract), [Naidu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250316596N/abstract). Thus, the apparent over-massive nature of LRDs might be an observational artifact of extreme accretion physics rather than a true violation of early black hole-galaxy co-evolution.

## Impact on the Host Galaxy: Quenching and ISM Regulation

Super-Eddington (SE) accretion episodes, while often short-lived, exert a profound influence on their host galaxies by injecting massive amounts of radiative and mechanical energy into the interstellar medium. In massive dark matter halos ($M_{\rm halo} \gtrsim 10^{12} M_\odot$), stellar feedback alone is insufficient to halt the secular condensation of gas [Hopkins et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..581H/abstract). Consequently, feedback from [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] is widely invoked as the primary mechanism to drive quenching, preventing "cooling catastrophes" and maintaining the quiescence of massive elliptical galaxies [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract), [Su et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.487.4393S/abstract). In these massive environments, observational evidence shows that the lack of gas angular momentum in giant elliptical galaxies leads to boosted black hole accretion rate to star formation rate (BHAR/SFR) ratios compared to isolated field galaxies, enabling more efficient central feeding [McDonald et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...908...85M/abstract).

During SE phases, accretion disks drive highly collimated jets and wide-angle winds. These galaxy-wide winds routinely achieve bulk velocities of $500 - 1000 \text{ km s}^{-1}$, which are sufficient to permanently expel up to 80% of the outflowing gas from the galactic potential. This stands in stark contrast to the closed-box recycling and [[galactic-fountains|Galactic fountains]] typical of stellar feedback [Brennan et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...860...14B/abstract). As these AGN-driven outflows expand, they evacuate [[nuclear-regions-of-galaxies|nuclear gas]] and inflate hot, over-pressured [[superbubbles|Superbubbles]] that sweep up the interstellar medium into dense shells. High-resolution hydrodynamic simulations indicate that this rapid evacuation can starve the central engine, self-quenching the SE accretion phase within extremely short timescales of $\sim 1 \text{ Myr}$ [Sassano et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.1837S/abstract), [Massonneau et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...670A.180M/abstract).

Crucially, purely [[mechanical-feedback|Mechanical feedback]] models often fail to simultaneously satisfy constraints from central black hole mass ratios and the observed X-ray luminosity of hot galactic gas [Shin et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...711..268S/abstract). Therefore, to match these observational constraints, theoretical models require a combination of radiative and mechanical feedback. The kinetic efficiency of these winds—defined as $\epsilon_w \equiv \dot{E}_w / (\dot{M}_{\rm acc} c^2)$—must be tightly constrained, with models favoring relatively low values of $10^{-4} \lesssim \epsilon_w \lesssim 10^{-3}$ to avoid entirely unbinding the host galaxy's gas supply while still offsetting circumgalactic cooling via shock thermalization [Ciotti et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...717..708C/abstract), [Ostriker et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...722..642O/abstract). Furthermore, in highly magnetized SE flows, the resulting relativistic jets are capable of extracting significant angular momentum, which can spin down the black hole within $5 - 10 \text{ Myr}$ [Kaaz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..248K/abstract).

While the canonical view of SE feedback emphasizes the suppression of star formation, the literature remains explicitly conflicted regarding its net effect across different environments. In gas-rich conditions, AGN outflows can instead over-pressurize dense interstellar clouds, triggering rapid gravitational collapse rather than disruption [Silk et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.364.1337S/abstract). This mechanism of [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]] introduces a complex duality to galaxy co-evolution, demonstrating that SE episodes can simultaneously drive fierce outflows that quench the inner galaxy while triggering new star formation at larger radii. The precise conditions that separate these destructive and constructive modes are discussed in the following section.

## Positive Feedback and Triggered Star Formation

While models of [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]] traditionally emphasize the role of [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] in quenching star formation, the paradoxical mechanism of [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]] can yield the exact opposite effect. In this regime, AGN-driven shocks and powerful jets interact constructively with the surrounding interstellar or circumgalactic medium, overpressurizing dense gas clouds and triggering intense starbursts. By compressing cold gas, these outflows force clouds to exceed the Jeans instability threshold, leading to rapid fragmentation and gravitational collapse [Silk et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.364.1337S/abstract), [Bieri et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.455.4166B/abstract). Recent semi-analytic models of galaxy formation, such as the FEGA framework, demonstrate that positive AGN feedback can effectively coexist with negative feedback. The inclusion of this constructive mode improves the reproduction of key galaxy scaling relations, further underscoring its relevance in broader galaxy evolution [Contini et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240510544C/abstract).

In the theoretical "J-mode" (jet-induced) star formation scenario, mechanical energy injected by an accreting supermassive black hole is isotropized by an inhomogeneous, [[clumpy-disks|clumpy medium]]. This drives super-Eddington outflows that highly compress gas, potentially generating ultruminous starbursts that rapidly build up massive stellar populations in early protospheroids [Silk et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005MNRAS.364.1337S/abstract). Hydrodynamic simulations support this framework, demonstrating that moderate levels of ambient overpressurization can turn stable gas discs unstable, boosting a galaxy's global star formation rate by up to an order of magnitude [Bieri et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.455.4166B/abstract). 

The transition between positive and negative feedback is highly sensitive to local gas conditions and outflow kinematics. Simulations of AGN winds striking Bonnor-Ebert spheres reveal a delicate ram pressure threshold. While elevated ram pressure accelerates the star formation timescale and increases the velocity of newborn stars, pressures exceeding $\sim 2 \times 10^{-8}$ dyn cm$^{-2}$ inject excessive internal kinetic energy into the clumps, ultimately preventing their collapse [Dugan et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...839..103D/abstract). Because jet and wind parameters vary spatially, positive and negative feedback can coexist within a single host galaxy. For instance, wide-angle winds may excavate central cavities devoid of dense gas—quenching nuclear star formation—while simultaneously driving an expanding, overpressurized ring of triggered star formation at radii $> 3$ kpc [Dugan et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...844...37D/abstract).

Positive feedback has been documented across a vast range of spatial scales in recent simulations. At the scale of cosmic filaments during the early universe ($z \sim 2$), overpressurized expanding cocoons driven by quasar jets can fragment hypermassive gas reservoirs, directly triggering the birth of clumpy, Milky Way-mass protogalaxies with extreme star formation rates [Mićić et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250910670M/abstract). Conversely, in the regime of [[dwarf-galaxy-physical-processes|Dwarf Galaxy Physical Processes]], AGN-driven outflows can compress gas efficiently enough to elevate the global star formation rate by roughly 25% [Su et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251020897S/abstract). In these gas-rich starburst dwarfs, the cooling timescale in the shock-compressed regions is short enough to radiate away thermal energy rapidly, preserving the high densities required for stellar ignition [Su et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251020897S/abstract). 

Despite these advances, the literature remains actively divided on whether the net global impact of AGN feedback is predominantly positive or negative. The dominant feedback mode is highly contingent on the geometry of the outflow and the density of the host environment. Furthermore, episodic outbursts can create complex, nonlinear cycles; mechanical feedback can strip gas of its angular momentum, causing it to fall back on near-parabolic orbits to fuel subsequent accretion, creating a mechanism where feedback actively drives future feeding [Dehnen et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...777L..28D/abstract).

## Numerical Modeling and Cosmological Simulations

Modeling super-Eddington accretion and its associated feedback requires bridging an immense range of spatial and temporal scales, from the megaparsec environments of galaxy evolution models down to the sub-parsec event horizon of [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]]. Historically, large-scale simulations have relied on analytical sub-grid models to estimate mass inflow, most notably the spherical [[accretion-processes|Bondi-Hoyle-Lyttleton accretion]] formula. However, it is now widely recognized that classical Bondi prescriptions are often physically inaccurate in regimes with efficient gas cooling and significant angular momentum, sometimes underestimating or overestimating accretion by orders of magnitude [Hobbs et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.421.3443H/abstract).

To overcome these limitations, modern zoom-in simulations incorporate multi-phase interstellar medium (ISM) thermodynamics and discrete feedback channels. Frameworks utilizing FIRE physics (Feedback In Realistic Environments) explicitly resolve the competition between stellar feedback and black hole feeding. These models demonstrate that bursty stellar feedback continuously evacuates gas from galactic nuclei, severely limiting early black hole growth until the host galaxy reaches a sufficient mass to retain a stable gas reservoir [Anglés-Alcázar et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.472L.109A/abstract). In massive halos, more recent iterations of these models include multi-channel [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]]—combining radiative heating, mechanical winds, and cosmic rays. Notably, cosmological zoom-in simulations reveal the critical role of cosmic rays accelerated by supermassive black hole jets in maintaining galaxy quenching and suppressing star formation across a wide range of halo masses [Wellons et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.520.5394W/abstract), allowing the models to self-consistently regulate star formation and match observed scaling relations [Byrne et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973..149B/abstract).

Recent breakthroughs have successfully bridged the gap between galactic inflows and the accretion disk itself using radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) frameworks. By coupling FIRE physics with small-scale (proto)stellar dynamics, simulations can now follow gas from cosmological initial conditions down to $< 300$ Schwarzschild radii [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..18H/abstract). These RMHD models confirm the analytical predictions and small-scale findings that super-Eddington accretion can be sustained at extreme rates of $10-100 \, M_\odot \, \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ without catastrophic fragmentation, due to the stabilizing influence of toroidal [[magnetic-disk-accretion|Magnetic disk accretion]] [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract).

At the horizon scale, General Relativistic Magnetohydrodynamic (GRMHD) codes are utilized to capture the strong gravity and spin evolution of the black hole. Novel multi-zone 3D GRMHD techniques have been developed to bridge vast spatial scales, successfully resolving density scalings of $\rho \propto r^{-1}$ from the outer Bondi radius directly down to the event horizon [Cho et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240513887C/abstract). When large-scale RMHD data is remapped into GRMHD simulations, vertical magnetic flux is observed to build up near the event horizon, triggering a magnetic state transition [Kaaz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..248K/abstract). These magnetically arrested, super-Eddington flows launch powerful relativistic jets that extract angular momentum so efficiently that they can spin down the black hole to near-zero spin within $5-10$ Myr [Ricarte et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...954L..22R/abstract), [Kaaz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..248K/abstract). To efficiently incorporate these high-resolution, small-scale dynamics into macroscopic models, researchers have recently introduced neural operator frameworks that learn these local physical processes and embed them as data-driven subgrid closures in larger cosmological simulations [Bhojwani et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251201576B/abstract).

Despite these computational advances, the literature remains conflicted regarding the sustainability and duty cycle of super-Eddington accretion. Some high-resolution hydrodynamic models suggest that the powerful jets and winds produced during the super-Eddington regime rapidly evacuate the local gas supply, quenching the accretion episode within $\sim 1$ Myr [Massonneau et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...670A.180M/abstract), [Sassano et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.1837S/abstract). Conversely, other targeted simulations find that in the gas-rich, low-angular-momentum environments of high-redshift protogalaxies, these episodic super-Eddington bursts can be sustained or occur frequently enough to naturally produce the "over-massive" black holes observed by JWST at $z > 6$ [Zana et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821114Z/abstract). Resolving this tension remains a primary objective for the next generation of multi-scale accretion models.

## Observational Signatures: ULXs and Tidal Disruption Events

Ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs) provide the most accessible nearby laboratories for studying sustained super-Eddington accretion [Amato et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251220265A/abstract). Defined empirically by their extreme X-ray luminosities ($L_X \sim 10^{40} \text{ erg s}^{-1}$), ULXs systematically exceed the classical [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]] for stellar-mass compact objects [Pakull et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010Natur.466..209P/abstract). While the brightest ULXs remain strong candidates for intermediate-mass black holes (e.g., HLX-1; [Grzędzielski et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...603A.110G/abstract)), the discovery of coherent X-ray pulsations in sources like M82 X-2 (NuSTAR J095551+6940.8) proves that a subset of these objects are highly magnetized neutron stars undergoing stable super-Eddington accretion [Lyutikov et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014arXiv1410.8745L/abstract), [Amato et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251220265A/abstract). The intense radiation pressure in these systems is expected to drive powerful outflows; this is observationally supported by massive shock-ionized nebulae surrounding sources like NGC 7793 S26, where microquasar-like jets dissipate mechanical power up to $10^{40} \text{ erg s}^{-1}$, vastly exceeding the radiative output of the core [Pakull et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010Natur.466..209P/abstract). Extending these multi-wavelength connections, recent research has established a millimetre fundamental plane of black hole accretion that effectively links stellar-mass black holes to supermassive black holes through their [[nuclear-regions-of-galaxies|nuclear 1mm]] and X-ray luminosities, providing a unified observational framework across mass scales [Elford et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260104007E/abstract).

On transient timescales, stellar tidal disruption events (TDEs) offer direct probes of episodic super-Eddington hyperaccretion. Following the disruption of a star, approximately half of the stellar debris undergoes [[fallback-accretion|Fallback accretion]] onto the central black hole at a rate that can initially surpass the Eddington limit by several orders of magnitude [Coughlin et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...781...82C/abstract). Rather than settling into a standard thin disk, this low-angular-momentum material is hypothesized to inflate into a quasi-spherical, weakly bound "zero-Bernoulli" envelope, channeling excess accretion energy into powerful relativistic jets, as observed in the jetted TDE Swift J1644+57 [Coughlin et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...781...82C/abstract). In the early universe, cosmological simulations suggest TDEs may have dominated the initial growth of light [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] (up to masses of $\sim 5 \times 10^5 M_\odot$) with disruption rates of $\sim 10^{-5} \text{ yr}^{-1}$ before standard gas accretion became the primary growth channel [Pfister et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.500.3944P/abstract).

A hallmark of the super-Eddington regime across both ULXs and TDEs is extreme X-ray variability and the launching of fast ionized winds. For instance, the TDE AT2022lri—originating from a low-mass black hole ($10^4 - 10^6 M_\odot$)—exhibited a peak X-ray luminosity of $1.5 \times 10^{44} \text{ erg s}^{-1}$, consistent with a face-on super-Eddington flow [Yao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...34Y/abstract). This event featured hours-to-days timescale X-ray dips accompanied by fluctuations in the inner disk temperature (dropping from $\sim 146$ eV to $\sim 86$ eV) and the spectroscopic detection of sub-relativistic ($0.1 - 0.3 c$) ionized outflows [Yao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...34Y/abstract). Similar signatures of super-Eddington winds have been caught during X-ray flares in [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|active galactic nuclei]] (e.g., I Zwicky 1), where variable blueshifted absorption features track rapidly cooling coronae and momentum-driven outflows [Ding et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...931...77D/abstract). Together, these rapid spectral timing features provide direct observational constraints on how the inner accretion flow restructures itself to expel excess mass and energy when the Eddington limit is breached. 

Beyond individual transient events, super-Eddington growth leaves unique statistical imprints on cosmological scales. Because photon trapping in super-Eddington disks limits the observed luminosity while the underlying mass continues to grow rapidly, it fundamentally alters the expected luminosity-host halo mass relationship. Consequently, quasar clustering serves as a unique statistical probe capable of distinguishing between standard Eddington-limited accretion and super-Eddington growth models [Oogi et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.471L..21O/abstract).

Finally, to fully capture the accumulated mechanical feedback from such energetic episodes, observational efforts must extend beyond localized transient events to large-scale environments. The extended structures of galaxy groups serve as the ultimate probe for the integrated impact of AGN outbursts over time. Specifically, the XMM-Newton Group Astrophysics Project (X-GAP) stands as a primary observational effort aimed at mapping the thermodynamic profiles and the intricate [[intracluster-and-intragroup-phenomena|cooling-heating balance]] in the hot atmospheres of these massive groups, directly linking central accretion events to galaxy-scale mechanical feedback [Eckert et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Galax..12...24E/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While super-Eddington accretion provides a compelling theoretical pathway for the rapid assembly of [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] in the early universe, fundamental uncertainties remain regarding the fueling of these flows, their long-term sustainability, and the ultimate impact of their feedback on host galaxies.

**The Angular Momentum Problem and Inflow Dynamics**
A major theoretical hurdle in sustaining super-Eddington rates is the "angular momentum problem." To reach the [[eddington-limit|Eddington limit]] and beyond, large reservoirs of gas must be transported from galactic scales down to the central parsec, requiring the efficient shedding of orbital angular momentum. Classical, spherically symmetric Bondi-Hoyle models fail to capture this barrier and significantly misrepresent [[net-accretion-rates|accretion rates]] in realistic environments [Hobbs et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.421.3443H/abstract). Recent high-resolution radiation-magnetohydrodynamic (RMHD) simulations suggest that the solution may lie in heavily magnetized, flux-frozen [[magnetic-disk-accretion|accretion flows]]. In these regimes, the inner disk is stabilized by toroidal magnetic fields with plasma $\beta \ll 1$, allowing sustained inflow rates of $10 - 100 \ M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ [Hopkins et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024OJAp....7E..19H/abstract). However, it remains an open question whether these flows can be maintained continuously, or if they are intrinsically episodic—limited to short $\sim 1 \text{ Myr bursts}$ before the local gas reservoir is exhausted or expelled by feedback [Massonneau et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...670A.180M/abstract), [Zana et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821114Z/abstract).

**Uncertainties in Wind-ISM Coupling**
A significant tension in the current literature surrounds the efficiency with which super-Eddington winds and jets couple to the interstellar medium (ISM). The exact [[mechanical-feedback|Mechanical feedback]] efficiency, defined as $\epsilon_w = \dot{E}_w / (\dot{M}_{\rm acc} c^2)$, is highly debated. Purely mechanical feedback models often struggle to simultaneously reproduce the observed scaling relations of black hole masses and the X-ray luminosities of galactic halos unless $\epsilon_w$ is constrained to surprisingly low values of $\sim 10^{-4}$ to $10^{-3}$, roughly an order of magnitude lower than what is often assumed in cosmological simulations [Ciotti et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...717..708C/abstract), [Ostriker et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...722..642O/abstract). 

Furthermore, the literature is starkly divided on whether this wind-ISM coupling results in positive or negative feedback. While the standard paradigm assumes that active galactic nuclei (AGN) driven winds quench galaxies by ejecting gas [Weinberger et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3291W/abstract), several studies argue for [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]]. High-velocity outflows can over-pressurize dense ISM clouds, triggering gravitational collapse; in some gas-rich dwarf starbursts, AGN shocks have been shown to enhance global star formation rates by up to $25\%$ [Dugan et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...844...37D/abstract), [Su et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251020897S/abstract). Resolving whether super-Eddington feedback primarily destroys or builds stellar mass depends heavily on unresolved local ram pressure thresholds and the multiphase structure of the surrounding gas.

**Future Observational Directions**
Observationally, the discovery of over-massive [[super-eddington-radio-loud-dust-obscured-galaxies|"Little Red Dots"]] by JWST has accelerated the need to understand extreme accretion. It remains heavily debated whether these objects represent true [[high-redshift-black-hole-growth|High-redshift black hole growth]] via super-Eddington phases, or if their black hole masses are systematically overestimated due to photon trapping and the unique spectral properties of optically thick, dust-free super-Eddington atmospheres [Lupi et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240617847L/abstract), [Naidu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250316596N/abstract).

Addressing these questions will rely on next-generation X-ray observatories capable of directly mapping the multiphase kinematics of AGN outflows. The proposed High Energy X-ray Probe (HEX-P) will leverage broad spectral coverage ($0.2-80$ keV) and unprecedented sensitivity to map the evolving geometry of the accretion disk-corona system and probe the launching mechanisms of super-Eddington winds [Connors et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024FrASS..1092682C/abstract). Furthermore, proposed ultraviolet missions such as CAFE and LyRIC will play a critical role in mapping diffuse emission in the Lyman UV band, allowing astronomers to trace feedback-driven outflows directly into the circumgalactic medium [Ji et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020SPIE11444E..07J/abstract). Indeed, as demonstrated by frameworks like the FOGGIE (Figuring Out Gas & Galaxies In Enzo) simulations, successfully capturing this multiphase circumgalactic medium will require future UV and optical instruments to possess exceptionally high sensitivity and spectral resolution ($< 30 \text{ km s}^{-1}$) to distinguish the complex structures and kinematics explicitly driven by accretion and feedback [Saeedzadeh et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251105644S/abstract). Concurrently, the Advanced X-ray Imaging Satellite (AXIS mission), planned for the 2030s, will provide the arcsecond spatial resolution necessary to untangle the complex, large-scale interactions between AGN winds, jets, and the circumgalactic medium [Russell et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Univ...10..273R/abstract). Together, these future facilities are expected to definitively constrain the energy and momentum-loading factors of AGN feedback across cosmic time.
