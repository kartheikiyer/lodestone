**High-redshift active galactic nuclei** (AGN) are extremely luminous galactic cores situated at high cosmological redshifts spanning the Cosmic Dawn and Cosmic Noon epochs (typically $z > 1$ to $z > 5$ and beyond). Powered by the rapid, often super-Eddington accretion of gas onto [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]], these objects serve as vital astrophysical laboratories for understanding the formation of [[massive-black-hole-seeds|Massive black hole seeds]] and the primordial assembly of their [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]]. While textbook models of [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]] establish a tight co-evolutionary scaling relation between central black hole mass and host spheroid stellar mass in the local Universe [Ho et al. (1998)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998astro.ph..3307H/abstract), high-redshift AGN provide a direct window into an epoch where these relationships had not yet matured. Because their rest-frame optical and ultraviolet emission is frequently subjected to severe attenuation by neutral gas and dust, characterizing these early accreting systems requires comprehensive multi-wavelength approaches, ranging from deep X-ray surveys probing [[compton-thick-agn|Compton-thick AGN]] to infrared spectroscopy targeting their broad emission lines. 

Recent observational campaigns, particularly those spearheaded by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), have revolutionized the demographic census of the high-redshift AGN population. A defining discovery has been the identification of [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]]—a widespread population of extremely compact, dust-reddened sources that comprise a substantial fraction of early broad-line AGN. Strikingly, dynamical and single-epoch virial mass estimates indicate that many of these early black holes are significantly "[[overly-massive-galaxies|over-massive]]" relative to the stellar mass of their hosts, yielding $M_{\rm bh}/M_*$ ratios that can exceed local values by one to two orders of magnitude [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract) [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). This suggests that in the earliest stages of galaxy formation, black hole growth via heavy direct-collapse seeds or runaway accretion may radically outpace stellar mass assembly, leaving nearly "naked" black holes within emerging [[protogalaxies|Protogalaxies]].

This article explores the physical mechanisms, environmental contexts, and cosmological impacts of high-redshift AGN. It examines their preferential location in dense large-scale structures, where frequent major mergers efficiently trigger nuclear activity and can form [[dual-active-galactic-nuclei|Dual active galactic nuclei]]. Furthermore, it evaluates their critical role in the epoch of cosmic reionization; while early JWST results suggested a surprising overabundance of UV-luminous AGN, the current consensus indicates that [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] remain the dominant source of ionizing photons, as an AGN-dominated reionization scenario would violate thermal constraints in the intergalactic medium [D'Aloisio et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.468.4691D/abstract) [Dayal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240111242D/abstract). Finally, the article outlines persistent open questions in the field, including the fierce debate over whether the over-massive nature of early black holes is a universal physical reality or an artifact of observational selection bias, and the ongoing theoretical challenge of distinguishing genuine AGN activity from [[extreme-starbursts|Extreme starbursts]] in [[deeply-obscured-nuclei|Deeply obscured nuclei]] [Arts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241205946A/abstract).

## Evolution of Discovery and Observational Techniques

Historically, the detection of high-redshift active galactic nuclei was heavily constrained by the limitations of rest-frame ultraviolet and optical surveys. Early discovery efforts largely relied on identifying radio-loud [[quasars|Quasars]] and [[high-redshift-radio-galaxies|High-redshift radio galaxies]], as radio emission remains impervious to the severe dust obscuration that plagues high-redshift systems [Coppejans et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.467.2039C/abstract). In the optical regime, high-redshift candidates were primarily identified using broadband color-selection techniques. These surveys targeted the distinctive spectral breaks caused by neutral hydrogen in the intergalactic medium, specifically relying on the characteristic flux dropouts from the Lyman break and Lyman forest absorption [Jarvis et al. (1998)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998AJ....116.2624J/abstract). However, these traditional UV/optical methods are strongly biased against dust-reddened systems and low-luminosity AGN whose nuclear light is swamped by [[host-galaxies|host galaxy]] starlight [Urry et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007arXiv0712.1041U/abstract). In fact, host galaxy light can overwhelm the optical signatures of low-luminosity [[seyfert-galaxies|Seyfert nuclei]], causing them to appear merely as "optically bright, X-ray-faint" normal galaxies in deep surveys [Peterson et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006AJ....131..133P/abstract). To circumvent biases from stellar light in standard BPT diagrams, researchers developed diagnostics using the He II $\lambda 4685$ emission line to detect faint AGN effectively hidden within highly star-forming galaxies [Bär et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.466.2879B/abstract).

To circumvent these biases further, deep X-ray surveys, such as the Chandra Deep Fields, extended the search into the sub-$L_*$ regime at $z > 3$. These observations revealed that the space density of the sub-$L_*$ population drops significantly at $z > 3$ [Vito et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.473.2378V/abstract), highlighting a "downsizing" or anti-hierarchical evolution where the space-density peak of luminous [[x-ray-agn|X-ray AGN]] occurs at significantly higher redshifts than that of fainter AGN [UEDA et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015PJAB...91..175U/abstract). Deep surveys established that a substantial fraction (approximately $60\%-80\%$) of the high-redshift AGN population is obscured by high column densities ($N_H > 10^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$) [Vito et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.473.2378V/abstract). In fact, multi-wavelength studies demonstrate that the fraction of absorbed AGN increases with redshift, following a power-law of roughly $(1+z)^{0.62}$, which suggests a fundamental evolution in the distribution of obscuring material over cosmic time [Hasinger et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008A&A...490..905H/abstract). Despite this progress, even the deepest X-ray observatories struggle to individually resolve heavily obscured or [[compton-thick-agn|Compton-thick AGN]] at cosmic dawn, leaving a significant fraction of early black hole growth undetected [Treister et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...722L.238T/abstract). To better understand these evolving demographics and plan future multi-wavelength campaigns, researchers heavily rely on building robust mock catalogs using N-body simulations and semi-empirical modeling, which are essential for constraining triggering mechanisms and guiding surveys in the [[early-universe|Early universe]] [Allevato et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...916...34A/abstract).

The deployment of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has fundamentally revolutionized high-redshift AGN demographics. Utilizing deep NIRCam photometry, researchers have identified a widespread population of extremely compact and highly dust-reddened [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|high redshift galaxies]]. Colloquially known as [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]], these sources can be efficiently isolated via color selection [Arts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241205946A/abstract). Population synthesis models underscore the importance of JWST color-color selections in the rest-frame ultraviolet and near-infrared to successfully separate these AGN-dominated systems from star-dominated [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] [Volonteri et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...849..155V/abstract).

The true physical nature of these candidates is actively being unveiled through systematic spectroscopic follow-up using the JWST NIRSpec instrument. Researchers have highlighted the diagnostic potential of JWST's near- and mid-infrared spectrometers in uncovering elusive AGN whose emission is often contaminated by host-galaxy star formation [Satyapal et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...906...35S/abstract). High-resolution infrared spectroscopy has successfully detected broad Balmer emission lines (e.g., H$\alpha$ and H$\beta$) in many of these sources, confirming that they are powered by accreting [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] rather than normal stellar populations [Übler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531..355U/abstract) [Brooks et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251119609B/abstract). This hidden population of faint AGN has profound cosmological implications; their collective emission may resolve the unexpected overabundance of luminous galaxies observed at $z > 10$ [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract). 

Crucially, JWST observations suggest that these early black holes are significantly "[[overly-massive-galaxies|over-massive]]" relative to their host galaxies, exhibiting black hole-to-stellar mass ratios ($M_{\rm bh}/M_*$) of $\sim 10^{-2}$ to $>2$, compared to the local value of $\sim 10^{-3}$ [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). However, the literature remains in active conflict regarding the physical interpretation of these findings. While dynamical mass measurements in some strongly lensed systems confirm the presence of genuinely over-massive black hole seeds [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract), other studies argue that single-epoch virial mass estimators may systematically overestimate black hole masses in this regime, or that the extreme luminosities in a subset of LRDs might still be dominated by intense stellar starbursts rather than AGN activity [Arts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241205946A/abstract). Resolving these tensions relies on continued multiwavelength campaigns to map the transition from obscured seeds to unobscured quasars.

## Little Red Dots and Compact AGN Populations

The advent of JWST has revealed a distinct population of high-redshift ($z > 5$) active galactic nuclei colloquially known as [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]] (LRDs). These sources are characterized by their extremely small effective radii, presenting as [[compact-galactic-nuclei|compact galactic nuclei]] with unusual spectral energy distributions. Observationally, they exhibit optically red continua—a direct consequence of severe dust attenuation—coupled with prominent Broad H$\alpha$ emission. Despite their faintness in X-ray bands, LRDs represent a substantial demographic, comprising 15% to 30% of the high-redshift broad-line AGN population [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract).

The primary power source of LRDs has been a subject of active debate. While some initial models proposed that [[extreme-starbursts|Extreme starbursts]] or compact stellar populations might replicate their luminosities, hydrodynamic simulations (such as the FLARES suite) indicate that starbursts alone systematically fail to reproduce the observed number densities and imply unphysical star formation rates [Arts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241205946A/abstract). Consequently, the consensus strongly favors an AGN-dominated scenario, viewing LRDs as the emerging cores of primeval galaxies hosting [[massive-black-hole-seeds|Massive black hole seeds]] in their earliest and most heavily obscured accretion phases [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract) [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract).

A defining and highly scrutinized feature of the LRD population is their apparent deviation from local black hole-stellar mass scaling relations. Single-epoch virial mass estimates suggest that LRDs host "[[overly-massive-galaxies|overmassive]]" black holes relative to their [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]], with $M_{\rm BH}/M_*$ ratios ranging from $\sim 10^{-2}$ to $>2$, compared to the local canonical value of $\sim 10^{-3}$ [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract) [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). While critics have questioned the validity of applying standard virial calibrations to these unique objects—suggesting that the masses could be overestimated or that the emission could be driven by dense nuclear star clusters—recent dynamical mass measurements have reinforced the AGN interpretation. High-resolution integral field spectroscopy of a strongly lensed LRD at $z = 7.04$ reveals Keplerian rotation around a central point mass of $5 \times 10^7 M_\odot$. This dynamical measurement independently confirms the virial mass estimate, yields a conservative mass ratio of $M_{\rm BH}/M_* > 2$, and definitively rules out a purely stellar origin [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). Furthermore, mapping LRDs onto $M_{\rm BH}-M_*$ diagrams demonstrates that they span a continuum linking ultra-compact dwarfs to primeval lenticular galaxies, occupying a parameter space entirely distinct from standard nuclear star clusters [Graham et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250310958G/abstract).

The identification of these essentially "naked" black holes implies that in the [[early-universe|Early universe]], the accretion-driven growth of [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] can significantly outpace the stellar mass assembly of their galactic hosts. The presence of this hidden AGN population at $z > 10$ may also naturally resolve the unexpected overabundance of highly luminous sources detected by early JWST campaigns, as their non-thermal continuum emission can significantly boost the cosmic ultraviolet luminosity function without violating morphological constraints [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract).

## Black Hole Seed Formation and Growth Mechanisms

The existence of supermassive black holes exceeding $10^8 M_\odot$ within the first billion years of the Universe ($z > 6$) requires extremely rapid mass assembly. The mechanisms driving this growth from initial [[massive-black-hole-seeds|Massive black hole seeds]] remain a central problem in [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]]. The growth timescale of an accreting black hole is fundamentally governed by its radiative efficiency and its Eddington ratio. To satisfy the timeline of the [[early-universe|Early universe]], theoretical models generally invoke one of two primary seed paradigms:

1. **Light Seeds:** Formed from the remnants of Population III stars. To reach supermassive scales by $z \sim 6$, light seeds can undergo moderate Eddington accretion [Brooks et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251119609B/abstract).
2. **Heavy Seeds:** Arising from a direct-collapse origin, massive primordial gas clouds bypass fragmentation to directly form a massive black hole. Alternatively, heavy seeds may form from shocked gas in a runaway gravitational collapse [van Dokkum et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250615619V/abstract). By starting with a significantly larger initial mass, heavy seeds drastically reduce the required number of $e$-folding times.

To help constrain these models, searches in the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) have identified a population of [[dwarf-black-holes|intermediate-mass black holes]] in [[late-type-galaxies-and-stellar-populations|late-type galaxies]], serving as local analogs for primordial light seeds [Greene et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004IAUS..222...33G/abstract). For instance, the detection of candidate central, low-mass black holes in bulgeless late-type galaxies (presenting as [[h-ii-galaxies|H II nuclei]]) provides a compelling local analog for primordial seeds [She et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...842..131S/abstract). For heavy seeds, empirical evidence for alternative direct-collapse pathways can be found in systems like the 'Infinity Galaxy' at $z=1.14$, which showcases a [[supermassive-black-holes|supermassive black hole]] potentially forming via direct collapse from shocked gas between colliding nuclei [van Dokkum et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250615619V/abstract). On the theoretical front, radiation-hydrodynamics simulations based on the IllustrisTNG framework predict that stellar mass assembly often precedes significant black hole accretion, with black holes preferentially forming in overdense regions [Bulichi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711605B/abstract).

Recent discoveries of high-redshift AGN, particularly the population of compact, dust-reddened objects known as [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]] (LRDs) at $z > 5$, have provided critical observational constraints on these mechanisms. LRDs are interpreted as the emerging cores of galaxies hosting massive black hole seeds in early accretion phases [Graham et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250310958G/abstract). While some models have attempted to explain LRD luminosities purely via [[extreme-starbursts|Extreme starbursts]], hydrodynamical simulations demonstrate that star formation alone cannot replicate their observed number densities and star formation rates, strongly favoring an AGN-dominated scenario [Arts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241205946A/abstract).

One of the most striking findings from JWST surveys is that these early black holes appear significantly "[[overly-massive-galaxies|over-massive]]" relative to their [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]]. While the local black hole-to-stellar mass ratio is $M_{\rm bh}/M_* \sim 10^{-3}$, high-redshift AGN frequently exhibit ratios ranging from $10^{-2}$ to $>2$ [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract) [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). A profound example is a strongly lensed LRD at $z=7.04$, where a direct dynamical mass measurement yielded a Keplerian rotation curve consistent with a $50 \times 10^6 M_\odot$ black hole. This system has an extreme ratio of $M_{\rm bh}/M_* > 2$, leaving little room for a stellar component [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). The discovery of such a nearly "naked" black hole indicates that black hole growth can significantly outpace the stellar mass assembly of the host galaxy, representing a massive black hole seed caught in its earliest accretion phase [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract).

However, the literature remains in active debate over whether this over-massive nature is a universal physical reality of early seed formation or an artifact of selection bias. Wide-field surveys may preferentially select the most luminous, actively accreting outliers. Conversely, deep stacking analyses of "normal" high-redshift galaxies ($z > 3$) have revealed a population of hidden AGN with median black hole masses of $10^{5.2} - 10^{6.1} M_\odot$. This undetected bulk population sits much closer to the locally derived $M_{\rm bh}-M_*$ relation and can be well-explained by light stellar remnant seeds undergoing moderate Eddington accretion [Brooks et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251119609B/abstract). Resolving this tension requires deeper census data to determine if individual bright detections merely sample the upper envelope of a population otherwise dominated by light seeds.

## Co-evolution and the M_bh-M_star Scaling Relation

In the local Universe, the masses of [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] ($M_{\rm bh}$) are observed to scale tightly with the stellar mass or velocity dispersion of their [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]]' spheroidal components, typically yielding a mass ratio of $M_{\rm bh}/M_* \sim 10^{-3}$ [Ho et al. (1998)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998astro.ph..3307H/abstract). Tracing the cosmic evolution of this relationship is essential for understanding whether black holes and galaxies grow in lockstep, or if one component systematically precedes the other. Estimating these masses accurately across cosmic time is crucial; for instance, the near-infrared radius-luminosity relationship provides a more extinction-resistant method for estimating black hole masses using Paschen broad emission lines [Landt et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.413L.106L/abstract).

Recent observations of high-redshift [[quasars|Quasars]] and compact, dust-reddened objects known as [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]] (LRDs) have challenged the paradigm of simultaneous co-evolution. Evidence bridging these epochs shows that the fraction of AGN hosted by [[luminous-red-galaxies|red versus blue galaxies]] has remained nearly constant since $z=0.8$, suggesting that the underlying triggering processes driving co-evolution have been largely stable over the last 5 Gyr [Georgakakis et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.418.2590G/abstract). At $z > 4$, dynamically measured and virial black hole masses frequently lie one to two orders of magnitude above the local scaling relations, exhibiting $M_{\rm bh}/M_*$ ratios of $\sim 10^{-2}$ to $>2$ [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract) [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). A striking example of this phenomenon is a strongly lensed LRD at $z = 7.04$. Deep spectroscopic observations of this system reveal Keplerian rotation around a central point mass of $50 \times 10^6 M_\odot$, with an inferred stellar-to-black hole mass ratio of $M_{\rm bh}/M_* > 2$. This object is effectively a "naked" black hole, indicating that it is a massive seed caught in an early [[overly-massive-galaxies|over-massive]] accretion phase before significant stellar mass assembly has occurred [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). Similarly, the discovery of offset and [[dual-active-galactic-nuclei|Dual active galactic nuclei]] at $z > 7$ indicates that early black hole growth may be rapidly driven by major mergers prior to the relaxation of the host galaxy [Übler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531..355U/abstract). Interestingly, while AGN hosts at $z \sim 1$ often exhibit higher levels of morphological disturbance compared to inactive galaxies, by $z \sim 2$ their disturbance levels are statistically similar to those of the inactive population, implying a decreasing role for major mergers in triggering nuclear activity at these higher redshifts [Rosario et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...573A..85R/abstract).

However, the literature reflects an active debate over whether this "over-massive" signature represents a universal physical property of early galaxy formation or is primarily an artifact of selection bias. Because flux-limited surveys inherently favor the detection of the most luminous, rapidly accreting objects, individual detections may preferentially sample only the extreme upper envelope of the underlying $M_{\rm bh}-M_*$ distribution [Brooks et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251119609B/abstract) [Cohn et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250400172C/abstract). 

Evidence supporting the selection bias hypothesis comes from recent stacking analyses of deep JWST fields. When stacking the spectra of standard high-redshift galaxies binned by luminosity and redshift, researchers detect broad Balmer emission implying median black hole masses of $10^{5.21} - 10^{6.13} M_\odot$ residing in host galaxies of $10^{7.84} - 10^{8.56} M_\odot$. These typical populations are at most a factor of 10 over-massive compared to the local relation, placing them significantly closer to the $z=0$ scaling laws than individually detected LRDs [Brooks et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251119609B/abstract). 

Despite these tensions, the discovery of extreme outliers requires theoretical models to accommodate pathways where black hole growth outpaces galaxy formation. Evidence suggesting that the standard scaling relations might actually be in place at high redshift comes from dynamical $M_{\rm bh}$ measurements in lensed $z \sim 2$ quiescent [[high-mass-quiescent-galaxies|"red nugget" galaxies]] and local [[massive-relic-galaxies|relic galaxies]]. The black holes in these systems lie simultaneously on both the $4 < z < 7$ $M_{\rm bh}-M_*$ relation and the local scaling relation for galactic *bulges*. This suggests that the $M_{\rm bh}-M_{\rm bulge}$ relation was likely established early on, and that the perceived "over-massive" nature of high-redshift black holes may arise when comparing them to the total stellar mass of local galaxies that have subsequently grown large stellar disks. Careful consideration of different evolutionary pathways is thus required when comparing high-redshift seeds to local scaling relations [Cohn et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250400172C/abstract). Additionally, the fundamental plane of black hole activity has recently been extended to [[low-luminosity-active-galactic-nuclei|low-luminosity radio AGN]] at redshifts up to $z=4$, indicating a persistent disk-jet connection across various accretion regimes throughout cosmic history [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240217991W/abstract).

## The Role of AGN in Cosmic Reionization

Cosmic reionization marks the phase transition of the [[circumgalactic-medium|intergalactic medium]] (IGM) from a highly neutral state to a fully ionized plasma, a process largely completed by $z \sim 5.9$ [Dayal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240111242D/abstract). The two primary candidate sources capable of producing the requisite flux of hydrogen-ionizing Lyman continuum photons are early [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] and high-redshift [[quasars|Quasars]] or active galactic nuclei (AGN). Evaluating the relative contribution of these two populations to the metagalactic ionizing background has been a central challenge in extragalactic astrophysics.

Evaluating the ionizing photon budget requires conceptualizing the comoving emissivity as the combined contributions from star-forming galaxies and AGN, each governed by their intrinsic photon production rates and their respective Lyman continuum escape fractions [Dayal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240111242D/abstract). The prevailing consensus indicates that star formation, particularly in low-mass galaxies ($M_* \le 10^9 M_\odot$), is the dominant driver of cosmic reionization [Dayal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240111242D/abstract). For a galaxy-dominated background to successfully reionize the universe, the mean escape fraction of ionizing photons from early galaxies must typically be $\gtrsim 10\%$ [Haardt et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...575L..16H/abstract). 

While the rapidly declining number density of bright quasars at $z \gtrsim 3$ traditionally constrained the AGN contribution to a minor role, alternative models have periodically suggested that a hidden population of faint AGN could dominate the ionizing photon budget. These AGN-dominated models have been proposed to explain large spatial opacity variations observed in the $z \sim 5.5$ H I Ly$\alpha$ forest. However, such scenarios are in strong tension with the thermal history of the IGM. An AGN-dominated ionizing background would initiate He II reionization at much earlier epochs, injecting excessive thermal energy and heating the IGM to temperatures well above those derived from observational constraints [D'Aloisio et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.468.4691D/abstract). Furthermore, analyses of the unresolved X-ray background limit the contribution of faint, high-redshift AGN to a maximum H II filling factor of roughly $13\%$ to $27\%$ by $z \simeq 6$ [Haardt et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...575L..16H/abstract).

Recent semi-analytic models incorporating constraints from JWST and ALMA reinforce the secondary role of AGN in the global reionization budget. Current estimates suggest that early star-forming systems supply approximately $77\%$ of the required Lyman continuum photons [Dayal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240111242D/abstract). AGN contribute the remaining $\sim 23\%$, predominantly sourced by [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] in higher-mass host galaxies. Because the comoving number density of AGN drops significantly at higher redshifts, their contribution only matches that of galaxies late in the reionization epoch, at $z \sim 6.2$ [Dayal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240111242D/abstract). Beyond the broad intergalactic medium, localized effects of AGN ionization are observable; for instance, high-redshift [[giant-ly-nebulae|Lyα blobs]] can be significantly powered by fluorescence caused by AGN-driven ionization of surrounding cold gas [Kimock et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...909..119K/abstract).

Despite their sub-dominant role in the global ionizing budget, the discovery of overabundant luminous sources at $z > 10$ by JWST—including the extremely compact, dust-reddened objects known as [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]]—suggests that early black hole accretion is more prevalent than previously assumed. A significant population of hidden, low-luminosity AGN at $z > 10$ featuring "[[overly-massive-galaxies|over-massive]]" black holes ($M_{\rm bh}/M_* \sim 10^{-2}$) may account for this excess UV luminosity without necessitating extreme starburst models [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract). The literature currently remains ambiguous regarding whether this high $M_{\rm bh}/M_*$ ratio is a universal physical property of massive black hole seeds or an artifact of observational selection biases favouring the brightest individual detections. Nevertheless, while these early AGN represent a critical phase in the formation of galactic cores, strict upper limits on the high-energy X-ray background and IGM temperatures ensure that their escaping ionizing radiation remains secondary to stellar emission during cosmic reionization.

## Environmental Context and Early Mergers

The cosmological evolution of [[supermassive-black-holes|Supermassive black holes]] is deeply intertwined with the assembly of large-scale structure. At high redshift, luminous active galactic nuclei (AGN) are preferentially located within massive dark matter halos and trace significant [[galaxy-overdensities|Galaxy overdensities]], such as [[nascent-galaxy-clusters|protoclusters]] and the intersections of cosmic filaments. Early constraints on this clustering demonstrated a strong spatial correlation function for [[x-ray-agn|X-ray selected AGN]] up to $z=3.5$, with comoving correlation lengths of $r_0 \sim 1.5-5.5 \ h^{-1}$ Mpc [Carrera et al. (1998)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998MNRAS.299..229C/abstract).

While AGN activity in the local Universe is typically suppressed in the dense, virialized cores of galaxy clusters, high-redshift observations reveal a distinctly different environmental dependence. Studies of [[high-redshift-galaxy-clusters|massive galaxy clusters]] at $z \sim 1$ demonstrate a statistically significant excess of X-ray selected AGN in the cluster outskirts, specifically at clustercentric distances of $2-2.5 R_{500}$ [Koulouridis et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...623L..10K/abstract). In fact, the AGN fraction in red-sequence cluster members increases by a factor of roughly 8 from low to high redshift ($z > 0.7$), a trend that closely mirrors the broader evolution of star formation in these dense environments [Bufanda et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.2531B/abstract). At these radii, the dynamically active environments—characterized by infalling groups and unvirialized substructure—elevate the galaxy merging rate. This environmental context provides the necessary physical mechanisms to funnel cold gas into the nuclear regions before it is heated or stripped by the intracluster medium [Ehlert et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.437.1942E/abstract). At $z \sim 1$, observations at $3.6\ \mu$m indicate that AGN environments show a distinct excess of [[massive-galaxies|Massive galaxies]], and crucially, this overdensity scales positively with [[radio-luminosity-function|radio luminosity]] rather than black hole mass [Falder et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010MNRAS.405..347F/abstract). Furthermore, [[host-galaxies|Host galaxies]] of X-ray AGN in distant clusters often reside in the 'green valley' on color-magnitude diagrams, suggesting they are in a transitional phase between an active starburst and a completely quenched state [Rumbaugh et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...746..155R/abstract).

Merger-driven fueling is widely recognized as the primary mechanism for triggering high-luminosity [[quasars|Quasars]] and radio-loud AGN at "Cosmic Noon" ($1 \lesssim z \lesssim 3$). The cosmic merger rate is often approximated by a power law $(1+z)^m$. If $m \sim 4$, this trajectory naturally correlates with the peak space density of luminous quasars, though the exact value of $m$ remains highly uncertain. High-resolution imaging of $z > 1$ radio-loud quasars reveals that nearly all such sources reside in host galaxies undergoing recent or ongoing major mergers, contrasting sharply with the more secular environments of lower-luminosity radio-quiet AGN [Breiding et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...91B/abstract). High-luminosity quasars also systematically inhabit small-scale overdensities compared to their lower-luminosity counterparts, further implicating galaxy interactions as a requirement for extreme accretion [Strand et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009PhDT........29S/abstract). Major mergers effectively funnel gas toward the galactic center, triggering radio-loud AGN activity [Breiding et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...91B/abstract). Furthermore, evidence from ALMA [C II] observations of $z > 4$ galaxies (like BRI 0952$-$0115) shows complex gas morphologies and broad spectral wings, indicating massive outflows driven by early mergers [Kade et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...673A.116K/abstract).

A direct consequence of this merger-driven fueling scenario in the early Universe is the formation of [[dual-active-galactic-nuclei|Dual active galactic nuclei]]. As gas-rich [[protogalaxies|Protogalaxies]] merge, they lead to the formation of binary or dual AGN, accreting heavily on kiloparsec- and parsec-scales. Advanced facilities like JWST have begun uncovering these systems at extreme redshifts. For instance, spectroscopic observations at $z = 7.15$ have identified a dual AGN system featuring a broad-line region offset by a projected distance of 620 pc from a highly obscured, narrow-line secondary AGN [Übler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531..355U/abstract). Such discoveries indicate that merger-driven fueling and early black hole coalescence are already well underway merely 700 million years after the Big Bang, establishing the massive seeds that eventually evolve into the luminous quasar populations observed at lower redshifts.

## Multi-wavelength Signatures and Obscuration

The identification and characterization of high-redshift active galactic nuclei inherently rely on multi-wavelength diagnostics, as rest-frame [[ultraviolet-galaxies|ultraviolet and optical emission]] is frequently suppressed by large columns of gas and dust. Deep X-ray, infrared, and radio surveys are required to break degeneracies between intrinsic accretion luminosity and line-of-sight obscuration.

[[x-ray-agn|X-ray spectroscopy]] remains a primary tool for measuring hydrogen column densities ($N_H$). At redshifts $3 \le z < 6$, the fraction of heavily obscured AGN ($\log N_H > 23$) is consistently high, estimated at $0.6-0.8$ [Vito et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.473.2378V/abstract) [Pouliasis et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...685A..97P/abstract). However, the literature reveals ongoing tension regarding the exact space density of [[compton-thick-agn|Compton-thick AGN]] ($N_H \ge 10^{24} \, \mathrm{cm}^{-2}$) at high redshift. While the Compton-thick fraction at low redshifts is well-constrained at around $21\%$, at high redshift, current upper limits constrain this fraction to $\lesssim 40\%$ [Laloux et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.518.2546L/abstract). Additionally, the obscuration is not solely confined to the nuclear torus; host galaxy inclination and intervening material on hundred-parsec scales can substantially contribute to the total measured X-ray column density [Malizia et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...639A...5M/abstract). These highly obscured systems are crucial for mapping early cosmic evolution, as approximately $22\%$ of total black hole growth is predicted to occur in obscured systems that remain individually undetected in the deepest current X-ray surveys [Treister et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...722L.238T/abstract). Tracking this elusive population is necessary to constrain the high-redshift [[x-ray-luminosity-function|X-ray luminosity function]], which currently aligns with pure density evolution models at $z \ge 3$, though luminosity-dependent density evolution remains a possible alternative [Pouliasis et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...685A..97P/abstract).

In the mid- to far-infrared bands, nuclear activity reprocesses ultraviolet photons into thermal dust emission, allowing researchers to separate AGN from [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] using specialized color-color diagnostics [Kirkpatrick et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...763..123K/abstract). The geometry of this obscuring material leaves distinct spectral signatures. However, nuclear obscuration is highly complex, clumpy, and dynamic, with the infrared thermal emission originating from a transition zone located between the broad- and narrow-line regions rather than a static, uniform torus [Ramos Almeida et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017NatAs...1..679R/abstract). Regarding the specific composition of this material, investigations into dust mineralogy reveal that AGN dust consists predominantly of alumina and amorphous silicates, possessing a relatively low crystallinity fraction of approximately 8% [Srinivasan et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017P&SS..149...56S/abstract). For example, at $z \sim 1-1.4$, powerful radio galaxies exhibit strong Silicate absorption features (mean optical depth $\tau_{9.7\mu \mathrm{m}} \sim 1.1$), whereas matched [[quasars|Quasars]] show silicate in emission. This dichotomy is strongly consistent with orientation-dependent unification schemes [Leipski et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...717..766L/abstract). Pushing to even higher redshifts, JWST observations have recently uncovered a population of extremely compact, dust-reddened objects at $z > 5$, dubbed [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]]. While some modeling argues these sources could be [[extreme-starbursts|Extreme starbursts]], dynamical mass measurements and the presence of broad emission lines strongly favor the interpretation that they harbor [[massive-black-hole-seeds|Massive black hole seeds]] in early, heavily obscured accretion phases [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract) [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract).

Finally, radio emission provides an entirely obscuration-independent selection method, though it is limited to the minority of the population with active jets. Observations of Radio-loud quasars at "cosmic noon" ($1 \lesssim z \lesssim 2$) show that nearly all reside in ongoing or recent major galaxy mergers, suggesting that violent interactions are key triggers for launching powerful relativistic jets at high redshift [Breiding et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...91B/abstract). Identifying the most distant radio AGN has traditionally relied on searching for ultra-steep spectral indices; however, very long baseline interferometry of $z > 4.5$ sources reveals a highly diverse population, with flat, steep, and peaked radio spectra present in roughly equal proportions [Coppejans et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.467.2039C/abstract). However, the existence of high-redshift X-ray jets that lack radio counterparts (e.g., B3 0727+409) implies a selection bias in traditional radio-led searches for jet activity [Simionescu et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...816L..15S/abstract). Pushing these boundaries further, the discovery of [[high-redshift-radio-galaxies|High-redshift radio galaxies]] at $z = 5.72$ using 150 MHz low-frequency selections helps map the presence and extent of powerful relativistic jets operating in the very early universe [Saxena et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018arXiv181008119S/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

Recent discoveries of high-redshift [[quasars|Quasars]] and [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]] (LRDs) have raised fundamental questions regarding the formation of [[massive-black-hole-seeds|Massive black hole seeds]]. Observations indicate that early black holes are frequently "[[overly-massive-galaxies|over-massive]]" compared to local scaling relations, exhibiting $M_{\rm bh}/M_*$ ratios ranging from $\sim 10^{-2}$ to $>2$, compared to the local value of $\sim 10^{-3}$ [Hegde et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...08..025H/abstract). A critical open question is whether this high ratio represents a universal physical reality of early seed formation or is merely an artifact of selection biases favouring the most luminous objects [Cohn et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250400172C/abstract) [Brooks et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251119609B/abstract). The discovery of a dynamically confirmed naked black hole at $z=7.04$, where the central black hole mass is more than twice the stellar mass of its host ($M_{\rm bh}/M_* > 2$), strongly supports scenarios where massive seed growth precedes significant stellar mass assembly [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract). Additionally, it remains unclear how non-nuclear or [[wandering-black-holes|Wandering black holes]] in nearby [[active-dwarf-galaxies|dwarf galaxies]] ($z < 0.055$) might affect the overall census of black hole demographics. These local analogs highlight a hidden population that must be accounted for when constraining early black hole seed formation models [Reines et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...888...36R/abstract). The exact physical nature of LRDs also remains an active debate, with tensions existing between AGN-dominated models and those invoking extreme, compact starbursts [Arts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241205946A/abstract) [Juodžbalis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821748J/abstract).

The role of galaxy mergers in triggering high-redshift nuclear activity is another area of active investigation. While major mergers are firmly linked to the triggering of powerful radio-loud AGN at cosmic noon ($z \sim 1-2$) [Breiding et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...91B/abstract), the dynamics of black hole coalescence in the [[early-universe|Early universe]] are only beginning to be mapped. The recent identification of an offset AGN at $z=7.15$ suggests that close pairs and [[dual-active-galactic-nuclei|Dual active galactic nuclei]] may be common in early galactic environments [Übler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531..355U/abstract). Understanding the frequency of these high-redshift mergers is essential for predicting the event rates of gravitational waves. Facilities such as the planned Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will be uniquely sensitive to the millihertz frequency gravitational waves emitted by the inspiral and merger of these early supermassive black hole binaries [Übler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531..355U/abstract).

Finally, the demographics of obscured and low-luminosity AGN at $z > 6$ remain poorly constrained. Deep surveys demonstrate that a large fraction (roughly $60\%$ to $80\%$) of high-redshift AGN are heavily obscured, though this fraction appears to show no strong dependence on redshift or luminosity within the $z=3-6$ regime [Vito et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.473.2378V/abstract) [Pouliasis et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...685A..97P/abstract). Current X-ray observatories lack the sensitivity to detect the bulk of this population. Next-generation X-ray missions—such as Athena, AXIS, Lynx, and the High-Energy X-ray Probe (HEX-P)—are required to probe these [[deeply-obscured-nuclei|Deeply obscured nuclei]] [Schirra et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.508.4816S/abstract) [Boorman et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024FrASS..1135459B/abstract). These advanced observatories will be capable of resolving the degeneracy between low-luminosity AGN and [[galaxy-agn-xrb-hard-x-ray-luminosity|X-ray binaries]] in high-redshift [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]], providing a complete census of early black hole growth [Schirra et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.508.4816S/abstract). This census will be greatly aided by new spectral energy distribution (SED) modeling tools, such as tailored CIGALE modules, that combine X-ray/infrared relations with advection-dominated accretion flow models to better characterize low-luminosity AGN [López et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...692A.209L/abstract). To complement these future high-energy constraints, observations with the upcoming Square Kilometre Array (SKA) will offer transformative insights. The SKA's unprecedented sensitivity will allow for the separate tracing of bulge star formation and nuclear activity in high-redshift galaxies at $z=1-4$ [McAlpine et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015aska.confE..83M/abstract), and potentially use the 21-cm line to map the ionization profiles around high-redshift AGN, providing independent tests of their contribution to cosmic reionization [Kono et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021arXiv210405212K/abstract). Additionally, future infrared missions like PRIMA will play a critical role in detecting deeply obscured nuclei via silicate absorption features at $z = 2-7$ [Donnan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025JATIS..11c1606D/abstract). 

Furthermore, future multiwavelength observations will be crucial for determining which mechanism dominates multiphase AGN outflows and their ultimate impact as a [[global-star-formation-suppression-mechanism|Global star formation suppression mechanism]] [Gourgouliatos et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018NatAs...2..167G/abstract) [Cicone et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018NatAs...2..176C/abstract) [Wylezalek et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018NatAs...2..181W/abstract). The most powerful wind feedback—likely responsible for establishing the $M_{\rm bh}-\sigma$ relation—may be fundamentally episodic and therefore difficult to observe directly [King et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ARA&A..53..115K/abstract). Additionally, theoretical models suggest that cosmic rays might act as a potent intrinsic feedback agent responsible for the quenching of star formation in isolated primordial galaxies [Owen et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv221206469O/abstract). Quantifying the true space density of faint AGN will also resolve whether they provide a non-negligible contribution to the intergalactic ionizing photon budget during the final stages of cosmic reionization [Haardt et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...575L..16H/abstract) [Dayal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240111242D/abstract).
