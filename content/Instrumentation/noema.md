The **Northern Extended Millimeter Array** (NOEMA) is the most sensitive facility for [[millimeter-wave-astronomy|Millimeter-wave Astronomy]] in the Northern Hemisphere. Operated by the Institut de Radioastronomie Millimétrique (IRAM), NOEMA is a radio interferometer designed for high-resolution spectroscopic imaging of molecular gas and cold dust emission. The observatory represents a comprehensive technological and structural upgrade from the legacy [[plateau-de-bure-interferometer|Plateau de Bure Interferometer]], featuring expanded baselines and ultra-wide-bandwidth correlators [da Cunha et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...765....9D/abstract). Providing critical northern-sky coverage and high-frequency capabilities, NOEMA functions as a highly complementary counterpart to the southern-sky [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)]], enabling astronomers to probe the cold universe across all declinations [Tan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...684A..23T/abstract).

In the local universe, NOEMA achieves sub-100 parsec spatial resolutions, permitting cloud-scale mapping of the multiphase interstellar medium (ISM). By resolving individual giant molecular clouds in nearby spiral and starburst galaxies, the array allows researchers to investigate how galactic environments influence star formation efficiencies and to map complex chemical variations among dense gas tracers. Furthermore, NOEMA's exceptional kinematic sensitivity makes it a critical tool for studying active galactic nuclei (AGN). It can trace massive, high-velocity molecular outflows driven by supermassive black holes, providing direct measurements of mass flow rates and feedback mechanisms that regulate and quench host galaxy star formation.

At cosmological distances, NOEMA is a premier instrument for characterizing the early universe and the peak epoch of stellar assembly, known as Cosmic Noon. Through wide-band "blind" spectroscopic scans, the array robustly traces the evolution of the cosmic molecular gas density from $z \sim 1$ out to $z \sim 6$ [Boogaard et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945..111B/abstract). It is uniquely suited for identifying and spectroscopically confirming heavily dust-obscured submillimeter galaxies that are frequently invisible to optical surveys [Cox et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A..26C/abstract). By leveraging the natural magnification of gravitational lensing, NOEMA can resolve the kinematics and sub-kiloparsec morphology of distant Einstein rings [Berta et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...646A.122B/abstract). Additionally, the array maps large-scale structure assembly, characterizing massive high-redshift protoclusters and measuring the thermodynamic pressure profiles of the hot intracluster medium via the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect.

Research utilizing NOEMA actively addresses several major open questions in modern astrophysics. Recent synergies with the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] have demonstrated that many optically dark, apparent ultra-high-redshift galaxy candidates are actually massive, dusty starbursts at $z \sim 2-4$, challenging standard theoretical models of early dust production and suggesting rapid ISM grain growth. As a cornerstone of contemporary radio astrophysics, NOEMA's evolving data reduction techniques and capabilities lay the essential scientific groundwork for next-generation observatories like the [[ngvla|ngVLA]].

## Historical Development: From PdBI to NOEMA

Building upon the foundational role of millimeter interferometry in detecting dense gas and dust accretion disks in the merging nuclei of ultraluminous infrared galaxies like Arp 220 [Scoville et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999Ap&SS.266..149S/abstract), the Northern Extended Millimeter Array (NOEMA) represents the culmination of a major historical upgrade path. This path transformed the legacy [[plateau-de-bure-interferometer|Plateau de Bure Interferometer]] (PdBI) into one of the most powerful facilities for [[millimeter-wave-astronomy|Millimeter-wave Astronomy]] in the Northern Hemisphere. Recognizing the ongoing need for unprecedented sensitivity and resolving power to complement the southern-sky [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)]], the IRAM observatory initiated a comprehensive expansion of the PdBI framework [da Cunha et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...765....9D/abstract).

The facility's structural evolution transitioned the observatory by expanding its number of antennas. By increasing the number of elements, the upgrade yielded a dramatic increase in total collecting area and a corresponding improvement in interferometric $u\upsilon$-plane coverage. This denser sampling of the $u\upsilon$-plane is critical for high-fidelity imaging, enabling advanced visibility-domain modeling techniques—such as analytical fitting using Spergel profiles—which require high signal-to-noise ratios and robust baseline coverage to minimize systematic biases [Tan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...684A..23T/abstract). Concurrently, the extension of the maximum baselines allowed NOEMA to achieve substantially higher angular resolution. This structural upgrade makes it possible to resolve the interstellar medium of nearby galaxies down to sub-100 pc cloud scales [Krieger et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915L...3K/abstract), [Querejeta et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680A...4Q/abstract) and to map the kinematics of sub-kiloparsec structures in high-redshift star-forming galaxies [Berta et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...646A.122B/abstract).

A cornerstone of the technological upgrade path was the implementation of wide-bandwidth backend electronics, most notably the Polyfix correlator [Meyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681L...3M/abstract). The Polyfix correlator processes remarkably large instantaneous bandwidths—spanning up to 31 GHz per band in recent spectroscopic surveys—vastly expanding the array's multiplexing capabilities compared to the legacy PdBI hardware [Berta et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...646A.122B/abstract), [Cox et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A..26C/abstract). This enhancement permits mapping multiple molecular transitions, such as CO, HCN, and $\text{N}_2\text{H}^{+}$, across wide bands alongside the underlying dust continuum [Stuber et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A.182S/abstract). The expanded capabilities also significantly benefit Galactic star formation studies; for example, NOEMA has been used to map hydrocarbons like c-C$_3$H$_2$ toward the protocluster OMC-2 FIR 4, probing energetic particle irradiation in nascent stellar environments [Favre et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...859..136F/abstract).

Crucially, the Polyfix correlator's expansive bandwidth has rendered NOEMA an exceptionally efficient machine for conducting "blind" spectroscopic line scans. Such scans are strictly necessary to secure robust spectroscopic redshifts for heavily dust-obscured, optically dark galaxies identified by infrared facilities like the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] and the [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]] [Cox et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A..26C/abstract), [Meyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681L...3M/abstract). With the augmented array and the Polyfix correlator working in tandem, NOEMA now routinely resolves the true nature of these extreme sources, securely placing many of them at $z \sim 2-5$ and allowing precision measurements of the cosmic molecular gas density out to $z \sim 6$ [Boogaard et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945..111B/abstract), [Meyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681L...3M/abstract). 

## Cloud-Scale Imaging of the Local Interstellar Medium

In the local universe, NOEMA's angular resolution corresponds to physical scales of 30-125 pc, sufficient to resolve individual giant molecular clouds (GMC) and map the multiphase interstellar medium across diverse galactic environments. 

A prime example of this capability is a 154-pointing NOEMA mosaic of the starburst galaxy M82, which mapped $^{12}$CO(1-0) emission at a 30 pc resolution, decomposing the molecular gas into approximately 2000 individual clouds [Krieger et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915L...3K/abstract). This resolved imaging revealed that clouds within the galaxy's tidal streamers maintain roughly constant sizes ($R \sim 50$ pc) and masses ($M \sim 10^5 M_\odot$), whereas clouds entrained in the hot nuclear outflow progressively decrease in size and mass with distance, likely due to evaporation in the hot wind [Krieger et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915L...3K/abstract). These findings build upon and complement earlier [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|ALMA]] observations that caught the cold molecular phase of M82's galactic wind, demonstrating the power of interferometry to trace multiphase outflows [Westmoquette et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013Natur.499..416W/abstract). Similarly, a wide-field survey of the face-on spiral IC 342 at a 60 pc resolution resolved over 600 GMCs, demonstrating that the clouds are broadly in virial equilibrium ($\alpha_{\rm vir} = 1-2$) [Querejeta et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680A...4Q/abstract). However, their structural properties are highly dependent on the galactic environment: median molecular gas surface densities ($\Sigma_{\rm mol}$) and velocity dispersions ($\sigma_{\rm CO}$) rise from $\Sigma_{\rm mol} \approx 80 \, M_\odot \text{ pc}^{-2}$ and $\sigma_{\rm CO} \approx 6.6 \text{ km s}^{-1}$ in the inter-arm regions to $\Sigma_{\rm mol} \approx 1100 \, M_\odot \text{ pc}^{-2}$ and $\sigma_{\rm CO} \approx 18.4 \text{ km s}^{-1}$ in the central kiloparsec [Querejeta et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680A...4Q/abstract). By contrast, ALMA observations of the star-forming disk of M83 at a 40 pc resolution reveal highly structured, filamentary gas concentrations in the inter-arm regions, where structures are able to survive for over 100 Myr [Koda et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...949..108K/abstract). In another striking case, NOEMA observations, combined with [[muse-instrument|MUSE data]], revealed a gravitationally unstable gas disk in the massive star-forming galaxy LARS 8, where the molecular gas depletion time demonstrably decreases towards the outskirts of the disk [Puschnig et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.3913P/abstract).

A flagship effort to characterize cloud-scale chemistry is the "Surveying the Whirlpool at Arcseconds with NOEMA" (SWAN) program, which mapped the inner $5 \times 7$ kpc of M51 across nine molecular transitions at a 125 pc resolution [Stuber et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A.182S/abstract), [Stuber et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250300534S/abstract). SWAN provides a uniquely detailed view of CO isotopologues and dense gas tracers outside the Milky Way. Across the mapped disk, the $^{13}$CO and C$^{18}$O isotopologue lines are, on average, $8 \pm 2$ and $29 \pm 6$ times fainter than $^{12}$CO, respectively [Stuber et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250300534S/abstract). The $^{13}$CO/C$^{18}$O line ratio exhibits moderate radial gradients and varies inversely with the local star formation rate surface density. These variations indicate that CO isotopologue ratios are not purely set by column density, but are also driven by selective nucleosynthesis and localized changes in gas opacity [Galić et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250815901G/abstract). Furthermore, non-LTE modeling of CO isotopologues in M51 reveals that the $\alpha_{\rm CO}$ conversion factor varies significantly in the galactic center and inner spiral arms compared to typical Milky Way values [den Brok et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025AJ....169...18D/abstract).

High-resolution imaging of dense gas tracers in M51 has also highlighted tensions in standard density-driven chemical models. While HCN is traditionally used to trace dense, star-forming gas, the SWAN survey reveals that HCN emission is artificially elevated in the central kiloparsec of M51, likely due to AGN-driven excitation or shocks [Stuber et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680L..20S/abstract), [Stuber et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250300534S/abstract). Conversely, N$_2$H$^+$ appears to be a more reliable tracer of the dense gas directly participating in star formation. On average, the N$_2$H$^+$/HCN ratio is $0.20 \pm 0.09$, but it exhibits regional variations of more than a factor of two across the galactic disk [Stuber et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680L..20S/abstract). NOEMA observations strongly suggest that secondary physical effects—such as gas temperature, opacity, and varying chemical abundances—must be incorporated into star formation models to accurately interpret N$_2$H$^+$/$^{12}$CO and HCN/$^{12}$CO ratios [Stuber et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680L..20S/abstract).

Beyond standard spiral galaxies, NOEMA's sensitivity is equally critical for analyzing interacting systems and extreme low-metallicity environments. For instance, mapping CO emission in the spiral-spiral merger Arp 238 and the spiral-elliptical merger Arp 142 demonstrated how collision speeds and tidal torques differently influence gas concentration and star formation efficiencies [Xu et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918...55X/abstract). Additionally, high-resolution CO(1-0) observations of what appeared to be overlapping interacting galaxies on the line-of-sight helped reclassify the system as an actual major merger experiencing interaction-induced star formation [Halle et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250718355H/abstract). In the metal-poor dwarf galaxy Kiso 5639 ($12 + \log(\text{O/H}) = 7.83$), NOEMA detected an enormous molecular cloud complex of $M \sim 2.9 \times 10^7 M_\odot$ with a surface density of $120 \, M_\odot \text{ pc}^{-2}$ [Elmegreen et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...859L..22E/abstract). Because of the low dust content, the CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor is highly elevated, reaching:
$$ \alpha_{\rm CO} \approx 100 \, M_\odot \text{ pc}^{-2} (\text{K km s}^{-1})^{-1} $$
Despite the harsh radiation environment, the high internal pressures within such massive clumps allow molecular gas to survive and fuel star formation, providing a local analog for massive clump formation in high-redshift, metal-poor galaxies. A similar extreme environment is found in the blue compact dwarf galaxy II Zw 40, where observations indicate a CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor 4 to 35 times higher than the Milky Way value, largely driven by its bursty star formation history at one-fifth solar metallicity [Kepley et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...828...50K/abstract).

## High-Redshift Galaxies and Cosmic Noon

NOEMA is a premier facility for tracing the molecular gas and dust reservoirs of galaxies during the peak epoch of star formation, known as Cosmic Noon ($z \sim 2$), and extending into the Epoch of Reionization. Because thick dust columns absorb rest-frame ultraviolet and optical light, massive submillimeter galaxies (SMGs) are often invisible in deep [[hst-observations|HST observations]] or to the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]]. NOEMA efficiently performs "blind" redshift searches, identifying CO, [CII], and [CI] emission lines in highly obscured systems to determine robust spectroscopic redshifts. This observational strategy is a cornerstone of millimeter-wave astronomy; for example, deep surveys utilizing the [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)]] employed [CI] and CO(4-3) line scans to provide the spectroscopic confirmation for the near-infrared-dark galaxy ADF22.A2 at $z = 3.99$, illustrating the broad power of these measurements for characterizing the massive progenitors of quiescent galaxies [Umehata et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...640L...8U/abstract).

These blind spectral line scans are fundamental for measuring the evolution of the cosmic molecular gas density out to $z \sim 6$. Deep NOEMA observations in heavily studied patches of the sky, such as the [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope Deep Surveys]] in the Hubble Deep Field North (HDF-N), have placed robust constraints on the CO luminosity function, including the first measurements of the CO(5-4) luminosity function at $z = 5.0$ [Boogaard et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945..111B/abstract). Joint analyses combining NOEMA data with ASPECS data from the Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA) show that the cosmic molecular gas density evolution is well-described by a single Schechter function. However, NOEMA's HDF-N measurements yield gas densities $0.15-0.4$ dex lower than ALMA measurements in the Ultra Deep Field at overlapping redshifts, highlighting the significant impact of cosmic field-to-field variance [Boogaard et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945..111B/abstract). Blind NOEMA observations frequently yield unexpected individual discoveries as well, such as the serendipitous CO detection of a main-sequence galaxy at $z \sim 3.2$ characterized by an exceptionally high molecular gas fraction of $0.9$ [Gowardhan et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...875....6G/abstract).

Beyond blind surveys, targeted spectral line scans have rapidly expanded the spectroscopic catalog of dust-obscured galaxies. The z-GAL large program utilized NOEMA to target 126 bright [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]] galaxies, successfully yielding robust spectroscopic redshifts for 124 of them, finding a median redshift of $z = 2.56$ and revealing broad linewidths ($\Delta V \sim 590 \text{ km s}^{-1}$) characteristic of massive, gas-rich systems [Cox et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A..26C/abstract), demonstrating the broad utility of large-scale CO redshift scans for significantly increasing the sample of spectroscopically confirmed SMGs and rigorously testing their role as progenitors of local early-type galaxies [Birkin et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022PhDT.........6B/abstract). NOEMA has also forged links between different populations of high-redshift objects. By detecting molecular gas in the system DLA0201+365g, NOEMA associated high-metallicity damped Lyman-$\alpha$ systems (DLAs) at $z \approx 2.46$ with massive, gas-rich galaxies [Kaur et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...934...87K/abstract).

Crucially, NOEMA has been instrumental in revising the nature of several ultra-high-redshift candidates. Recent continuum and molecular line scans have demonstrated that a significant fraction of luminous red $1.5-2.0\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ dropouts—initially classified as $z > 10$ candidates by JWST—are actually massive, optically dark starbursts at $z \sim 2-4$ [Meyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681L...3M/abstract). For example, NOEMA observations of the highly obscured ($A_V \sim 5$) SMG 850.1 at $z = 4.26$ revealed a stellar mass of $M_* \sim 10^{11.8}\ M_\odot$ and a compact stellar mass distribution ($R_e \sim 1$ kpc), identifying it as a likely progenitor of local ultramassive early-type galaxies [Smail et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...958...36S/abstract). Pushing even further back into the Epoch of Reionization, NOEMA secured the [CII] detection of the gravitationally lensed Lyman-break galaxy A1703-zD1 at $z = 6.827$, demonstrating its power to confirm the earliest galaxy populations [Molyneux et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.512..535M/abstract). Similarly, utilizing both NOEMA and ALMA, researchers characterized the host galaxy of the most distant known blazar at $z = 7$, revealing a low [CII]-to-infrared luminosity ratio that is indicative of significant synchrotron contamination from the active nucleus [Banados et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240812299B/abstract).

High-resolution capabilities provide direct insight into the internal gas dynamics and star-formation efficiency of these early galaxies. Observations of bright SMGs frequently measure short gas depletion timescales, $\tau_{\rm depl} \sim 50$ Myr, underscoring the extreme, bursty nature of star formation in these systems [Jones et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...916...46J/abstract). Meanwhile, joint kinematic modeling of CO(3-2) emission and H$\alpha$ in a massive star-forming galaxy at $z = 1.4$ suggested that baryons, rather than dark matter, dominate the dynamical mass within the galaxy's effective radius [Übler et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...854L..24U/abstract). The physical distribution of this gas and dust remains an active area of investigation. While some kinematic models suggest that dust emission is significantly more concentrated than the molecular gas, detailed self-consistent radiative transfer modeling of NOEMA observations (such as the $z = 4.055$ starburst GN20) indicates that the underlying gas and dust distributions are intrinsically similar. In these models, the distinct apparent surface brightness profiles are largely artifacts of high dust opacity in the dense galactic nucleus [Boogaard et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251017804B/abstract). 

## Gravitational Lensing and Einstein Rings

By combining its intrinsic angular resolution with the natural magnification provided by foreground galaxy clusters or massive elliptical galaxies, NOEMA can probe the interstellar medium of high-redshift starbursts at sub-kiloparsec physical scales. Lensing effectively boosts both the spatial resolution and the flux of background sources, making NOEMA a critical tool for mapping the kinematics and molecular gas reservoirs of the early universe.

When the background source, the intervening lens, and the observer are closely aligned, the background emission is distorted into a ring. Millimeter-wave interferometry's ability to resolve these extended structures in both dust continuum and molecular line emission allows for detailed source-plane reconstruction. For instance, observations with the [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)]] of the $z = 3.63$ major merger G09v1.97 resolved a nearly complete $1.\!\!^{\prime\prime}5$ diameter ring. Kinematic and morphological modeling in the source plane revealed a compact star-forming disk with an effective radius of $0.7 \pm 0.1$ kpc, subject to a continuum magnification factor of $\mu \sim 10-11$ and line magnifications ranging from $\mu = 5$ to $22$ [Yang et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...624A.138Y/abstract). NOEMA possesses similar mapping capabilities. High-resolution ($0.\!\!^{\prime\prime}3$) NOEMA observations of the $z=2.95$ galaxy HerBS-89a uncovered a partial $1.\!\!^{\prime\prime}0$ ring; source-plane reconstructions mapped the system down to $\sim 800$ pc scales with an average magnification factor of $\mu \simeq 5.0$, identifying two compact, rotating components in the process of merging [Berta et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...646A.122B/abstract). 

NOEMA has also been instrumental in confirming the extremes of the lensed universe. By detecting the CO(4-3) and CO(5-4) transitions at $>8\sigma$ significance, NOEMA provided the spectroscopic confirmation for the COSMOS-Web ring, the most distant Einstein ring known to date. The observations established the background source redshift at $z = 5.1043$ and characterized it as a dust-obscured starburst with a short gas depletion time ($\tau_{\rm dep} < 100$ Myr) [Shuntov et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250220136S/abstract). Furthermore, lensing enables NOEMA to spatially resolve heavily obscured "dark" galaxies that remain invisible in deep optical and near-infrared imaging, as demonstrated by the detailed reconstruction of the aforementioned $z=4.26$ ultramassive system 850.1 in the cluster A 1489 [Smail et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...958...36S/abstract). 

Lensing also empowers astronomers to investigate the immediate environs of active nuclei, representing a broad achievement of millimeter-wave interferometry. For example, in the lensed quasar MG J0414+0534 at $z=2.64$, ALMA observations detecting 380 GHz water and high-$J$ CO emission revealed a compact circumnuclear disc bisected by the quasar's radio jets [Stacey et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.493.5290S/abstract). These general capabilities highlight how advanced arrays like ALMA and NOEMA successfully probe the active environments of distant AGN.

## AGN Feedback and Molecular Outflows

Observations utilizing NOEMA provide critical kinematic and morphological data to trace the interaction between supermassive black holes (SMBHs) and their host galaxies. In the context of [[millimeter-wave-astronomy|Millimeter-wave Astronomy]], mapping cold molecular gas at sub-kiloparsec scales enables quantitative studies of AGN feedback, including the characterization of molecular outflows and the complex dynamics of actively accreting, merging systems. Highlighting the critical importance of millimeter-wave AGN studies, it is estimated that approximately 22% of total black hole growth occurs in heavily obscured systems that remain individually undetected in deep X-ray surveys [Treister et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...722L.238T/abstract).

NOEMA's spectral resolution and sensitivity are instrumental in detecting high-velocity molecular outflows driven by AGN, which are capable of physically sweeping gas out of the host galaxy and suppressing star formation. In the nearby Narrow Line Seyfert 1 galaxy IRAS17020+4544, NOEMA resolved a multiphase outflow extending 2-3 kpc from the nucleus, with gas velocities reaching up to $v_{\rm out} = \pm 1900$ km s$^{-1}$ [Longinotti et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.521.2134L/abstract). This system exhibits a mass outflow rate of $\dot{M}_{\rm H_2} = 139 \pm 20 \, M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$. The momentum boost relative to the nuclear X-ray wind is roughly a factor of 30, indicative of an energy-conserving expanding shock [Longinotti et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.521.2134L/abstract). NOEMA's sensitivity also extends to absorption studies; in the interacting system G0248+430, the array discovered CO(1-0) and CN(1-0) absorption at $z = 0.05$ within a tidal tail, providing a unique line-of-sight probe of the molecular content in merging environments [Combes et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...623A.133C/abstract).

NOEMA has also pushed these outflow studies into the high-redshift universe. For instance, in the $z = 3.912$ broad absorption line (BAL) quasar APM 08279+5255, observations of blueshifted CO(4-3) emission revealed a massive molecular outflow with maximum velocities of $-1340$ km s$^{-1}$ and a mass flow rate between $3000$ and $7400 \, M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ [Feruglio et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...608A..30F/abstract). The momentum boost of the molecular outflow relative to the AGN radiation pressure was measured to be:
$$ \frac{\dot{P}_{\rm OF}}{\dot{P}_{\rm AGN}} \sim 2 - 6 $$
The literature notes an active debate regarding whether such outflows are definitively energy-conserving or momentum-conserving, as current kinematic data often struggle to unambiguously distinguish between the two expansion modes at high redshift [Feruglio et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...608A..30F/abstract).

NOEMA observations of circumnuclear gas also clarify the feeding and feedback mechanisms in merging systems hosting dual AGN. In a sample of kiloparsec-scale X-ray weak dual AGN candidates, NOEMA mapped CO(2-1) emission to measure molecular gas masses of $M_{\rm H_2} \sim 0.2 \times 10^9$ to $21 \times 10^9 \, M_\odot$ [Hou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...943...50H/abstract). The derived hydrogen column densities are moderate ($N_{\rm H} \sim 5 \times 10^{21} - 2 \times 10^{23}$ cm$^{-2}$) and the gas is highly compact. These findings argue against the necessity of heavy obscuration to explain the systems' X-ray weakness. Instead, the data favor a feedback scenario where recent pericentric passages of the merging galaxies expel circumnuclear gas, dynamically suppressing further SMBH accretion [Hou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...943...50H/abstract).

Furthermore, NOEMA's kinematic mapping has been used to investigate extreme SMBH dynamics. In the cool-core cluster quasar 3C 186 ($z = 1.06$), CO(4-3) observations identified a massive rotating molecular disk ($M_{\rm H_2} \sim 8 \times 10^{10} \, M_\odot$) that is co-spatial with the host galaxy but spatially offset by $\sim 1''$ from the quasar's continuum emission [Castignani et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661L...2C/abstract). Coupled with a spectral shift of $\sim 2000$ km s$^{-1}$ between the narrow and broad line regions, the NOEMA data strongly support the scenario of a recoiling SMBH kicked out of the galactic center following a gravitational wave recoil event from a binary black hole merger [Castignani et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661L...2C/abstract).

Finally, through [[high-angular-resolution-astronomy|High angular resolution astronomy]], interferometric observations of dense gas tracers demonstrate how AGN mechanically and radiatively alter their immediate host environments. Early evidence from Nobeyama Millimeter Array (NMA) observations indicated that luminous infrared galaxies (LIRGs) hosting buried AGN show elevated HCN/HCO$^+$ intensity ratios, suggesting enhanced HCN abundance driven by X-ray or radiative pumping [Imanishi et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007AJ....134.2366I/abstract). High-resolution mapping continues to classify distinct molecular distributions in such active systems; for example, ALMA observations of the Seyfert galaxy NGC 1068 show that shock-related species like SO and HC$_3$N are heavily concentrated in the circumnuclear disk [Takano et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014PASJ...66...75T/abstract). Similarly, the [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)]] resolved the central kiloparsec of the luminous Seyfert galaxy NGC 7469 to reveal an elevated HCN/HCO$^+$ intensity ratio at the AGN position [Izumi et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...811...39I/abstract). In parallel, NOEMA has mapped highly complementary AGN-driven excitation effects, which enhance HCN emission relative to N$_2$H$^+$, in the central kiloparsec of M51 [Stuber et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680L..20S/abstract), [Stuber et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250300534S/abstract).

## Large-Scale Structures and Cluster Pressure Profiles

The assembly of the universe's most massive large-scale structures is inextricably tied to the evolution of dusty star-forming galaxies and their associated molecular gas reservoirs. NOEMA serves as a critical facility for identifying and characterizing these overdensities, typically observed as massive galaxy groups and protoclusters at high redshift. By executing spectral scans of [[millimeter-wave-astronomy|millimeter-wave transitions]]—primarily $^{12}$CO and atomic carbon ([CI])—NOEMA provides the unambiguous spectroscopic redshifts and gas mass measurements required to confirm these structures and map their kinematics.

A cornerstone effort in this domain is the NOEMA formIng Cluster survEy (NICE), a large program targeting 69 massive galaxy group candidates at $z > 2$. Within the COSMOS field, the NICE survey has spectroscopically confirmed eight massive galaxy groups at $1.65 \leq z \leq 3.61$. These systems are characterized by intense integrated infrared star formation rates ranging from $260$ to $1300\ M_{\odot}\ {\rm yr}^{-1}$ . Kinematic and spatial modeling indicates these groups reside in single, collapsed dark matter halos with masses spanning $\log(M_{\rm h}/M_{\odot}) = 12.8 - 13.7$. Baryonic accretion rates for these halos are estimated between $10^3$ and $8 \times 10^3\ M_{\odot}\ {\rm yr}^{-1}$. The NICE data reveal a quasi-linear relationship between the ratio of the star formation rate to the baryonic accretion rate and the theoretical halo mass limit for cold streams [Sillassen et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...690A..55S/abstract). Cosmological simulations suggest that these structures are the direct progenitors of massive local galaxy clusters with $z=0$ halo masses exceeding $10^{14}\ M_{\odot}$ [Sillassen et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240702973S/abstract). Similarly, adjacent to the [[goods-survey|GOODS survey]] North field, NOEMA molecular line scans uncovered a tight overdensity of extremely bright SMGs at $z \sim 3.14$ exhibiting immense gas reservoirs ($M_{\rm gas} \sim 10^{11}\ M_{\odot}$) and individual star formation rates approaching $\sim 2500\ M_{\odot}\ {\rm yr}^{-1}$ [Jones et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...916...46J/abstract).

In addition to probing the cold molecular gas feeding these progenitors, NOEMA is capable of mapping the hot intracluster medium (ICM) of established galaxy clusters via the thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect. The tSZ effect manifests as a spectral distortion of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) due to the inverse Compton scattering of CMB photons by hot electrons in the ICM. The amplitude of this distortion is proportional to the line-of-sight integral of the electron pressure:
$$y = \frac{\sigma_T}{m_e c^2} \int P_e \, dl$$
where $\sigma_T$ is the Thomson scattering cross-section, $m_e c^2$ is the electron rest mass energy, and $P_e$ is the thermal electron pressure. Because the tSZ surface brightness is a direct proxy for the integrated pressure of the ICM, high-resolution mapping allows astrophysicists to reconstruct the radial pressure profile $P_e(r)$ of galaxy clusters. 

In [[millimeter-wave-astronomy|Millimeter-wave Astronomy]], mapping the tSZ effect at high angular resolution presents observational challenges: the spatial filtering of extended emission by interferometers, and the presence of contaminating foreground or background point sources. NOEMA overcomes these hurdles through exceptional sensitivity, enabling the precise identification and subtraction of interloping millimeter-wave point sources (such as SMGs and AGN) that could otherwise mask the tSZ decrement. To recover the full thermodynamic structure of the ICM across large spatial scales, NOEMA data must be jointly modeled with zero-spacing observations from single-dish facilities. This synergistic approach was successfully applied to the massive galaxy cluster CL J1226.9+3332 at $z = 0.89$. By combining high-resolution NOEMA observations with single-dish data from the IRAM 30-metre telescope, the [[green-bank-telescope|Green Bank Telescope]], and the [[caltech-submillimeter-observatory|Caltech Submillimeter Observatory]], researchers reconstructed the cluster's pressure profile from its core ($\sim 15$ kpc) out to its outskirts ($\sim 1500$ kpc), revealing a notably flat core indicative of a dynamically disturbed system [Muñoz-Echeverría et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250914048M/abstract).

## Open Questions and Multi-Wavelength Synergies

As a premier facility for Northern Hemisphere [[millimeter-wave-astronomy|millimeter astronomy]], NOEMA is highly synergistic with multi-wavelength observatories, playing a crucial role in our understanding of galaxy assembly and the cosmic star formation history.

A prominent recent synergy is NOEMA's collaboration with the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]]. Initial JWST observations revealed a surprisingly large population of luminous, red galaxy candidates at $z > 10$. However, subsequent NOEMA dust continuum and molecular line scans have demonstrated that a significant fraction of these "ultra-high-redshift" candidates are, in fact, optically dark, dust-obscured massive starbursts at $z \sim 2-4$ [Meyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681L...3M/abstract), [Smail et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...958...36S/abstract). Identifying these interlopers is critical for accurately measuring the cosmic star formation history, as these dusty galaxies alone contribute $\gtrsim 3-6\%$ of the total cosmic star formation rate density at $z \sim 3.5$ [Meyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681L...3M/abstract). 

High-resolution imaging campaigns combining JWST and NOEMA have also opened debates regarding the physical distribution of the interstellar medium in high-redshift starbursts. For example, observations of the $z=4.055$ starburst GN20 reveal that while the dust continuum appears significantly more concentrated than the molecular gas, this discrepancy is largely an artifact of radiative transfer effects rather than an intrinsic physical separation [Boogaard et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251017804B/abstract). Resolving whether dust and gas are co-spatial across diverse environments remains an active area of research. Furthermore, standard dust production models—relying on supernovae and asymptotic giant branch stars—struggle to explain the massive dust reservoirs inferred in galaxies even at intermediate epochs. For example, observations of the $z=0.99$ galaxy CLASH 2882 derive a substantial dust mass of $\approx 5 \times 10^7 \mu^{-1} M_\odot$ (where the magnification $\mu=2.7$), suggesting that rapid grain growth in the dense interstellar medium is required to reproduce such quantities [Dwek et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...813..119D/abstract).

Looking forward, NOEMA's capabilities will be heavily complemented by next-generation radio observatories, including the [[ngvla|ngVLA]]. While NOEMA and the [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)]] excel at mapping thermal dust continuum and mid-to-high-$J$ CO transitions, the ngVLA will provide unparalleled sensitivity to the extended, low-surface-brightness reservoirs of cold molecular gas via low-$J$ CO transitions [Casey et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015arXiv151006411C/abstract), [Emonts et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ASPC..517..587E/abstract). Together, these facilities will trace the complete thermodynamic state of the gas driving galaxy assembly.

Several other open questions will guide NOEMA's future survey strategies:
*   **Cosmic Variance in Deep Fields:** Joint analyses of NOEMA (e.g., in the HDF-N) and ALMA deep fields show that the evolution of the cosmic molecular gas density is broadly consistent with a single Schechter function. However, tensions of $0.15-0.4$ dex persist between fields, raising questions about the extent to which cosmic field-to-field variance impacts our global view of gas evolution [Boogaard et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945..111B/abstract).
*   **Dense Gas Tracers:** High-resolution NOEMA mapping of local galaxies (e.g., the SWAN survey) highlights significant spatial variations in the $\text{HCN}/\text{N}_2\text{H}^+$ ratio. The literature remains divided on the exact chemical and physical drivers of these variations, and whether $\text{HCN}$ or $\text{N}_2\text{H}^+$ is the more reliable universal tracer for dense, star-forming gas in the presence of active galactic nuclei or extreme starbursts [Stuber et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...680L..20S/abstract), [Stuber et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250300534S/abstract).
