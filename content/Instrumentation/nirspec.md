## Article

The Near-Infrared Spectrograph (NIRSpec) is the primary [[near-infrared-astronomy|near-infrared]] [[spectrographs|spectroscopic instrument]] aboard the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]]. Operating over a continuous wavelength range of 0.6 to 5.3 μm, NIRSpec provides a generational leap in sensitivity and [[multiplexing-astronomy|Multiplexing (astronomy)]] capabilities for space-based astrophysics, a design motivated by the scientific need to optimize observations in the near-infrared for early galaxy discovery and characterization [Giardino et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ASPC..507..305G/abstract), [Kalirai et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ConPh..59..251K/abstract), [Maseda et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A..73M/abstract). The instrument features three highly adaptable observing modes: Fixed Slit (FS) spectroscopy for high-contrast targets, [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] (IFS) for [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|spatially resolved 3D mapping]] of extended sources, and Multi-Object Spectroscopy (MOS). The MOS mode utilizes a programmable microshutter array—a grid of nearly 250,000 individually addressable doors—allowing astronomers to obtain simultaneous spectra for hundreds of sources across a 9 arcminute$^2$ field of view. Together with a tiered spectral resolution system ranging from $R \sim 100$ to $R \sim 2700$, NIRSpec serves as a critical tool for tracing rest-frame optical emission lines from the local Universe out to the Epoch of Reionization.

Since its deployment, NIRSpec has fundamentally transformed [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]] and our understanding of early galaxy assembly. Through major [[deep-field-campaigns|Deep field campaigns]] like JADES and CEERS, NIRSpec has shifted the high-redshift frontier from [[observational-photometry|photometric candidate selection]] to robust spectroscopic confirmation. While these initial massive surveys definitively confirmed large populations of galaxies at $6 < z < 10$ [Finkelstein et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250104085F/abstract), [Curtis-Lake et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251001033C/abstract), [Finkelstein et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983L...4F/abstract), targeted ultra-deep follow-up campaigns have pushed the boundaries further, definitively identifying galaxies at $z > 10$ [Hainline et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240404325H/abstract), [Hsiao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240416200H/abstract). These observations have unveiled surprising physical conditions in the early Universe, including exceptionally high electron densities, early metal enrichment, and the unexpected presence of massive, "dormant" (quiescent) galaxies fully assembled within the first few hundred million years after the Big Bang [Nanayakkara et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024NatSR..14.3724N/abstract), [Covelo-Paz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250622540C/abstract). 

Beyond the first galaxies, NIRSpec has provided critical insights into supermassive black hole co-evolution and active galactic nuclei (AGN) feedback. Spatially resolved observations have mapped powerful ionized outflows and revealed a high fraction (20-30%) of dual AGNs among surveyed AGN systems at cosmic noon, an observation that moderately exceeds the $\sim 10\%$ occurrence rate predicted by current cosmological simulations [Perna et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..59P/abstract). 

## Instrument Architecture and Operating Modes

NIRSpec provides versatile spectroscopic capabilities on board the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]], covering critical rest-frame optical emission lines, such as $\mathrm{H}\alpha$ and $[\mathrm{O\,III}]$, in galaxies extending well into the Epoch of Reionization at $z > 7$ [Hsiao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240416200H/abstract). It offers three nominal resolving powers: a [[low-resolution-observations|low-resolution]] prism ($R \sim 100$) for high-sensitivity continuum and broad-line detection, medium-resolution gratings ($R \sim 1000$) for line diagnostics, and high-resolution gratings ($R \sim 2700$) for detailed kinematic studies [Giardino et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ASPC..507..305G/abstract), [Curtis-Lake et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251001033C/abstract). In practice, these modes are often highly complementary to other onboard instruments; for example, the CANUCS survey has demonstrated the powerful synergy between NIRSpec and [[niriss|NIRISS]] [[grism-spectroscopy|grism spectroscopy]] for obtaining accurate spectroscopic redshifts of lensed multiple images in galaxy clusters [Rihtaršič et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240610332R/abstract), [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract).

**Multi-Object Spectroscopy (MOS)**
The MOS mode provides unprecedented near-infrared multiplexing capabilities via a Microshutter Assembly (MSA). The MSA consists of $730 \times 342$ individually addressable microshutters. Each shutter measures $0.2^{\prime\prime}$ in width by $0.5^{\prime\prime}$ in the cross-dispersion direction, covering a total field of view of roughly $9\ \mathrm{arcmin}^2$ [Giardino et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ASPC..507..305G/abstract). Observers typically group these into "slitlets" of three adjacent shutters to allow for local background subtraction via telescope nodding. However, utilizing shorter two-shutter slitlets or even single shutters, combined with master background subtraction from empty shutters, can further maximize the instrument's multiplexing efficiency [Belli et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251011775B/abstract), [Worku et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251211985W/abstract). Furthermore, a novel "slit-stepping" strategy has been developed to map extended targets using the MSA. By stepping the MSA across target galaxies in discrete spatial increments, observers can build custom 3D data cubes for dozens of galaxies at once, yielding data at $>15\times$ the observational efficiency of the standard [[integral-field-spectroscopy|integral field mode]] [Barišić et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240808350B/abstract), [Barišić et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983..139B/abstract). 

The MOS mode is also uniquely suited for broad discovery capabilities. The BoRG-JWST survey, for instance, uses targeted MOS observations to spectroscopically confirm UV-bright $7 < z < 10$ galaxy candidates originally discovered in [[wide-field-camera-3|Hubble/WFC3]] pure-parallel fields [Roberts-Borsani et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...18R/abstract). In addition, early pre-launch predictions that deep NIRSpec MSA observations would contain abundant serendipitous emission-line galaxies have proven correct, with these serendipitous targets potentially providing the largest numbers of $z > 6$ spectroscopic confirmations overall [Maseda et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.486.3290M/abstract). A striking example of this broad discovery potential is the serendipitous discovery of a star-forming galaxy at $z \sim 2.9$ lensed by the galactic bulge of CGCG 012-070 [Riffel et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250706696R/abstract).

**Integral Field Spectroscopy (IFS)**
For dedicated spatially resolved observations, NIRSpec offers an [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] mode utilizing a $3^{\prime\prime} \times 3^{\prime\prime}$ Integral Field Unit (IFU) [Giardino et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ASPC..507..305G/abstract). This mode provides spatially resolved spectroscopy at $\sim 0.1^{\prime\prime}$ scales, which is critical for tracing gas kinematics, outflow morphologies, and metallicity gradients in both local and high-redshift targets [Perna et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...679A..89P/abstract).

**Fixed Slit (FS) Mode**
Finally, NIRSpec includes a set of fixed slits designed for high-contrast single-object spectroscopy [Giardino et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ASPC..507..305G/abstract). This mode utilizes a set of high-contrast fixed slits for single-object spectroscopy.

## In-Flight Performance and Data Calibration

Prior to the launch of the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]], the expected performance of NIRSpec was heavily characterized through cryogenic ground testing and simulated mock catalogs. Early estimates predicted that deep exposures ($\sim 100$ ks) with the low-resolution prism would achieve signal-to-noise ratios greater than 3 for optical emission lines in galaxies at $z \gtrsim 6$ down to $m_{\text{AB}} \approx 30$ [Chevallard et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483.2621C/abstract). 

Upon deployment, in-flight performance validation revealed that NIRSpec's capabilities systematically exceed these pre-launch estimates, particularly regarding its [[spectral-resolution|spectral resolution]], $R \equiv \lambda / \Delta\lambda$. Precise on-orbit calibrations using the narrow H and He emission lines of the planetary nebula SMP LMC 58 demonstrated that the in-flight spectral resolution exceeds the values published in the initial user documentation by 11-53% in Fixed Slit mode and 1-24% in [[integral-field-spectroscopy|IFU mode]] [Shajib et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...702L..12S/abstract), [Shajib et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250703746S/abstract).

Despite the superb overall sensitivity and resolution, the in-flight calibration of the IFU mode identified a prominent systematic artifact. The point-spread function (PSF) of the NIRSpec IFU is spatially undersampled, which introduces low-frequency resampling noise—commonly referred to as "wiggles"—when the detector pixels are interpolated into a 3D data cube [Dumont et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...703A..54D/abstract). Because the default data reduction pipeline does not automatically correct these artifacts, uncorrected wiggles can severely bias the measurement of line equivalent widths and kinematic properties at the single-spaxel level. In affected regions, errors in the line-of-sight velocity and velocity dispersion can be $\sim 17$ and $\sim 36$ times larger than statistical uncertainties, respectively [Dumont et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250309697D/abstract). 

To mitigate this, community-driven empirical correction tools have been developed. [[astronomical-software|Software]] such as WICKED (WIggle Corrector Kit for NIRSpEc Data) employs a Fast Fourier Transform algorithm to identify affected spaxels and empirically removes the artifacts using structural templates and low-degree polynomials. This methodology recovers the true continuum shape up to 3.5 times better than uncorrected data and yields line-of-sight velocity measurements that are over 100 times more accurate at high signal-to-noise ratios ($S/N \sim 40$) [Dumont et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...703A..54D/abstract), [Dumont et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250309697D/abstract).

## The High-Redshift Frontier: First Light and Reionization

The deployment of NIRSpec has fundamentally transformed [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]], pushing the spectroscopic frontier into the first few hundred million years of cosmic time. Prior to JWST, the identification of the earliest galaxies relied heavily on photometric "dropout" techniques, which carried a significant risk of low-redshift interlopers and redshift overprediction. Deep spectroscopic surveys utilizing NIRSpec have transitioned this field to robust [[spectroscopy-surveys|spectroscopic ground truth]]. 

Major [[deep-field-campaigns|Deep field campaigns]], such as the JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey (JADES) and the Cosmic Evolution Early Release Science (CEERS) survey, have successfully targeted thousands of faint sources. JADES, for instance, utilizes approximately 770 hours of Cycle 1 observation time to study galaxy evolution in the GOODS-S and GOODS-N fields [Eisenstein et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230602465E/abstract). The survey includes an extensive imaging component covering nearly $200\ \text{arcmin}^2$ with nine filters, facilitating robust target selection for subsequent NIRSpec follow-up [Rieke et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020IAUS..352..337R/abstract). Through this approach, JADES has yielded robust rest-UV luminosity functions at $z=6-9$ [Curtis-Lake et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251001033C/abstract), while CEERS has spectroscopically confirmed dozens of systems at $6 < z < 10$ [Finkelstein et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250104085F/abstract), [Finkelstein et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983L...4F/abstract). In parallel, the UNCOVER Treasury survey leverages the NIRSpec PRISM to follow up approximately 500 galaxies in the Abell 2744 cluster field, using strong gravitational lensing to probe the pre-reionization era [Bezanson et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv221204026B/abstract), [Bezanson et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...974...92B/abstract). Additionally, the PEARLS project utilizes NIRSpec for high-redshift quasar spectroscopy as a critical component of its multi-mode survey strategy [Windhorst et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165...13W/abstract). In the most distant regimes ($z > 11$), ultra-deep NIRSpec PRISM observations of targets like JADES-GS-z11-0 have revealed that photometric redshift overpredictions are often caused by large neutral hydrogen fractions and Damped Lyman-alpha (DLA) absorption wings, which modify the Lyman-alpha break shape [Hainline et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240404325H/abstract).

Beyond simple redshift confirmation, NIRSpec provides unprecedented access to the physical conditions of the early interstellar medium. [[high-resolution-spectroscopy|High-resolution spectroscopy]] of the gravitationally lensed galaxy MACS0647-JD yielded a direct metallicity measurement at $z=10.167$, finding a surprisingly enriched environment of $0.13\,Z_{\odot}$ [Hsiao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240416200H/abstract). Furthermore, the resolution of the [O II] $\lambda\lambda3726,3729$ doublet in this system indicates an exceptionally high electron density of $\log(n_e/\text{cm}^{-3}) \approx 2.9$. This aligns with a broader observed trend where electron densities scale as $n_e \propto (1+z)^{1.2}$, vastly exceeding typical values found in local galaxies [Abdurro'uf et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240416201A/abstract).

NIRSpec has also provided critical insights into the epoch of reionization through the study of Lyman-alpha emitters. The detection of Lyman-alpha emission at $z > 7$ indicates diverse and complex scenarios for the escape of ionizing photons. While some highly ionized, metal-poor galaxies appear capable of ionizing their own large bubbles ($>1$ physical Mpc), others lack the intrinsic ionizing photon production required to clear their surroundings, implying that external sources must contribute to the local reionization topology [Jung et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...967...73J/abstract).

Finally, NIRSpec observations have forced a paradigm shift regarding the assembly and quenching of early galaxies. Standard theoretical models predicted that massive quiescent galaxies would primarily form and quench after Cosmic Noon. However, NIRSpec has spectroscopically confirmed a population of massive quiescent galaxies ($M_* > 10^{10} M_{\odot}$) already in place at $z \sim 3-4$, necessitating extremely efficient early baryon-to-star conversion rates [Nanayakkara et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024NatSR..14.3724N/abstract). At even higher redshifts, researchers have identified "dormant" or "mini-quenched" galaxies at $5 < z < 7.4$ characterized by strong Balmer breaks and low H$\alpha$ equivalent widths [Covelo-Paz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250622540C/abstract), with post-starburst systems confirmed as early as $z=9.625$ [Messa et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250718705M/abstract). These discoveries suggest that early galaxy evolution may be punctuated by episodic or "breathing" modes of star formation driven by rapid internal feedback.

## Galaxy Assembly and Evolution at Cosmic Noon

The epoch of "cosmic noon" ($1 \lesssim z \lesssim 3$) represents the peak of cosmic star formation and supermassive black hole accretion. For galaxies in this redshift regime, NIRSpec provides unparalleled access to rest-frame optical emission and absorption features across the 1-5 $\mu$m window. [[deep-spectroscopic-surveys|Deep spectroscopic surveys]] such as the Blue Jay survey use multiplexed microshutter observations to build representative samples of galaxies spanning $1.7 < z < 3.5$ down to [[stellar-mass-function|stellar masses]] of $M_* \sim 10^{8.7} M_\odot$, allowing precise reconstructions of their star formation histories [Belli et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251011775B/abstract). Similarly, the SMILES survey leverages medium-resolution grating spectroscopy to systematically track multi-phase outflows and ionizing properties during this peak epoch of galaxy assembly [Zhu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250812599Z/abstract).

NIRSpec has advanced our understanding of morphological evolution by mapping resolved kinematics and chemical abundances at kiloparsec scales. Utilizing the customized slit-stepping technique, the MSA-3D program has generated spatially resolved data cubes for normal star-forming galaxies at $0.5 \lesssim z \lesssim 1.5$ [Barišić et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240808350B/abstract), [Barišić et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983..139B/abstract). Observations of individual $z \sim 1.1$ systems reveal rotationally supported disks with moderate velocity dispersions (e.g., $\sigma = 36^{+5}_{-4}$ km s$^{-1}$) and negative radial metallicity gradients ($-0.020 \pm 0.002$ dex kpc$^{-1}$) that closely resemble the properties of local spiral galaxies [Barišić et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240808350B/abstract), [Barišić et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983..139B/abstract). These findings indicate that mature, thin-disk architectures were already firmly in place by $z \sim 1$.

While many galaxies at cosmic noon lie on the star-forming main sequence, the spectroscopic confirmation of early massive quiescent systems (as mentioned above) challenges standard hierarchical assembly models. The existence of such old, massive systems requires a much higher early baryon-to-star conversion efficiency than is typically assumed in theoretical models [Nanayakkara et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024NatSR..14.3724N/abstract).

## AGN Feedback and Supermassive Black Hole Co-evolution

The advent of JWST has transformed the study of supermassive black hole (SMBH) and host galaxy co-evolution. Through [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]], NIRSpec enables spatially resolved investigations of both local and high-redshift nuclear environments. In the local Universe, NIRSpec IFU observations are being utilized to map the stellar kinematics and line-of-sight velocity distributions in anchor systems like M87. By resolving the central 240 parsecs of M87, these precise kinematic measurements aim to resolve long-standing discrepancies in SMBH mass estimates [Al-Amri et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251002439A/abstract). 

At the opposite end of cosmic history, the Galaxy Assembly with NIRSpec IFS (GA-NIFS) program aims to trace star formation, kinematics, and metallicity gradients across over 40 high-redshift galaxies and AGNs using 273 hours of allocated observation time [Perna et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023IAUS..373..268P/abstract), [Perna et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023IAUS..373...60P/abstract). As part of this comprehensive program, researchers measured rest-frame optical lines in $z \simeq 6.8$ quasars, yielding accurate black hole masses of $M_{\text{BH}} \approx 1.85 \times 10^9\ M_\odot$ to $2.9 \times 10^9\ M_\odot$ with Eddington ratios of $\lambda_{\text{Edd}} \sim 0.4-0.8$ [Marshall et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A.191M/abstract). Crucially, these early SMBHs appear to lie above the local $M_{\text{BH}}$-$M_{\text{host}}$ scaling relations, suggesting that black hole growth may outpace the assembly of the host galaxy's stellar mass at high redshift [Marshall et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A.191M/abstract).

A central pillar of galaxy evolution models is quasar feedback, wherein AGN-driven winds expel gas and suppress star formation. NIRSpec observations have provided robust quantitative evidence of this process by mapping ubiquitous ionized outflows. The efficiency of this feedback is often parameterized by the mass loading factor, defined as $\eta = \dot{M}_{\text{out}} / \text{SFR}$. In several $z \sim 6.8$ systems, mass outflow rates ($\dot{M}_{\text{out}}$) reach up to $525\ M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$, dwarfing the host star formation rates ($\text{SFR} < 54\ M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$) and yielding mass loading factors $\eta \gg 1$ [Marshall et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A.191M/abstract). Spatially resolved studies of cosmic noon targets confirm similar extremes; for instance, the $z \sim 3.5$ galaxy GS5001 hosts an outflow of $23 \pm 5 M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ [Lamperti et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.153L/abstract), while the complex $z \sim 3.3$ quasar environment LBQS 0302-0019 drives outflows up to $\sim 10^4 M_\odot$ yr$^{-1}$ [Perna et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...679A..89P/abstract). The physical mechanisms driving these outflows can be complex; observations of the $z=1.59$ obscured quasar XID2028 resolved an expanding bubble formed by the interaction between a low-luminosity radio jet and the interstellar medium [Cresci et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...672A.128C/abstract). Similar self-regulating feeding and feedback cycles have also been traced in local Seyfert galaxies, such as NGC 7469 [Bianchin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...965..103B/abstract).

The unprecedented [[high-angular-resolution-astronomy|spatial resolution]] and sensitivity of NIRSpec have further clarified the role of galaxy mergers in triggering AGN activity. Deep integral-field observations of systems like LBQS 0302−0019 have unveiled multi-component interacting systems hosting massive rotating disks, powerful outflows, and obscured companion AGNs at projected separations of $\sim 20$ kpc [Perna et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...679A..89P/abstract). Extrapolating across the broader GA-NIFS sample, researchers have identified a surprisingly high fraction of dual AGN systems. Specifically, multiple and dual AGNs were found in 20-30% of surveyed AGN systems at $z \sim 3$ [Perna et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..59P/abstract). This result more than doubles the previously known count of dual AGNs at $z > 3$. While standard cosmological simulations predict a $\sim 10\%$ dual AGN fraction for these epochs, the observed prevalence moderately exceeds these predictions, suggesting that current theoretical models may underestimate the frequency of merger-driven SMBH accretion in the early Universe [Perna et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..59P/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While the NIRSpec instrument has resolved many long-standing questions regarding the high-redshift universe, its extraordinary sensitivity has introduced a host of new astrophysical puzzles. 

A primary challenge lies in interpreting the unexpectedly rapid assembly and quenching of early galaxies. Standard galaxy formation models struggle to account for the growing number of massive, quiescent galaxies observed at $z > 3$ [Nanayakkara et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024NatSR..14.3724N/abstract). The discovery of intermediate-mass "dormant" galaxies at $z = 5-7.4$ and extreme mini-quenched post-starburst systems as early as $z = 9.625$ suggests that early galaxy assembly may be driven by episodic, "breathing" modes of star formation [Covelo-Paz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250622540C/abstract), [Messa et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250718705M/abstract). The precise feedback mechanisms that trigger and maintain these dormant phases remain a major open question.

Another ongoing tension involves the physical conditions of the interstellar medium during the Epoch of Reionization. NIRSpec observations show that early galaxies exhibit highly elevated electron densities compared to local counterparts, reaching $n_e \sim 1000$ cm$^{-3}$ at $z \sim 10$ [Abdurro'uf et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240416201A/abstract). The physical mechanisms driving these extreme densities and concurrent rapid metal enrichment are actively debated [Hsiao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240416200H/abstract). Additionally, observations of Lyman-$\alpha$ emitters at $z > 7$ suggest diverse scenarios for the escape of ionizing photons, indicating that external sources may be required to facilitate Lyman-$\alpha$ escape from bubbles smaller than 1 Mpc [Jung et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...967...73J/abstract). Constraining the faint-end luminosity function with deep NIRSpec surveys is therefore critical to balancing the reionization photon budget [Bunker et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020IAUS..352..342B/abstract), [Curtis-Lake et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251001033C/abstract). Furthermore, these deep spectra are continuously scrutinized for He II emission lines, which would provide the first definitive evidence of pristine Population III stars [Bunker et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020IAUS..352..342B/abstract).

In the domain of active galactic nuclei, [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] has uncovered a remarkably high fraction of dual AGNs (20-30%) among surveyed AGN systems at $z \sim 3$, which moderately exceeds the $\sim 10\%$ occurrence rate predicted by cosmological simulations [Perna et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..59P/abstract). Understanding whether this discrepancy points to selection effects or a fundamental misunderstanding of early supermassive black hole fueling is a priority for future studies.

Addressing these questions will increasingly rely on multi-wavelength synergy. Because key rest-frame optical diagnostic lines (such as H$\alpha$) shift beyond NIRSpec's $5.3\ \mu\text{m}$ cutoff at $z > 7$, complete characterization of the earliest galaxies necessitates follow-up with the [[miri-instrument|MIRI (instrument)]] [Hsiao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240416200H/abstract). Simultaneously, combining NIRSpec's ionized gas and stellar kinematics with submillimeter data from the [[atacama-large-millimeter-array-alma|Atacama Large Millimeter Array (ALMA)]] is essential for mapping cold molecular gas, dust attenuation, and far-infrared [C II] emission (e.g., [Rigby et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv231210465R/abstract), [Rigby et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...978..108R/abstract)) and [O III] emission (e.g., [Ren et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.4722R/abstract)) in both star-bursting galaxies and AGN hosts. This coordinated approach will be vital for constructing a cohesive picture of galaxy evolution from the first light to Cosmic Noon.
