## Article

**Spatially extended outflows** are large-scale ejections of multiphase gas and plasma propelled from the [[nuclear-regions-of-galaxies|Nuclear Regions of Galaxies]] out into the circumgalactic medium, frequently reaching distances of $10$ to $30$ kiloparsecs [Shaban et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...936...77S/abstract), [Pessa et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240816067P/abstract). As a crucial mechanism of baryon cycling, extended outflows transport mass, metals, and kinetic energy out of the interstellar medium. Depending on the depth of the host galaxy's gravitational potential, this ejected material may permanently escape into the intergalactic medium—particularly in dwarf galaxies—or eventually stall and recycle back onto the galactic disk via [[galactic-fountains|Galactic fountains]] [Arribas et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..14A/abstract), [Roberts-Borsani et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.493.3081R/abstract).

These macroscopic flows are fundamentally driven by highly energetic processes within the host galaxy. The primary engines are stellar feedback from concentrated supernovae—often manifesting as [[superwinds|Superwinds]]—and the tremendous radiative and mechanical power injected by [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] [Heckman et al. (1993)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1993ASSL..188..455H/abstract), [Harrison et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.3306H/abstract). Extended outflows are inherently a [[multiphase-wind|Multiphase wind]] phenomenon. While historical models relied heavily on observations of the warm ionized gas, it is now established that outflows simultaneously entrain hot [[diffuse-x-ray-emitting-gas|Diffuse X-ray emitting gas]], neutral atomic material, and vast quantities of cold molecular gas [Veilleux et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...776...27V/abstract), [Veilleux et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&ARv..28....2V/abstract). In fact, massive [[molecular-outflows|Molecular outflows]] frequently dominate the total mass and momentum budgets of these events, leading to total multiphase mass-loading factors that routinely exceed unity.

Within the broader framework of [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]], spatially extended outflows are widely theorized to regulate galaxy evolution by suppressing—or "quenching"—star formation [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract). Yet, this paradigm of strictly negative feedback is increasingly challenged. Observations indicate that outflows can act as a double-edged sword, shock-compressing ambient [[molecular-clouds-and-gas-clumping|Molecular Clouds and Gas Clumping]] to trigger "positive feedback," or even directly hosting *in situ* star formation within the high-velocity outflowing material itself [Maiolino et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017Natur.544..202M/abstract), [Gallagher et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.485.3409G/abstract). Accurately characterizing the morphology and impact of these winds remains an observational challenge; recent literature cautions that atmospheric "beam smearing" in ground-based data can artificially inflate the apparent size and kinetic power of what may actually be compact nuclear ejections [Holden et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.536.1857H/abstract). Recent advances in [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|Resolved spectroscopic observations]] have become essential for distinguishing these compact components from large-scale flows.

## Driving Mechanisms: AGN vs. Stellar Feedback

Spatially extended outflows are primarily powered by two distinct physical mechanisms: stellar feedback associated with intense star formation, and the energy injected by [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]]. While both mechanisms are capable of driving a [[multiphase-wind|Multiphase wind]], they exhibit different kinematics, energetics, and scaling relations.

**Supernova Driving and Stellar Feedback**
In non-AGN star-forming galaxies, extended outflows—often termed [[superwinds|Superwinds]]—are driven by the thermal and mechanical energy of concentrated supernova explosions and stellar winds [Heckman et al. (1993)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1993ASSL..188..455H/abstract). The initiation of these starburst-driven winds typically requires a threshold star formation rate (SFR) surface density of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{SFR}} \gtrsim 0.01 \mathrm{\, M_\odot \, yr^{-1} \, kpc^{-2}}$ to overcome the gravitational potential of the galactic disk and launch into the halo [Roberts-Borsani et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.493.3081R/abstract). 

The kinematics of stellar feedback-driven outflows follow tight scaling relations, with the maximum outflow velocity scaling approximately as $v \propto \mathrm{SFR}^{0.3}$ [Weiner et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...692..187W/abstract), [Heckman et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...809..147H/abstract). Velocities in pure starbursts generally remain below $500 \mathrm{\, km \, s^{-1}}$, which is often insufficient for the gas to escape the dark matter halos of massive galaxies, resulting in gas recycling via a [[galactic-fountains|galactic fountain]] [Arribas et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..14A/abstract). The efficiency of supernova thermal heating is estimated to be approximately $16\%$ in starburst dwarfs [McQuinn et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.477.3164M/abstract). To explain the observed kinematics of the cold and warm gas phases, theoretical frameworks frequently invoked momentum-driven winds, where ambient interstellar clouds are swept up and accelerated by the combined ram pressure of hot supernova ejecta and radiation pressure from young stars [Hong et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010arXiv1008.4242H/abstract). However, recent [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|Resolved spectroscopic observations]] often reject momentum-driven predictions, finding results more consistent with energy-driven models instead [Reichardt Chu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.511.5782R/abstract). Additionally, pre-existing cosmic ray halos can suppress large-scale winds and redirect feedback toward local recycling flows. Consequently, slow outflows are more likely in young galaxies because they lack these extended CR halos [Owen et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250916524O/abstract).

**AGN Feedback: Radiation Pressure and Radio Jets**
Outflows driven by AGN reach significantly higher velocities, routinely exceeding $1000 - 2000 \mathrm{\, km \, s^{-1}}$ on kiloparsec scales [Harrison et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.3306H/abstract). The overall fraction of AGNs displaying outflows is fairly constant at approximately 60% across many orders of magnitude in luminosity [Ganguly et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...672..102G/abstract). The kinetic power of these AGN-driven winds typically ranges from $0.1\%$ to $5\%$ of the bolometric luminosity ($L_{\mathrm{bol}}$). Mass loading factors ($\eta = \dot{M}_{\mathrm{out}} / \mathrm{SFR}$) generally range from $0.1$ to $1$, but can exceed unity in highly luminous quasars [Leung et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...886...11L/abstract). Notably, Active galactic nuclei are increasingly recognized as dominant sources of feedback in low-mass dwarf galaxies as well, with outflows often exceeding the host's escape velocity [Manzano-King et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...884...54M/abstract).

The acceleration mechanism for the massive, cold molecular phase in these outflows remains a subject of active debate. One leading mechanism is [[radiation-driven-winds|Radiation-driven winds]], where AGN radiation pressure acts directly on dust grains in a momentum-driven regime , [Pereira-Santaella et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...616A.171P/abstract). Furthermore, a statistical analysis of 122 robust ultra-fast outflow detections shows that line width and velocity are positively correlated, suggesting clumpy, multi-component winds launched from radii as small as the innermost stable circular orbit [Laurenti et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251206077L/abstract). As these inner winds propagate outward, observations using clumpy ISM models reveal that AGN winds exhibit rapid acceleration specifically starting at $\sim 1$ kpc from the nucleus, consistent with a transition from momentum-driven to energy-conserving phases [Marconcini et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025NatAs...9..907M/abstract). Alternatively, even in "radio-quiet" AGN, small-scale or low-power [[relativistic-jets|radio jets]] can strongly couple with the interstellar medium. These jet-ISM interactions inflate [[over-pressured-jets|over-pressured cocoons]] that drive lateral shocks, creating intense turbulence perpendicular to the jet axis and accelerating multiphase gas to high velocities [Tadhunter et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014Natur.511..440T/abstract), [Venturi et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...648A..17V/abstract). In support of this, statistics from the MaNGA survey demonstrate that radio-detected AGNs exhibit an outflow detection rate of approximately 56%, more than double that of their radio-undetected counterparts [Nandi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250306719N/abstract). Disentangling the relative contributions of radiation pressure and low-power jets in these systems remains an observational challenge.

## The Multiphase Nature of Outflowing Material

Spatially extended outflows are inherently multiphase structures, comprising a complex mixture of hot plasma traced by [[diffuse-x-ray-emitting-gas|Diffuse X-ray emitting gas]] ($T \sim 10^6 - 10^8$ K), warm ionized gas ($T \sim 10^4$ K), neutral atomic gas, and cold molecular material ($T < 100$ K). Historically, feedback models relied heavily on observations of the warm ionized phase using optical and ultraviolet spectroscopy. However, comprehensive multi-wavelength campaigns have revealed that this component often constitutes only a minor fraction ($<10\%$) of the total outflowing mass [Fluetsch et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483.4586F/abstract). For instance, while calculations of the [[ionised-mass-outflow-rate|Ionised Mass Outflow Rate]] typically yield mass loading factors of $\eta < 1$, the total mass loading factor—defined as the ratio of the total mass outflow rate to the star formation rate,
$$ \eta = \frac{\dot{M}_{\rm out}}{\mathrm{SFR}} $$
—can easily exceed 10 in a complete [[multiphase-wind|Multiphase wind]] accounting [Arribas et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..14A/abstract), [Baron et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509.4457B/abstract).

The cold and neutral components heavily dominate the mass and momentum budgets of these outflows. Observations utilizing ALMA and Herschel have detected widespread [[molecular-outflows|Molecular outflows]] traced by CO, HCN, and OH transitions, confirming the presence of cold gas moving at velocities of $1000-2000$ km/s [Veilleux et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...776...27V/abstract), [Pereira-Santaella et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...616A.171P/abstract). Additionally, high HCN/HCO+ line ratios frequently observed in these outflows likely result from high-temperature shock chemistry within the outflowing gas [Nishimura et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...686A..48N/abstract). Theoretical work indicates that the survivability of molecular gas in these outflows is governed by the generalized Eddington ratio and the dissociation parameter, with strong starbursts more likely to host molecular-dominated winds [Vijayan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.4132V/abstract). Similarly, neutral atomic gas, frequently traced by Na D or Mg II absorption, can be up to 100 times more massive than the co-spatial ionized phase [Baron et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509.4457B/abstract). In particular, atomic HI absorption provides a unique probe of AGN-driven outflows down to parsec scales, revealing [[gas-clumping|clumpy structures]] that align with numerical predictions for young radio jet interactions [Morganti et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241206988M/abstract). Interactions between these massive molecular outflows and the interstellar medium can also accelerate cosmic rays, resulting in detectable high-energy gamma-ray emission [McDaniel et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...943..168M/abstract); for instance, gamma-ray emission in [[narrow-line-regions-and-seyferts|Seyfert galaxies]] such as NGC 4151 has been specifically attributed to particle acceleration at the termination shocks of ultra-fast outflows [Peretti et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230303298P/abstract). Remarkably, these cold components are not confined to the galactic nucleus; they can extend $10-30$ kpc into the circumgalactic medium in powerful starbursts and [[luminous-intermediate-redshift-quasars|luminous quasars]] [Veilleux et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&ARv..28....2V/abstract).

The physical origin and acceleration mechanism of this fast-moving cold gas remain a critical unsolved problem in galaxy feedback theory. The literature reflects an active, unresolved debate over whether pre-existing molecular clouds from the interstellar medium are entrained and accelerated by the hot wind, or whether the cold material forms *in situ*. The entrainment scenario struggles to explain how fragile clouds survive the hydrodynamic instabilities and shocks inherent in a hot, high-velocity flow over distances of $10+$ kpc without being entirely shredded. 

Consequently, theoretical models increasingly favor *in situ* formation via a phase transition driven by the rapid radiative cooling of the hot wind or its mixed interface layers [Costa et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448L..30C/abstract), [Schneider et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...862...56S/abstract). In this picture, as the hot fluid expands, thermal instabilities cause the gas to catastrophically cool, condensing into dense, high-velocity cold clumps (a process heavily influenced by [[gas-clumping|Gas clumping]]). Observations of charge-exchange X-ray emission suggest that these clumps can be continually ablated by the surrounding hot flow, undergoing cycles of destruction and reformation as they are advected outward into the circumgalactic environment [Wu et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.491.5621W/abstract).

## Observational Methodologies and Resolving Techniques

Characterizing spatially extended outflows requires resolving the complex kinematics of multiple gas phases across sub-kiloparsec to circumgalactic scales. Modern observational methodologies rely on a combination of high-resolution [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|Resolved spectroscopic observations]] and interferometry to disentangle outflowing material from underlying galactic dynamics and to accurately quantify mass and energy budgets.

**Integral Field Spectroscopy and Infrared Imaging**
Optical and near-infrared integral field spectroscopy (IFS) has revolutionized the study of ionized and neutral gas kinematics by providing simultaneous spatial and spectral information. Instruments such as the Multi Unit Spectroscopic Explorer (MUSE) on the Very Large Telescope (VLT) allow for 3D mapping of prominent emission lines like [O III] $\lambda5007$ and H$\alpha$. For nearby [[narrow-line-regions-and-seyferts|Narrow-Line Regions and Seyferts]], surveys like MAGNUM utilize MUSE to achieve spatial resolutions down to $\sim 10$ pc. This high-resolution mapping successfully disentangles biconical outflows from gravitational disk motions and identifies shock-ionized regions perpendicular to the axes of [[relativistic-jets|radio jets]] [Venturi et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021IAUS..359..212V/abstract), [Venturi et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...648A..17V/abstract), [Marconcini et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...677A..58M/abstract). At higher redshifts, the Keck Cosmic Web Imager (KCWI) is critical for mapping extended Ly$\alpha$ emission and absorption features in the circumgalactic medium, revealing the radial dependence of outflow velocities and mass fluxes [Erb et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...953..118E/abstract). Furthermore, to accurately quantify the mass and energy of these winds, obtaining direct measurements of the gas electron density is crucial. Recent methods successfully use Si II* fine-structure absorption lines to directly measure the gas electron density within starburst outflows . In the infrared, new Fizeau imaging with the Large Binocular Telescope Interferometer (LBTI) allows for sub-10 pc resolution of dusty AGN outflows, bridging the gap between interferometric and single-telescope scales [Isbell et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025NatAs.tmp...15I/abstract). In addition, JWST/NIRSpec has enabled the mapping of near-infrared coronal lines in dwarf galaxies, showing that these highly ionized species trace compact outflows that extend up to 10% of the host galaxy size [Aravindan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251205041A/abstract).

A critical methodological challenge in ground-based IFS is "beam smearing" induced by atmospheric seeing. If uncorrected, the spatial smearing of compact, high-velocity nuclear emission across the field of view can lead to severe overestimates of both the spatial extent and the mass outflow rates of the ionized gas [Holden et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.536.1857H/abstract).

**Millimeter and Submillimeter Interferometry**
Because the cold molecular phase frequently dominates the mass and momentum budgets of galactic winds [Veilleux et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&ARv..28....2V/abstract), high-resolution millimeter/submillimeter observations are essential. The Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array (ALMA) achieves sub-arcsecond spatial resolution, enabling the direct imaging of [[molecular-outflows|Molecular outflows]]. ALMA observations of CO transitions and [[dense-gas-tracers|Dense gas tracers]] like HCN have confirmed that cold, massive outflowing gas can extend out to several kiloparsecs in luminous starbursts and quasars [Pereira-Santaella et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...616A.171P/abstract). Conversely, high-resolution ALMA observations also reveal that some massive molecular outflows driven by young AGNs are highly compact ($r < 120$ pc), highlighting the need for spatial resolution to properly characterize them [Holden et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530..446H/abstract). Complementing these, far-infrared observations using the Herschel Space Observatory have detected widespread OH absorption, further confirming the massive nature of these molecular winds [Veilleux et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...776...27V/abstract). At $z > 4$, high-resolution ALMA observations of OH 119 $\mu$m absorption have also been used to resolve clumpy, $500$-pc-scale substructures within molecular outflows, demonstrating that these winds are ubiquitous in massive, dusty galaxies in the early Universe [Spilker et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...905...85S/abstract).

**Radio Interferometry**
To understand the driving mechanisms of these multiphase winds, particularly the role of small-scale or low-power jets, radio interferometry is widely employed. Facilities such as the Very Large Array (VLA), the Very Long Baseline Array (VLBA), and the NOrthern Extended Millimeter Array (NOEMA) provide critical views of jet-ISM interactions. High-resolution radio continuum maps resolve the morphology of sub-kiloparsec jets and lobes while simultaneously tracing the synchrotron emission from shock fronts driven by expanding radio plasma [Yang et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.482.1701Y/abstract), [Morganti et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv220104157M/abstract). Combining radio interferometry with IFS and ALMA data has demonstrated that even in purportedly "radio-quiet" [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]], compact jets can strongly couple with the interstellar medium, transferring kinetic energy to the gas and driving both massive ionized and molecular outflows [Tadhunter et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014Natur.511..440T/abstract), [Venturi et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...648A..17V/abstract).

## Morphology and Spatial Extent

Spatially extended outflows exhibit a diverse range of physical scales and geometries, fundamentally shaped by their driving mechanisms and the surrounding interstellar medium. In star-forming disk galaxies, outflows predominantly adopt a biconical morphology, expanding outward along the path of least resistance—typically the [[minor-axis-outflows|minor axis]] of the galactic disk [Guo et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Natur.624...53G/abstract), [Marlowe et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...438..563M/abstract). For example, the MAUVE survey highlights NGC 4383 as a nearby baseline for well-resolved 6-kpc bipolar outflows in H I-rich environments, showing complex kinematics at 100 pc resolution [Watts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.1968W/abstract). The geometry of these bipolar flows is often parameterized by their [[opening-angle|Opening angle]], which can vary widely from $\approx 17^{\circ}$ to $60^{\circ}$ depending on the degree of collimation provided by the host galaxy's central gas distribution [McPherson et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.6170M/abstract), [Pessa et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240816067P/abstract). 

The internal spatial structure of these winds is highly heterogeneous. Feedback from concentrated starbursts thermalizes stellar winds and supernovae, inflating hot, over-pressurized superbubbles that expand and eventually break out of the galactic disk [Marlowe et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...438..563M/abstract), [Kim et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...853..173K/abstract). As the hot wind fluid sweeps up and accelerates cooler, denser ambient gas, Rayleigh-Taylor and Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities shred the entrained material. This results in a complex [[multiphase-wind|Multiphase wind]] characterized by a [[small-scale-filamentary-streams|filamentary structure]], which is frequently observed in optical emission lines (e.g., H$\alpha$ and [O III]) and cool absorption tracers like Mg II [Rossa et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008arXiv0804.3819R/abstract), [Wu et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.491.5621W/abstract).

The maximum spatial extent of galactic outflows spans from sub-kiloparsec nuclear regions to tens of kiloparsecs into the circumgalactic medium. Observations of resonant Mg II and Fe II* emission map cool gas halos extending up to $10 - 30$ kpc around both starbursts and AGN host galaxies [Shaban et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...936...77S/abstract), [Pessa et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240816067P/abstract). At high redshifts ($z \sim 2-3$), spatially extended Ly$\alpha$ emission is statistically indistinguishable along different azimuthal axes within $30$ kpc, implying that outflows uniformly dominate the circumgalactic medium in all directions . For broad absorption line (BAL) quasars, statistical analysis of BAL outflows suggests typical launching distances of tens of parsecs; while this is beyond the inner accretion disk, these outflows remain compact relative to galaxy-wide scales [He et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019NatAs...3..265H/abstract). Moreover, kinematic modeling of quasar outflows indicates that acceleration often occurs at distances of 10-100 parsecs, suggesting a launching radius comparable to the dusty torus [He et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022SciA....8.3291H/abstract). Furthermore, "fossil" outflows can continue to coast outwards to large radii long after the central engine has quenched, existing as detached, large-scale shells that persist for tens of megayears [Zubovas et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.4055Z/abstract), [Zubovas et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.4819Z/abstract). 

However, the literature reflects a significant and active tension regarding the true physical extent of some AGN-driven outflows. While many studies report galaxy-wide impacts, high-resolution integral field spectroscopy suggests that atmospheric beam smearing in ground-based observations can artificially inflate the apparent size of unresolved nuclear winds. When carefully corrected for these seeing effects, many seemingly extended outflows are found to be highly compact, confined to the inner $r \lesssim 1$ kpc of the host galaxy [Rose et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.474..128R/abstract), [Holden et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.536.1857H/abstract).

Finally, the long-term spatial distribution of outflowing gas depends on its terminal velocity relative to the host's gravitational potential. While highly energetic [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] or dwarf starbursts can eject gas directly into the intergalactic medium, a substantial fraction of the denser, slower-moving material in massive galaxies remains gravitationally bound. In massive galaxies, the hot circumgalactic medium can also act as a physical barrier that suppresses or even entirely halts galactic outflows at low redshifts [Singh et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.459....2S/abstract). This gas ultimately stalls and recycles back onto the galactic disk, establishing large-scale [[galactic-fountains|Galactic fountains]] that continuously redistribute metals and regulate subsequent star formation [Kim et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...853..173K/abstract), [Husemann et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...627A..53H/abstract).

## Kinematics, Energetics, and Mass Loading

The overall impact of spatially extended outflows on galaxy evolution is quantified through their mass outflow rate ($\dot{M}_{\text{out}}$), their kinetic power ($\dot{E}_{\text{kin}}$), and their dimensionless mass loading factor, defined as $\eta \equiv \dot{M}_{\text{out}}/\text{SFR}$, where $\text{SFR}$ is the host galaxy's star formation rate. Measuring these parameters across different gas phases provides critical constraints on feedback models.

Outflow kinematics are fundamentally tied to their driving mechanisms. Outflows driven by star formation, frequently observed as [[superwinds|Superwinds]], typically exhibit velocities of $100-500 \text{ km s}^{-1}$ and follow empirical scaling relations where the outflow velocity depends on the star formation rate, $v_{\text{out}} \propto \text{SFR}^{0.3}$ [Weiner et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...692..187W/abstract), [Heckman et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...809..147H/abstract). Notably, even the most extreme starburst-driven outflows follow these same scaling relations, suggesting a single physical driving mechanism operates across all starburst intensities . These scaling relations between outflow velocity and host star formation rate also persist when probed via background quasar absorption lines, pointing toward a universal wind formalism [Schroetter et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...687A..39S/abstract). A threshold SFR surface density of $\Sigma_{\text{SFR}} \gtrsim 0.01 \, M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1} \text{ kpc}^{-2}$ is generally required to launch these winds into the halo [Roberts-Borsani et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.493.3081R/abstract). At higher redshifts, JWST observations of [OIII] emitters at $z=2.5-9$ show mass-loading factors exceeding unity for low-mass galaxies, mirroring local starburst properties but with higher median outflow velocities of $\sim 530 \mathrm{\, km \, s^{-1}}$ [Cooper et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250218310C/abstract). In contrast, outflows powered by [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] reach significantly higher maximum velocities, commonly exceeding $1000-2000 \text{ km s}^{-1}$. These AGN-driven events also exhibit extreme velocity dispersion, tracing highly turbulent and shock-heated gas in the interstellar medium [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract), [Harrison et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.3306H/abstract). Recently, the first characterizations of highly ionized AGN outflows using XRISM/Resolve have identified multi-component winds, including a broad Fe XXVI feature moving at $0.05c$ [Mehdipour et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...699A.228M/abstract).

When calculating the total mass loading factor, a multiphase accounting is essential. Relying solely on the [[ionised-mass-outflow-rate|Ionised Mass Outflow Rate]] typically yields an $\eta \lesssim 1$ [Arribas et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..14A/abstract). However, the neutral atomic and cold molecular phases—often manifesting as massive [[molecular-outflows|Molecular outflows]]—dominate the total mass and momentum budgets. When these cold phases are included, the multiphase mass loading factor can exceed 10 in luminous quasars and starbursts [Pereira-Santaella et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...616A.171P/abstract), [Baron et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509.4457B/abstract). In the most powerful AGN-driven events, the total mass outflow rate can reach up to $\sim 4000 \, M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ on kiloparsec scales [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract), vastly exceeding the galaxy's concurrent SFR.

In the context of [[galactic-outflows-and-wind-energetics|Galactic Outflows and Wind Energetics]], the physical driving mechanism of large-scale AGN outflows is commonly modeled in an energy-driven regime. In this limit, a fast inner wind shocks against the interstellar medium, transferring thermal energy that drives a large-scale, adiabatic expansion. Such models predict that $\sim 0.1-5\%$ of the AGN bolometric luminosity ($L_{\text{bol}}$) is converted into the kinetic power of the wind [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract), [Harrison et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.3306H/abstract). Conversely, momentum-driven models are frequently invoked for pure starbursts, which successfully reproduce high-redshift mass-metallicity scaling relations and circumgalactic absorption kinematics [Finlator et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.385.2181F/abstract).

Despite the ubiquity of these energetic winds, their true quenching efficiency remains an active subject of debate. While theoretical frameworks require widespread gas clearing to suppress star formation, several observational studies suggest that many AGN-driven outflows are confined to sub-kiloparsec scales and do not globally disrupt the host galaxy's disk [Zubovas et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.4055Z/abstract), [Rose et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.474..128R/abstract). Furthermore, measurements of mass outflow rates and kinetic powers are highly sensitive to the assumed spatial extent of the emission. Recent work cautions that "beam smearing" from atmospheric seeing in ground-based observations can artificially inflate the apparent radial extent of compact nuclear outflows, potentially leading to order-of-magnitude overestimates of their large-scale energy and mass loading [Holden et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.536.1857H/abstract).

## Impact on Galaxy Evolution and Star Formation Quenching

Spatially extended outflows are widely invoked as a fundamental mechanism of [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]]. By ejecting or heating a galaxy's interstellar medium (ISM), these winds are thought to suppress future star formation—a process termed negative feedback. In systems driven by [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]], large-scale outflows reach typical velocities of $v \sim 1000-2000\ \mathrm{km\ s}^{-1}$ and carry kinetic powers estimated at $\dot{E}_{\mathrm{kin}} \approx 0.001 - 0.05 L_{\mathrm{bol}}$. This is theoretically sufficient to unbind the host galaxy's gas on dynamical timescales and drive it off the star-forming main sequence, creating a "red and dead" galaxy [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract), [Harrison et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.3306H/abstract). In starburst-dominated systems, [[superwinds|Superwinds]] are preferentially detected in galaxies situated above the main sequence, suggesting that outflows provide a self-regulating mechanism that bounds the upper envelope of star formation [Cicone et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016A&A...588A..41C/abstract). From a cosmological perspective, the global outflow budget is dominated by star formation at low masses and jet heating at high masses, with radiation-driven winds never becoming the dominant process across cosmic history [Buchner et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689L...2B/abstract). At the peak epoch of star formation ($z \sim 2-3$), it is estimated that nearly half of the heavy element yield from star formation is ejected via low-ionization outflows .

Despite this theoretical paradigm, the literature remains deeply conflicted regarding the immediate efficiency of outflow-induced quenching. Several high-resolution observational studies caution that the spatial extent and mass outflow rates of many AGN winds have been substantially overestimated due to "beam smearing" in ground-based data. These studies find that energetic outflows are often confined to sub-kiloparsec scales rather than being genuinely galaxy-wide, casting doubt on their ability to globally quench star formation [Karouzos et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...833..171K/abstract), [Holden et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.536.1857H/abstract). Furthermore, high-resolution simulations indicate that while thermal AGN feedback efficiently drives hot, diffuse winds, it struggles to disrupt the dense molecular gas embedded in the galactic disk. Consequently, outflows may not cause immediate global quenching, but rather slowly deplete the gas reservoir over long ($\gtrsim 1\ \mathrm{Gyr}$) timescales [Gabor et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441.1615G/abstract). In moderately gas-rich disks, this localized depletion of the central gas reservoir by expanding winds is predicted to manifest as "inside-out quenching" [Zubovas et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.4055Z/abstract). Indeed, outflows in recently quenched galaxies at $z=4$ and $z=7$ suggest that extreme mass-loading factors ($>10$) may be necessary to explain rapid transitions to quiescence in the early universe [Valentino et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...699A.358V/abstract). This is corroborated by observations showing rapid star formation quenching can be facilitated via massive neutral gas ejection [Belli et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.630...54B/abstract). Additionally, the ultimate fate of the outflowing gas depends heavily on the host's gravitational potential: while dwarf galaxies can suffer severe gas depletion as material escapes into the intergalactic medium, massive galaxies tend to retain their ejected gas, which is later recycled back into the disk via [[galactic-fountains|Galactic fountains]] [Arribas et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..14A/abstract).

In stark contrast to pure quenching models, there is growing consensus that outflows can act as a double-edged sword by inducing "positive feedback." Standard textbook theory assumes that star formation is strictly confined to dense [[molecular-clouds-and-gas-clumping|Molecular Clouds and Gas Clumping]] within a quiescent galactic disk. However, as a [[multiphase-wind|Multiphase wind]] shock-compresses ambient gas at its leading edges, it can trigger new bursts of star formation—a phenomenon even observed in dwarf galaxies harboring massive black holes [Schutte et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022Natur.601..329S/abstract). More radically, recent [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|Resolved spectroscopic observations]] reveal that stars can form directly *inside* the outflowing material itself. These newly formed stars inherit the extreme radial velocities of the wind, with [[spatially-extended-star-formation|in-situ star formation]] rates exceeding $15\ M_{\odot}\ \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ directly detected in local galactic outflows [Maiolino et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017Natur.544..202M/abstract). This widespread star formation inside outflows represents a newly recognized mode of galaxy assembly, potentially contributing substantially to the early formation of galactic spheroids and chemically enriching the circumgalactic medium as these high-velocity stars eventually undergo supernova explosions on extended orbits [Gallagher et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.485.3409G/abstract).

## Fossil Outflows and Episodic Activity

Galactic outflows are traditionally modeled and observed concurrently with the central engine that drives them. However, owing to the highly variable nature of accretion onto supermassive black holes—often referred to as AGN flickering—and [[episodic-jet-activity|Episodic jet activity]], the injection of energy and momentum is not continuous. When the central active galactic nucleus (AGN) dims or switches off entirely, the previously launched outflow does not immediately arrest. Instead, it coasts outward due to its inertia, persisting in the circumgalactic medium as a "fossil" or relic outflow [Zubovas et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.4055Z/abstract).

During the active phase, an AGN can inject kinetic luminosities of $\sim 0.05 L_{\rm Edd}$ and momentum rates of $\sim 20 L_{\rm Edd}/c$ into the host galaxy [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract). Following the cessation of nuclear activity, the thermal expansion of the remnant-shocked wind continues to power the outflow's expansion to larger radii. Consequently, in massive gas-rich galaxies, these coasting structures can reach distances of tens of kiloparsecs long after the original driver has faded [Zubovas et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.4055Z/abstract). Hydrodynamical simulations indicate that fossil outflows should be dynamically common, particularly in gas-poor systems, and may outnumber actively driven outflows by a factor of a few in the local Universe [Zubovas et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.4819Z/abstract). Morphologically, relic outflows are predicted to be lopsided, spatially detached from the [[nuclear-regions-of-galaxies|Nuclear Regions of Galaxies]], and generally colder, featuring a more prominent [[molecular-outflows|molecular phase]] than their actively driven counterparts [Zubovas et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.4819Z/abstract).

A primary observational signature of a fossil outflow is a severe discrepancy between the measured kinetic power of the wind and the current radiative output of the central engine. Surveys of [[molecular-outflows|Molecular outflows]] in the local Universe find that in roughly $10\%$ of objects, the outflow energetics significantly exceed the theoretical maximum values capable of being produced by the observed AGN luminosity. These extreme systems are interpreted as fossil outflows resulting from a past, much more luminous AGN episode [Fluetsch et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483.4586F/abstract). Evidence for relic structures is critical for assessing the long-term impact of [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]] on its environment.

## Historical Development of Outflow Models

The theoretical framework for spatially extended outflows has evolved from idealized analytic solutions to complex, multiphase numerical simulations. Foundational studies often relied on the classical Chevalier-Clegg model, which treated starburst-driven [[superwinds|Superwinds]] as freely expanding, adiabatic hot gas flows. However, this model struggles to reproduce the observed linear scaling between wind X-ray luminosity and the star formation rate (SFR) over large dynamic ranges. Modern versatile wind models address this by incorporating radiative losses and adopting mass-loading factors ($\eta = \dot{M}_{\rm out}/\mathrm{SFR}$) that scale inversely with galaxy mass [Bustard et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...819...29B/abstract). Such scaling is critical for reproducing the stellar mass-gas-phase metallicity relation, which is governed by an equilibrium between star-formation enrichment and dilution from infall, moderated by the strength of the outflows [Finlator et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.385.2181F/abstract).

In the context of [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]], early theoretical models focused on the energy and momentum required to regulate black hole growth and establish the observed $M-\sigma$ relation. Analytical models posit that AGN outflows are momentum-driven at small radii, where the quasar radiation field rapidly Compton-cools the wind shock. This inner momentum-driven regime is required to naturally recover the $M-\sigma$ scaling [King et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.415L...6K/abstract). As the wind expands to larger radii ($\sim 1$ kpc) where cooling becomes inefficient, the outflow transitions into an energy-driven phase. These models successfully predict that the thermal energy in the shocked wind can continue to drive expansion long after the central engine has switched off, generating "fossil" outflows capable of sweeping a galaxy clear of its gas on dynamical timescales [King et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.415L...6K/abstract), [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract).

A major paradigm shift in outflow theory has been the recognition that outflows are not uniformly hot, but constitute a [[multiphase-wind|Multiphase wind]]. A critical, unresolved problem in the literature is the physical acceleration mechanism for the massive cold molecular and neutral phases observed at large galactocentric distances. Rather than accelerating preexisting cold clouds from the interstellar medium—which are highly susceptible to hydrodynamic destruction—recent simulations demonstrate that fast-moving ($v \sim 1000\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$) cool ($T \sim 10^4$ K) gas can form *in situ* via the rapid radiative cooling of a hot wind [Schneider et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...862...56S/abstract). Similarly, in powerful quasars, the interaction between an energy-driven outflow and a pre-enriched circumgalactic medium can trigger catastrophic cooling, generating $>10^9\ M_\odot$ of comoving cold gas that matches observed high-velocity line widths [Costa et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.448L..30C/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While the ubiquity of spatially extended outflows across cosmic time is well established, fundamental gaps remain in our understanding of their driving mechanisms, their physical state, and their ultimate impact on galaxy evolution. 

A critical unsolved problem in the study of any [[multiphase-wind|Multiphase wind]] is the issue of cloud survival. Observations across the electromagnetic spectrum show that the cold molecular and neutral phases often dominate the outflow mass and momentum budgets. [[molecular-outflows|Molecular emission lines]] (e.g., CO, OH, and HCN) frequently exhibit high-velocity wings, confirming the presence of cold, dense gas moving at velocities of $1000-2000$ km s$^{-1}$ out to distances of several kiloparsecs [Veilleux et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...776...27V/abstract), [Pereira-Santaella et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...616A.171P/abstract). It is currently unclear how these cold molecular clumps survive disruption by hydrodynamical instabilities within a hot, supersonic wind. A major review highlights that the acceleration of cool gas remains the critical unsolved problem, though star formation provides a force $\sim L/c$ that compares well with energetic observations [Thompson et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ARA&A..62..529T/abstract). The literature remains sharply divided on the acceleration mechanism: some models propose that pre-existing cold clouds are entrained and accelerated directly from the interstellar medium, while others suggest the cold material forms *in situ* through the rapid radiative cooling of the hot expanding wind [Schneider et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...862...56S/abstract), [Cottle et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...864...96C/abstract).

The broader role of [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]] in quenching star formation also remains a subject of active debate. While theoretical models often rely on large-scale AGN-driven winds to clear galaxies of gas on dynamical timescales [Zubovas et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745L..34Z/abstract), observational estimates of outflow kinetic power frequently fall below the theoretical thresholds required for global quenching (often measuring $<1\%$ of the bolometric luminosity $L_{\rm bol}$) [Karouzos et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...833..171K/abstract), [Rose et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.474..128R/abstract). Compounding this tension is the limitation of ground-based spatial resolution; recent work demonstrates that "beam smearing" due to atmospheric seeing can artificially broaden the apparent size of unresolved nuclear winds. Failing to account for this effect can lead to overestimates of both the spatial extent and the mass outflow rates by orders of magnitude [Holden et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.536.1857H/abstract). Consequently, the true fraction of gas that permanently escapes into the intergalactic medium versus the fraction that eventually recycles via [[galactic-fountains|Galactic fountains]] is poorly constrained.

Furthermore, the standard paradigm of purely negative feedback is challenged by observations of positive feedback. Outflows can act as a double-edged sword, over-pressurizing ambient gas to trigger or host [[spatially-extended-star-formation|in situ star formation]] at rates exceeding $15 \, \mathrm{M}_\odot \, \mathrm{yr}^{-1}$ [Maiolino et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017Natur.544..202M/abstract), [Gallagher et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.485.3409G/abstract). Quantifying the net contribution of outflow-born stars to the early formation of galactic spheroids represents a major frontier. Addressing these tensions requires mapping the multiphase structure of outflows across a much wider dynamic range. In the X-ray regime, next-generation observatories will provide spectral resolution essential for characterizing the temperature, density, and kinematics of the hot [[diffuse-x-ray-emitting-gas|Diffuse X-ray emitting gas]] that permeates the circumgalactic medium [Laha et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5...13L/abstract), [Vijayan et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.510..568V/abstract).
