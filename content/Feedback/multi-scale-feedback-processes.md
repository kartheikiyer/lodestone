## Article

In contemporary astrophysics, **multi-scale feedback processes** encompass the diverse mechanisms by which the localized release of [[mechanical-feedback|mechanical]] and [[radiative-feedback|radiative energy]]—primarily from stars and supermassive black holes—regulates the global thermodynamic and chemical evolution of galaxies. Situated at the core of modern galaxy formation theory, these processes solve the fundamental [[energy-dissipation|"overcooling"]] and mass-overproduction problems inherent to purely gravitational collapse models. By driving [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]], this energy injection creates a [[self-regulated-feedback-mechanisms|self-regulated baryon cycle]] that continuously balances the infall of pristine cosmic gas with the expulsion of metal-enriched material. This non-linear coupling spans an immense dynamic range, operating from sub-parsec scales where [[stellar-feedback-and-winds|protostellar winds]] disperse natal molecular clouds, to megaparsec scales where relativistic jets heat the intergalactic medium and sculpt the cosmic web.

The modern theoretical framework divides these regulatory mechanisms into several distinct but interacting regimes, broadly categorized as [[stellar-and-agn-feedback|Stellar and AGN Feedback]]. In low-mass galaxies ($M_{\rm halo} < 10^{12} M_\odot$), regulation is dominated by stellar processes. Recent advancements emphasize that supernova explosions alone cannot regulate star formation; instead, [[strong-pre-sn-feedback|Strong pre-SN feedback]] mechanisms—such as [[radiation-pressure-feedback|Radiation pressure feedback]], photoionization, and stellar winds—are strictly required to lower ambient gas densities before supernovae detonate [Hopkins et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..950H/abstract), [Hopkins et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..581H/abstract). In more massive halos, [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] becomes the dominant quenching mechanism. AGN feedback operates bimodally through a high-luminosity "quasar" mode that drives isotropic winds, and a mechanical "radio" mode whose highly collimated jets maintain the long-term thermal equilibrium of galaxy clusters [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract). Furthermore, modern models increasingly recognize the critical role of non-thermal pressure supports, such as cosmic rays and amplified magnetic fields, which drive [[gentle-agn-feedback|gentle, long-lived outflows]] capable of altering central dark matter profiles.

Accurately modeling this vast hierarchy of physical processes presents a formidable computational challenge. Because [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] cannot explicitly resolve the parsec-scale physics of individual supernovae or black hole accretion disks across cosmological volumes, they rely heavily on [[subgrid-physics-prescriptions|Subgrid physics prescriptions]] [Thacker et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000ApJ...545..728T/abstract), [Vogelsberger et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.436.3031V/abstract). The physical fidelity of these subgrid models is rigorously tested against multi-wavelength observational signatures. These empirical constraints range from the low-redshift Lyman-alpha forest and galactic metallicity gradients to the Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect, which traces the large-scale, feedback-driven evacuation of hot baryons from massive cluster cores [Battaglia et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...725...91B/abstract), [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.5391A/abstract). 

This article explores the physical mechanisms, historical development, and observational consequences of multi-scale feedback. It also highlights critical open questions at the frontier of theoretical astrophysics. These include identifying the exact physical transport mechanisms of cosmic rays, resolving the complex non-linear coupling between independent feedback channels in [[multi-scale-multi-science-systems|Multi-scale multi-science systems]], and determining the role of feedback during the Epoch of Reionization. Notably, the recent discovery of unexpectedly massive, mature galaxies at extreme redshifts by JWST has invigorated debates over whether [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]]—where AGN or stellar shocks locally compress gas to trigger starbursts—plays a much larger role in early galaxy assembly than traditional [[negative-feedback|Negative feedback]] paradigms suggest.

## Introduction to Multi-scale Feedback

Multi-scale feedback dictates the [[galactic-gas-dynamics-and-flows|thermal, chemical, and structural evolution of galaxies]] by coupling localized energy injection to the global gas distribution. Through a continuous baryon cycle, the radiative cooling of hot gas fuels star formation and supermassive black hole accretion, which in turn trigger energetic feedback that reheats or expels the surrounding gas. This non-linear interplay creates a state of [[self-regulated-feedback-mechanisms|self-regulation]], balancing the inflow of cosmic gas with internal energy release across a vast hierarchy of physical scales.

In halos with masses $M_{\rm halo} < 10^{12} M_\odot$, the dominant regulatory mechanism is [[stellar-feedback-and-winds|Stellar Feedback and Winds]]. While early theoretical models traditionally modeled stellar feedback solely via supernova (SN) explosions, recent literature demonstrates that [[sn-feedback-model|SN feedback]] acting in isolation is insufficient. Without prior clearing of the dense Interstellar Medium (ISM), SN energy rapidly radiates away in an "overcooling" phase. To achieve self-regulation, modern models require a multi-channel approach where "pre-SN" mechanisms—such as [[radiation-pressure-feedback|Radiation pressure feedback]], photoionization, and stellar winds—act immediately upon star formation [Hopkins et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..581H/abstract), [Emerick et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865L..22E/abstract). These early energy injection channels are critical for dispersing giant molecular clouds on timescales of $1-3$ Myr, lowering the local ISM density so that subsequent SNe can couple efficiently to the diffuse gas and drive [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]] [Chevance et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509..272C/abstract).

In massive galaxies and cluster environments, [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] takes over as the primary regulatory driver. AGN feedback broadly operates through two modes: highly collimated kinetic jets (the "radio mode") and [[radiation-driven-winds|isotropic radiative winds]] (the "quasar mode") [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract), [Huško et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.5988H/abstract). By injecting vast amounts of energy into the Circumgalactic Medium (CGM) and the intracluster medium, AGNs act as [[preventative-feedback|Preventative feedback]], maintaining the surrounding halo at high temperatures and suppressing further gas accretion. The impact of these feedback processes extends far beyond the host galaxy itself; cosmological simulations demonstrate that baryonic material is frequently redistributed out to a "closure radius" of $1.5 - 2.5 R_{200}$, significantly lowering the baryon fraction within the virial radius [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.5391A/abstract).

The multi-scale nature of these processes means they rarely operate in isolation. [[stellar-and-agn-feedback|Stellar and AGN Feedback]] channels are non-linearly coupled and can mutually amplify or suppress one another. For example, in intermediate-mass halos where both processes are active, the simultaneous action of AGN and SN feedback can mutually weaken their individual suppressive effects by up to an order of magnitude due to the disruption of coherent gas flows [Booth et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013NatSR...3.1738B/abstract). Furthermore, while feedback is primarily viewed as a negative (quenching) regulator, localized compression from AGN-driven outflows or SN shocks can locally enhance gas density and trigger bursts of [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]] [Su et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251020897S/abstract). 

Finally, the physics of multi-scale feedback is increasingly recognized to depend heavily on non-thermal pressure supports. Cosmic rays and amplified magnetic fields, owing to their long cooling times, are critical for driving [[gentle-agn-feedback|gentle, persistent outflows]] that shape the CGM and can even alter central dark matter profiles through cusp-to-core transitions in dwarf galaxies [Ruszkowski et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&ARv..31....4R/abstract), [Koudmani et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240902172K/abstract), [Thomas et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251016125T/abstract).

## Historical Development of Feedback Models

Early theories of galaxy formation were heavily reliant on gravitational collapse. However, purely gravitational, self-similar models failed to reproduce the thermodynamic properties of the [[heating-mechanisms-astrophysics|intracluster medium]] and predicted a massive overproduction of stellar mass [Borgani et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008SSRv..134..269B/abstract). To resolve this, theorists introduced energy injection from supernovae (SNe). Because early [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] could not resolve the parsec-scale physics of individual stellar explosions, these mechanisms were necessarily approximated using [[heuristic-models|Heuristic models]] and [[subgrid-physics-prescriptions|Subgrid physics prescriptions]] that injected thermal or momentum energy directly into the gas at the resolution scale.

Despite the inclusion of SN feedback, simulations persistently suffered from the [[energy-dissipation|overcooling problem]]. In early models, the injected thermal energy was rapidly radiated away by the dense, high-pressure gas surrounding young star clusters, preventing the generation of large-scale galactic winds and failing to suppress [[star-formation-processes|star formation]] [Agertz et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...18A/abstract). A major paradigm shift occurred with the recognition that "early" or pre-SN stellar feedback is a strict requirement for self-regulation. Mechanisms such as [[radiation-pressure-feedback|Radiation pressure feedback]], photoionization, and [[stellar-feedback-and-winds|stellar winds]] inject momentum and energy into giant molecular clouds during the first $1-3$ Myr of a star cluster's life [Emerick et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865L..22E/abstract), [Chevance et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509..272C/abstract). This early feedback disperses the densest gas, lowering the local density so that subsequent SN explosions can efficiently couple their [[energy-release-mechanisms|mechanical energy]] to the interstellar medium rather than radiating it away [Hopkins et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..581H/abstract). For instance, simulations from the MAGICC project demonstrated that early thermal feedback, acting effectively like a UV ionization source, is essential to reproduce the observed stellar mass-halo mass relationship at $z=2$ [Stinson et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.428..129S/abstract). Interestingly, while early feedback is critical for regulation, it can paradoxically suppress large-scale galactic outflows by disrupting molecular clouds before supernovae can cluster and collectively drive efficient [[superwinds|Superwinds]] [Smith et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.506.3882S/abstract).

While multi-channel stellar feedback successfully regulates galaxies in low-mass halos ($M_{\rm halo} < 10^{12} M_\odot$), it is widely agreed to be insufficient to quench star formation in high-mass systems. To reconcile simulations with the observed stellar mass-halo mass relationship and the existence of a "red and dead" sequence of massive early-type galaxies, theoretical models evolved to include feedback from [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|supermassive black holes]] [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract), [Vogelsberger et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.436.3031V/abstract). Initial implementations of active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback utilized simple isotropic thermal energy injection. As resolution and physical fidelity improved, models transitioned to [[multi-channel-agn-feedback|Multi-channel AGN feedback]], explicitly distinguishing between a "quasar" or radiative mode (driving isotropic winds) and a "radio" or kinetic mode (driving highly collimated jets) [Huško et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.5988H/abstract). Kinetic jets, in particular, have proven far more efficient at maintaining cool cores and regulating star formation in the most massive galaxy groups and clusters. 

The historical progression from isolated, single-channel "blow-out" prescriptions to modern, multi-scale frameworks has revealed that feedback pathways are not strictly additive. Recent numerical studies demonstrate that stellar and AGN feedback are non-linearly coupled; in intermediate-mass halos where both processes operate efficiently, they can actually mutually weaken one another's impact by up to an order of magnitude due to the disruption of coherent gas flows [Booth et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013NatSR...3.1738B/abstract). Consequently, contemporary models have largely abandoned the paradigm of monolithic feedback events, instead treating galaxy evolution as a [[self-regulated-feedback-mechanisms|self-regulated equilibrium cycle]] continuously moderated by the simultaneous interplay of multi-scale stellar and AGN processes.

## Stellar Feedback: From Protostellar Scales to Superwinds

Traditional models of galaxy formation relied primarily on supernova (SN) explosions to regulate star formation and drive [[superwinds|Superwinds]]. However, [[high-resolution-simulations|high-resolution cosmological simulations]] have revealed that SN feedback alone results in the overproduction of stars and fails to properly regulate the interstellar medium (ISM) due to the [[energy-dissipation|"overcooling"]] problem [Hopkins et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..581H/abstract). 

The current consensus establishes that early, non-explosive feedback—often grouped under the umbrella of [[strong-pre-sn-feedback|Strong pre-SN feedback]]—is a critical requirement for realistic galaxy evolution models. Processes occurring on protostellar and giant molecular cloud (GMC) scales, specifically [[radiation-pressure-feedback|Radiation pressure feedback]], photoionization, and [[stellar-feedback-and-winds|stellar winds]], must act to disrupt the dense natal environment before the first SNe detonate. By driving rapid molecular cloud dispersal, this pre-supernova feedback lowers the ambient gas density, which prevents artificial overcooling and allows subsequent SNe to couple their [[mechanical-feedback|mechanical energy]] efficiently to the diffuse ISM [Emerick et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865L..22E/abstract), [Agertz et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...18A/abstract). Observational and theoretical constraints indicate that this dispersal occurs on timescales of $1-3\text{ Myr}$ after the formation of unembedded massive stars [Chevance et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509..272C/abstract), [Kruijssen et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019Natur.569..519K/abstract).

Incorporating these pre-SN radiative effects profoundly alters galaxy-scale metrics. Early feedback reduces the global [[star-formation-processes|star formation rate]] (SFR) by factors of $\sim 5$ compared to SN-only models, demonstrating its essential role in dwarf galaxies [Emerick et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865L..22E/abstract). Furthermore, to achieve self-regulation that matches the observed Kennicutt-Schmidt law, simulations often require a local star formation efficiency per free-fall time of $\varepsilon_{\rm ff} \approx 10\%$ [Agertz et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...18A/abstract). Interestingly, the coupling efficiency of this early feedback is relatively low; only a few tens of percent of the injected energy and momentum interact directly with the parent GMC before escaping [Chevance et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509..272C/abstract).

While the necessity of pre-SN feedback is undisputed, there is ongoing debate regarding the dominant physical channel. Depending on the galactic environment, mass, and metallicity, some studies emphasize the role of photoionization and HII region expansion [Hasegawa et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.428..154H/abstract), [Andersson et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...681A..28A/abstract). Others identify radiation pressure or [[momentum-driven-feedback-mechanisms|Momentum-driven Feedback Mechanisms]] as the primary drivers of GMC disruption [Hopkins et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..950H/abstract), [Hopkins et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.421.3522H/abstract). In massive, gas-rich starbursts, radiation pressure frequently dominates, whereas in lower-density Milky Way analogues and dwarfs, a combination of photoheating and shock-heated stellar winds shapes the ISM [Hopkins et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.421.3522H/abstract).

Resolving the multi-scale interplay of [[stellar-feedback-and-winds|Stellar Feedback and Winds]] requires careful numerical treatment. Low-resolution simulations can suffer from a "resolution bias," artificially over-clearing gas because they fail to resolve feedback-resistant dense clumps [Bourne et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.453.1829B/abstract). To capture the correct driving of [[galactic-outflows-and-inflows|Galactic Outflows and Inflows]], [[subgrid-physics-prescriptions|Subgrid physics prescriptions]] for SNe must often explicitly inject radial momentum if the Sedov-Taylor phase is unresolved [Kimm et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.2900K/abstract), [Smith et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.478..302S/abstract). Ultimately, the synergistic, non-linear coupling of pre-SN radiative mechanisms and SN explosions establishes a self-regulated multiphase ISM, driving large-scale outflows that govern the [[mass-loading-factor|Mass loading factor]] and enrich the circumgalactic medium [Pawlik et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009MNRAS.396L..46P/abstract), [Hopkins et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..581H/abstract). Furthermore, models utilizing only supernova explosions frequently fail to produce realistic dark matter profiles in dwarf galaxies, explicitly requiring the inclusion of cosmic ray and radiative pressure channels to achieve the observed cusp-to-core transitions [Martin-Alvarez et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.3806M/abstract). At the extreme resolution limit, capturing the earliest phases of chemical enrichment is paramount. The AEOS project, which performs star-by-star cosmological simulations at $1\text{ pc}$ resolution, demonstrates how individual [[sn-feedback-model|Population III supernovae]] establish the foundational metallicity floors that guide subsequent early galaxy formation [Brauer et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...980...41B/abstract).

## AGN Feedback: Quasar and Radio Modes

Feedback from supermassive black holes (SMBHs) is universally recognized as the primary mechanism for quenching star formation in massive dark matter halos ($>10^{12} \text{ M}_{\odot}$) [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract), [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.5391A/abstract). Without it, models of galaxy formation overproduce massive, blue, and highly compact systems, failing to reproduce the observed red sequence and established SMBH-galaxy scaling relations, such as the $M_{\rm BH}-\sigma$ relation [Vogelsberger et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.436.3031V/abstract), [Rosito et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...652A..44R/abstract). The energetic output governed by [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] is traditionally categorized into two distinct phenomenological regimes: the high-luminosity "quasar" mode and the mechanical "radio" mode. 

**The Quasar Mode (Radiative/Wind)**
The quasar mode occurs during phases of rapid, optically thick accretion near or above the Eddington limit. Energy couples to the surrounding interstellar medium (ISM) via intense [[radiation-pressure|radiation pressure]] and [[radiation-driven-winds|broad absorption line winds]]. These isotropic outflows can reach velocities exceeding $1000 \text{ km s}^{-1}$, rapidly excavating the central $1-10 \text{ kpc}$ of the host galaxy's gas reservoir [Laha et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5...13L/abstract), [Torrey et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.497.5292T/abstract). Furthermore, the radiative output extends beyond simply driving winds; incorporating an observationally calibrated X-ray radiation field from the AGN can be twice as effective as local thermal energy injection at suppressing star formation in massive early-type galaxies [Hambrick et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...738...16H/abstract). This mode is primarily responsible for establishing the foundational SMBH-galaxy scaling relations by self-regulating the black hole's mass against the depth of the galactic potential well [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract). On galactic scales, ultrafast outflows (UFOs) driven by the AGN can inject considerable momentum into the inhomogeneous ISM, flooding intercloud channels and effectively dispersing dense clouds across kiloparsec scales [Wagner et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...763L..18W/abstract). To capture these dynamics, recent frameworks like the MISTRAL model employ stochastic bipolar momentum deposition to mimic outflows driven by radiatively efficient accretion, which successfully regulates star formation without requiring explicit supermassive black hole mass-dependent scaling schemes . Furthermore, the causal mechanism for expelling obscuring gas in these powerful blow-out events may be intrinsically linked to dust production in supernovae, which subsequently couples strongly to the central radiative AGN feedback [Ishibashi et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.1291I/abstract). However, continuous thermal injection mimicking quasar winds in [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] is often insufficient on its own to keep the most massive halos permanently quenched against subsequent gas accretion [Vogelsberger et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.436.3031V/abstract).

**The Radio Mode (Kinetic/Jets)**
The radio mode operates at lower [[net-accretion-rates|Net accretion rates]] and is characterized by highly collimated, [[mechanical-feedback|relativistic jets]]. This mechanical feedback is essential for suppressing cooling flows in massive galaxy groups and clusters. At a fundamental thermodynamic level, internally driven AGN feedback is energetically favoured, as outflows act to reduce the Gibbs free energy of the galactic atmosphere when cooling gains exceed losses [Pope et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.427....2P/abstract). Rather than coupling strongly with the dense galactic disk, jet-driven outflows efficiently deposit energy into the circumgalactic and intracluster media, inflating massive buoyant bubbles observed as X-ray cavities [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract). By providing distributed [[heating-mechanisms-astrophysics|Heating mechanisms (astrophysics)]], these jets offset radiative losses and maintain the thermodynamic equilibrium of the hot halo gas [Meece et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...841..133M/abstract), [Borgani et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008SSRv..134..269B/abstract). Comparative numerical studies demonstrate that kinetic jet feedback is markedly more efficient than isotropic thermal feedback at quenching star formation in brightest cluster galaxies (BCGs) and maintaining realistic, cool-core temperature profiles [Huško et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.5988H/abstract). Recent observations also highlight the complex geometric impact of these mechanical modes, such as the precessing jet in the active galaxy VV 340a, which drives multi-kpc [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|outflows]] at rates of $\sim 20 \text{ M}_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ [Kader et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260108791K/abstract).

**Observational Constraints and Tensions**
Current [[subgrid-physics-models|Subgrid physics models]] for AGN feedback are increasingly constrained by the Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) effect and X-ray scaling relations, which trace the significant redistribution of baryons to the halo outskirts [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.5391A/abstract). Empirical estimates demonstrate that AGN feedback must be extremely efficient, requiring high coupling and momentum loading factors to properly explain established black hole and stellar mass scaling relations [Zubovas et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.479.3189Z/abstract). The distinction between isotropic winds and collimated jets produces disparate impacts on large-scale structure; for instance, observations of the low-redshift Ly$\alpha$ forest column density distribution strongly favor models employing high-speed, collimated jets over purely isotropic wind models [Tillman et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945L..17T/abstract). 

Furthermore, the paradigm that AGN feedback acts strictly as a [[negative-feedback|Negative feedback]] mechanism is the subject of active debate. While long-term quenching is undisputed, recent high-resolution models indicate that AGN-driven shocks can also compress dense gas clouds, triggering transient [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]]. In gas-rich starburst dwarf environments, this localized compression can temporarily enhance global star formation rates by up to $\sim 25\%$ [Su et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251020897S/abstract), [Wagner et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016AN....337..167W/abstract). Furthermore, the sphere of influence of AGN outflows is not confined to the host galaxy. Large-scale models reveal that AGN feedback can impact neighboring star-forming galaxies out to distances of $2\text{ Mpc}$, with proximity to an active AGN typically leading to suppressed star formation in massive neighboring galaxies, while potentially enhancing star formation in low-mass, gas-rich systems [Das et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250708790D/abstract). Disentangling these competing effects and accurately partitioning energy between the quasar and radio modes remains a central challenge in modern astrophysics.

## The Role of Non-Thermal Components: Magnetic Fields and Cosmic Rays

While thermal pressure from supernovae often dominates short-term energy injection, non-thermal components—specifically magnetic fields and cosmic rays (CRs)—are increasingly recognized as crucial, long-lived energy reservoirs in galactic ecosystems. In the interstellar medium (ISM), these non-thermal components can reach rough equipartition with the thermal and turbulent energy densities, fundamentally altering the thermodynamics and hydrodynamics of the host galaxy [Ruszkowski et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&ARv..31....4R/abstract).

Within the framework of Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), weak primordial seed fields are amplified to microgauss levels through feedback-driven turbulence. The injection of kinetic energy from [[stellar-and-agn-feedback|Stellar and AGN Feedback]] generates Kolmogorov-like turbulence characterized by a [[wind-mechanical-energy|kinetic energy spectrum]] $E_K(k) \propto k^{-5/3}$. This turbulent cascade drives a small-scale Galactic dynamo, which amplifies the magnetic field and produces a Kazantsev spectrum $E_M(k) \propto k^{3/2}$ during its kinematic phase [Rieder et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.457.1722R/abstract). Supernova feedback is particularly efficient at restructuring these magnetic fields, lofting them up to $10 \text{ kpc}$ above the galactic disk and generating a significant [[mechanical-heating|random field component]] [Ntormousi et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...619L...5N/abstract). These processes are empirically grounded by observations of local starburst analogues such as M82, where core magnetic field strengths reach $\sim 100\ \mu\text{G}$ and shape the associated cosmic ray loss processes [Adebahr et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A&A...555A..23A/abstract).

Accelerated primarily at supernova remnant shocks, CRs provide a form of non-thermal pressure that suffers from substantially longer cooling times than the thermal plasma. This allows CRs to exert a "gentle" but persistent pressure gradient capable of driving large-scale [[galactic-outflows-and-wind-energetics|Galactic Outflows and Wind Energetics]]. In dwarf galaxies, cosmic ray feedback produces more extended, rotationally supported stellar disks compared to purely thermal feedback models, and plays a key role in smoothing out central dark matter distributions to drive a cusp-to-core transition [Ruszkowski et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&ARv..31....4R/abstract), [Koudmani et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240902172K/abstract), [Martin-Alvarez et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.3806M/abstract).

The efficiency of CR feedback is highly sensitive to the exact physical transport mechanism—specifically, the balance between CR diffusion and streaming. In the paradigm of Alfven-wave regulated transport, CRs stream along magnetic field lines and excite Alfvén waves via the gyroresonant instability. As CR gyrophases bunch up lopsidedly, they transfer momentum to the waves, which in turn scatter the CRs and limit their bulk streaming velocity to near the Alfvén speed [Thomas et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.485.2977T/abstract), [Lemmerz et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240604400L/abstract). The effective CR transport speed depends heavily on local ISM phases due to competing wave-damping mechanisms: ion-neutral damping dominates in cold, dense gas, whereas non-linear Landau damping limits wave growth in warm, diffuse environments. State-of-the-art [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] suggest that the effective CR diffusion coefficient converges to $D_{\rm CR} \approx 10^{28} - 10^{29} \text{ cm}^2 \text{ s}^{-1}$ depending on the local gas density [Thomas et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251016125T/abstract). 

Despite their theoretical appeal in regulating late-time star formation, the literature exhibits significant tension regarding the impact of CRs during the Epoch of Reionization. Recent high-resolution simulations indicate that CR pressure supports low-density gas, filling in the evacuated cavities normally carved out by supernova explosions. By preventing the clearing of these low-density channels, CR feedback can severely suppress the escape fraction of ionizing photons, leading to incomplete reionization scenarios that strongly contradict observational constraints [Farcy et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...698A..89F/abstract). This discrepancy highlights major unresolved ambiguities in how CR transport and multi-phase ISM microphysics are coupled in current numerical models, such as those used in the [[thesan|THESAN]] project.

## Baryonic Flows: Accretion, Outflows, and Recirculation

The evolution of galaxies is fundamentally governed by a [[self-regulated-feedback-mechanisms|self-regulated baryon cycle]] consisting of gas accretion, star formation, and the subsequent ejection and recirculation of material [Mitra et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1184M/abstract). This dynamic equilibrium determines the baryonic content of halos and dictates the chemical evolution of the interstellar and circumgalactic media.

**Bimodal Accretion and Preventative Feedback**
Gas enters galactic halos via two primary channels. In the "cold mode" ($T \lesssim 10^5$ K), gas accretes along cosmic web filaments without experiencing a virial shock, delivering fuel directly to the galactic disk. Conversely, the "hot mode" consists of gas that is shock-heated to near the halo virial temperature. While hot mode accretion becomes increasingly dominant in halos above $M_{\rm halo} \gtrsim 10^{11} M_\odot$, the majority of stars at any redshift form from gas originally accreted via the cold mode [van de Voort et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.414.2458V/abstract). 

However, [[net-accretion-rates|Net accretion rates]] onto the central galaxy are not determined by gravity alone. [[preventative-feedback|Preventative feedback]]—driven primarily by active galactic nuclei (AGN) in high-mass halos ($M_{200} \gtrsim 10^{12} M_\odot$) and by the ultraviolet background in dwarf halos—heats the circumgalactic medium (CGM) and modulates or completely suppresses the first-time infall of pristine gas [Mitchell et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.511.2948M/abstract), [Lu et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015arXiv150402109L/abstract), [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.5391A/abstract). In addition to internal feedback, global environmental factors influence accretion; for example, the Cosmic Web Detachment (CWD) model provides a complementary quenching mechanism, proposing that star-formation quenching can result from galaxies detaching from primordial gas filaments due to intergalactic shell-crossing [Aragon Calvo et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019OJAp....2E...7A/abstract).

**Outflows and Mass Loading**
Once gas collapses into the interstellar medium, stellar and AGN feedback can eject it via [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]]. The efficiency of this ejective feedback is quantified by the [[mass-loading-factor|Mass loading factor]], defined as the ratio of the outflow rate to the star formation rate, $\eta = \dot{M}_{\rm out} / \dot{M}_*$. 

Cosmological simulations rely heavily on the scaling of $\eta$ with galaxy mass to reproduce the observed stellar mass function. Numerical and analytic models suggest that $\eta$ scales inversely with the galaxy circular velocity, roughly $\eta \propto V_c^{-1}$ for [[momentum-driven-feedback-mechanisms|momentum-driven winds]] or $\eta \propto V_c^{-2}$ for energy-driven winds [Hopkins et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.421.3522H/abstract), [Puchwein et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.428.2966P/abstract). Observationally constrained analytic models suggest that the time-averaged mass loading factor reaches an upper limit of $\eta \sim 20$ for dwarf galaxies ($M_* \sim 10^9 M_\odot$) and decreases monotonically to $\eta \sim 1$ for massive galaxies ($M_* \sim 10^{11} M_\odot$) [Lu et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...808..129L/abstract). These outflows are highly energetic, capable of redistributing baryons to a "closure radius" of $R_c \sim 1.5 - 2.5 R_{200c}$, thereby significantly lowering the total baryon fraction within the virial radius of groups and clusters [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.5391A/abstract).

**Recirculation and Chemical Evolution**
Ejected gas does not universally escape the dark matter halo. A substantial fraction of the outflowing material stalls and falls back onto the galactic disk, forming [[galactic-fountains|Galactic fountains]]. This continuous recycling of wind material is a critical determinant of the total mass and multiphase structure of the CGM [Mitchell et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.511.2948M/abstract), [Su et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.471..144S/abstract). 

Crucially, because these outflows are driven by localized [[stellar-feedback-and-winds|Stellar Feedback and Winds]], they manifest as metal-enriched winds. If the outflowing gas is preferentially enriched compared to the ambient interstellar medium, the required mass-loading factors to match observed galaxy metallicities are strongly constrained [Lu et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...808..129L/abstract). The interplay of localized metal injection and macroscopic fountain flows directly dictates the spatial distribution of heavy elements. While many cosmological simulations predict an "inside-out" growth scenario resulting in steep, negative metallicity gradients at high redshift, models incorporating strong, distributed mechanical feedback predict flatter gradients [Gibson et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A&A...554A..47G/abstract), [Ibrahim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250111209I/abstract). Because different stellar sources eject material under different physical conditions, the resulting metal distribution statistics naturally break chemical elements into distinct nucleosynthetic 'familiars', preserving the imprint of their disparate ejection mechanisms in the galactic halo [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.540.3906Z/abstract). Resolving the exact coupling of these metal-enriched flows remains an active area of debate, as varying the sub-grid feedback prescriptions can drastically alter the predicted thermodynamic and chemical state of the CGM [Rey et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.543...12R/abstract).

## Numerical Implementation and the Resolution Frontier

Simulating galaxy formation requires bridging the immense dynamic range between sub-parsec stellar or black hole accretion scales and megaparsec cosmological volumes. Because the physical scales of individual supernova (SN) explosions and Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) cannot be explicitly resolved in large-scale runs, numerical models rely on [[subgrid-physics-prescriptions|Subgrid physics prescriptions]] to inject feedback energy and momentum into the resolved interstellar medium [Thacker et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000ApJ...545..728T/abstract). The efficacy of these models is exquisitely sensitive to the underlying numerical resolution and the hydrodynamical methodology, whether Eulerian grid codes utilizing Adaptive Mesh Refinement (AMR) or Lagrangian schemes such as Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics (SPH).

A critical challenge in capturing the multiphase interstellar medium is the "resolution bias" [Bourne et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.453.1829B/abstract). In reality, dense gas clumps are highly resistant to feedback-driven clearing. However, low-resolution simulations artificially smooth these density peaks into a more uniform, lower-density medium. Consequently, the injected feedback energy couples too efficiently to the surrounding gas, resulting in simulated galaxies that are too easily disrupted or evacuated of their baryons [Bourne et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.453.1829B/abstract). Recent [[high-resolution-simulations|high-resolution tests]] demonstrate that coarse-resolution simulations fundamentally struggle to determine whether a local SN event will drive a large-scale outflow or simply suppress local star formation—a distinction that only becomes self-consistent at solar-mass resolutions [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251002432Z/abstract). The direct regulatory power of these implementations has been starkly demonstrated through controlled numerical experiments; for instance, feedback-halting experiments in simulations using [[astrophysical-simulation-codes|Astrophysical Simulation Codes]] like FIRE reveal that abruptly terminating feedback leads to an immediate surge in the [[mass-quenching-efficiency|star formation efficiency]], indicating that feedback acts to moderate star formation directly through kinetic energy injection and turbulence. To overcome these profound challenges, novel simulation frameworks, such as RIGEL, utilize explicitly solved radiative transfer on a star-by-star basis at solar-mass resolution to capture the non-linear coupling of early feedback within a multiphase ISM [Deng et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.231D/abstract). Observationally calibrating these high-resolution models relies on analyzing the spatial decorrelation of molecular gas and young stars on $\sim 100 \text{ pc}$ scales, which serves as a direct empirical probe of feedback-driven cloud dispersal timescales [Semenov et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918...13S/abstract).

When thermal energy is injected into dense, poorly resolved gas, it is typically radiated away before it can perform hydrodynamic work, a numerical artifact known as the [[energy-dissipation|overcooling problem]]. To circumvent this, subgrid models often disable cooling temporarily or transition to mechanical feedback schemes [Thacker et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000ApJ...545..728T/abstract). Modern mechanical prescriptions explicitly inject the residual radial momentum of the unresolved Sedov-Taylor expansion phase to ensure that SNe successfully drive turbulence and galactic winds [Kimm et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.2900K/abstract), [Smith et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.478..302S/abstract). Similarly, for AGN, resolving the [[characteristic-astrophysical-scales|Bondi accretion radius]] remains computationally prohibitive in large volumes. Subgrid choices regarding the geometry of energy injection significantly alter the resultant coupling efficiency and the morphology of the driven outflows .

Finally, the discretization of continuous mass and energy fluxes requires [[heuristic-models|stochastic modeling]] of feedback events. This stochasticity introduces fundamental variance into macroscopic galaxy properties. Studies using identical initial conditions have shown that random sampling in bursty [[stellar-and-agn-feedback|SN and AGN feedback]] models can induce variations of up to $25\%$ in the final stellar masses of individual galaxies [Borrow et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526.2441B/abstract). Furthermore, analyzing the scatter across re-simulations reveals that the correlation between black hole mass and stellar mass disappears at the high-mass end, indicating that the feedback cycle may be interrupted by external processes [Borrow et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526.2441B/abstract). Capturing the true predictive power of a cosmological simulation thus requires carefully disentangling the physical impact of feedback from the inherent noise of its numerical implementation.

## Observational Signatures and Cosmological Scaling Relations

The empirical validation of [[stellar-and-agn-feedback|Stellar and AGN Feedback]] models relies on their ability to reproduce a wide array of observational signatures across diverse astrophysical scales. Feedback processes leave distinct imprints on [[global-star-formation-suppression-mechanism|galaxy luminosity functions]], the thermodynamic state of the circumgalactic medium (CGM), and the large-scale structure of the intergalactic medium (IGM). 

On galactic scales, feedback is the primary mechanism for regulating star formation and driving the observed stellar mass-halo mass (SMHM) relationship. In low-mass galaxies, early pre-supernova feedback acts rapidly to disperse giant molecular clouds, reducing the star formation rate (SFR) by factors of $\sim 5$ or more compared to models that only include supernovae [Emerick et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865L..22E/abstract). This self-regulation allows [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]] to match the global Kennicutt-Schmidt law [Agertz et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...804...18A/abstract), [Hopkins et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.417..950H/abstract). In massive halos ($M_{\rm halo} \gtrsim 10^{12} \mathrm{M}_\odot$), [[active-galactic-nuclei-agn-physics|Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) Physics]] is strictly required to prevent catastrophic [[energy-dissipation|overcooling]]. AGN feedback is the principal driver of "quenching," halting star formation to reproduce the observed red sequence and maintaining the $M_{\rm BH}-\sigma$ relation [Fabian et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ARA&A..50..455F/abstract), [Rosito et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...652A..44R/abstract). The statistical power of these prescriptions is demonstrated by the [[overwhelmingly-large-simulations-project|EAGLE simulation suite]], which shows that calibrated subgrid feedback can successfully reproduce the cosmic growth of the stellar mass density and the observed bimodal sequences of star-forming and passive galaxies [Furlong et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.450.4486F/abstract).

The chemical evolution of galaxies provides another critical constraint. The global mass-metallicity relation and radial metallicity gradients act as fossil records of metal-enriched galactic outflows [Ibrahim et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.3276I/abstract). Recent implementations of the mechanical [[sn-feedback-model|SN feedback model]] successfully reproduce observed gradients at $z=0$ and predict "inside-out" growth characterized by negative gradients up to $z \sim 5$ [Ibrahim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250111209I/abstract). However, significant tension remains: some high-redshift observations suggest flat or even positive metallicity gradients, indicating that current subgrid metal ejection and mixing recipes may still be incomplete [Ibrahim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250111209I/abstract).

Beyond the galactic disk, the low-redshift Lyman-alpha (Ly$\alpha$) forest serves as a highly sensitive diagnostic for the energetics and geometry of AGN outflows. Because the Ly$\alpha$ forest traces diffuse neutral hydrogen in the IGM, its column density distribution (CDD) can differentiate between feedback modes. Simulations employing isotropic thermal winds typically overpredict the number of Ly$\alpha$ absorbers at column densities $N_{\rm HI} < 10^{14} \mathrm{cm}^{-2}$. In contrast, highly collimated, high-velocity kinetic jets couple weakly with the dense interstellar medium but efficiently heat the IGM over long ranges, yielding a quantitatively superior fit to the observed Ly$\alpha$ CDD across five orders of magnitude [Tillman et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945L..17T/abstract). 

On the largest scales of galaxy groups and clusters, feedback dramatically alters the distribution of hot baryons. [[non-gravitational-heating|Non-gravitational heating]] from AGN jets redistributes gas outward, significantly lowering the baryon fraction within the virial radius and pushing material to a characteristic "closure radius" of $1.5 - 2.5 R_{200}$ [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.5391A/abstract). This large-scale evacuation directly modifies X-ray scaling relations and suppresses the thermal and kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) effect power spectra. Because baryonic feedback also suppresses the [[galaxy-sized-halo-energy-spectrum|total matter power spectrum]] on small scales, accurately calibrating these SZ and X-ray signatures is essential.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

The current frontier in modeling [[multi-scale-multi-science-systems|Multi-scale multi-science systems]] lies in understanding the non-linear, multi-physics coupling between distinct energy injection channels. While earlier heuristic frameworks often treated [[stellar-and-agn-feedback|Stellar and AGN Feedback]] as independent additive processes, recent high-resolution simulations demonstrate that different channels can mutually weaken one another. For instance, pre-emptive gas heating or expulsion by one mechanism can alter the local density, thereby reducing the coupling efficiency of subsequent energy injection by up to an order of magnitude [Booth et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013NatSR...3.1738B/abstract). Furthermore, the integration of non-thermal processes remains an open theoretical challenge. The exact physical transport mechanisms of cosmic rays—specifically the balance between diffusion and streaming—and their efficiency across different phases of the interstellar medium are actively debated [Ruszkowski et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&ARv..31....4R/abstract). 

Extending feedback models to the first stars and galaxies introduces new complexities, particularly regarding the reionization history of the Universe. In the dust-poor environments of Cosmic Dawn, radiation pressure from Lyman-$\alpha$ scattering may inject up to $\sim 100$ times more momentum into the interstellar medium than ultraviolet continuum radiation, acting as a dominant form of [[radiative-feedback|Radiative feedback]] [Nebrin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240919288N/abstract). Meanwhile, the role of cosmic rays during reionization is highly controversial. While they provide necessary pressure support, recent simulations suggest that cosmic rays may actually suppress the escape fraction of ionizing photons by filling the low-density cavities otherwise carved by the [[sn-feedback-model|SN feedback model]], thereby negatively affecting the timeline of reionization [Farcy et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...698A..89F/abstract). 

The advent of JWST has upended traditional timelines for early galaxy assembly, revealing populations of unexpectedly massive, mature galaxies at $z > 10$. This rapid growth challenges conventional paradigms where feedback acts strictly as a [[negative-feedback|Negative feedback]] regulator. Consequently, determining whether [[positive-feedback-astrophysics|Positive feedback (astrophysics)]]—where AGN-driven outflows or jets compress gas to locally trigger starbursts—significantly contributes to early galaxy growth is a major open question. Recent theoretical frameworks propose that [[multi-channel-agn-feedback|Multi-channel AGN feedback]] may evolve from a short-lived, momentum-conserving phase that stimulates vigorous star formation at high redshifts, to late-time, energy-conserving outflows that quench star formation after $z \sim 6$ [Silk et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...961L..39S/abstract).

Breaking the theoretical degeneracies between different [[subgrid-physics-models|Subgrid physics models]] will require next-generation multi-wavelength diagnostics. At small cosmological scales, different feedback implementations imprint distinct signatures on the 21cm power spectrum, offering a novel probe for evaluating feedback models during the epoch of reionization through simulations like [[thesan|THESAN]]. Ultimately, reconciling the multi-phase nature of [[galactic-outflows-and-feedback|Galactic Outflows and Feedback]] across cosmic time requires seamlessly coupling these parsec-scale physical prescriptions with large-scale [[cosmological-hydrodynamic-simulations|Cosmological Hydrodynamic Simulations]].
