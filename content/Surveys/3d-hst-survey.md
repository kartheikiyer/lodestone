The **3D-HST survey** is a major near-infrared spectroscopic Treasury program that utilized [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope Deep Surveys]] to study [[mass-assembly-survey|Mass Assembly Survey]], structural transformation, and star formation during the peak epoch of [[cosmic-surveys|Cosmic surveys]], often termed "cosmic noon" ($1 < z < 3.5$) [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..200...13B/abstract). Operating across the five deep fields of the [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]], 3D-HST employed slitless grism spectroscopy—a technique that disperses the light of all objects in a telescope's field of view without the use of slits or fibers. Building upon the statistical baselines established by earlier wide-area and multi-wavelength campaigns [Colless et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001MNRAS.328.1039C/abstract), , 3D-HST provided spatially resolved, rest-frame optical [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]] for thousands of distant [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]]. This continuous spectral coverage bridged a critical gap in [[extragalactic-astronomical-surveys|Extragalactic astronomical surveys]], allowing astronomers to seamlessly link the structural morphologies of high-redshift galaxies with their internal kinematics and stellar populations.

A foundational achievement of 3D-HST is its derivation of highly accurate [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|Spectrophotometric redshifts]], which combine low-resolution grism spectra with broadband photometry to dramatically reduce the uncertainties and "catastrophic outliers" inherent to purely photometric [[galaxy-redshift-survey|Galaxy redshift survey]] catalogs. These precise distance measurements enabled rigorous characterizations of the size-mass relation, revealing that massive early-type galaxies underwent rapid structural evolution and were significantly more compact in the early universe than their late-type counterparts [van der Wel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...28V/abstract). Furthermore, the survey's extensive catalog of emission-line fluxes has provided robust constraints on star formation stochasticity, environmental quenching timescales, and interstellar dust conditions. In particular, the data strongly support a two-component dust model where actively star-forming H II regions experience significantly greater attenuation than the older, integrated stellar continuum.

In the broader context of [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]], 3D-HST has acted as a vital connective bridge, supplying the pre-selection catalogs and morpho-kinematic baselines necessary for targeted ground-based multi-object and [[spatially-resolved-galaxy-analysis|Spatially Resolved Galaxy Analysis]] campaigns, such as the [[mosdef-survey|MOSDEF survey]] and the [[kmos3d-survey|KMOS3D survey]]. Despite its sweeping successes, the survey has highlighted several open questions that continue to drive extragalactic research. These include unexplained inconsistencies between dynamical and stellar mass estimates in high-redshift mergers, the extent to which "progenitor bias" affects the observed size-mass evolution of post-starburst galaxies [Zhang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240721257Z/abstract), and the looming methodological challenge of whether future wide-field space missions can achieve their required redshift accuracy without the intensive, multi-wavelength [[ancillary-data|Ancillary data]] historically provided by the CANDELS fields. Ultimately, the legacy of 3D-HST continues to deeply inform the observational strategies of modern facilities like the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] and the [[vera-c-rubin-observatory-lsst|Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST)]].

## Overview of the 3D-HST Treasury Program

Operating primarily with the Wide Field Camera 3 (WFC3) G141 grism, alongside parallel optical observations using the Advanced Camera for Surveys (ACS) G800L grism, the 3D-HST survey maps approximately $625$ arcmin$^2$—covering three quarters of the five [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]] fields [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..200...13B/abstract). 

The primary scientific motivation of 3D-HST is to study the physical processes that drive [[galaxy-mass-assembly-ultra-deep-spectroscopic-survey|Galaxy Mass Assembly ultra-deep Spectroscopic Survey]] during the critical epoch when approximately 60% of all [[cosmic-surveys|Cosmic surveys]] star formation occurred, quasar activity peaked, and the structural regularity of modern galaxies emerged [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..200...13B/abstract). By providing continuous rest-frame optical spectra from $1.1$ to $1.6$ $\mu\mathrm{m}$ at a high [[galaxy-survey-specifications|Galaxy survey specifications]] of $\sim 0\farcs13$, 3D-HST enables the extraction of spatially resolved kinematic and structural properties, extending traditional emission-line and continuum studies out to cosmic noon. Furthermore, forward modeling techniques applied to the slitless spectroscopy have proven capable of constraining basic rotation curve parameters—such as plateau velocity and central velocity gradients—in well-resolved galaxies, effectively mitigating the self-contamination issues inherent to slitless methods [Outini et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...633A..43O/abstract).

Prior to the advent of the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]], the synthesis of 3D-HST rest-frame optical spectra with [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]] imaging provided the definitive dataset for studying the evolution, mass assembly, and structural transformation of roughly 7,000 galaxies during the universe's most active star-forming epoch [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..200...13B/abstract). Beyond its primary extragalactic focus, the survey has also importantly contributed to Galactic astronomy; observations from the 3D-HST and WISPS parallel fields identified over 150 ultracool dwarfs, providing deep baseline data used to constrain the scaleheights and ages of Milky Way stellar populations [Aganze et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...934...73A/abstract). The application of near-infrared [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]] methodologies also conceptually links such extragalactic efforts to dedicated Galactic surveys like the [[apogee-survey|APOGEE survey]], which utilize high-resolution H-band spectroscopy to decode stellar populations and chemical abundances in a complementary manner [Majewski et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017AJ....154...94M/abstract).

## Historical Evolution of Grism Spectroscopy

The historical progression of slitless [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]]—from early photographic plates to the precision near-infrared capabilities of the 3D-HST survey—reflects broader advancements in detector technology and [[deep-field-campaigns|Deep field campaigns]]. 

Early implementations of the technique primarily took the form of the objective-prism survey, which utilized Schmidt telescopes and photographic plates to capture low-dispersion spectra over wide fields [Smith et al. (1975)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1975ApJ...202..591S/abstract). While highly efficient for identifying bright emission-line galaxies and quasars, these photographic studies were fundamentally limited by non-linear detector responses and high sky backgrounds.

The transition to digital detectors marked a major milestone for [[sky-mapping-and-surveys|Sky Mapping and Surveys]]. The KPNO International Spectroscopic Survey (KISS) at [[kitt-peak-national-observatory|Kitt Peak National Observatory]] became the first purely digital objective-prism survey [Salzer et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000AJ....120...80S/abstract). By coupling the objective-prism method with modern CCD detectors, KISS significantly extended the technique's faint flux limits, successfully measuring [O III] $\lambda 5007$ and H$\alpha$ emission in galaxies approaching $z \approx 0.1$ [Salzer et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000AJ....120...80S/abstract), [Salzer et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002AJ....123.1292S/abstract). 

Despite these improvements, ground-based slitless spectroscopy was long considered a complicated and confused technique. Because the entire field of view is dispersed, the sky background remains high, and overlapping spectra from adjacent sources severely contaminate the data—an issue known as the "self-contamination" effect [Outini et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...633A..43O/abstract). Consequently, extracting reliable kinematic or structural information for faint extragalactic sources was exceptionally challenging.

HST grism evolution revolutionized the field by moving these observations above the atmosphere. The combination of extremely low sky background and the high spatial resolution of [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope Deep Surveys]] largely mitigated historical crowding issues, making space-based slitless spectroscopy a uniquely powerful tool for [[extragalactic-astronomical-surveys|Extragalactic astronomical surveys]] [Outini et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...633A..43O/abstract). This evolution included optical components like the ACS/G800L grism and culminated in the near-infrared capabilities of the WFC3. The 3D-HST survey represents the apex of this progression, employing advanced forward-modeling of dispersed 2D spectra to cleanly disentangle overlapping sources and achieve unprecedented redshift and velocity precisions for faint galaxies at high redshift [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..200...13B/abstract), [Morris et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015AJ....149..178M/abstract). This robust capability was critical in evaluating even the most extreme high-redshift candidates. For example, 3D-HST grism observations of the candidate $z \sim 12$ galaxy UDFj-39546284 identified a tentative emission line that proved to likely be [O III] at $z=2.19$, rather than [[lyman-break-selection-and-galaxies|Lyman-Break Selection and Galaxies]] at a significantly higher redshift [Brammer et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...765L...2B/abstract).

## Redshift Determination and Spectrophotometric Precision

A core achievement of the 3D-HST survey is its ability to bridge the gap between deep imaging and high-resolution spectroscopy by combining HST/WFC3 G141 grism spectra with extensive multi-wavelength broadband photometry from fields such as the [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]]. By utilizing template-fitting tools such as [[eazy|EAZY]] to integrate these datasets, researchers are able to derive highly accurate [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|Spectrophotometric redshifts]] (SPZs), thereby reducing the reliance on purely photometric methods that are prone to degeneracies and catastrophic outliers.

The integration of slitless grism data with broadband photometry yields a redshift precision of $\sigma(z) \approx 0.003(1+z)$, corresponding to a rest-frame [[galaxy-survey-specifications|Galaxy survey specifications]] of $\sigma(v) \approx 1000$ km s$^{-1}$ for galaxies at $1 < z < 3.5$ [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..200...13B/abstract), [Bezanson et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...822...30B/abstract), [Morris et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015AJ....149..178M/abstract). This methodology represents a $17\%$ to $60\%$ improvement in overall redshift accuracy over traditional photometric methods, primarily by tightly constraining the positions of the 4000 $\mathrmmathrm{\AA}$ and Balmer breaks. These capabilities contrast notably with contemporary ground-based efforts such as the PRIsm MUlti-object Survey (PRIMUS), which utilized a low-dispersion prism to achieve a redshift precision of $\sigma(z) \approx 0.005$. While PRIMUS successfully mapped a much larger, albeit shallower, sample of approximately 130,000 galaxies, 3D-HST provided superior precision and resolution for faint, high-redshift sources [Cool et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...767..118C/abstract). Advanced template-fitting routines for SPZs evaluate the [[pseudo-spectroscopic-redshifts|Pseudo-spectroscopic redshifts]] probability density function by simultaneously fitting broadband fluxes and low-dispersion spectra. This approach successfully accounts for correlated noise between adjacent spectral pixels using a covariance matrix formalism, while also incorporating the morphological broadening of the galaxy along the grism dispersion axis [Joshi et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...883..157J/abstract). 

The high-fidelity grism redshifts provided by 3D-HST have been instrumental in diagnosing and mitigating "catastrophic outliers" present in standard photometric catalogs [Raihan et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.497.1404R/abstract). Comparisons between 3D-HST grism redshifts and pure photo-$z$ catalogs reveal that photometric scatter is not uniform across the galaxy population. While typical galaxies exhibit a photo-$z$ scatter of $\sigma_z \approx 0.01 - 0.03(1+z)$, dusty, red star-forming systems (with rest-frame $U-V > 2$) demonstrate significantly elevated scatter, sometimes reaching $\sim 0.1(1+z)$ [Bezanson et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...822...30B/abstract). Furthermore, photometric redshift accuracy exhibits a strong, previously under-appreciated dependence on galaxy size, a factor analyzed in [[k-selected-surveys|K-selected surveys]]. At a fixed magnitude, the largest galaxies exhibit up to $30\%$ more scatter and a five-fold increase in outlier rates compared to compact sources [Bezanson et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...822...30B/abstract).

Finally, the 3D-HST dataset has been used to calibrate the reliability of standard error estimates in photometric surveys. Analyses indicate that purely photometric redshift probability density functions typically underestimate the true redshift uncertainty by a factor of $1.1$ to $1.6$. Importantly, simply broadening the probability density function uniformly does not sufficiently account for catastrophic fitting outliers [Bezanson et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...822...30B/abstract). To refine modeling approaches and mitigate such errors, 3D-HST spectroscopic catalogs have increasingly been utilized to train machine learning algorithms (such as SPIDERz in the [[galaxy-integral-field-spectroscopy-tool|Galaxy Integral-field Spectroscopy Tool]]), testing whether the inclusion of galaxy shape and morphological information could improve photometric redshift estimation, though studies found it provided no statistically significant benefit [Jones et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...600A.113J/abstract). This underscores the necessity of grism-calibrated spectrophotometric redshifts for achieving the precision required in modern demographic and environmental galaxy studies. Building on this legacy, the ASTRODEEP-JWST project (a component of [[future-astronomical-facilities-and-surveys|Future Astronomical Facilities and Surveys]]) has produced consistent photometric and redshift catalogs for half a million sources across the 3D-HST fields, continuing to enhance the precision of extragalactic demographic studies [Merlin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.240M/abstract).

## Structural Parameters and the Size-Mass Relation

By combining precise spectroscopic and photometric redshifts with robust [[galaxy-mass-assembly-ultra-deep-spectroscopic-survey|Galaxy Mass Assembly ultra-deep Spectroscopic Survey]], the 3D-HST survey has provided a foundational dataset for tracing the structural evolution of galaxies. When coupled with high-resolution near-infrared imaging from the [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]], these data establish a comprehensive view of the size-mass distribution of galaxies from $z=3$ to the present [van der Wel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...28V/abstract). To quantify the spatial extent and shapes of these galaxies, researchers fit two-dimensional Sersic profiles to the imaging data, extracting the effective radius ($R_{\rm eff}$) as a primary structural parameter. 

The high fidelity of 3D-HST measurements, particularly the ability to cleanly separate [[massive-galaxy-catalogue|Massive Galaxy Catalogue]] members (ETGs) and [[ohio-state-bright-spiral-galaxy-survey|Ohio State Bright Spiral Galaxy Survey]] types (LTGs) using rest-frame colors and star formation activity, reveals distinct structural scaling laws. At fixed stellar mass, early-type galaxies are systematically smaller than their late-type counterparts across all observed redshifts [van der Wel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...28V/abstract). Furthermore, the size-mass distribution exhibits significantly different slopes depending on the galaxy population. For massive early-type galaxies ($M_* > 2 \times 10^{10}\ M_\odot$), the size-mass relation is steep, scaling as $R_{\rm eff} \propto M_*^{0.75}$. In contrast, late-type galaxies with $M_* > 3 \times 10^9\ M_\odot$ follow a much shallower slope of $R_{\rm eff} \propto M_*^{0.22}$ [van der Wel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...28V/abstract). Despite these population-level differences, the intrinsic scatter in the size-mass relation remains remarkably tight, measured at $\lesssim 0.2$ dex for all galaxy types and across all redshifts.

The large dynamic range of the survey in both mass and redshift allows for a detailed assessment of morphological evolution over cosmic time. Both populations were substantially more compact in the early universe, but they grow at fundamentally different rates. Early-type galaxies undergo rapid size evolution, with their average effective radius increasing proportionally to $(1+z)^{-1.48}$. Late-type galaxies evolve more moderately, scaling as $(1+z)^{-0.75}$ [van der Wel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...28V/abstract). Interestingly, the logarithmic size distribution of late-type galaxies is not symmetric; it exhibits a pronounced tail skewed toward smaller sizes. Consequently, a subset of compact late-type galaxies overlaps in size with the early-type galaxy population at all redshifts and masses. Understanding the impact of spatial resolution on these measured structural parameters is critical; simulations based on the [[califa-survey|CALIFA survey]] demonstrate how the recovery of H II region distributions and metallicities degrades as spatial resolution decreases with higher redshifts, underscoring the necessity of space-based imaging for accurate morphological assessments [Mast et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...561A.129M/abstract). More recently, data from the [[desi-bright-galaxy-survey|DESI Bright Galaxy Survey]] utilized the structures of 3D-HST quiescent and star-forming galaxies as a comprehensive baseline to show that massive post-starburst galaxies at $z \sim 1.2$ possess highly dense cores and are significantly more compact than the general quiescent population, offering vital clues to how progenitor bias governs observed structural timelines [Zhang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240721257Z/abstract).

The 3D-HST data also reveal a distinct timeline for the formation and subsequent structural evolution of the most extreme dense systems. The number density of massive ($\sim 10^{11}\ M_\odot$), compact ($R_{\rm eff} < 2$ kpc) early-type galaxies is observed to increase from $z=3$, peaking around cosmic noon ($z \sim 1.5-2$), before strongly decreasing at later cosmic times [van der Wel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...28V/abstract). The accurate derivation of these evolutionary trends relies heavily on the survey's robust stellar mass measurements, which are supported by tight empirical correlations between stellar mass and near-infrared absolute magnitude identified for [[lyman-break-selection-and-galaxies|Lyman-Break Selection and Galaxies]] in the $1.16 < z < 1.90$ regime [Nagaraj et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...912..145N/abstract). Together, these findings demonstrate how the mass assembly of galaxies is intricately linked to their structural transformations.

## Star Formation and Chemical Enrichment at Cosmic Noon

The 3D-HST survey leverages near-infrared slitless [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]] to access rest-frame optical emission lines for galaxies at $1 < z < 3.5$. By operating across the fields established by the [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]], 3D-HST maps tracers such as H$\alpha$ emission and [O III] $\lambda 5007$ to provide a robust census of high-redshift [[lyman-break-selection-and-galaxies|Lyman-Break Selection and Galaxies]]. At $1.90 < z < 2.35$, grism-selected [O III]-emitting galaxies alone account for roughly half of the total [[galaxy-mass-assembly-ultra-deep-spectroscopic-survey|Galaxy Mass Assembly ultra-deep Spectroscopic Survey]] rate density [Bowman et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...920...78B/abstract). 

Accurate measurements of star formation depend critically on understanding dust attenuation. By properly correcting for dust reddening, researchers can evaluate the stochasticity, or "burstiness," of star formation [Price et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...86P/abstract). By comparing short-timescale tracers like H$\alpha$ emission to longer-timescale near-ultraviolet fluxes—a burst indicator denoted as $\eta = \log_{10}(\mathrm{SFR}_{\mathrm{H}\alpha}/\mathrm{SFR}_{\mathrm{NUV}})$—researchers have measured an intrinsic star formation stochasticity of $0.06$ to $0.16$ dex at $z \sim 1$, providing critical constraints for hydrodynamical simulations. Additionally, studies of 3D-HST data have demonstrated that standard UVJ selection diagrams saturate at very low specific star formation rates; hence, analyses have utilized 3D-HST data to propose new far-ultraviolet and mid-infrared (FUV/MIR) color criteria in [[panchromatic-surveys|Panchromatic surveys]] that select quiescent populations more efficiently [Leja et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...880L...9L/abstract).

3D-HST’s high spatial resolution also enables the mapping of starburst geometries. In visually identified mergers at $z \sim 1.5$, spatially resolved emission-line maps reveal that star formation is frequently concentrated in a single compact region coinciding with one of the progenitor nuclei. This conflicts with predictions from some idealized merger simulations, which expect star formation to be distributed across both components when gas fractions are equal [Schmidt et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..285S/abstract). Conversely, complementary efforts such as the AGHAST survey in GOODS-N expanded on 3D-HST constraints by identifying that Luminous and Ultraluminous Infrared Galaxies (LIRGs and ULIRGs) at high redshift often exhibit broadly distributed, spatially resolved star formation rather than purely compact central bursts [Weiner et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014AAS...22322707W/abstract). To properly interpret these spatially resolved emission maps at high redshift, researchers often rely on detailed physical baselines established in the local universe, such as those provided by the [[phangs-survey|PHANGS survey]], which maps the interstellar medium of nearby disc galaxies at cloud-scale (50 pc) resolutions [Emsellem et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...659A.191E/abstract).

Alongside star formation, the survey provides vital constraints on chemical enrichment and [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]]. The 3D-HST catalog of emission-line galaxies at $1.2 < z < 1.9$ closely tracks the established $z \sim 2$ mass-metallicity relation [Nagaraj et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...912..145N/abstract). Furthermore, high-resolution grism observations of strongly lensed sources, such as the $z = 1.847$ starburst dwarf SL2SJ02176-0513, capture extreme low-metallicity environments undergoing intense bursts of star formation, providing high-redshift analogs to local [[montreal-blue-galaxy-survey|Montreal Blue Galaxy survey]] examples [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...758L..17B/abstract). The 3D-HST spectroscopic catalogs have also proven vital in the search for escaping extreme ultraviolet radiation; for example, they were combined with ultra-deep [[canada-france-imaging-survey|Canada-France Imaging Survey]] (CLAUDS) $u$-band data to identify candidate [[ultra-high-z-galaxy-search|Ultra-high-z galaxy search]] candidates (LyC emitting galaxies) at $3.5 < z < 5.1$, providing critical insights into the sources that drove cosmic reionization [Meštrić et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.494.4986M/abstract).

Despite the constraints provided by emission lines, interpreting the overarching star formation history of the universe remains sensitive to modeling systematics. Applying non-parametric Bayesian spectral energy distribution fitting (such as the Prospector-$\alpha$ framework) to the panchromatic 3D-HST photometry yields inferred star formation rates that are $0.1$ to $>1$ dex lower than standard UV+IR estimates, alongside stellar masses that are $0.1$ to $0.3$ dex higher [Leja et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...877..140L/abstract). This discrepancy is largely driven by the updated models attributing more rest-frame emission to older stellar populations ($t > 100$ Myr). If confirmed, this paradigm shift implies that the universe at cosmic noon was older and more quiescent than previously assumed, potentially resolving longstanding tensions between the integrated cosmic star formation rate density and observed [[stellar-mass-function|Stellar Mass Function]] results [Leja et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...877..140L/abstract).

## Dust Attenuation and Interstellar Medium Conditions

The combination of rest-frame optical [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]] and multi-wavelength photometry in the 3D-HST survey provides a powerful tool for probing the [[things-survey|THINGS survey]] (interstellar medium) of high-redshift galaxies. A key diagnostic is the Balmer decrement, which allows for direct measurements of the dust attenuation toward nebular H II regions ($A_{V,\mathrm{HII}}$). Stacking analyses of galaxies at $1.36 \le z \le 1.5$ demonstrate that the nebular attenuation is systematically higher than the integrated stellar continuum attenuation ($A_{V,\mathrm{star}}$), following the relation $A_{V,\mathrm{HII}} \approx 1.86 A_{V,\mathrm{star}}$ [Price et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...86P/abstract). This quantitative offset strongly supports a two-component dust model, wherein young stars in birth clouds experience greater local extinction than older stellar populations residing in the diffuse ISM. Furthermore, the degree of extra nebular attenuation correlates with global galaxy properties: it increases with total [[mass-assembly-survey|Mass Assembly Survey]] and star formation rate (SFR), but decreases with increasing specific star formation rate (sSFR) [Price et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...788...86P/abstract). Utilizing empirical relations anchored by 3D-HST observations, researchers developed the "DustE" population model, which applies the survey's robust attenuation signatures to produce much more realistic [[panchromatic-surveys|Panchromatic surveys]] diagrams (UVJ) for synthetic, dusty star-forming galaxies in [[cosmic-surveys|Cosmic surveys]] like the TNG100 simulations [Nagaraj et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...29N/abstract). 

Spatially resolved studies leveraging 3D-HST catalogs alongside early [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] imaging further indicate that the prominent color gradients observed in $z \sim 2$ galaxies are primarily driven by radially decreasing dust attenuation rather than gradients in stellar age [Miller et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...941L..37M/abstract).

In the most extreme environments, such as compact star-forming galaxies at $z \sim 2.2$, the ISM exhibits starburst-like physical conditions. Follow-up observations of a specific 3D-HST target (GS30274) reveal exceptional ISM properties, including high gas densities ($\sim 6 \times 10^4$ cm$^{-3}$) and intense UV radiation fields ($\sim 2 \times 10^4 G_0$). This compact core is converting gas to stars with remarkable efficiency, yielding a gas depletion time of $t_{\mathrm{H2,dep}} \le 140$ Myr, which is significantly shorter than that of typical extended star-forming galaxies at the same epoch [Popping et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...602A..11P/abstract).

## Galaxy Quenching and Environmental Influences

The precise [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|Spectrophotometric redshifts]] provided by the 3D-HST survey have enabled robust measurements of [[red-sequence-cluster-survey|Red-sequence Cluster Survey]] (environmental density) and large-scale structure during "cosmic noon" ($z \sim 1-2$). By accurately placing galaxies within their local environments, researchers can trace the role of structure in driving [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]] and isolate the mechanisms responsible for galaxy transformation. 

A primary application of the 3D-HST dataset has been quantifying the onset and timescales of satellite quenching. Using a catalog of environmental measures derived from 3D-HST and the [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]], Fossati et al. investigated galaxies down to a magnitude limit of $JH_{140} < 24$ over the redshift range $0.5 < z < 3.0$ [Fossati et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...835..153F/abstract). They determined that the characteristic timescale for satellite quenching is $t_{\rm quench} \sim 2-5 \text{ Gyr}$. The quenching process exhibits a prolonged "delay phase," during which satellite galaxies maintain star formation rates comparable to central galaxies, followed by a rapid, exponential drop in star formation ($\tau_f \sim 0.4-0.6 \text{ Gyr}$). Crucially, this quenching timescale is found to be remarkably independent of [[madcows|MaDCoWS]] (halo mass) for environments up to $M_h \lesssim 10^{14} M_\odot$. This independence suggests that satellite quenching at high redshift is driven primarily by "starvation"—the gradual exhaustion of a retained multi-phase gas reservoir—rather than by rapid environmental stripping mechanisms such as ram pressure.

Large-scale overdensities, frequently traced by extended Ly$\alpha$ nebulae, also exhibit pronounced environmental signatures in 3D-HST data. A study of 86 galaxies in the vicinity of six Ly$\alpha$ nebulae at $z \sim 2.3$ in the [[goods-survey|GOODS survey]] South field revealed that galaxies within 320 proper kpc of a nebula are systematically altered by their environment [Wells et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...941..180W/abstract). Compared to isolated field galaxies, these overdense populations are approximately $0.5$ magnitudes brighter, and possess higher stellar masses, higher star formation rates, and larger effective radii. Despite these enhancements, the galaxies remain on the standard star-forming main sequence, indicating that these rich environments accelerate mass assembly without immediately triggering the quenching process. Furthermore, stacking analyses of Ly$\alpha$ halos around [O III]-selected 3D-HST galaxies at $1.9 < z < 2.35$ demonstrate that while the outer halos ($r > 40 \text{ kpc}$) match typical Ly$\alpha$ emitters, the inner regions ($r < 10 \text{ kpc}$) are an order of magnitude fainter [Lujan Niemeyer et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...934L..26L/abstract). This suggests that the Ly$\alpha$ photons dominating these massive halos often originate from the circumgalactic medium or the broader overdense environment rather than the central galaxies themselves.

Finally, high-density environments at $z \sim 1$ are characterized by elevated merger fractions, which complicate kinematic classifications. Analyses combining 3D-HST pair catalogs with integral-field kinematics indicate that approximately one-fourth of high-redshift galaxies kinetically classified as rotating disks are actually in close pairs and affected by perturbations [Rodrigues et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.1157R/abstract). This underscores that hierarchical assembly and active merging sequences remain primary drivers of galaxy transformation within high-redshift overdensities.

## Synergies with Ground-Based and Multiwavelength Surveys

The 3D-HST survey operates as a critical bridge between space-based [[deep-imaging-surveys|Deep imaging surveys]] and ground-based spectroscopic campaigns. By combining WFC3/G141 near-infrared slitless spectroscopy with the extensive [[ancillary-data|Ancillary data]] available in the [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]] fields, 3D-HST provides a robust baseline of physical properties—such as stellar masses, star formation rates, and rest-frame colors—for galaxies at cosmic noon ($1 < z < 3.5$) [Brammer et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..200...13B/abstract). This foundational precision fundamentally underpins target selection and environmental contextualization for subsequent higher-resolution multiwavelength follow-up.

3D-HST has acted as a primary target catalog for targeted, high-resolution multi-object spectroscopy. A prominent example is the [[mosdef-survey|MOSDEF survey]], which utilized 3D-HST photometric and spectroscopic catalogs to identify approximately 1,500 galaxies at $1.37 \leq z \leq 3.8$ [Kriek et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJS..218...15K/abstract). Using the [[k-band-multi-object-spectrograph|K-band Multi-Object Spectrograph]] (Keck/MOSFIRE), MOSDEF obtained moderate-resolution ($R \approx 3000-3650$) rest-frame optical spectra to study nebular emission and stellar kinematics. The project relied on 3D-HST to pre-select targets across diverse galaxy populations (e.g., unobscured, dusty, and quiescent systems) and to provide broad-band spectral energy distributions for detailed modeling of merger histories and star formation stochasticity [Kriek et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJS..218...15K/abstract), [Runco et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.4405R/abstract). Similarly, the [[kmos3d-survey|KMOS3D survey]] has leveraged 3D-HST redshifts and morphological data to pre-select targets and contextualize integral-field kinematic maps of galaxies at $z \sim 1$. The combination of 3D-HST morpho-kinematic baselines and KMOS3D spatially resolved data has been crucial in distinguishing isolated, rotating disks from merging systems within the hierarchical assembly framework [Rodrigues et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.1157R/abstract). Another key [[deep-spectroscopic-surveys|Deep spectroscopic surveys]] follow-up project is the VANDELS ESO public spectroscopic survey, which utilized ultra-deep VIMOS spectroscopy in the CANDELS-UDS and CDFS fields to probe the physics, metallicities, and outflows of star-forming galaxies at $z > 3$.

Beyond individual targets, 3D-HST properties are actively used to map massive large-scale structure. In extensive cross-correlation studies with the CLAMATO Lyman-alpha forest, researchers have utilized 3D-HST foreground galaxies to effectively measure halo masses and characterize large-scale galaxy bias at $z \sim 2.3$ [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250907453Z/abstract). To contextualize these high-redshift environments, researchers frequently compare them against vital low-redshift benchmarks like the [[gama-survey|GAMA survey]], which provides high-fidelity catalogs of local groups and pairs, enabling comprehensive comparative environmental studies across cosmic time [Driver et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013IAUS..295..155D/abstract).

Furthermore, the high-fidelity redshifts from 3D-HST play a vital role in [[kilo-degree-survey-kids|Kilo-Degree Survey (KiDS)]] (weak lensing) calibration for wide-area cosmological surveys. Accurate weak lensing mass estimates require highly precise constraints on the redshift distribution of background source galaxies. Consequently, 3D-HST catalogs serve as high-quality reference samples to identify and correct for "catastrophic outliers" in broad-band photometric redshift distributions [Bezanson et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...822...30B/abstract), [Raihan et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.497.1404R/abstract). Uncorrected, these outliers would systematically bias the mass proxies used in galaxy cluster surveys. The precise error characterization derived from the 3D-HST fields—including data from the [[cs82-survey|CS82 Survey]] and the [[vera-c-rubin-observatory-lsst|Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST)]]—is therefore heavily utilized to optimize the performance of photometric redshift algorithms for current and future wide-field facilities [Bezanson et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...822...30B/abstract), [Raihan et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.497.1404R/abstract), [de Jong et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013Msngr.154...44D/abstract).

As a large-scale space-based slitless spectroscopic campaign, 3D-HST also serves as a direct precursor to the wide-field grism surveys planned for upcoming [[future-astronomical-facilities-and-surveys|Future Astronomical Facilities and Surveys]]. The empirical [O III] $\lambda 5007$ luminosity functions and number density predictions derived from 3D-HST are directly informing the target selection and survey strategies for these next-generation observatories [Joshi et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...883..157J/abstract), [Bowman et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...920...78B/abstract). For instance, the reference design for the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope relies on these legacies, planning to map 10 million H$\alpha$ redshifts to precisely study cosmic expansion [Wang et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...928....1W/abstract). 

## Future Directions: From 3D-HST to JWST and Beyond

The 3D-HST survey demonstrated the immense scientific return of space-based, wide-field near-infrared slitless [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]]. The methodological legacy of 3D-HST directly informs the observational strategies, target selection, and data analysis techniques for the next generation of [[cosmic-surveys|Cosmic surveys]].

Future wide-field space missions will rely heavily on slitless grism spectroscopy to map large-scale structure and galaxy evolution over unprecedented volumes. 3D-HST has served as a critical pathfinder for characterizing the populations these missions will observe. For instance, the [O III] $\lambda 5007$ luminosity function derived from 3D-HST grism data at $z \sim 2$ provides a foundational calibration for the expected number counts and galaxy bias of emission-line galaxies that will be targeted in the near future [Bowman et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...920...78B/abstract). Furthermore, the tight correlation between rest-frame near-infrared absolute magnitude and [[mass-assembly-survey|Mass Assembly Survey]] established by 3D-HST will enable the rapid calculation of stellar masses for the millions of galaxies these new observatories will detect [Nagaraj et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...912..145N/abstract). 

Techniques pioneered on 3D-HST data are also driving the analysis pipelines for future [[future-astronomical-facilities-and-surveys|Future Astronomical Facilities and Surveys]]. Forward-modeling of overlapping slitless spectra has proven capable of recovering basic rotation curve parameters, mitigating the primary drawback of spectral self-contamination [Outini et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020A&A...633A..43O/abstract). Despite these advances, an open question remains regarding whether upcoming wide-area surveys can achieve their stringent redshift accuracy requirements without the intensive multi-wavelength ancillary data currently available in well-studied regions like the [[candels-survey|CANDELS survey]] fields [Joshi et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...883..157J/abstract). Concurrently, ground-based counterparts like the [[vera-c-rubin-observatory-lsst|Vera C. Rubin Observatory (LSST)]] are refining their own observational strategies. Furthermore, important future synergies will emerge from radio facilities; the [[meerkat-absorption-line-survey|MeerKAT Absorption Line Survey]] (MIGHTEE-HI), for instance, measures the neutral hydrogen content of galaxies out to $z = 0.6$, complementing 3D-HST's established constraints on stellar mass and star formation histories.

While 3D-HST extensively mapped the peak epoch of galaxy assembly at cosmic noon, the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] is now pushing this spectroscopic frontier even earlier into the universe's history. Programs such as [[jades|JADES]] (JWST Advanced Deep Extragalactic Survey) utilize deep [[nirspec|NIRSpec]] microshutter assembly (MSA) spectroscopy and NIRCam imaging to characterize galaxies from $z = 0.5$ up to $z > 13$ [D'Eugenio et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240406531D/abstract), [D'Eugenio et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJS..277....4D/abstract). Other robust successor missions build on this framework: the UNCOVER Treasury program provides ultra-deep NIRSpec spectroscopy and NIRCam imaging of clusters like those in the [[massive-cluster-survey|MAssive Cluster Survey]], while the MEGA survey utilizes JWST MIRI mid-infrared imaging covering nearly 70% of the CEERS NIRCam area in the [[extended-groth-strip|Extended Groth strip]] (EGS) to fill critical gaps in our understanding of heavily obscured galaxy evolution and [[swift-bat-agn-spectroscopic-survey|Swift-BAT AGN Spectroscopic Survey]] (active galactic nuclei) at cosmic noon [Suess et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240413132S/abstract), [Backhaus et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025AJ....170..300B/abstract). Early JWST observations are already building upon 3D-HST targets, helping to firmly constrain the demographics and quenching timescales of galaxies in the early universe [Wells et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...941..180W/abstract). Pushing the frontier even further, JWST's GLIMPSE survey utilizes the immense strong gravitational lensing of clusters to probe background galaxies deep into the epoch of cosmic reionization [Atek et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251107542A/abstract).
