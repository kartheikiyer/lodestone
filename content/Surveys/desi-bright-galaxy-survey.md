## Article

The Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument ([[desi-survey|DESI survey]]) Bright Galaxy Survey (BGS) is a massive, highly complete [[galaxy-redshift-survey|Galaxy redshift survey]] designed to map the [[three-dimensional-galaxy-surveys|three-dimensional distribution]] of approximately 10 million galaxies in the local universe. Conducted primarily during bright lunar observing conditions—when elevated sky backgrounds preclude the efficient observation of fainter, high-redshift targets—the BGS covers approximately 14,000 [[survey-area|square degrees]] of the extragalactic sky [DESI Collaboration et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016arXiv161100036D/abstract), [Hahn et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165..253H/abstract). By securing high-precision spectroscopic [[redshift-catalogs|redshifts]] out to $z \approx 0.6$, with a median [[redshift-distribution|Redshift distribution]] of $z \approx 0.2$, the survey's primary cosmological objective is to trace the [[low-redshift-large-scale-structure|Low-redshift large-scale structure]]. This dense mapping enables the most precise local measurements to date of Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) and Redshift-Space Distortions (RSD), which act as vital probes for constraining the cosmic expansion history and the growth rate of large-scale structure [Hahn et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165..253H/abstract).

The survey builds upon the foundational legacy of earlier mapping efforts, scaling their magnitude-limited approaches to unprecedented cosmological volumes. Beyond pure spatial mapping, BGS spectra are analyzed using advanced Bayesian frameworks to extract full probabilistic posterior distributions for physical properties, revolutionizing local [[extragalactic-astronomy|galaxy demographic studies]]. The immense statistical power of the BGS has also highlighted several open questions in extragalactic astrophysics. Analyses of BGS clustering report a satellite galaxy assembly bias consistent with zero, challenging standard empirical models of galaxy formation [Shao et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251020896S/abstract). Furthermore, spatial statistics indicate no clear transition to cosmic homogeneity even at scales of $400\,h^{-1}\text{Mpc}$, suggesting that the local universe exhibits Gumbel-distributed density fluctuations rather than the Gaussian distribution expected for a spatially homogeneous field, indicating no clear transition to spatial homogeneity on these scales [Sylos Labini et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251121585S/abstract). 

## Historical Context and Survey Lineage

The architectural foundation of the [[desi-survey|DESI survey]] BGS is built upon several generations of [[large-galaxy-catalogs|extragalactic catalogs]] and [[redshift-catalogs|redshift surveys]] that progressively mapped the low-redshift universe. Historically, the "Bright Galaxy Survey" nomenclature was previously utilized for the [[pscz|IRAS 60-micron flux-limited sample]], which provided an early, foundational infrared census of the local universe [Sanders et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995AJ....110.1993S/abstract). Early efforts to systematically classify [[nearby-galaxies-catalog|nearby galaxies]] in the optical culminated in the [[rc3-catalog|RC3 catalog]], which compiled photometric and morphological data for $\sim 23,000$ objects [de Vaucouleurs et al. (1991)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1991rc3..book.....D/abstract). Other targeted efforts, such as the [[ohio-state-bright-spiral-galaxy-survey|Ohio State Bright Spiral Galaxy Survey]], mapped the near-infrared and optical morphology of bright nearby spirals [Eskridge et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJS..143...73E/abstract). As observational techniques advanced, the [[two-micron-all-sky-survey-2mass|Two Micron All-Sky Survey (2MASS)]] Extended Source Catalog provided an all-sky census of $\sim 10^6$ galaxies out to $K_s \leq 13.5$, minimizing dust obscuration in the Galactic plane and serving as the bedrock for the subsequent 2MASS Redshift Survey [Huchra et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJS..199...26H/abstract). Beyond the optical and infrared, [[blind-hi-surveys|blind 21 cm surveys]] like the [[hipass|HIPASS]] Bright Galaxy Catalog identified H I-bright galaxies in the southern sky, successfully cataloging [[low-luminosity-galaxies|low surface brightness systems]] often missed by optical surveys [Ryan-Weber et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002AJ....124.1954R/abstract).

The turn of the 21st century marked a paradigm shift from shallow imaging catalogs to dedicated, high-multiplexing spectroscopic campaigns. Two pioneering surveys, the [[2dfgrs-galaxy-catalogue|2dFGRS galaxy catalogue]] and the [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]] (SDSS), fundamentally transformed the study of [[low-redshift-large-scale-structure|Low-redshift large-scale structure]]. Using the [[apm-galaxy-catalogue|APM galaxy catalogue]] as its input, 2dFGRS obtained spectra for $\sim 250,000$ galaxies down to $b_J = 19.45$ [Colless et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001MNRAS.328.1039C/abstract). Concurrently, SDSS mapped over a quarter of the sky and, over its full legacy, eventually acquired $\sim 10^6$ galaxy redshifts (with foundational early data releases cataloging hundreds of thousands of galaxies [Blanton et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005AJ....129.2562B/abstract)). Together, these surveys rigorously quantified the [[galaxy-luminosity-function|Galaxy luminosity function]] and established that red, passively evolving galaxies are more strongly clustered than blue, [[emission-line-galaxies|star-forming galaxies]] [Lahav et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ntto.conf..145L/abstract). They also revealed large-scale environmental anomalies, such as a $\sim 30\%$ deficiency in galaxy counts in the Southern Galactic Cap—termed the "Local Hole" [Busswell et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004MNRAS.354..991B/abstract).

Subsequent observational programs refined this picture by pushing to greater depths and higher targeting completeness. The [[6df-galaxy-survey|6dF Galaxy Survey]] provided an expansive southern hemisphere sample [Jones et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009MNRAS.399..683J/abstract). Recently, this dataset was further utilized to compile a comprehensive catalog of over 2,500 broad-line [[swift-bat-agn-spectroscopic-survey|Active Galactic Nuclei]], a precursor effort that directly complements modern active galaxy targeting strategies like the DESI BGS-AGN selection [Jeat Hon et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241008536J/abstract). The [[gama-survey|GAMA survey]] provided a dense map down to $r < 19.8$ [Liske et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.2087L/abstract), achieving a remarkable $>98.5\%$ [[redshift-completeness-fraction|Redshift completeness fraction]] in its equatorial regions [Baldry et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.474.3875B/abstract). The DESI BGS represents the culmination of this decades-long trajectory, translating the highly complete, magnitude-limited approaches of these earlier localized surveys into a massive, cosmologically definitive dataset.

## Target Selection and Sample Design

To construct a highly complete, [[volume-limited-samples|magnitude-limited sample]], the BGS [[target-selection|Target selection]] pipeline utilizes optical and near-infrared photometry from the DESI [[legacy-surveys|Legacy surveys]] [Myers et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165...50M/abstract). Furthermore, the BGS utilizes auxiliary [[pseudo-spectroscopic-redshifts|photometric redshift]] and [[galaxy-surveys-and-catalogs|cluster catalogs]] derived from the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys DR9, which provide robust redshift and [[stellar-mass-function|stellar mass estimates]] for hundreds of millions of galaxies [Zou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022RAA....22f5001Z/abstract). These efforts are continually updated by large-scale photometric redshift campaigns; for example, the recent DR10 catalog provides photo-z estimates for over 1.5 billion galaxies processed via the EAZY and CatBoost algorithms [Li et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024AJ....168..233L/abstract). To handle extended sources within the footprint, the BGS relies on the Siena Galaxy Atlas (SGA-2020) to define highly accurate coordinates, sizes, and tailored photometric masks for large nearby galaxies [Moustakas et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJS..269....3M/abstract). The main survey is divided into two primary tiers based on $r$-band magnitude limits:
*   **BGS Bright**: A high-priority, essentially magnitude-limited sample of galaxies with $r < 19.5$. This tier yields a target density of approximately $800 \text{ deg}^{-2}$ [Ruiz-Macias et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020RNAAS...4..187R/abstract).
*   **BGS Faint**: A lower-priority sample targeting galaxies with $19.5 < r < 20.175$, incorporating additional surface brightness and color-selection criteria, resulting in a target density of approximately $600 \text{ deg}^{-2}$ [Ruiz-Macias et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020RNAAS...4..187R/abstract), [Hahn et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165..253H/abstract).

Robust star-galaxy separation is critical to defining the sample. The pipeline integrates Gaia photometry to distinguish extragalactic targets from foreground stars, restricting stellar contamination to $< 1\%$ [Ruiz-Macias et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502.4328R/abstract), [Hahn et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165..253H/abstract). However, this standard algorithm introduces a systematic tension by preferentially excluding [[swift-bat-agn-spectroscopic-survey|quasar host galaxies]], which appear morphologically stellar. To resolve this, a specialized BGS-AGN selection strategy was implemented to re-incorporate these active galactic nuclei into the survey, effectively recovering quasars and achieving $93\%$ purity [Juneau et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240403621J/abstract), [Juneau et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025AJ....169..157J/abstract). 

To further refine the catalog, DESI applies rigorous geometrical and photometric masking. These masks remove spurious targets originating from fragmented stellar halos and saturated pixels [Ruiz-Macias et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502.4328R/abstract). Ultimately, [[analysed-science-data-sets|early data releases]] demonstrate that the selected targets yield highly reliable catalogs [DESI Collaboration et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024AJ....168...58D/abstract), satisfying strict design requirements that ensure a [[redshift-completeness|Redshift completeness]] exceeding 95% across varied observing conditions [Hahn et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165..253H/abstract).

## Spectroscopic Pipeline and Physical Property Estimation

Beyond basic redshift extraction, the DESI [[spectroscopy-surveys|spectroscopic pipeline]] incorporates unsupervised anomaly detection using autoencoders to compress spectra into a redshift-invariant latent space. This framework isolates low-probability objects, successfully identifying rare quasar variants, galaxy mergers, and spectroscopically misclassified stars [Liang et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...956L...6L/abstract).

To derive detailed physical properties, the collaboration developed the PRObabilistic Value-Added BGS (PROVABGS) framework. Unlike precursor surveys that frequently provided single-point parameter estimates, PROVABGS employs a Bayesian approach to infer full posterior distributions for stellar mass ($M_*$), star formation rate (SFR), stellar metallicity ($Z$), and stellar age ($t_{\text{age}}$) [Hahn et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...945...16H/abstract). Because Bayesian SED modeling is computationally expensive for target catalogs containing over 10 million galaxies, PROVABGS utilizes a neural emulator for stellar population synthesis. This emulator accelerates posterior inference by a factor of 100 with negligible ($\ll 1\%$) error [Kwon et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJS..265...23K/abstract). 

Joint analysis of DESI spectroscopy and photometry from the Legacy surveys significantly tightens constraints on these physical properties. Using this framework, researchers have measured the probabilistic [[stellar-mass-function|Stellar Mass Function]] (pSMF) at low redshift. By using hierarchical population inference to rigorously propagate $M_*$ uncertainties, the BGS pSMF shows excellent agreement with previous literature [Hahn et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv230606318H/abstract), [Hahn et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...56H/abstract). Accurate SED modeling remains sensitive to wavelength coverage, however; omitting mid-infrared photometry during the fit can lead to artificially elevated AGN fractions, though it does not significantly affect the resulting stellar mass or star formation rate estimates [Siudek et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.308S/abstract). Additionally, these efforts are complemented by resources like RCSEDv2, which provides homogeneously re-processed [[panchromatic-surveys|multi-wavelength physical properties]] for millions of galaxies by integrating photometry from the DESI Legacy Survey with 10 other major spectroscopic surveys, establishing a vital foundation for future DESI spectral analysis [Chilingarian et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ASPC..535..179C/abstract).

## Observational Systematics and Fiber Assignment

The DESI focal plane is equipped with 5000 robotic fiber positioners. While this architecture allows for massive spectroscopic multiplexing, physical size and collision avoidance constraints introduce unavoidable non-uniformities in observational completeness. In average or underdense regions, spectroscopic completeness exceeds $95\%$; however, in the dense cores of massive [[cluster-candidates|Cluster candidates]] and [[optically-selected-clusters|Optically selected clusters]], local completeness can drop below $10\%$ due to physical collisions between positioners [Smith et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484.1285S/abstract). 

Failing to account for this missing population systematically suppresses small-scale clustering amplitudes. To recover unbiased clustering statistics, the BGS employs Pair Inverse Probability (PIP) weighting. This technique assigns statistical weights to observed galaxy pairs based on the exact probability that both galaxies could have been simultaneously assigned fibers. When combined with angular upweighting and targeted priority randomization, PIP weighting successfully reconstructs the true underlying spatial distribution [Smith et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484.1285S/abstract). 

To accurately interpret these corrected statistics, the technical pipeline for constructing [[low-redshift-large-scale-structure|Large-Scale Structure]] (LSS) catalogs incorporates high-precision "random catalogs" that rigorously model the DESI footprint and complex observing history [Ross et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240516593R/abstract). Theoretical modeling is further supported by the development of fast Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) fitting methods and high-fidelity mock galaxy catalogs based on the AbacusSummit simulations, specifically tuned to match BGS clustering measurements [Smith et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532..903S/abstract). Early theoretical preparations also relied on the Rosella mock catalog, a high-fidelity BGS mock constructed from the P-Millennium simulation using subhalo abundance matching [Safonova et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.505..325S/abstract). In addition to AbacusSummit and Rosella, high-resolution mock lightcones for all four DESI tracers, including the BGS, have also been generated using the UCHUU simulation and subhalo abundance matching (SHAM) to reproduce clustering evolution and HOD properties [Prada et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...698A.170P/abstract).

Additionally, imaging systematics such as atmospheric seeing and galactic extinction induce non-cosmological density fluctuations in the target catalog. For instance, the BGS target density is artificially suppressed by approximately $7\%$ in regions of high stellar density due to blending and [[galaxy-identification|star-galaxy separation]] artifacts [Ruiz-Macias et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502.4328R/abstract). 

Finally, while overall redshift success is excellent, small fluctuations of up to $3\%$ correlate with effective spectroscopic signal-to-noise ratio, focal plane position, and survey speed. To prevent these variations from masquerading as cosmic structure, inverse-probability weights are applied to effectively nullify spurious correlations, ensuring that observational systematics alter the best-fit cosmological parameters by less than $15\%$ of their statistical error budget [Krolewski et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240517208K/abstract), [Krolewski et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025JCAP...01..147K/abstract).

## Cosmological Probes: BAO and RSD

The highly complete, dense sampling of the BGS provides the most precise local measurements of Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO) and Redshift-Space Distortions (RSD) to date [Hahn et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023AJ....165..253H/abstract). Combined with other DESI tracers, the survey reduces BAO statistical uncertainty to 0.28% at $z < 1.1$ [DESI Collaboration et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024AJ....167...62D/abstract). Earlier baseline efforts, such as the historical development of model-independent radial BAO measurements using pixel-based correlation functions from the final [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|BOSS sample]] [Marra et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.487.3419M/abstract), provide a foundational methodology that the immense statistical power of [[extended-baryon-oscillation-spectroscopic-survey|DESI BGS]] aims to improve upon. Recent validations of the DESI Data Release 2 (DR2) clustering data have confirmed the consistency of BAO measurements with DR1 results, while significantly reducing statistical uncertainties across multiple tracers, including the BGS [Andrade et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250314742A/abstract).

Measurements of RSD in the BGS are critical for constraining the growth rate of structure ($f\sigma_8$). This statistical power is significantly enhanced when integrated with the DESI Peculiar Velocity Survey. Specifically, researchers have calibrated the Fundamental Plane relation using DESI velocity dispersions to derive precise distances to early-type galaxies, enabling direct local $H_0$ measurements [Said et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240813842S/abstract). Concurrently, the Tully-Fisher relation has been calibrated for over 10,000 spatially-resolved spiral galaxies within the BGS to derive independent distances and peculiar velocities [Douglass et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251203227D/abstract). Directly measuring peculiar velocities with these combined techniques reduces the uncertainty on the growth rate by a factor of approximately 2.5 at $z \sim 0.1$ [Said et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240813842S/abstract), yielding a highly precise consensus measurement of $f\sigma_8 = 0.450 \pm 0.055$ [Saulder et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.1106S/abstract), [Bautista et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251203228B/abstract). 

The sheer number density of the BGS also opens pathways for novel tests of fundamental physics. Cross-correlating distinct BGS sub-populations enables the detection of the sample-dependent relativistic Doppler contribution to the power spectrum. Forecasts suggest this relativistic dipole can be detected at a significance of $19\sigma$ in the $0.2 \lesssim z \lesssim 0.3$ range [Bonvin et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.4611B/abstract), [Montano et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240706284M/abstract). Furthermore, joint analyses of BGS redshifts with size measurements from the upcoming Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST) are expected to enable the detection of the Doppler magnification dipole (size variation due to peculiar velocities) with high signal-to-noise in the low-redshift universe [Ye et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251117858Y/abstract). Optimal redshift weighting schemes applied to BGS data also significantly improve bounds on the time variation of the effective Newton's constant, $G_{\rm eff}$, reducing its uncertainty by a factor of 2.2 to 12.8 at $z \lesssim 0.45$ [Li et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...877...32L/abstract).

Despite these successes, spatial analyses of the BGS footprint up to scales of $r \sim 400 \, h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$ indicate a persistent power-law decay ($r^{-0.8}$) in the conditional average density with Gumbel-distributed density fluctuations, challenging the expectation of a transition to Gaussian spatial homogeneity on these scales [Sylos Labini et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251121585S/abstract).

## Galaxy Populations, Voids, and Cluster Science

By combining advanced group finding algorithms with [[large-area-weak-lensing-surveys|weak lensing measurements]], BGS data has enabled highly precise direct measurements of the galaxy-halo connection. Notably, cross-correlations have constrained the satellite galaxy assembly bias parameter to $Q_{\rm sat} = 0.05 \pm 0.14$. This result, strictly consistent with zero, introduces significant tension with standard empirical galaxy formation models that predict stronger assembly bias signatures [Shao et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251020896S/abstract). 

The dense spectroscopic sampling provides a critical calibration tool for [[redmapper|optically selected clusters]], such as those in the [[redmapper|RedMaPPer]] catalog. Spectroscopic follow-up reveals that projection effects—where foreground or background galaxies are mistakenly bound to a cluster halo—are highly dependent on [[optical-richness|Optical richness]] (being more than twice as large in low-richness systems) and increase by as much as $25\%$ between $z \sim 0.1$ and $z \sim 0.2$ [Myles et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250606249M/abstract). The data also confirms a tight linear scaling relation between mean spectroscopic richness and cluster halo mass. To further map these high-density regions, new tools such as the CluMPR algorithm have utilized Legacy Survey DR9 data to identify approximately 300,000 cluster candidates based on [[stellar-mass-function|stellar mass]] and photometric redshift overdensities [Yantovski-Barth et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531.2285Y/abstract). Beyond traditional cluster science, the extensive DESI footprint is being used for homogeneous estimates of globular cluster abundance around roughly 700 [[nearby-galaxies-catalog|nearby galaxies]] to constrain virial mass relations [Le et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...978...33L/abstract).

In the lowest-density regimes, the volume-limited BGS sample has been leveraged to construct robust catalogs of cosmic voids. Utilizing geometric algorithms such as VoidFinder and $V^2$, researchers have identified nearly 1,500 interior voids out to $z = 0.24$. These void catalogs provide isolated environments critical for studying the variation in galaxy properties with cosmic environment and for broader cosmological studies [Rincon et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241100148R/abstract), [Rincon et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...982...38R/abstract).

Finally, detailed demographic studies using BGS data have highlighted new phenomena in the faint-end behavior of nearby galaxies. Recent measurements of the [[galaxy-luminosity-function|Galaxy luminosity function]] using deep imaging and rigorous BGS completeness weights have revealed an unexpected upturn at the faint end at [[faint-galaxy-statistics|absolute magnitudes]] $M_{r} - 5\log h > -15$, an effect found to be particularly strong in red galaxy populations [Moore et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251101803M/abstract). Furthermore, DESI's extensive spectroscopic data provides a unique chance to measure the evolution of dark matter fractions and central dark matter densities in early-type galaxies across the last 8 Gyr [Tortora et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022FrASS...8..197N/abstract). Beyond optical wavelengths, BGS galaxies are also being cross-matched with X-ray surveys like the Chandra Source Catalog to identify normal galaxy candidates and study X-ray-to-optical flux ratios [Kim et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJS..268...17K/abstract).

## Recent Advances and Future Directions

The unprecedented spectroscopic density of the BGS has made it an ideal reference sample for cross-correlation analyses with [[large-area-weak-lensing-surveys|Large area weak lensing surveys]], such as the [[dark-energy-survey|Dark Energy Survey]], the [[kilo-degree-survey-kids|Kilo-Degree Survey (KiDS)]], and the [[hyper-suprime-cam-surveys|Hyper Suprime-Cam Surveys]]. One critical application is clustering-based redshift calibration to constrain the [[redshift-distribution|Redshift distribution]] of [[background-galaxies|source galaxies]] in photometric surveys, thereby reducing dominant systematic uncertainties in weak lensing cosmology [Ruggeri et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251215963R/abstract). Additionally, BGS lens galaxies have been utilized to measure the excess surface mass density ($\Delta\Sigma$) via cross-correlation with source shapes from HSC, KiDS, and DES, establishing a robust baseline for future cosmological lensing analyses [Heydenreich et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250621677H/abstract). Furthermore, cross-correlations have enabled high-precision measurements of intrinsic alignments; observations indicate that blue, star-forming galaxies exhibit an alignment consistent with zero, whereas red, quenched galaxies are strongly aligned, with the amplitude scaling tightly with luminosity and stellar mass [Siegel et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711530S/abstract).

As the DESI BGS maps the northern and equatorial sky, next-generation spectroscopic facilities are being developed to extend these measurements. In the southern hemisphere, the 4MOST Cosmology Redshift Survey (4MOST-CRS) will serve as a complementary counterpart, obtaining redshifts over approximately 5,700 square degrees. Alongside the CRS, the 4MOST Hemisphere Survey of the Nearby Universe (4HS) aims to map mass and motion in the southern [[local-volume-and-nearby-galaxy-studies|Local Volume]] with high completeness [Taylor et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Msngr.190...46T/abstract). Forecasts indicate that combining DESI and 4MOST-CRS data will yield the most stringent constraints on baryon acoustic oscillations from spectroscopic galaxy clustering, improving precision by $12\%$ in the overlapping $0.4 < z < 0.8$ redshift range [Verdier et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250807311V/abstract), [Bandi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251002449B/abstract).

Looking further ahead, the community is defining the science case for Stage V surveys. Future concepts, such as the Wide-field Spectroscopic Telescope, aim to achieve multiplexing of $\mathcal{O}(1000)$ simultaneous spectra with sub-$2\times 10^{-4}(1+z)$ redshift precision. These Stage V surveys are expected to probe fundamental physics that remains inaccessible to current experiments, including resolving the absolute neutrino mass hierarchy ($\Sigma m_\nu \lesssim 0.015 \text{ eV}$) and testing multi-field inflation by measuring primordial non-Gaussianity at the level of $f_{\rm NL} \sim 1$ [Guidi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251215294G/abstract).
