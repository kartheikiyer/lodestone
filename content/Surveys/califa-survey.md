The **Calar Alto Legacy Integral Field Area (CALIFA) survey** is a pioneering [[extragalactic-astronomical-surveys|extragalactic]] observational project designed to map the spatially resolved properties of galaxies in the local universe. Conducted using the PMAS/PPAK instrument on the 3.5-meter telescope at the [[calar-alto-observatory|Calar Alto Observatory]], the survey utilized [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] (IFS) to obtain continuous, two-dimensional maps of stellar populations, gas kinematics, and chemical abundances for a representative sample of nearly 900 local galaxies. Historically, CALIFA served as a critical transitional bridge between early, small-scale IFS mosaics and next-generation, highly multiplexed campaigns like the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] [Bundy et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015IAUS..311..100B/abstract) and SAMI [Bryant et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.447.2857B/abstract) surveys. By systematically resolving the full optical extent of its targets, CALIFA overcame the severe aperture biases inherent in earlier single-fiber studies, fundamentally advancing our understanding of galactic mass assembly, chemical enrichment, and secular evolution.

Conceived from its inception as a [[legacy-surveys|legacy project]], CALIFA has made its fully reduced data available through a series of public releases, culminating in a homogeneously "remastered" extended Data Release (eDR) that achieves near seeing-limited spatial resolution. The optical data also serve as a foundational benchmark for multi-wavelength synergies, most notably the EDGE-CALIFA extension, which maps the corresponding molecular gas disks using CO interferometry. 

## Historical Context and Survey Design

By the early 21st century, large-scale statistical studies of the local galaxy population were dominated by single-aperture observations like the [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]] (SDSS). However, representing an entire galaxy with a single central fiber spectrum introduces profound aperture biases. Because single-fiber measurements typically only sample the nuclear regions, they fail to capture radial gradients and can severely mischaracterize a galaxy's evolutionary state. For example, local early-type galaxies undergoing "inside-out" growth frequently host peripheral star formation that falls outside a standard $3^{\prime\prime}$ SDSS fiber. Consequently, single-fiber spectroscopy can miss $50\%$ to $99.9\%$ of the total star formation rate in these systems, erroneously classifying them as "retired" galaxies powered solely by older stellar populations [Gomes et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016A&A...586A..22G/abstract).

To overcome these limitations, the community recognized the need to spatially resolve galaxies over their full optical extent. A critical stepping stone was the PPAK IFS Nearby Galaxies Survey (PINGS), which mosaicked 17 disk galaxies to cover nearly $80$ arcmin$^2$ of the sky . Building directly upon the technological foundation of PINGS, CALIFA scaled this approach to a statistically robust sample, proving that extensive, high-resolution 2D spectral mapping was feasible. Simulated [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|high-redshift observations]] of CALIFA and PINGS data later demonstrated that the projected spatial scale per spaxel is the most critical metric for accurately recovering astrophysical parameters in [[cosmic-surveys|cosmological surveys]] [Mast et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...561A.129M/abstract). This foundation also paved the way for higher-resolution successors, such as the PHANGS-[[muse-instrument|MUSE]] survey, which achieves cloud-scale ($\sim 50$ pc) resolution in nearby disc galaxies, deeply complementing CALIFA's wider local universe coverage [Emsellem et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...659A.191E/abstract).

### Sample Selection and Instrumentation
The CALIFA "mother sample" was strictly diameter-selected from the SDSS DR7 photometric catalog, targeting objects with an $r$-band isophotal major axis between $45^{\prime\prime}$ and $79.2^{\prime\prime}$ and a local redshift constraint of $0.005 < z < 0.03$ [Sánchez et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...538A...8S/abstract), [Walcher et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...569A...1W/abstract). This geometric selection successfully produced a statistically representative volume of the local galaxy population over a stellar mass range of $10^{9.7}$ to $10^{11.4} M_\odot$. Within these bounds, the sample is largely free from significant biases against intrinsically large or small galaxies, with only a minor selection bias noted for low-mass systems ($M_\star < 10^{9.7} M_\odot$) where there is a slight prevalence of nearly edge-on late-type galaxies [Walcher et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...569A...1W/abstract).

Observations utilized the Potsdam Multi-Aperture Spectrophotometer (PMAS) in its PPAK wide-field IFU mode at the [[calar-alto-observatory|Calar Alto Observatory]]. The PPAK instrument provides a hexagonal field of view of roughly $1.3$ arcmin$^2$, which achieves a $100\%$ spatial covering factor via a customized three-pointing dithering scheme [Sánchez et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...538A...8S/abstract). The observational strategy employed two overlapping spectral setups: 
*   **V500:** A low-resolution grating covering $3745{-}7500$ Å with a spectral resolution of $6.0$ Å full-width at half-maximum (FWHM) ($R \sim 850$), optimal for characterizing stellar populations and mapping major emission lines [Sánchez et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...538A...8S/abstract), [García-Benito et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...576A.135G/abstract).
*   **V1200:** A medium-resolution grating covering $3650{-}4840$ Å with a FWHM of $2.3$ Å ($R \sim 1650$), specifically designed for extracting detailed internal kinematics [Sánchez et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012A&A...538A...8S/abstract), [García-Benito et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...576A.135G/abstract).

### Data Releases
CALIFA has made its processed data fully available to the community. Successive public releases (DR1, DR2, DR3) steadily increased the sample size, pipeline quality, and introduced "COMBO" datacubes that merged both the V500 and V1200 setups into a continuous spectrum [Husemann et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013A&A...549A..87H/abstract), [García-Benito et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...576A.135G/abstract), [Sánchez et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016A&A...594A..36S/abstract). In addition to the spectroscopic data, an extensive photometric compendium was released for the DR3 sample, providing 1D surface brightness profiles and 2D bulge-disk decompositions using IMFIT and GALFIT [Gilhuly et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.477..845G/abstract). The most recent extended and remastered data release (eDR) brought the total dataset to 895 galaxies. The eDR incorporated a new cube-reconstruction algorithm that significantly improved spatial registration and achieved near seeing-limited spatial resolution ($\text{FWHM}_{\text{PSF}} \sim 1.0^{\prime\prime}$) [Sánchez et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526.5555S/abstract). Specialized software toolkits have been developed to exploit these cubes, such as LZIFU for multi-component emission-line fitting [Ho et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016Ap&SS.361..280H/abstract), [Ho et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ascl.soft07018H/abstract) and PYFIT3D for simultaneous extraction of stellar and gas properties [Sánchez et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526.5555S/abstract).

## Spatially Resolved Stellar Populations and Galaxy Growth

By applying stellar synthesis techniques to IFS data, CALIFA has confirmed the "inside-out" growth scenario for massive galaxies. In this evolutionary framework, galaxies build their central regions early on, resulting in older, more metal-rich cores, while continuing to accrete gas and form stars in their extended outskirts [Delgado et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013IAUS..295..300D/abstract). The radial structure of these properties is fundamentally linked to the local stellar mass surface density ($\mu_\star$), demonstrating that local density acts as a primary driver of galaxy evolution [Delgado et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013IAUS..295..300D/abstract). For a given global stellar mass, earlier Hubble types are generally more compact, older, and more metal-rich at all radii, evidencing that galaxy quenching is tied to morphology rather than solely driven by mass [González Delgado et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015arXiv150602809G/abstract).

Morphological and kinematic studies further complement these stellar population gradients. Investigations into the intrinsic shape of bulges in the CALIFA survey reveal that 66% of CALIFA bulges are nearly oblate systems, whereas triaxial bulges are primarily found in barred galaxies, suggesting different evolutionary pathways for massive versus less massive bulges [Costantin et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...609A.132C/abstract). 

Spatially resolved mapping has additionally challenged the textbook assumption of a universal stellar Initial Mass Function (IMF). In early-type galaxies, CALIFA data show that the local IMF correlates tightly with local metallicity, becoming increasingly "bottom-heavy" in metal-rich central populations [Martín-Navarro et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...806L..31M/abstract). Combined with kinematic constraints and cosmological simulations, these stellar populations indicate that the dynamically hot stellar components within galactic bulges are frequently generated through secular heating over time rather than exclusively through major mergers [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...699A.320Z/abstract). Despite these advances, the derivation of spatial properties carries methodological caveats; comparisons between different approaches—such as applying the MUFFIT code to [[javalambre-sky-surveys-j-pas-and-j-plus|J-PLUS]] photometric data versus running STARLIGHT and STECKMAP on CALIFA IFU data—reveal systematic discrepancies in the absolute values of the extracted ages and metallicities [San Roman et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...622A.181S/abstract).

## Ionized Gas Physics and Chemical Abundances

By analyzing the fluxes and ratios of strong optical emission lines, the survey allows for precise determinations of gas kinematics, excitation mechanisms, and gas-phase chemical enrichment patterns. A major result from CALIFA is the detailed characterization of the radial oxygen abundance profile in disk galaxies, which exhibits three distinct regimes. In the mid-disk, between $0.5$ and $2.0$ effective radii ($R_e$), the abundance profile follows a characteristic negative gradient with a slope of approximately $-0.1$ dex/$R_e$. In the outer disk ($> 2.0 R_e$), the abundance gradient universally flattens [Sánchez et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015Galax...3..164S/abstract). The behavior in the innermost regions ($< 0.5 R_e$), however, remains actively debated. While some analyses identify a mass-dependent central drop in oxygen abundance [Sánchez et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015Galax...3..164S/abstract), other studies characterize this inner region as a simple flattening or break in the gradient, a feature present in 18 galaxies (roughly 20% of the surveyed sample) [Zinchenko et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.2715Z/abstract). Beyond radial gradients, the 2D maps show that global azimuthal asymmetries in the oxygen abundance are remarkably small ($\lesssim 0.05$ dex) [Zinchenko et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.2715Z/abstract).

The local oxygen abundance is found to be tightly correlated with the local stellar mass surface density (the $\Sigma$-Z relation), indicating that chemical enrichment is heavily dominated by local processes rather than global outflows or radial mixing [Sánchez et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015Galax...3..164S/abstract). CALIFA data has also been utilized to study other elements; for instance, an empirical calibration constructed from literature data of Galactic and extragalactic HII regions has been applied to the emission line flux ratio $\mathrm{He}_{\lambda5876}/\mathrm{H}\alpha$ from CALIFA data to map the singly ionized helium abundance. The observed variation of helium with respect to oxygen tracks the mass-dependent chemical enrichment of the interstellar medium [Valerdi et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.505.5460V/abstract).

## Galaxy Dynamics and Environmental Interactions

By extracting stellar kinematics up to several effective radii ($R_e$), the CALIFA survey provides a comprehensive mapping of galactic dynamics across the Hubble sequence. An analysis of stellar angular momentum, quantified via the $\lambda_{R_e} - \epsilon_e$ diagnostic diagram, shows that elliptical and lenticular (S0) galaxies are highly consistent with results from the [[atlas3d-survey|Atlas3D survey]]. Spiral galaxies, however, exhibit distinct kinematic behaviors. CALIFA identifies a population of low-mass spirals with unexpectedly low angular momentum, suggesting they may be strongly dark matter-dominated, indicating that simple stellar population fading cannot entirely account for the morphological transformation from spirals to S0s [Falcón-Barroso et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015IAUS..311...78F/abstract). CALIFA's spatially resolved maps also allow the derivation of averaged integrated velocity dispersion radial profiles, providing crucial aperture corrections for [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|high-redshift observations]] [Falcón-Barroso et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...597A..48F/abstract).

Detailed modeling reveals the profound impact of non-circular flows induced by galactic bars. Kinematic analyses demonstrate that approximately 32% of intermediate-inclination, gas-rich disk galaxies host detectable non-circular motions exceeding $15 \text{ km s}^{-1}$, the vast majority of which ($\sim 92\%$) are driven by bars [Holmes et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.4397H/abstract). Notably, roughly 9% of galaxies exhibit kinematic signatures of bar-induced flows without possessing a visible photometric bar [Holmes et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.4397H/abstract). These bar-induced flows transport gas inward, frequently rejuvenating central star formation. This is evidenced by a distinct class of "turnover" galaxies, where the central regions display a recent ($< 2 \text{ Gyr}$) drop in the 4000 Å break alongside an upturn in Balmer absorption features [Lin et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...838..105L/abstract). Morphological decompositions further show that 25% of the CALIFA sample contains barlens or boxy/peanut structures built concurrently with the narrow bar over prolonged timescales [Laurikainen et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...618A..34L/abstract).

Beyond internal secular evolution, CALIFA data exposes the kinematic imprint of external environmental interactions. Tidal interactions are observed to elevate gas accretion rates, which can trigger starbursts or drive prominent galactic outflows, such as the $\sim 223 \text{ km s}^{-1}$ outflow detected via strong and blueshifted Na I absorption in the interacting system NGC 5394 [Roche et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.453.2349R/abstract). Furthermore, tidal interactions in the CALIFA sample are found to contribute to the regulation of star formation, with these perturbed galaxies exhibiting flatter slopes on the resolved star-formation main sequence [Morales-Vargas et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.499.4370M/abstract). Such perturbed systems also show evidence of metal-poor gas inflows and a more intense reactivation of star formation within the last 1 Gyr [Morales-Vargas et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.508.4216M/abstract).

On even larger scales, CALIFA has uncovered a "mysterious" dynamical coherence between the internal rotational direction of galaxies and the average line-of-sight motion of their neighbors. This coherence is most pronounced between faint, blue, or kinematically misaligned central galaxies and bright neighbors within $800$ kpc, where the luminosity-weighted mean velocity of neighbors reaches $61.7 \pm 17.6 \text{ km s}^{-1}$ relative to the central galaxy's outer rotation [Lee et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872...78L/abstract). The signal persists, albeit weaker, out to scales of $1{-}6$ Mpc for red neighbor populations, with a coherence velocity of $30.6 \pm 10.9 \text{ km s}^{-1}$ [Lee et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...884..104L/abstract). The physical origin of this alignment remains an active subject of debate; while some studies suggest it is primarily driven by localized galaxy-galaxy interactions and kinematic "galaxy conformity" [Lee et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...893..154L/abstract), others argue it reflects the long-term bulk flows and rotational properties of the cosmic web itself [Lee et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...884..104L/abstract).

## Synergies and Multi-wavelength Extensions

Because the CALIFA mother sample was diameter-selected from the [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]], the targeted galaxies already possess extensive multi-wavelength photometric coverage. This allows the spatially resolved optical data to be directly cross-referenced with external catalogs to build a comprehensive picture of galaxy assembly and feedback.

The most significant multi-wavelength extension of the project is the EDGE-CALIFA survey, which augments the optical data with resolved CO observations. Utilizing the Combined Array for Millimeter-wave Astronomy (CARMA), the EDGE survey mapped $126$ CALIFA galaxies in the $^{12}$CO and $^{13}$CO molecular transitions [Bolatto et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...846..159B/abstract). Early results from the EDGE survey demonstrated how gas depletion time (or star formation efficiency) varies with local environments, including stellar surface density and galactic radius [Utomo et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016IAUS..315..258U/abstract). By matching the molecular gas maps with CALIFA's stellar and ionized gas maps, EDGE-CALIFA revealed that the spatially resolved correlation between the $H_2$ surface density and the star formation rate surface density is the tightest local scaling relation, outperforming relations that rely on stellar mass [Wong et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJS..271...35W/abstract). Further complementing optical IFS studies of the interstellar medium, high-resolution [[radio-surveys|radio benchmarks]] like the Karl G. Jansky Very Large Array Local Group L-Band Survey (LGLBS) provide $10{-}20$ pc scale views of atomic gas in Local Group galaxies [Koch et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJS..279...35K/abstract).

CALIFA's multi-wavelength synergies also extend to the high-energy regime, addressing severe observational biases inherent in purely optical active galactic nucleus (AGN) identification. High-resolution X-ray observations from the Chandra observatory targeting $138$ CALIFA galaxies demonstrated that optical emission-line criteria alone can underestimate the true AGN population by up to $70\%$, as host galaxy attenuation and restrictive equivalent width criteria often mask the nuclear signature. The integration of X-ray data identified $34$ new *bona fide* AGNs and $23$ candidates, revising the overall AGN fraction in the CALIFA sample up to $7{-}10\%$ [Osorio-Clavijo et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.522.5788O/abstract). In parallel, specialized IFS campaigns like the WiFeS S7 survey have been conducted to target the Narrow Line Regions (NLR) of nearby Seyfert galaxies to study AGN excitation and feedback in greater detail [Scharwächter et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ASSP...42..263S/abstract).

Building on the successful blueprint of CALIFA, the survey has also inspired sibling [[legacy-surveys|legacy projects]] at the same observatory. Notably, the CAVITY (Calar Alto Void Integral-field Treasury surveY) project utilizes the identical PPAK instrument to target and characterize the galaxy population residing within cosmic voids, providing a direct environmental contrast to the main CALIFA sample [García-Benito et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.161G/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While the CALIFA survey has successfully resolved many aspects of inside-out galaxy growth, several critical astrophysical debates highlighted by the survey remain active. A primary focus of current research involves disentangling the physical drivers of bottom-heavy IMFs in early-type galaxies and reducing the methodological dependencies of stellar population synthesis codes [Martín-Navarro et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...806L..31M/abstract), [San Roman et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...622A.181S/abstract). Ambiguities also persist in galactic chemistry, particularly regarding the exact mechanism causing the drop or flattening of oxygen abundances in the extreme central regions of galactic nuclei [Sánchez et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015Galax...3..164S/abstract), [Zinchenko et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.2715Z/abstract). Furthermore, the large-scale dynamical coherence observed between galaxy rotation and neighbor motions requires further theoretical modeling to determine whether it is a product of local conformity or cosmic web alignment [Lee et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...884..104L/abstract), [Lee et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...893..154L/abstract). 

To address these open questions and push beyond CALIFA's spatial resolution, the community is developing next-generation, high-multiplex future facilities. Alongside the continued legacy of ongoing observations, projects like the Hector survey are planned as high-multiplex successors aiming to obtain integral field spectroscopy for 100,000 galaxies [Bland-Hawthorn et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015IAUS..309...21B/abstract). Similarly, the WEAVE survey acts as a next-generation multiplexed facility designed to study stellar populations and kinematics in cluster and field galaxies using both multi-object and integral-field modes [Jin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.2688J/abstract). A direct successor concept is the LUCA project (Local Universe from Calar Alto), which proposes the construction of the IFU-6000 instrument—a massive integral field spectrograph utilizing 6,000 optical fibers—to map the local universe in 3D with unprecedented spatial resolution [Prada et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020arXiv200210795P/abstract). Looking even further ahead, the Widefield Spectroscopic Telescope (WST) concept outlines a 12-meter facility planned to operate high-multiplex multi-object spectroscopy and a giant IFS to unravel large-scale galaxy assembly [Bacon et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240512518B/abstract).

In the broader context of [[future-astronomical-facilities-and-surveys|Future Astronomical Facilities and Surveys]], CALIFA continues to serve as a critical $z \sim 0$ benchmark for the massive statistical samples being assembled by panoptic campaigns. Prominent among these is the fifth iteration of the [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]] (SDSS-V), a pioneer of panoptic spectroscopy whose Local Volume Mapper (LVM) program is providing a massive IFS map of the Galaxy and Local Group [Kollmeier et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250706989K/abstract). Meanwhile, at intermediate redshifts ($z \sim 0.3$), the [[magpi-survey|MAGPI survey]] uses the [[muse-instrument|MUSE instrument]] to track the evolution of galaxy kinematics and chemistry since the 'Middle Ages' of the universe, providing a direct evolutionary link to local populations [Foster et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021PASA...38...31F/abstract). Together with major surveys like the [[gama-survey|GAMA survey]] and multi-wavelength observatories like [[erosita|eROSITA]], these forthcoming missions build upon the comprehensive spatial foundations laid by CALIFA.
