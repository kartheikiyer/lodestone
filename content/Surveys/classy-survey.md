The **COS Legacy Archive Spectroscopic Survey (CLASSY)** is a comprehensive [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope]] observing program that provides a high-resolution, far-ultraviolet (FUV) spectral atlas of 45 local [[emission-line-galaxies|star-forming galaxies]]. Utilizing the [[cosmic-origins-spectrograph-cos-surveys|Cosmic Origins Spectrograph (COS)]], the survey provides high-quality, broad-coverage, high-resolution FUV spectra [Leitherer et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020Galax...8...13L/abstract), [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract). The primary objective of CLASSY is to establish a high-fidelity empirical baseline of local starbursts that spans a broad range of stellar masses, [[chemical-abundances|metallicities]], and star formation rates. By thoroughly characterizing these local analogs, the survey provides the critical calibrations necessary for interpreting the rest-frame UV spectra of distant galaxies, particularly those currently being observed by the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]].

Historically, the evolution of UV [[extragalactic-astronomical-surveys|Extragalactic astronomical surveys]] has been defined by a trade-off between sky coverage and spectral resolution. While wide-field missions like [[galex|GALEX]] successfully mapped the recent [[cosmic-evolution-and-star-formation-history|cosmic star formation history]], their low spectral resolution integrated complex interstellar features and blended closely spaced absorption lines. In contrast, CLASSY’s high resolving power allows astronomers to explicitly disentangle the narrow absorption features of the static interstellar medium (ISM) from the broader, blueshifted kinematic signatures of starburst-driven galactic outflows. This precision overcomes the traditional reliance on crude spectral sampling and [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|Spectrophotometric redshifts]] often required in [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]], bridging the gap between detailed local astrophysics and the unresolved, distant universe.

The CLASSY dataset has yielded several foundational insights into galactic feedback, ISM diagnostics, and Chemical Abundances. Kinematic analyses of the FUV spectra demonstrate that outflow velocities correlate with a galaxy's circular velocity, whereas mass-loading factors scale inversely with it, reaching efficiencies as high as $\sim 10$ in the shallow potential wells of [[low-luminosity-galaxies|dwarf galaxies]] [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract). Furthermore, the survey has challenged the textbook assumption that nitrogen-to-oxygen (N/O) enrichment follows a simple age-metallicity degeneracy, revealing instead that N/O ratios are significantly modulated by local gas density, star formation compactness, and specific feedback mechanisms. The survey has also successfully calibrated low-ionization state UV absorption lines, such as C II, as robust proxies for neutral hydrogen gas, and quantified how systematic aperture effects influence inferred physical parameters like dust reddening and fluorescent emission. 

Despite these advancements, the CLASSY survey highlights several active tensions and open questions in the study of galaxy evolution, particularly regarding the optimal framework for modeling outflow column densities and reconciling 1D "down-the-barrel" spectroscopic techniques with true 3D physical structures. As observational frontiers push deeper into the epoch of reionization, a pressing question is whether these local CLASSY benchmarks can accurately represent the extreme ionization parameters and pristine metallicities expected in [[first-light-galaxies|First-light galaxies]] at $z > 10$.

## Historical Context and Evolution of UV Galaxy Surveys

The rest-frame ultraviolet (UV) is a critical window for studying massive star formation and the interstellar medium. The evolution of UV [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]] began with modest wide-field experiments that laid the groundwork for modern high-resolution atlases. A notable early milestone was the deployment of the balloon-borne FOCA camera, which imaged galaxies at $\sim 2000$ \AA\ [Treyer et al. (1997)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1997AIPC..408...99T/abstract). By securing optical spectra for 45 of these UV-selected sources at $0 < z < 0.3$, researchers conducted a pioneering [[galaxy-redshift-survey|UV redshift survey]] that provided an early, independent estimate of the local volume-averaged star formation rate, revealing a higher abundance of star-forming galaxies than previous optical studies suggested [Treyer et al. (1997)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1997AIPC..408...99T/abstract).

A revolutionary leap in UV astronomy occurred with the launch of the Galaxy Evolution Explorer ([[galex|GALEX]]), which performed the first dedicated space-based UV sky survey [Martin et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003AAS...203.9601M/abstract), [Bianchi et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014Ap&SS.354..103B/abstract). [[galex|GALEX]] mapped the sky in two broad photometric bands: the far-UV (FUV; $\lambda_{\rm eff} \sim 1528$ \AA) and near-UV (NUV; $\lambda_{\rm eff} \sim 2310$ \AA) [Bianchi et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014Ap&SS.354..103B/abstract). Its massive database ultimately included over 200 million source measurements and more than 100,000 low-resolution ($R \sim 100-300$) UV spectra [Martin et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005IAUS..216..221M/abstract), [Bianchi et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014Ap&SS.354..103B/abstract). This wealth of data cemented the empirical calibration of the UV-to-star-formation-rate relation and mapped the recent star formation history of the universe. However, [[galex|GALEX]] was fundamentally limited by its relatively crude angular resolution of $\approx 5''$, which caused source blending that could bias photometric measurements by $>0.2$ magnitudes in dense fields [Osborne et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJS..268...26O/abstract). Furthermore, its low spectral resolving power was insufficient to separate complex, blended interstellar absorption features or to spatially resolve galactic outflows.

The limitations of early panoramic surveys necessitated a transition to high-resolution, targeted [[spectroscopy-surveys|spectroscopy]] to fully interpret the physical conditions of star-forming galaxies. Prior to CLASSY, the broader landscape of extragalactic astronomy was transformed by core legacy deep surveys such as the VIMOS VLT Deep Survey (VVDS), which mapped galaxy evolution over 13 billion years and established the tiered wide/deep survey strategy [Le Fèvre et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014Msngr.155...33L/abstract). For low-redshift studies, the [[gama-survey|Galaxy And Mass Assembly (GAMA) survey]] emerged as a primary spectroscopic resource, providing the large-scale environmental context that targeted surveys like CLASSY lack [Liske et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.2087L/abstract). In parallel, efforts such as the Spitzer-SDSS-GALEX Spectroscopic Survey (SSGSS) pioneered multi-wavelength approaches by combining UV and mid-infrared spectroscopy to study star-forming galaxies [O'Dowd et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...741...79O/abstract). At higher redshifts, [[deep-spectroscopic-surveys|Deep spectroscopic surveys]] like VANDELS targeted over 2,100 star-forming galaxies at $z > 1$ (with 85% at $z > 3$) to measure physical properties such as [[chemical-abundances|metallicities]], ages, and outflow velocities from high signal-to-noise ratio absorption-line studies [Pentericci et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...616A.174P/abstract), [McLure et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.479...25M/abstract). Interpreting the exquisite rest-frame UV data from these campaigns, and the data now emerging from the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]], required a robust, high-resolution local benchmark. This was realized through CLASSY, which bridged the gap between the low-resolution mapping of the [[galex|GALEX]] era and the stringent calibration demands of modern [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|high-redshift extragalactic astronomy]] [Leitherer et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020Galax...8...13L/abstract), [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract). Unlike earlier UV surveys that integrated properties over entire galaxies with low dispersion, CLASSY's resolution enables precise measurements of outflow kinematics, neutral gas column densities, and complex chemical abundances.

## Sample Selection and Observational Strategy

To serve as a reliable baseline for interpreting high-redshift galaxies, the CLASSY [[target-selection|Target selection]] comprises a carefully curated sample of 45 local starbursts that span a wide variety of intrinsic properties. The full sample spans four orders of magnitude in key metrics, featuring stellar masses up to $\sim 10^{10} M_\odot$. A specific subset of 38 galaxies successfully replicated by mock profiles spans the narrower mass range of $10^6 - 10^9 M_\odot$, while gas-phase metallicities across the sample range between $0.02 - 0.55 Z_\odot$ [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract), [Gazagnes et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...952..164G/abstract). By observing this diverse local benchmark, CLASSY maps the parameter space of the early universe without the ambiguities introduced by the crude spectral sampling typical of [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|high-redshift observations]] [Leitherer et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020Galax...8...13L/abstract). 

This makes CLASSY highly complementary to other multi-wavelength nearby galaxy benchmarks, such as the SIRTF Nearby Galaxies Survey (SINGS), which focuses on characterizing infrared emission and its connection to star formation and the ISM across a broad range of galaxy types [Kennicutt et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003AAS...203.9010K/abstract). Furthermore, CLASSY plays a crucial role in interpreting large-scale photometric datasets emerging from [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|JWST]], such as the ASTRODEEP-JWST program, by providing the high-resolution spectroscopic benchmarks necessary to decipher physical parameters for half a million sources in [[deep-field-campaigns|extragalactic deep fields]] [Merlin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.240M/abstract). Similarly, it provides a vital local reference for the CEERS Photometric and Physical Parameter Catalog, a key JWST comparison sample that supplies [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|photometric redshifts]] and physical parameters for 80,000 galaxies in the [[extended-groth-strip|Extended Groth Strip (EGS)]] field [Cox et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251008743C/abstract).

The survey's observational strategy relies on high signal-to-noise ratio spectroscopy obtained via the [[cosmic-origins-spectrograph-cos-surveys|Cosmic Origins Spectrograph (COS)]]. The data provide high-quality, broad-coverage, high-resolution FUV spectra [Leitherer et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020Galax...8...13L/abstract), [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract). This technical setup was explicitly chosen to enable the simultaneous Voigt profile fitting of Low-Ionization State (LIS) absorption features (e.g., Si II, C II, and O I), which successfully models the neutral and ionized absorbing gas [Parker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..104P/abstract). 

A fundamental consideration in the CLASSY observational strategy is the physical area subtended by the 2.5-arcsecond COS aperture. Extensive investigations have been conducted to understand how aperture losses and spatial resolution affect inferred physical parameters during [[spectroscopic-follow-up|Spectroscopic follow-up]] and cross-calibration with ground-based optical observations. Understanding these geometric effects is essential for accurate characterization of the FUV line profiles and ensuring that CLASSY's local baseline can be accurately projected onto unresolved, high-redshift targets.

## Gas Kinematics and Absorption-Line Outflows

A major scientific driver of the CLASSY program is the characterization of galactic winds and feedback mechanisms in local star-forming galaxies. The high-resolution far-ultraviolet data provided by such [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]] allow for the detailed study of outflows through the analysis of blueshifted metal-line absorption features spanning a wide range of ionization potentials, such as C II, Si II, and O I.

By disentangling the absorption produced by the static interstellar medium from the kinematics of the outflows, CLASSY observations demonstrate that both the mean outflow velocity and the velocity width correlate significantly with the host galaxy's circular velocity, as well as with its star formation rate and stellar mass over ranges of four orders of magnitude [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract). This kinematic behavior can be contextualized within broader evolutionary trends. For example, results from the KMOS Galaxy Evolution Survey (KGES) show that stellar mass, rather than redshift, primarily determines the disk fraction and angular momentum of [[emission-line-galaxies|star-forming galaxies]] out to $z \sim 1.5$ [Tiley et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.506..323T/abstract). Against this backdrop of mass-driven rotational kinematics, the outflow efficiency in the CLASSY sample tells a complementary story: the mass-loading factor scales steeply and inversely with both the galaxy's circular velocity and the outflow velocity, following a log-log slope of approximately $-1.6$. In the lowest-mass [[low-luminosity-galaxies|dwarf galaxies]], this factor can reach values as high as $\sim 10$, underscoring the dominant role of stellar feedback in shallow gravitational potential wells [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract).

The survey also places rigorous empirical constraints on the energetics and chemical transport of starburst-driven winds. Analysis of the warm ionized phase indicates that these outflows typically carry between $10\%$ and $100\%$ of the momentum injected by massive stars, but only $1\%$ to $20\%$ of the available kinetic energy [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract). Furthermore, the warm phase carries a minority of the expelled metals, accounting for at most $\sim 20\%$ of the newly synthesized [[chemical-abundances|silicon]] produced by supernovae within the starburst [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract).

Beyond integrated kinematics, CLASSY spectra permit measurements of the internal structure and density of the outflowing clouds. Utilizing the relative column densities of ground-state transitions and collisionally excited fine-structure lines, such as Si II*, researchers have directly measured the electron density ($n_e$) of the outflowing gas. These densities correlate with the star formation rate surface density. Depending on whether photoionization models are used or the clouds are assumed to be in pressure equilibrium with the hot wind fluid, the absorbing gas is estimated to be located at distances of 1-2 or $\sim 5$ times the starburst radius ($r_*$), respectively [Xu et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948...28X/abstract). At these radii, the inferred cloud sizes are sufficiently large to survive disruption by the hot surrounding wind fluid.

## Diagnosing the Multiphase Interstellar Medium

The FUV spectra obtained by the CLASSY survey provide a unique empirical window into the multiphase interstellar medium (ISM). By targeting a wide array of fine-structure transitions and low-ionization state (LIS) absorption lines, the survey enables precise constraints on gas densities, column densities, and the spatial structure of both the neutral and ionized gas. 

A primary diagnostic of the warm ionized phase—particularly within starburst-driven galactic outflows—is the measurement of the electron density, $n_e$. In CLASSY spectra, $n_e$ is directly determined using the relative column densities of ground-state silicon (Si II) and its fine-structure excited state (Si II*). Because the Si II* state is primarily populated via collisional excitation by free electrons, the ratio of these lines acts as a sensitive density probe [Xu et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948...28X/abstract). Deriving $n_e$ allows for the estimation of further physical properties of the outflowing clouds, such as their volume filling factors, masses, and physical distances from the central starburst [Xu et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948...28X/abstract). 

Beyond the ionized outflows, CLASSY provides critical calibrations for the neutral ISM. The survey characterizes the neutral gas by performing simultaneous Voigt profile fits to multiple LIS UV absorption lines, including O I, Si II, S II, C II, and Al II. For $\sim 75\%$ of the CLASSY sample, a single-component Voigt profile accurately models the LIS absorption, successfully returning the average properties, such as column density and covering fraction, of the neutral absorbing gas [Parker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..104P/abstract). By directly comparing these UV absorption features with 21 cm H I observations, CLASSY has demonstrated that C II is the most robust UV tracer of neutral gas trends. This empirical linkage establishes LIS absorption lines as a powerful predictive tool for deriving neutral hydrogen properties when direct 21 cm mapping is unfeasible, providing a calibration that is vital for interpreting rest-frame UV spectra of early galaxies obtained by the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] [Parker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..104P/abstract).

## Chemical Abundances and Nitrogen Enrichment

The CLASSY survey provides a unique dataset for investigating the chemical evolution of star-forming galaxies, particularly the relationship between the nitrogen-to-oxygen ratio (N/O) and gas-phase metallicity (O/H). Accurate measurements of [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] are achieved using the direct-$T_{\rm e}$ method. By directly measuring electron temperatures ($T_{\rm e}$) and densities ($n_{\rm e}$) from [[emission-line-galaxies|auroral emission lines]], this robust approach circumvents the systematic uncertainties and calibration dependencies inherent in strong-line metallicity methods [Stephenson et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023RNAAS...7...31S/abstract), [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract).

Traditional models of galactic chemical evolution often describe the N/O ratio as following a simple age-metallicity degeneracy, where nitrogen is produced as both a primary and secondary element by intermediate-mass and massive stars over different timescales. However, observations from CLASSY demonstrate that the N/O-O/H relation exhibits a significant degree of scatter that cannot be entirely explained by metallicity or stellar mass alone [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711658A/abstract). The CLASSY galaxies follow the general N/O-O/H trends observed in local extragalactic H II regions and dwarf galaxies [Stephenson et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023RNAAS...7...31S/abstract), [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract). Notably, comparisons with [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|high-redshift sources]] indicate that galaxies at $z = 2-6$ exhibit similar N/O ratios at a fixed metallicity, suggesting that there is no significant redshift evolution in the N/O enrichment pathways across cosmic time [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract), [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711658A/abstract). This lack of evolution is a critical result, as it validates the use of local CLASSY analogs to interpret the rest-frame ultraviolet spectra of high-$z$ systems.

The detailed multiphase data available for the sample reveals that the scatter in the N/O-O/H plane is strongly influenced by the physical conditions of the ISM and the specifics of recent star formation. A significant positive correlation is observed between the N/O ratio and the electron density, typically traced by $n_{\rm e}$([S II]), indicating that local density structures play a key role in shaping chemical enrichment [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract). Furthermore, galaxies characterized by high star formation rates and highly compact star-forming regions tend to exhibit elevated N/O ratios. Conversely, there is no strong correlation found between N/O and total stellar mass, shifting the paradigm away from mass-driven chemical evolution in these systems [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711658A/abstract).

CLASSY has also highlighted the independent role of stellar feedback in modulating chemical abundances. Elevated N/O ratios are specifically linked to the presence of high-velocity galactic outflows, defined by $v_{\rm out} \gtrsim 350$ km s$^{-1}$, and corresponding low mass-loading factors [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract). These kinematic signatures suggest that feedback from young, massive stellar populations selectively retains or ejects different nucleosynthetic products, fundamentally altering the chemical makeup of the interstellar medium. Consequently, the nitrogen enrichment in starburst environments is now understood to be a complex product of local gas density, star formation compactness, and feedback-driven outflows, rather than a simple function of global galaxy mass or age [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract), [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711658A/abstract).

## Aperture Effects and Multi-wavelength Synergy

A crucial component of the CLASSY survey's utility as a local benchmark for [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]] relies on the integration of far-ultraviolet (FUV) spectra with multi-wavelength ancillary data. However, cross-wavelength comparisons frequently necessitate combining observations taken with different instruments and aperture sizes, such as the 2.5 arcsec circular aperture of the [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope]]'s [[cosmic-origins-spectrograph-cos-surveys|Cosmic Origins Spectrograph (COS)]], the 3.0 arcsec fibers of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey, and various long-slit or [[manga-survey|integral-field unit (IFU)]] configurations.

Understanding how these aperture mismatches influence derived physical parameters is essential for interpreting both local and high-redshift spectra. Spatially resolved baselines provided by large-scale [[galaxy-integral-field-spectroscopy-tool|integral field spectroscopy]] campaigns are foundational in this regard. The CALIFA survey, for instance, has yielded significant insights into inside-out galaxy growth as a spatially resolved baseline [Delgado et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013IAUS..295..300D/abstract), and has established the importance of aperture corrections specifically for stellar kinematics—corrections that prove vital when comparing local observations to poorly resolved high-redshift sources [Falcón-Barroso et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...597A..48F/abstract). Similarly, the SAMI Galaxy Survey serves as a crucial benchmark, having provided integral field spectroscopy for over 3,400 galaxies to contextualize the spatially resolved measurements that CLASSY's targeted studies help calibrate [Bryant et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.447.2857B/abstract). In the broader statistical context, large-scale homogenized catalogs such as the spectroscopic component of RCSEDv2 provide homogeneously analyzed kinematics and abundances for over 4 million galaxies, emphasizing the need for robust spatial and spectral matching across differing surveys [Toptun et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ASPC..535..417T/abstract), [Goradzhanov et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ASPC..535..175G/abstract).

Comparisons within the CLASSY sample demonstrate that fundamental nebular properties—such as electron temperature ($T_e$), electron density ($n_e$), and gas-phase metallicity ($12 + \log(\text{O/H})$)—remain remarkably consistent across different optical aperture sizes [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...935...74A/abstract). This indicates that the local ionized gas conditions in these [[emission-line-galaxies|Emission-line galaxies]] are sufficiently uniform to permit reliable cross-wavelength analyses regardless of the specific extraction region. Conversely, dust reddening measurements are highly sensitive to aperture effects. The color excess $E(B-V)$ derived from optical Balmer decrements can decrease by up to $53\%$ as the extraction aperture increases, primarily due to spatial variations in the central regions of the starburst, before leveling out at scales comparable to the 2.5 arcsec COS aperture [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...935...74A/abstract). 

Aperture effects also play a critical role in the interpretation of UV spectral morphology. Mock spectra generated from a single virtual galaxy in a high-resolution radiation-hydrodynamic simulation have shown that aperture losses can explain the observed weakness of fluorescent emission lines in many of the survey's FUV spectra [Gazagnes et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...952..164G/abstract). If these geometric losses are not properly modeled, they can severely bias the interpretation of "down-the-barrel" absorption and emission profiles.

In addition to optical nebular lines, CLASSY strongly leverages radio observations to probe the neutral interstellar medium. Observations of the 21 cm H I emission line have been obtained for 35 CLASSY galaxies, revealing a subset of gas-poor or non-detected systems that may be entering a post-starburst evolutionary phase [Parker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...977..104P/abstract). Because 21 cm H I mapping is observationally prohibitive at cosmological distances, calibrating UV Low-Ionization State (LIS) absorption lines against these radio benchmarks establishes the vital empirical framework needed to predict neutral gas properties in the early universe.

## Advanced Spectral Modeling and Uncertainties

Measuring the mass, momentum, and energetics of starburst-driven galactic outflows relies critically on accurately determining the column density of the entrained gas. In the CLASSY survey, these parameters are predominantly extracted from 1D "down-the-barrel" far-ultraviolet spectra. However, converting observed absorption line profiles into physical gas masses represents a major analytical challenge. The literature currently reflects a significant and unresolved tension regarding which modeling framework most accurately recovers these physical outflow parameters, particularly between empirical Apparent Optical Depth (AOD) integration and Semi-Analytic Line Transfer (SALT) models.

The Apparent Optical Depth technique, typically employed alongside partial covering fraction assumptions, maps the observed residual flux of an absorption line directly to an optical depth, $\tau(v)$, as a function of velocity, allowing the total column density to be derived via a direct line-of-sight integration. While the AOD method is a standard empirical tool, it is highly susceptible to the finite spectral resolution of the observing instrument. Recent analyses of CLASSY spectra demonstrate that a substantial fraction of outflowing mass is often carried by dense gas concentrated in a very narrow range of low velocities. Because this optically thick, low-velocity gas is effectively hidden by finite resolution limits, empirical AOD methods systematically underestimate the true gas column density [Huberty et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975...58H/abstract).

To overcome the limitations of purely empirical line-of-sight integrations, Semi-Analytic Line Transfer (SALT) models treat the outflow as a continuous fluid and solve the fundamental radiative transfer equations to generate synthetic line profiles. SALT models account for hidden low-velocity gas by parameterizing the macroscopic wind kinematics and thermodynamics, often assuming power-law profiles for the radial density ($\rho \propto r^{-\alpha}$) and velocity ($v \propto r^{\beta}$). While SALT successfully recovers mass hidden from AOD measurements, it introduces its own systemic risks: SALT models may artificially overestimate column densities if the assumed idealized power-law profiles do not accurately reflect the complex, multiphase physical reality of actual galactic winds [Huberty et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975...58H/abstract). 

This ambiguity in spectral modeling is further compounded by the aforementioned instrumental and geometric aperture effects [Gazagnes et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...952..164G/abstract). Consequently, reconciling the discrepancy between AOD and SALT methodologies requires rigorously accounting for both aperture losses and the underlying theoretical assumptions about the spatial distribution of dense gas in galactic outflows.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While the CLASSY survey has provided a robust baseline for interpreting the rest-frame far-ultraviolet (FUV) spectra of star-forming galaxies, several open questions remain regarding the physical nature of stellar feedback, multiphase outflows, and early chemical enrichment.

**Multiphase Outflows and Cloud Survival**
A major focus of ongoing research is mapping the full radial extent and mass budget of galactic winds. CLASSY data trace warm ionized outflows, revealing that outflow clouds are typically located at distances $1$ to $5 r_*$ (where $r_*$ is the starburst radius) and are large enough to survive their destructive interaction with the hot wind fluid [Xu et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948...28X/abstract). However, the warm phase observed in FUV absorption lines accounts for only $\sim 20\%$ of the supernova-produced silicon [Xu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...933..222X/abstract), leaving the total mass and metal budget highly uncertain. Resolving the discrepancies between AOD and SALT spectral modeling techniques will be critical for accurately measuring mass-loading factors moving forward [Huberty et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975...58H/abstract).

**Chemical Scatter and Stellar Feedback**
CLASSY results challenge traditional astrophysical paradigms by demonstrating that N/O enrichment is heavily shaped by the compactness of star formation and local gas density rather than just stellar mass or age [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract), [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711658A/abstract). The exact physical mechanism driving this density-abundance coupling remains an open theoretical question. Furthermore, the strong link between elevated N/O ratios and high-velocity outflows ($v_{\rm out} > 350$ km s$^{-1}$) suggests that stellar feedback directly modulates local chemical scatter, prompting the need for more advanced hydrodynamical simulations to understand nucleosynthetic retention in starburst environments.

**Observational Systematics and High-Redshift Analogs**
Finally, as astronomy moves further into the era of the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]], CLASSY's primary legacy will be tested against observations from cosmic noon and the epoch of reionization. While CLASSY confirms that there is no significant redshift evolution in N/O for a fixed metallicity up to $z \sim 6$ [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.544.1588A/abstract), [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250711658A/abstract), it remains an open question whether these local benchmarks can accurately proxy the extreme ISM conditions—such as extreme ionization parameters and pristine metallicities—expected in the [[first-light-galaxies|First-light galaxies]] at $z > 10$. For instance, CLASSY's UV insights will specifically aid in interpreting multi-phase outflows and ionizing properties observed in current JWST/NIRSpec datasets like the SMILES survey during cosmic noon and beyond [Zhu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250812599Z/abstract). Ultimately, characterizing these high-fidelity local templates contextualizes CLASSY within the long-term astronomical goal of exhausting the information content of [[extragalactic-astronomical-surveys|extragalactic datasets]], moving toward the 'final galaxy survey' paradigm where all statistical properties of the universe's galaxy population are definitively mapped and understood [Rhodes et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020AJ....160..261R/abstract). This overarching goal will be further advanced by upcoming flagship missions. For instance, the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope's High Latitude Wide Area Survey represents the next frontier for wide-area galaxy clustering and weak lensing [Eifler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241104088E/abstract). Concurrently, the forthcoming SPHEREx all-sky survey will map the universe on unprecedented scales, though navigating challenges such as galaxy-galaxy blending—which is expected to impact up to 9% of sources in deep fields—will require precise, high-resolution empirical priors like those established by CLASSY to ensure accurate cosmological analyses [Dachan et al.
