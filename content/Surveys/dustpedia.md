**DustPedia** is a collaborative research initiative and comprehensive multi-wavelength archive designed to characterize the physical properties of cosmic dust, the interstellar medium (ISM), and [[dust-obscured-star-formation-and-galaxies|Dust-Obscured Star Formation and Galaxies]] in the [[local-volume-and-nearby-galaxy-studies|Local Volume and Nearby Galaxy Studies]]. Capitalizing on the legacy of the [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]], the project provides a highly homogenized dataset for 875 nearby galaxies (recession velocities $v \le 3000\text{ km s}^{-1}$) across 42 broad-band filters spanning from the ultraviolet to the microwave [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. Historically, early [[far-infrared-astronomy|Far-infrared astronomy]] observations from facilities like IRAS were fundamentally limited by low spatial resolution, which often forced researchers to treat galaxies as single-temperature point sources [[bibcode-1989a-arv-1-49c|bibcode:1989A&ARv...1...49C]]. DustPedia overcomes these limitations by integrating modern, high-resolution imagery to explicitly trace the complete energy balance of nearby galaxies—the physical principle that stellar radiation absorbed by interstellar dust grains in the ultraviolet and optical is entirely thermally re-emitted in the far-infrared and sub-millimeter regimes. 

A major technical achievement of the project is its rigorous standardization of archival data. By combining custom Herschel reductions with observations from GALEX, the [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]], the [[two-micron-all-sky-survey-2mass|Two Micron All-Sky Survey (2MASS)]], [[allwise|AllWISE]], Spitzer, and Planck, DustPedia utilizes specialized pipelines to extract consistent, aperture-matched photometry. These precise spectral energy distributions (SEDs) form the quantitative foundation for 1D energy-balance fitting and complex 3D Monte Carlo radiative transfer modeling. The resulting analyses have driven significant advances in [[local-volume-and-nearby-galaxy-studies|Local Volume and Nearby Galaxy Studies]], identifying critical sub-kiloparsec ISM scaling relations and demonstrating that global dust mass correlates more strongly with atomic rather than molecular gas in late-type galaxies. Radiative transfer models applied to the sample have also quantified competing dust heating mechanisms; while young stars ($<100$ Myr) dominate global dust heating, spatially resolved mapping proves that older stellar populations and active galactic nuclei (AGN) can drive the majority of diffuse dust heating in galactic bulges, barred regions, and central sub-kpc environments. Furthermore, the DustPedia catalog serves as a vital observational benchmark for evaluating star formation and ISM feedback prescriptions in modern cosmological hydrodynamical simulations, such as EAGLE and TNG50 [[bibcode-2020mnras-494-2823t|bibcode:2020MNRAS.494.2823T]], [[bibcode-2022mnras-516-3728t|bibcode:2022MNRAS.516.3728T]].

Despite establishing a robust empirical baseline for the local universe, DustPedia's detailed mapping has highlighted several enduring anomalies in [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]]. Significant open questions remain regarding the physical origin of the extraplanar dust observed in the halos of large edge-on galaxies, the unexplained $500\ \mu\text{m}$ sub-millimeter excess prevalent in low-metallicity dwarf irregulars, and the physical drivers behind "quenched rings" of ancient stars newly discovered in the outskirts of grand-design spirals. As the field currently navigates a gap in active, deep far-infrared space missions, researchers are leveraging the DustPedia archive to train machine learning algorithms as "virtual FIR telescopes," predicting missing sub-millimeter fluxes from existing optical and mid-infrared data. Ultimately, these $z \sim 0$ scaling relations provide an essential calibrator for interpreting the massive, heavily obscured galaxies currently being uncovered by ALMA and the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] during [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]].

## Project Overview and the DustPedia Sample

DustPedia is a collaborative, European Union-funded research initiative designed to maximize the scientific return of the [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]] by constructing a comprehensive, multi-wavelength legacy dataset of galaxies in the [[local-volume-and-nearby-galaxy-studies|Local Volume and Nearby Galaxy Studies]] [[bibcode-2022a-a-668a-130c|bibcode:2022A&A...668A.130C]]. The project addresses the critical need for homogenized [[ancillary-data|Ancillary data]], providing a uniform foundation for studying the interplay between stars, gas, and interstellar dust across a wide range of galactic environments.

The core DustPedia galaxy sample consists of 875 nearby galaxies. Inclusion in the sample required that a target be observed by Herschel, reside at a local distance corresponding to a recession velocity of $v \leq 3000$ km/s, and possess an extended optical diameter of at least 1 arcminute [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]], [[bibcode-2018a-a-620a-112b|bibcode:2018A&A...620A.112B]]. This selection yields a statistically robust representation of the local galaxy population. The sample spans diverse morphological types, encompassing galaxies with stellar masses ranging from $M_{\star} \sim 10^7$ to $10^{11} M_{\odot}$ and star formation rates spanning several orders of magnitude [[bibcode-2020a-a-633a-100c|bibcode:2020A&A...633A.100C]], [[bibcode-2022a-a-668a-130c|bibcode:2022A&A...668A.130C]].

To capture the full energy balance of these galaxies—tracing everything from unattenuated stellar emission in the ultraviolet to thermal dust continuum in the [[far-infrared-astronomy|Far-infrared astronomy]] and sub-millimeter—the DustPedia archive integrates data across 42 broad-band filters [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. In addition to custom reductions of Herschel SPIRE and PACS maps, the project incorporates standardized archival imagery from GALEX, the [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]], the Digitized Sky Survey (DSS), the [[two-micron-all-sky-survey-2mass|Two Micron All-Sky Survey (2MASS)]], [[allwise|AllWISE]], Spitzer, and Planck, along with supplementary photometry from IRAS [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]].

A major technical achievement of the project is the homogenization of these disparate data sources. Traditional cross-survey comparisons are often plagued by inconsistencies in aperture selection, background subtraction, and resolution. To mitigate this, the DustPedia team developed the Comprehensive & Adaptable Aperture Photometry Routine (CAAPR), a pipeline specifically engineered to perform consistent, aperture-matched photometry across all bands while rigorously extrapolating aperture noise to ensure cross-compatible uncertainties [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. This effort yielded a total of 21,857 photometric measurements, with a typical DustPedia galaxy possessing coverage in 25 distinct wavebands [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]].

The resulting multi-wavelength catalog and associated 2D imagery provide the community with an unprecedented tool for [[local-volume-and-nearby-galaxy-studies|Local Volume and Nearby Galaxy Studies]]. Alongside contemporary large-scale multiwavelength resources like the Siena Galaxy Atlas (SGA-2020) [[bibcode-2023apjs-269-3m|bibcode:2023ApJS..269....3M]], which provides standardized optical and infrared surface brightness profiles for hundreds of thousands of nearby galaxies, DustPedia stands as a definitive benchmark. Methodologies for analyzing local galaxy structure have been further refined through systematic surface photometry programs using databases like the 2MASS [[all-sky-surveys|All-sky surveys]] image set [[bibcode-2012pasa-29-174s|bibcode:2012PASA...29..174S]]. By successfully bridging the observational gap between ultraviolet and microwave wavelengths, the DustPedia sample serves as a foundation for deriving spatially resolved interstellar medium scaling relations, calibrating dynamic dust-to-gas ratios, and executing high-resolution 3D radiative transfer models of galaxies in the local Universe [[bibcode-2020a-a-633a-100c|bibcode:2020A&A...633A.100C]], [[bibcode-2020a-a-643a-90n|bibcode:2020A&A...643A..90N]].

## Historical Context: From IRAS to DustPedia

The systematic study of the interstellar medium (ISM) and its dust content has undergone a profound transformation since the first infrared [[all-sky-surveys|All-sky surveys]]. The unprecedented data quality provided by the Infrared Astronomical Satellite (IRAS) in the 1980s catalyzed a series of foundational investigations into the origin of galactic infrared and sub-millimeter radiation, as well as the nature of the stellar populations responsible for heating interstellar dust grains [[bibcode-1989a-arv-1-49c|bibcode:1989A&ARv...1...49C]]. However, early [[far-infrared-astronomy|Far-infrared astronomy]] was fundamentally limited by low spatial resolution and restricted wavelength coverage, which often forced researchers to model galaxies as single-temperature point sources.

The transition to high-resolution, multi-wavelength astrophysics accelerated with the launch of the Spitzer Space Telescope and the [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]]. A critical milestone in this era was the Spitzer Infrared Nearby Galaxies Survey (SINGS), which provided comprehensive mid-infrared imaging and spectroscopic mapping for a diverse sample of local galaxies. Additionally, the Spitzer Local Volume Legacy (LVL) served as a direct precursor to DustPedia, obtaining complete IRAC and [[mips-spitzer-instrument|MIPS (Spitzer instrument)]] imaging for a sample of 258 nearby galaxies [[bibcode-2007aas-211-9502k|bibcode:2007AAS...211.9502K]]. This era also saw the execution of precursor programs like the Spitzer-SDSS-GALEX Spectroscopic Survey (SSGSS), which combined multi-wavelength data to investigate the links between dust mass, star formation history, and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) luminosity [[bibcode-2011apj-741-79o|bibcode:2011ApJ...741...79O]]. SINGS was subsequently succeeded by the [[kingfish-survey|KINGFISH survey]] (Key Insights on Nearby Galaxies: a Far-Infrared Survey with Herschel), which targeted 61 nearby galaxies ($d < 30$ Mpc) to obtain complete far-infrared and sub-millimeter maps spanning $70$ to $500\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ [[bibcode-2011pasp-123-1347k|bibcode:2011PASP..123.1347K]]. By combining the SINGS and KINGFISH datasets, astronomers achieved the panchromatic coverage necessary to spatially resolve individual star-forming regions and explicitly trace the ISM's primary heating and cooling channels [[bibcode-2011pasp-123-1347k|bibcode:2011PASP..123.1347K]]. High-resolution stellar surveys such as [[phat-survey|PHAT survey]] for M31 have uniquely complemented these FIR surveys by providing resolved UV-to-NIR photometry for thousands of stellar clusters [[bibcode-2012apj-752-95j|bibcode:2012ApJ...752...95J]].

At the same time, the [[hatlas|HATLAS]]/[[gama-survey|GAMA survey]] conducted a statistical census of dust emission in optically selected galaxies, establishing that dust mass robustly correlates with stellar mass, and that blue star-forming galaxies are systematically more luminous and warmer in dust emission than their red-sequence counterparts [[bibcode-2012mnras-421-3027b|bibcode:2012MNRAS.421.3027B]]. The era of high-resolution FIR surveys also benefited from [[akari|AKARI]] Deep Field-South observations, which provided essential mid- to far-infrared source catalogs to constrain galaxy evolution models [[bibcode-2011mnras-411-373c|bibcode:2011MNRAS.411..373C]]. Furthermore, programs like the Spitzer Extended Deep Survey (SEDS) have been crucial for tracing stellar mass assembly back to the era of reionization [[bibcode-2011aspc-446-347f|bibcode:2011ASPC..446..347F]].

These high-resolution observations also exposed the limitations of traditional energy-balance paradigms. For instance, far-infrared photometry from the [[kingfish-survey|KINGFISH survey]] demonstrated that utilizing simple single-temperature modified blackbody curves failed to capture the full multiphase temperature distribution of galaxy dust. When applying more sophisticated physical models, the derived dust masses were found to be, on average, nearly a factor of two higher than those predicted by single-temperature fits [[bibcode-2012apj-745-95d|bibcode:2012ApJ...745...95D]]. Furthermore, a significant tension in the literature emerged regarding low-metallicity environments: dwarf irregular galaxies were observed to exhibit a pronounced sub-millimeter excess at $500\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ that standard dust emission models could not reproduce [[bibcode-2012apj-745-95d|bibcode:2012ApJ...745...95D]]. The physical origin of this excess remains an open question in the field.

To address these lingering uncertainties and fully exploit the observational legacy of modern space telescopes, the community recognized the need for rigorous archive homogenization. The DustPedia project was developed to synthesize these disparate multi-wavelength catalogs into a uniform framework. Capitalizing on the Herschel archive, DustPedia compiled imagery and consistent aperture-matched photometry for 875 nearby galaxies ($v \leq 3000\ \mathrm{km\ s}^{-1}$) across 42 bands from the ultraviolet to the microwave [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. By integrating custom Herschel reductions with standardized observations from GALEX, [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]], the [[two-micron-all-sky-survey-2mass|Two Micron All-Sky Survey (2MASS)]], [[allwise|AllWISE]], Spitzer, Planck, and IRAS, DustPedia generated a definitive database of 21,857 photometric measurements [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. This massive unification of data has provided the quantitative foundation required for modern 3D radiative transfer modeling and the evaluation of resolved scaling relations in the local Universe.

## Multiwavelength Data Integration and Standardized Photometry

To accurately model the physical processes driving galaxy evolution, it is necessary to capture the complete energy balance of the interstellar medium. This requires the seamless integration of the unattenuated stellar continuum in the ultraviolet (UV) and optical with the thermal re-emission from dust in the far-infrared and sub-millimeter regimes. To achieve this, the DustPedia project provides a comprehensive, standardized photometric dataset for 875 extended, nearby galaxies—defined as having radial velocities $v \leq 3000\text{ km s}^{-1}$—that were mapped by the [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]] [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]], [[bibcode-2018a-a-620a-112b|bibcode:2018A&A...620A.112B]].

The project synthesizes imagery and photometry across 42 distinct UV-to-microwave bands. This effort combines custom data reductions of Herschel observations with standardized archival data from multiple space- and ground-based facilities, including GALEX, the [[sloan-digital-sky-survey-and-sub-surveys|Sloan Digital Sky Survey and Sub-surveys]] (SDSS), DSS, [[allwise|AllWISE]], Spitzer, and Planck [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. [[near-infrared-astronomy|Near-Infrared Astronomy]] constraints for large, extended galaxies in the DustPedia sample are often supplemented by morphological and photometric data from the [[two-micron-all-sky-survey-2mass|Two Micron All-Sky Survey (2MASS)]] Large Galaxy Atlas [[bibcode-2003aj-125-525j|bibcode:2003AJ....125..525J]], while the 2MASS All-Sky Point Source Catalog serves as the foundational near-infrared all-sky photometric census for determining the stellar mass distribution in these systems [[bibcode-2003ipac-data-i2s|bibcode:2003ipac.data...I2S]]. Integrating such highly heterogeneous data poses significant challenges due to vastly different spatial resolutions, point-spread functions (PSFs), and background noise characteristics.

To overcome these systematic barriers, the DustPedia team developed the Comprehensive & Adaptable Aperture Photometry Routine (CAAPR). The CAAPR pipeline was specifically engineered to perform consistent, aperture-matched photometry across all 42 photometric bands [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. By standardizing the extraction of photometric fluxes, CAAPR ensures that multiwavelength measurements map to the exact same physical regions of a target galaxy. A critical innovation within the CAAPR pipeline is its novel method for robustly extrapolating aperture noise; this approach reliably determines cross-compatible uncertainties even for archival observations where the field of view provides a very limited amount of background sky [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. This is particularly crucial given the residual uncertainties inherent in background subtraction for sub-millimeter and millimeter photometry, which often complicate extended source measurements [[bibcode-2015mnras-451-1606f|bibcode:2015MNRAS.451.1606F]].

The application of CAAPR to the DustPedia sample, supplemented by external photometry from IRAS and Planck, yielded a total of 21,857 individual photometric measurements. As a result, a typical DustPedia galaxy possesses highly reliable, aperture-matched photometry spanning approximately 25 different bands [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-37c|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..37C]]. 

This rigorous UV-to-microwave homogenization is foundational for both global and spatially resolved panchromatic studies. By relying on standardized archival data, researchers can execute precise spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting to derive physical parameters—such as stellar mass, dust mass, and [[monochromatic-sfr-estimators|Monochromatic SFR estimators]] (SFR)—down to sub-kiloparsec scales, typically ranging from $0.3$ to $3.4\text{ kpc}$ [[bibcode-2020mnras-493-4107e|bibcode:2020MNRAS.493.4107E]], [[bibcode-2022a-a-668a-130c|bibcode:2022A&A...668A.130C]]. Consequently, the CAAPR-processed DustPedia catalog serves as a vital benchmark in [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]], enabling detailed structural decomposition across wavelengths and providing robust empirical constraints for modern cosmological hydrodynamics simulations [[bibcode-2020a-a-641a-119b|bibcode:2020A&A...641A.119B]], [[bibcode-2020mnras-494-2823t|bibcode:2020MNRAS.494.2823T]]. Such rigorous efforts parallel other major contemporary homogenization initiatives. For instance, the RCSEDv2 catalog standardizes multi-wavelength photometric data across several wide-field surveys for millions of galaxies [[bibcode-2024aspc-535-417t|bibcode:2024ASPC..535..417T]]. Similar to DustPedia's homogenization efforts, the Spitzer Data Fusion and SERVS projects provide consolidated far-ultraviolet to far-infrared flux measurements for millions of IRAC-selected sources [[bibcode-2015fers-confe-27v|bibcode:2015fers.confE..27V]]. Additionally, the integration of UV and optical [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] data is also advanced by the SwiM catalog, which combines Swift/UVOT imaging with [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] spectroscopy to study resolved star formation and attenuation [[bibcode-2023apjs-268-63m|bibcode:2023ApJS..268...63M]]. Panchromatic studies are increasingly supported by high-resolution H-alpha maps from [[phangs-survey|PHANGS survey]], enabling multi-phase investigations into the underlying processes driving star formation [[bibcode-2025arxiv250318791c|bibcode:2025arXiv250318791C]].

## Energy-Balance Modeling and Dust Heating

The fundamental principle of energy-balance modeling asserts that the stellar radiation absorbed by interstellar dust in the ultraviolet and optical is entirely re-emitted at longer wavelengths. Mathematically, this enforces the condition that the total energy emitted by dust equals the absorbed stellar energy $E_a(z)$, often expressed as:
$$ \int \varepsilon_d(\nu, z) d\nu = E_a(z) $$
where $\varepsilon_d$ is the dust emission coefficient (the energy emitted by dust per unit volume and frequency). In the DustPedia project, this principle is heavily leveraged using spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting codes such as CIGALE and [[magphys|MAGPHYS]]. By applying CIGALE to aperture-matched photometry across the DustPedia sample, researchers have quantified the fraction of bolometric luminosity absorbed by dust, $f_{\rm abs}$. On average, 19% of the total stellar radiation in these local galaxies is absorbed and re-processed by dust, a fraction that rises to 25% for late-type spirals [[bibcode-2018a-a-620a-112b|bibcode:2018A&A...620A.112B]].

While global energy-balance SED fitting provides robust galaxy-integrated properties, the highly structured morphology of dust requires 3D spatial modeling. To account for complex geometries, local heating variations, and internal dust attenuation, DustPedia heavily utilizes the 3D Monte Carlo radiative transfer code SKIRT. SKIRT models map the 3D radiation field, explicitly tracking the absorption, scattering, and thermal re-emission of photons by dust grains [[bibcode-2020a-a-637a-25n|bibcode:2020A&A...637A..25N]]. Note that variations in the 3D extinction curve on kiloparsec scales, as identified in the Milky Way, present additional challenges for modeling dust evolution and applying local extinction curves to distant galaxies [[bibcode-2017apj-838-36s|bibcode:2017ApJ...838...36S]].

A major application of SKIRT within the DustPedia framework is identifying the primary sources of dust heating. While classic models often attributed cold dust heating entirely to recent star formation, resolved 3D models confirm a mixed heating scenario. Young stellar populations ($<100$ Myr) are typically the dominant heating agents, globally supplying approximately 50% to 71% of the necessary luminosity [[bibcode-2020a-a-637a-25n|bibcode:2020A&A...637A..25N]], [[bibcode-2020a-a-643a-90n|bibcode:2020A&A...643A..90N]]. However, older stellar populations ($\geq 8$ Gyr) contribute substantially to cold dust heating, particularly in specific galactic environments characterized by high stellar mass surface densities. For instance, in barred galaxies, the heating fraction from young stars drops to $\sim 53\%$ in the bar region and $\sim 38\%$ in the bulge, where evolved stars become the dominant heating source [[bibcode-2020a-a-637a-25n|bibcode:2020A&A...637A..25N]]. This spatial variation creates a distinct dust temperature ($T_{\rm dust}$) gradient, with $T_{\rm dust}$ typically peaking at $\sim 24$ K in the galactic center and declining to $\sim 15$ K in the disk outskirts [[bibcode-2025a-a-701a-74t|bibcode:2025A&A...701A..74T]]. Spatially resolved analyses suggest that there is no single universal heating mechanism; rather, the relative contribution of young versus evolved stars varies widely between individual galaxies depending on their specific evolutionary state and structure [[bibcode-2025a-a-701a-74t|bibcode:2025A&A...701A..74T]].

Furthermore, SKIRT models have quantified external and non-stellar heating sources. In the interacting M51 system (one of the well-studied [[messier-objects|Messier Objects]]), the companion galaxy NGC 5195 provides $\sim 5.8\%$ of the global dust heating for the combined interacting system, with the remainder primarily driven by young (71.2%) and old (23%) stellar populations within NGC 5194 itself [[bibcode-2020a-a-643a-90n|bibcode:2020A&A...643A..90N]]. In active galaxies like NGC 1068, AGN-powered heating of diffuse dust beyond the torus can dominate the inner few hundred parsecs ($>90\%$ of heating) while remaining at percentage levels in the wider galactic disk [[bibcode-2020a-a-638a-150v|bibcode:2020A&A...638A.150V]].

## The Interstellar Medium and Scaling Relations

DustPedia provides a unified framework for examining the multi-phase interstellar medium (ISM) across both global and sub-galactic scales. By combining multi-wavelength photometry—including [[far-infrared-astronomy|Far-infrared astronomy]] data from the [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]]—with atomic and molecular gas catalogs, the project enables precise quantitative constraints on how dust, gas, and stellar components co-evolve.

At the global level, a key finding is that the total dust mass correlates tightly with the total gas mass. Unexpectedly, in late-type galaxies, the dust mass exhibits a stronger correlation with atomic gas (H I) than with molecular gas (H$_2$) [[bibcode-2020a-a-633a-100c|bibcode:2020A&A...633A.100C]]. DustPedia's integrated findings complement other major initiatives like the [[jingle-survey|JINGLE survey]], which uses [[james-clerk-maxwell-telescope|James Clerk Maxwell Telescope]] 850 $\mu$m observations to benchmark scaling relations between dust and gas, specifically noting a non-linearity between 850 $\mu$m and CO line luminosities [[bibcode-2018mnras-481-3497s|bibcode:2018MNRAS.481.3497S]]. To mitigate the intrinsic scatter observed in these standard scaling relations, researchers have proposed a "fundamental plane" of star formation for DustPedia star-forming galaxies that tightly links molecular gas mass, stellar mass, and the [[monochromatic-sfr-estimators|Monochromatic SFR estimators]] [[bibcode-2025a-a-699a-346s|bibcode:2025A&A...699A.346S]]. Moreover, in low-luminosity galaxies (with SFR $< 1\ M_{\odot}\text{ yr}^{-1}$), the ratio of SFR to total infrared luminosity ($L_{\text{IR}}$) systematically exceeds standard values, suggesting that unobscured star formation overcomes the effects of dust heating by older stars in these regimes [[bibcode-2024galax-12-37b|bibcode:2024Galax..12...37B]].

The DustPedia dataset also highlights significant tensions with traditional textbook assumptions regarding chemical evolution. While early models often assumed a constant dust-to-metal (DTM) ratio, empirical results show that the DTM ratio evolves by orders of magnitude depending on a galaxy's gas-phase metallicity and evolutionary state. For unevolved, low-metallicity galaxies with an oxygen abundance of $12 + \log(\text{O/H}) < 8.2$, the DTM ratio is roughly 2.1 to 6 times lower than the typical value ($M_d/M_Z \sim 0.214$) found in more evolved sources [[bibcode-2019a-a-623a-5d|bibcode:2019A&A...623A...5D]]. This steep dependence indicates that grain growth in the ISM becomes the dominant dust production mechanism only above approximately one-fifth of solar metallicity [[bibcode-2021a-a-649a-18g|bibcode:2021A&A...649A..18G]]. In these same extreme environments, studies note that spatially resolved polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) emission features in low-metallicity dwarf galaxies show remarkable uniformity despite varying local radiation field strengths, providing an empirical baseline for DustPedia's more complex resolved models [[bibcode-2010apj-724-215h|bibcode:2010ApJ...724..215H]]. Furthermore, spatially resolved analysis of the dwarf irregular galaxy NGC 1569 uses DustPedia data to derive new local dust-to-gas ratios and demonstrates that stellar feedback generates significant outflows in the galaxy [[bibcode-2023mnras-526-2479g|bibcode:2023MNRAS.526.2479G]]. Additionally, recovering the expected decrease in the dust-to-gas ratio with increasing morphological stage requires the explicit adoption of a metallicity-dependent CO-to-H$_2$ conversion factor ($X_{\text{CO}}$); assuming a constant $X_{\text{CO}}$ fails to reproduce this trend [[bibcode-2020a-a-633a-100c|bibcode:2020A&A...633A.100C]].

Beyond global measurements, DustPedia data have been used to establish resolved scaling relations down to scales of 0.3 kpc in nearby face-on spirals. These sub-galactic analyses confirm that relations such as the dust-to-total-gas ratio persist at local scales, implying that ISM processes and star formation are locally self-regulated [[bibcode-2022a-a-668a-130c|bibcode:2022A&A...668A.130C]]. Interestingly, the spatial correlation between dust and molecular gas is tightest at sub-kpc and kpc scales, whereas the correlation between dust and atomic gas is stronger when integrated globally [[bibcode-2022a-a-668a-130c|bibcode:2022A&A...668A.130C]].

Spatially resolved mapping also confirms the existence of a universal, local star-forming main sequence, defined by the tight correlation between the stellar mass surface density ($\Sigma_*$) and the star formation rate surface density ($\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}$). Derived from over 30,000 physical cells, this relation takes the form:
$$ \log (\Sigma_{\text{SFR}}) = 0.82 \log (\Sigma_*) - 8.69 $$
This relation proves that star formation is fundamentally a locally regulated process [[bibcode-2020mnras-493-4107e|bibcode:2020MNRAS.493.4107E]]. However, as discussed in subsequent sections, this universal sequence can be modulated by internal morphological features like stellar bars.

Finally, the geometric distribution of these ISM components scales differently from the stars. Nonparametric morphological studies reveal that dust is generally less centrally concentrated, more asymmetric, and clumpier than the stellar distribution [[bibcode-2020a-a-641a-119b|bibcode:2020A&A...641A.119B]]. Radial profile fitting demonstrates that the dust-mass surface-density scale-length is, on average, 1.8 times larger than the stellar scale-length [[bibcode-2017a-a-605a-18c|bibcode:2017A&A...605A..18C]]. In contrast to the exponential profiles typical of stellar disks, submillimeter dust emission profiles frequently exhibit a central depletion, resulting in Sérsic indices systematically below $n=1$ [[bibcode-2019a-a-622a-132m|bibcode:2019A&A...622A.132M]]. Detailed multizone chemical evolution modeling, such as that performed for the DustPedia galaxy M74 (a member of the [[messier-objects|Messier Objects]]), utilizes these derived density profiles to support an "inside-out" disc formation scenario characterized by a radially increasing gas accretion timescale [[bibcode-2023mnras-523-2351c|bibcode:2023MNRAS.523.2351C]].

## Morphological and Environmental Influences

To understand the physical drivers of galaxy evolution, the DustPedia dataset has been heavily utilized to disentangle the effects of large-scale environments from internal morphological structures. A comparative analysis of 461 late- and early-type galaxies residing in the Virgo cluster versus the field reveals that local environmental density contrast plays a surprisingly subordinate role in driving global chemical and dust evolution [[bibcode-2019a-a-626a-63d|bibcode:2019A&A...626A..63D]]. For galaxies of a given morphological type, integrated properties such as the dust-to-gas ratio, specific star formation rate, and star formation efficiency exhibit little to no variation between cluster and field environments. Instead, intrinsic morphology and internal physical processes are the primary determinants of a galaxy's star-forming properties and interstellar medium balance [[bibcode-2019a-a-626a-63d|bibcode:2019A&A...626A..63D]].

Internal structural features, particularly stellar bars and spiral arms, exert a much stronger influence on the local distribution of dust and the regulation of star formation. For example, a detailed multiwavelength decomposition of M51 (from the [[messier-objects|Messier Objects]]) demonstrates that spiral arms contribute most significantly to emission in the ultraviolet, and failing to model these arm structures independently can lead to a 5-10% error in estimating disc exponential scale lengths [[bibcode-2024mnras-528-1276m|bibcode:2024MNRAS.528.1276M]]. The literature also contains active debate regarding the exact role of bars—specifically whether they enhance star formation by funneling gas inward or suppress it via dynamical heating and gas redistribution. The regulation of star formation by internal structure is clearly visible when examining the spatially resolved main sequence (introduced previously). While grand-design spirals strictly maintain this local main sequence down to sub-kiloparsec scales [[bibcode-2020mnras-493-4107e|bibcode:2020MNRAS.493.4107E]], the presence of a bar fundamentally disrupts it. Spatially resolved panchromatic SED modeling (utilizing codes such as [[magphys|MAGPHYS]]) of barred DustPedia galaxies demonstrates a distinct central quiescent signature in the bar-hosting regions, characterized by systematically lower star formation rates (SFRs) falling well below the main sequence. This signature strongly supports an "inside-out" quenching scenario driven by the rapid depletion of local gas reservoirs [[bibcode-2024a-a-687a-255s|bibcode:2024A&A...687A.255S]].

Spatially resolved analyses also reveal complex radial variations in mass-weighted stellar ages ($t_{\text{MW}}$). While the inner discs of these galaxies typically feature actively star-forming regions with $t_{\text{MW}} \sim 4$ Gyr, their bulges naturally harbor older stellar populations of $t_{\text{MW}} \sim 7$ Gyr [[bibcode-2024a-a-686a-124l|bibcode:2024A&A...686A.124L]]. Strikingly, recent investigations have also uncovered "quenched rings" of ancient stellar populations in the extreme outskirts of nearby grand-design spirals, such as NGC 4321 and NGC 5194 (classic [[messier-objects|Messier Objects]]) [[bibcode-2024a-a-686a-124l|bibcode:2024A&A...686A.124L]]. These outskirt regions drop significantly below the spatially resolved main sequence. The physical origin of these quenched rings remains an open question, with proposed mechanisms including environmental starvation at the disk edge or a cessation of pristine gas accretion from the circumgalactic medium due to high specific angular momentum [[bibcode-2024a-a-686a-124l|bibcode:2024A&A...686A.124L]].

## Cross-Comparison with Cosmological Simulations

Because simulations predict physical properties rather than observable fluxes, testing modern hydrodynamical models requires forward-modeling simulated galaxies into the observational plane. By applying 3D radiative transfer codes to simulate dust attenuation and re-emission, researchers generate synthetic observations that can be directly compared against the extensive multi-wavelength catalogs provided by DustPedia. This cross-comparison serves as a stringent benchmark for the subgrid physics governing star formation and interstellar medium (ISM) feedback in cosmological simulations. Complementary to these direct forward-modeling efforts, stacking analyses from surveys like [[hermes|HerMES]] have been used to reconstruct source counts below the confusion limit and estimate the total contribution of galaxies to the Cosmic Infrared Background [[bibcode-2012a-a-542a-58b|bibcode:2012A&A...542A..58B]].

**EAGLE Simulations**
An analysis of $\sim 7000$ simulated galaxies at $z=0$ from the EAGLE suite utilized DustPedia as an observational reference for testing UV-to-submillimeter spectral energy distributions (SEDs) [[bibcode-2020mnras-494-2823t|bibcode:2020MNRAS.494.2823T]]. While the EAGLE models show a high level of overall agreement in the optical and far-infrared regimes—successfully reproducing empirical scaling relations for diffuse dust and stellar mass—significant tensions remain in specific bands. Notably, the simulated SEDs systematically overestimate mid-infrared fluxes and underestimate far-UV attenuation relative to the DustPedia sample [[bibcode-2020mnras-494-2823t|bibcode:2020MNRAS.494.2823T]]. These discrepancies are generally attributed to limitations in how the simulations prescribe subgrid treatments for dense, star-forming regions.

**TNG50**
DustPedia data has also been used to calibrate and test $\sim 14,000$ low-redshift ($z \leq 0.1$) galaxies from the high-resolution TNG50 simulation. Using synthetic observations, researchers have consistently derived simulated luminosity functions across 14 broad-band filters from the UV to the submillimeter [[bibcode-2022mnras-516-3728t|bibcode:2022MNRAS.516.3728T]]. The fiducial radiative transfer models yield excellent agreement with DustPedia at the "knee" of the luminosity functions, with typical deviations of just $\pm 0.04$ dex. However, the literature notes clear discrepancies at the extremes: when integrated within an aperture of $5 R_{1/2}$, the TNG50 models overpredict galaxy counts. These overestimates range from 0.004 to 0.27 dex at the faint end, and from 0.12 dex up to 0.8 dex at the bright end [[bibcode-2022mnras-516-3728t|bibcode:2022MNRAS.516.3728T]]. Interestingly, lower-resolution iterations of the simulation (such as TNG50-2, which utilizes an eight times lower mass resolution) frequently achieve better quantitative agreement with DustPedia observational constraints, highlighting lingering sensitivities to numerical resolution and chosen apertures.

**ARTEMIS**
High-resolution models of Milky Way-mass systems, such as the 45 galaxies from the ARTEMIS project, have similarly been benchmarked against DustPedia objects closely matched in stellar mass and star formation rate [[bibcode-2022mnras-512-2728c|bibcode:2022MNRAS.512.2728C]]. The post-processed ARTEMIS galaxies successfully recover the observed global scaling relations for broadband fluxes and integrated physical properties. Nevertheless, tensions arise in the thermal and geometric properties of the dust. Specifically, the simulations predict representative dust temperatures that are systematically lower than those observed in DustPedia, and they consistently underestimate the magnitude of FUV/UV extinction [[bibcode-2022mnras-512-2728c|bibcode:2022MNRAS.512.2728C]]. Furthermore, while ARTEMIS captures broad wavelength-dependent morphological trends, spatially resolved analyses reveal that the simulated galaxies appear significantly more clumpy and less symmetric than their real-world counterparts at comparable physical scales [[bibcode-2022mnras-512-2728c|bibcode:2022MNRAS.512.2728C]].

## Recent Advances: Machine Learning and Virtual FIR Telescopes

Following the decommissioning of facilities like the [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]], [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]] faces a critical observational gap. Deep [[far-infrared-astronomy|Far-infrared astronomy]] observations are essential for characterizing dust mass, dust temperature, and total infrared luminosity. However, because no dedicated deep far-infrared (FIR) space missions are currently active, researchers have sought new methodologies to bridge the gap between existing multi-wavelength archives and future FIR missions. 

Historically, predicting FIR emission for galaxies with only ultraviolet (UV) to mid-infrared (MIR) coverage relied on traditional energy-balance modeling. While useful, these physical models are fundamentally limited by systematic uncertainties in dust opacity, emissivity, and the assumed geometry of the interstellar medium. Recent advances have demonstrated that machine learning algorithms can circumvent some of these physical modeling limitations by directly learning the non-linear mapping between UV-MIR photometry and FIR emission [[bibcode-2020a-a-634a-57d|bibcode:2020A&A...634A..57D]].

A prominent example of this approach is the FIREnet framework, which utilizes shallow neural networks trained on overlapping low-redshift samples from the DustPedia archive and the [[hatlas|HATLAS]] survey. By using 14 broadband UV-MIR fluxes as inputs, the network predicts fluxes across six Herschel bands, as well as bulk dust properties like dust mass, dust luminosity, and effective temperature. Quantitatively, this machine learning approach achieves a root mean square error (RMSE) of $0.19$ dex in log-space, significantly outperforming traditional UV-MIR energy-balance SED fitting, which typically yields an RMSE of $0.38$ dex [[bibcode-2020a-a-634a-57d|bibcode:2020A&A...634A..57D]]. Another related approach to this FIR gap relies on empirical libraries; for example, researchers frequently utilize the Schreiber et al. (2018) dust SED library for predicting total infrared luminosities and dust masses from single-band measurements with the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]] and ALMA [[bibcode-2018a-a-609a-30s|bibcode:2018A&A...609A..30S]].

By applying these trained networks to vast optical and MIR catalogs, astronomers can effectively create a "virtual FIR telescope." This technique vastly expands the sample size of galaxies with estimated FIR properties, allowing archival data mining to proceed even in the absence of direct Herschel or ALMA observations. Similar to FIR virtual telescopes, automated mining pipelines like A3COSMOS efficiently exploit archival ALMA data to provide robust physical properties for hundreds of distant galaxies [[bibcode-2019apjs-244-40l|bibcode:2019ApJS..244...40L]].

However, the literature notes important tensions and caveats regarding these empirical methods. While machine learning outperforms traditional energy-balance models within its domain of applicability, it is inherently sensitive to training set biases. The framework's predictions can degrade significantly if the input data have large uncertainties (such as in the WISE $22$ $\mu$m band) or if a target galaxy possesses a spectral energy distribution that falls outside the parameter space of the local galaxies used to train the network [[bibcode-2020a-a-634a-57d|bibcode:2020A&A...634A..57D]]. Consequently, while virtual FIR telescopes are powerful tools for extrapolating FIR properties in well-sampled regimes, they cannot entirely replace the need for future FIR observatories to probe rare or highly evolved systems.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While the DustPedia project has established a definitive local benchmark for the interstellar medium, current research is actively shifting toward connecting these $z \sim 0$ scaling relations with the distant universe. A major observational hurdle is the current "FIR gap"—the lack of active deep far-infrared space missions required to directly measure cold dust emission. To circumvent this, researchers are developing machine learning frameworks trained on DustPedia and [[hatlas|HATLAS]] data to predict FIR fluxes from ultraviolet-to-mid-infrared photometry. These models provide an essential empirical calibration for upcoming large-scale optical and near-infrared observations [[bibcode-2020a-a-634a-57d|bibcode:2020A&A...634A..57D]]. For instance, early results from the Euclid survey have stacked archival [[herschel-space-observatory|Herschel Space Observatory]] and SCUBA-2 data for millions of main sequence galaxies, revealing that average dust temperatures converge to $T_2 = 23.2 \pm 0.1$ K at $z < 1$. This plateau suggests an increasing role of older stellar populations in dust heating over cosmic time, mirroring the complex heating mechanisms mapped in local DustPedia galaxies [[bibcode-2025arxiv251102989e|bibcode:2025arXiv251102989E]]. Comparison of dust attenuation diagnostics across multiple epochs, such as those from the WySH survey, suggests a mild decrease in global dust attenuation with increasing redshift [[bibcode-2010aj-140-253m|bibcode:2010AJ....140..253M]].

At higher redshifts, DustPedia's local constraints inform the interpretation of extreme systems targeted by [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]] using ALMA and the [[james-webb-space-telescope-jwst|James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)]]. ALMA observations of [[high-redshift-dusty-star-forming-galaxies|High-redshift dusty star-forming galaxies]] at $z \simeq 1-3$ reveal a dust emissivity index of $\beta \simeq 1.9 \pm 0.4$. This value is remarkably consistent with local samples, indicating little evolution in fundamental dust-grain properties and suggesting that high-redshift dust reservoirs are similarly driven by grain growth in the interstellar medium [[bibcode-2021apj-919-30d|bibcode:2021ApJ...919...30D]]. The synergy between ALMA and the JWST is also uncovering populations of [[heavily-dust-obscured-starbursts|Heavily dust-obscured starbursts]] (with average visual extinctions $A_V \simeq 2.5$), including sources at $z > 4$ that were entirely missed by previous optical and near-infrared surveys [[bibcode-2025apj-979-229m|bibcode:2025ApJ...979..229M]], [[bibcode-2025arxiv250202637g|bibcode:2025arXiv250202637G]]. JWST initiatives like [[jades|JADES]] are now providing the largest statistical samples of spectra to characterize galaxy populations in the first billion years after the Big Bang [[bibcode-2024arxiv240406531d|bibcode:2024arXiv240406531D]]. For these extreme cases, combining [[radio-surveys|Radio surveys]] and FIR/sub-millimeter data is an essential strategy for characterizing the age and dust temperature of galaxies too enshrouded to be detected in optical or NIR bands [[bibcode-2024galax-12-14b|bibcode:2024Galax..12...14B]]. Mapping the resolved dust and gas properties of these distant systems remains a critical open question for models of high-redshift star formation [[bibcode-2020rsos-700556h|bibcode:2020RSOS....700556H]]. This provides a stark contrast to local properties: while DustPedia models typically reveal extended, clumpy local dust distributions, high-redshift observations from [[goods-survey|GOODS survey]]-ALMA 2.0 suggest that dust continuum emission at 1.1 mm is generally much more compact than the stellar distribution [[bibcode-2022a-a-658a-43g|bibcode:2022A&A...658A..43G]].

Within the local universe itself, several physical anomalies identified in DustPedia and related samples remain unresolved. First, standard dust models consistently fail to predict the "sub-millimeter excess" observed at $500\ \mu\text{m}$ in low-metallicity dwarf irregular galaxies [[bibcode-2012apj-745-95d|bibcode:2012ApJ...745...95D]]. Second, the physical origin and support mechanisms of extraplanar dust recently confirmed in the halos of large edge-on galaxies require further theoretical investigation [[bibcode-2022mnras-515-5698m|bibcode:2022MNRAS.515.5698M]]. Third, spatially resolved spectral energy distribution fitting has revealed unexpected "quenched rings" of ancient stellar populations (mass-weighted ages $\sim 7$ Gyr) in the extreme outskirts of grand-design spirals like NGC 4321 (part of the [[messier-objects|Messier Objects]]), raising questions about whether this quenching is driven by environmental starvation or circumgalactic gas accretion limits [[bibcode-2024a-a-686a-124l|bibcode:2024A&A...686A.124L]]. Finally, the exact impact of active galactic nuclei on diffuse dust heating remains ambiguous; while young stars dominate global heating, 3D radiative transfer models of Seyfert galaxies show that an AGN can heat diffuse dust well beyond the torus, contributing $>90\%$ of the heating in the central few hundred parsecs and potentially biasing global star formation rate derivations if not properly modeled [[bibcode-2020a-a-638a-150v|bibcode:2020A&A...638A.150V]]. Future investigations into dust heating by central engines will be facilitated by the eJWST AGN catalogue, providing a systematic resource of JWST's high-resolution AGN observations [[bibcode-2026arxiv260114905l|bibcode:2026arXiv260114905L]].
