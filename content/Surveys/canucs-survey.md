The **Canadian NIRISS Unbiased Cluster Survey (CANUCS)** is a James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) Cycle 1 Guaranteed Time Observation program designed to probe the early universe. By targeting five massive foreground galaxy clusters—such as Abell 370 and MACS J0416—the survey utilizes strong gravitational lensing to magnify intrinsically faint [[background-galaxies|Background galaxies]] that would otherwise remain undetectable. Building upon the legacy of previous [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope Deep Surveys]] like the [[clash-survey|CLASH survey]] and [[relics-survey|RELICS survey]], CANUCS combines high-resolution NIRCam imaging, with a specific emphasis on medium-band filters, and wide-field NIRISS slitless spectroscopy. This instrumental synergy allows researchers to construct highly precise lens models constrained by hundreds of secure multiple images, effectively turning the galaxy clusters into "cosmic telescopes" capable of resolving the structural and chemical properties of galaxies stretching deep into the Epoch of Reionization [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract).

A primary objective of CANUCS is to characterize [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]] and identify the sources responsible for cosmic reionization. The survey has provided critical constraints on the [[nuv-luminosity-function|NUV luminosity function]] at $z > 7$, revealing a steep decline in galaxy space density from $z \sim 8$ to $z \sim 12$ and a distinct absence of extremely bright sources at $z > 12$ [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract). Crucially, the survey's dense panchromatic sampling has helped resolve early observational tensions with the standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmological model; CANUCS data demonstrated that purportedly massive "double-break" galaxies in the early universe are actually lower-mass systems whose red colors are driven by extreme emission lines rather than evolved stellar populations [Desprez et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.2935D/abstract). By leveraging extreme magnification, CANUCS also enables [[spatially-resolved-galaxy-analysis|Spatially Resolved Galaxy Analysis]] of ultra-low-mass dwarf galaxies ($M_* < 10^9 M_\odot$). Observations confirm that these nascent systems exhibit highly bursty star formation, extremely low gas-phase metallicities, and high ionizing photon production efficiencies, reinforcing their hypothesized role as the primary drivers of reionization.

Beyond global population statistics, this article details the internal dynamics of early mass assembly, tracking how [[very-young-galaxies|Very Young Galaxies]] grow via inside-out disk formation, major mergers, and the accretion of massive star-forming clumps. The survey also investigates the enigmatic population of [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]] (LRDs)—compact, high-redshift sources whose spectral energy distributions suggest a complex interplay between dust-obscured post-starburst stellar populations and active galactic nuclei (AGNs). While CANUCS has captured rare evidence of environmental quenching in interacting LRD pairs and significantly refined our understanding of [[first-light-galaxies|First-light galaxies]], several open questions remain. Ongoing research within the collaboration seeks to unambiguously determine the power sources behind LRDs, precisely calibrate [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|Spectrophotometric redshifts]] against the damping wings of the neutral intergalactic medium, and map exactly how early structure formation transitions across cosmic dawn.

## Survey Overview and Experimental Design

The Canadian NIRISS Unbiased Cluster Survey (CANUCS) is a JWST Cycle 1 GTO (Guaranteed Time Observation) program engineered to probe the early universe through the observational synergies of high-resolution imaging, slitless spectroscopy, and strong gravitational lensing. The survey targets five massive galaxy clusters, including [[massive-cluster-survey|MACS J0416.1-2403]], MACS J0417.5-1154, and [[abell-catalog-of-clusters|Abell 370]] [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract), [Strait et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...949L..23S/abstract), [Gledhill et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973...77G/abstract). By using these massive foreground clusters as cosmic telescopes, CANUCS achieves high magnification factors that enable the detection of intrinsically faint background galaxies—reaching absolute UV magnitudes as faint as $M_{\rm UV} \gtrsim -12$ and stellar masses below $10^4 M_\odot$ [Vanzella et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.251V/abstract).

A defining feature of the CANUCS experimental design is its extensive use of NIRCam medium-band filters. The imaging strategy covers each cluster field with 12 to 14 NIRCam filters, including 7 to 9 medium-band filters [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract). This dense panchromatic sampling is critical for deriving high-precision photometric redshifts and distinguishing genuine high-redshift [[lyman-break-selection-and-galaxies|Lyman-break candidates]] from low-redshift [[interloper-galaxies|Interloper galaxies]]. Notably, this NIRCam medium-band strategy proved essential in interpreting anomalous [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]]; by sampling the spectral energy distributions finely, CANUCS data demonstrated that the red colors of many high-redshift candidates are driven by high-equivalent-width emission lines mimicking Balmer breaks, rather than massive, evolved stellar populations [Desprez et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.2935D/abstract).

Complementing the imaging, CANUCS utilizes the NIRISS grism for wide-field slitless spectroscopy. Utilizing the F115W, F150W, and F200W grisms, the survey captures rest-frame optical emission lines—such as [O II] $\lambda 3727$, [O III] $\lambda\lambda 4959, 5007$, and H$\alpha$—for galaxies at $1 < z < 3$ without the need for target pre-selection [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract). This unbiased spectroscopic approach not only yields a high density of robust [[galaxy-redshift-survey|redshifts]] but also enables spatially resolved studies of star formation. For example, the NIRISS grism data allows for the extraction of spatially resolved H$\alpha$ maps of lensed galaxies, providing a detailed view of sub-galactic star-forming clumps and quenching mechanisms [Estrada-Carpenter et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532..577E/abstract).

The dense spectroscopic coverage feeds directly back into the survey's foundational lensing models. The unbiased nature of the NIRISS and complementary NIRSpec observations has yielded unprecedented numbers of multiple-image constraints. In the MACS J0416.1-2403 field alone, CANUCS observations have identified over 300 secure multiple images, anchoring the cluster mass model to a root-mean-square positional precision of $\sim 0.52^{\prime\prime}$ [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract). Similar improvements have been published for the mass models of Abell 370 [Gledhill et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973...77G/abstract).

Ultimately, the CANUCS survey design demonstrates that targeting lensing clusters can be more efficient than blank-field [[deep-imaging-surveys|Deep imaging surveys]] (such as [[jades|JADES]]) for discovering the highest-redshift galaxies ($z > 15$) by maximizing the survey area subject to extreme magnification [Vujeva et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...974...23V/abstract). However, CANUCS researchers have explicitly noted that this targeted approach remains highly sensitive to cosmic variance. The survey found a steep decline in the space density of galaxies from $z \sim 8$ to $z \sim 12$ and a distinct lack of extremely bright galaxies relative to other fields, highlighting the influence of cosmic variance on the apparent ultra-high-redshift UV luminosity function [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract).

## Evolution of Cluster Lensing Surveys

The historical development of cluster lensing surveys is anchored in the utility of massive galaxy clusters to act as "cosmic telescopes." By exploiting gravitational lensing magnification, these surveys can magnify background sources that would otherwise fall below the detection limits of contemporary astronomical facilities, enabling the study of intrinsically faint, high-redshift galaxy populations with unprecedented spatial resolution [Rex et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010A&A...518L..13R/abstract).

The foundation for the CANUCS survey was established by a lineage of [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope Deep Surveys]], which demonstrated the power of strong gravitational lensing to efficiently deliver large samples of high-redshift galaxies. Notable among these early milestones were the [[clash-survey|CLASH survey]] (Cluster Lensing And Supernova survey with Hubble) and the Hubble Frontier Fields. Building directly on these successes, the [[relics-survey|RELICS survey]] (Reionization Lensing Cluster Survey) targeted 41 massive clusters at $0.182 \leq z \leq 0.972$ utilizing a 188-orbit HST program combined with Spitzer IRAC imaging. RELICS significantly advanced the field by discovering over 300 galaxy candidates at $6 \leq z \leq 10$ and identifying spatially resolved lensed arcs out to $z \sim 10$ [Coe et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...884...85C/abstract). 

CANUCS represents the latest advancement in this lineage, transitioning from the optical and near-infrared imaging constraints of HST and Spitzer to the superior sensitivity and spectroscopic capabilities of JWST. This transition has dramatically refined strong lensing mass models by increasing the number of secure multiple images with [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|spectroscopic redshifts]]. For example, CANUCS observations of the galaxy cluster [[massive-cluster-survey|MACS J0416.1-2403]] yielded a record number of secure multiple images across over a hundred background sources—a greater than 20% increase over previous models, reducing the positional uncertainty to $\sim 0.52''$ [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract). Such highly precise mass models are essential for accurately deriving the intrinsic source properties, such as the stellar masses, star formation rates, and [[nuv-luminosity-function|UV absolute magnitudes]] ($M_{\text{UV}}$) of the magnified galaxies [Gledhill et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973...77G/abstract).

From a survey design perspective, utilizing lensing magnification remains highly competitive with ultra-deep blank field surveys. Analyses of survey efficiency indicate that massive foreground galaxy clusters—specifically those selected to maximize the area of high magnification within a single NIRCam module—provide the highest probability of probing the $15 \leq z \leq 20$ regime and discovering the highest-redshift [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|galaxies]] possible with JWST [Vujeva et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...974...23V/abstract). 

Within CANUCS, extreme lensing magnification factors routinely exceed $\mu \sim 40$ [Strait et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...949L..23S/abstract) and can reach $\mu \gtrsim 100$ for sources straddling cluster critical lines [Vanzella et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A.173V/abstract). This massive boost in flux allows CANUCS to detect ultra-low-mass systems ($M_\star \sim 10^4 - 10^7 M_\odot$) and characterize individual, parsec-scale star clusters with masses as low as $10^5 M_\odot$ at $z > 8$ [Mowla et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.636..332M/abstract). By leveraging these refined cluster models, CANUCS bridges the observational gap between local dwarf galaxies and the earliest building blocks of the universe.

## High-Precision Gravitational Lens Modeling

A fundamental component of the CANUCS survey is the high-precision modeling of massive foreground galaxy clusters, such as [[abell-catalog-of-clusters|Abell 370]], [[massive-cluster-survey|MACS J0416.1-2403]], and MACS J0417. By exploiting strong gravitational lensing, the survey leverages the immense mass of these clusters to magnify exceptionally faint [[background-galaxies|Background galaxies]], enabling deep [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]] at cosmic dawn. Theoretical forecasts suggest that survey fields targeting massive clusters with high median magnifications within a single JWST NIRCam module are statistically more efficient for discovering [[nascent-galaxies|Nascent galaxies]] at $15 \le z \le 20$ than equivalently deep blank-field observations [Vujeva et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...974...23V/abstract).

To accurately infer the intrinsic physical properties of these distant sources, CANUCS employs the parametric lens modeling software Lenstool to reconstruct the cluster mass distribution. These models heavily depend on identifying sets of multiple images originating from the same background source. Prior to JWST, lens models relied on constraints from the [[hubble-space-telescope-deep-surveys|Hubble Space Telescope Deep Surveys]] and ground-based integral field spectrographs like [[multi-unit-spectroscopic-explorer-wide-survey|MUSE]]. CANUCS significantly improves upon these baseline models by incorporating newly discovered multiple images and securing their spectroscopic redshifts using JWST's NIRISS and NIRSpec instruments. 

In the field of MACS J0416.1-2403, CANUCS observations have yielded the largest sample of spectroscopically confirmed multiple images in any galaxy cluster to date. The updated lens model is constrained by 303 secure multiple images from 111 distinct background sources—an increase of more than 20% over previous pre-JWST models [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract). This dense network of spectroscopic constraints allows the model to reproduce the observed image positions with a root-mean-square spatial offset of just $0.52''$ [Rihtaršič et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...696A..15R/abstract). 

Similar modeling enhancements have been applied to Abell 370. Using the CANUCS dataset, researchers mapped the cluster's critical curve for sources at $z = 9.0$, finding its overall geometry consistent with previous models but with refined localized constraints [Gledhill et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973...77G/abstract). Accurate delensing requires rigorous quantification of the magnification uncertainty, particularly near the critical curve where magnification factors rapidly diverge. For example, in Abell 370, CANUCS successfully identified and spectroscopically confirmed a $z \sim 8$ galaxy appearing as two lensed images. Applying the updated mass model, the survey extracted magnification factors of $7.4^{+0.5}_{-0.3}$ and $9.4^{+0.5}_{-0.4}$ for the two images [Gledhill et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973...77G/abstract). Accounting for these magnification uncertainties is essential for deriving reliable intrinsic properties; delensing this specific source revealed it to be a low-mass system with $\log(M_*/M_\odot) = 7.49^{+0.04}_{-0.05}$ forming stars at a rate of $2.8^{+0.4}_{-0.3} \, M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ [Gledhill et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...973...77G/abstract). Ultimately, these high-precision mass models ensure that the intrinsic properties of the ultra-faint dwarf galaxies driving cosmic reionization can be robustly decoupled from the lensing effects of the foreground cluster.

## Galaxy Populations in the Epoch of Reionization

The CANUCS survey provides critical constraints on [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]] during the Epoch of Reionization. Utilizing deep JWST/NIRCam imaging across five widely separated sightlines, CANUCS characterizes the [[nuv-luminosity-function|NUV luminosity function]] out to $z \sim 16$. Observations reveal a steep decline in the space density of galaxies from $z \sim 8$ to $z \sim 12$, which aligns with standard models of constant star-formation efficiency without requiring special adjustments [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract). Moving even further back in time, the UV luminosity function normalization and luminosity density decline by a factor of $\sim 7$ between $z = 11$ and $z = 15$, which represents a noticeably *less steep* evolution than is observed at $z < 11$ [Asada et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250703124A/abstract). Within the unmasked, delensed survey area of 53.4 arcmin$^2$, CANUCS identifies no galaxies at $z > 12.5$ and no sources brighter than $M_{\rm UV} = -20$ at $z > 10$ [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract). This observed dearth of exceptionally bright, ultra-high-redshift galaxies contrasts with early results from other JWST programs (such as CEERS and GLASS). The literature indicates that this tension is largely driven by strong field-to-field cosmic variance, emphasizing the necessity of multi-field surveys to accurately constrain galaxy abundances at $z > 10$ [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract).

The robust identification of these early sources relies heavily on [[lyman-break-selection-and-galaxies|Lyman-Break Selection and Galaxies]]. The inclusion of 7-9 medium-band NIRCam filters in the CANUCS observing strategy is instrumental in minimizing contamination from low-redshift interlopers and accurately constraining [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|photometric redshifts]] [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract). Furthermore, CANUCS data have highlighted a critical systematic effect in high-redshift templates: standard intergalactic medium attenuation models assume a sharp Lyman break, which systematically overestimates photometric redshifts at $z > 7$. Correcting this bias requires an updated analytical transmission curve that incorporates the Ly-$\alpha$ damping wing from proximate dense neutral hydrogen gas clouds [Asada et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983L...2A/abstract).

CANUCS has also played a pivotal role in resolving early observational tensions with the $\Lambda$CDM cosmological paradigm. Initial JWST studies reported a surprising abundance of very massive galaxies at $z > 7$, identified via "double-break" spectral energy distributions nominally corresponding to both Lyman and Balmer breaks. However, spectroscopic follow-up of such candidates within the CANUCS fields demonstrates that these red colors are predominantly driven by star-forming galaxies with extremely high-equivalent-width emission lines, rather than the Balmer breaks of massive, evolved stellar populations [Desprez et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.2935D/abstract), [Bradač et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...961L..21B/abstract). The masses of these candidates are therefore much lower than initially inferred, confirming that they do not violate standard structure formation models [Desprez et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.2935D/abstract).

The survey also maps the Star Formation Main Sequence (SFMS) for these early populations down to stellar masses of $10^{7.6} M_\odot$. Results spanning $1 < z < 9$ indicate that the SFMS maintains a slope of $\sim 0.7-0.8$ with little evolution up to $z \sim 5$. Notably, observations reveal a transition at $M_* \approx 10^{9.5} M_\odot$, which may be linked to the physical assembly of self-regulating disks [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250922871M/abstract). 

Finally, the survey characterizes the broader physical properties of the low-mass dwarf galaxies ($M_* < 10^9 M_\odot$) hypothesized to be the primary drivers of cosmic reionization. These galaxies exhibit bursty star formation histories and high ionizing photon production efficiencies, with a median sample value of:
$$ \log \xi_{\rm ion} = 25.39 \pm 0.6 $$
[Harshan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.1112H/abstract). A notable discovery is the highly magnified, extremely metal-poor Lyman-$\alpha$ emitter CANUCS-A370-z8-LAE at $z = 8.203$. Its compact starbursting nature provides direct observational evidence of the low-mass systems actively reionizing their local environments [Willott et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988...26W/abstract).

## Dwarf Galaxies and the Ionizing Photon Budget

A central objective of the CANUCS survey is to characterize the physical properties of ultra-low-mass dwarf galaxies ($M_* < 10^9 M_\odot$), which are widely considered to be the primary sources of ionizing photons during the epoch of reionization. Leveraging strong gravitational lensing, the survey is capable of resolving galaxies down to intrinsic [[nuv-luminosity-function|UV magnitudes]] of $M_{\rm UV} \approx -16$ and stellar masses as low as $M_* \sim 10^6 M_\odot$, providing a critical window into [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]].

Quantitative measurements of the ionizing photon production efficiency, $\xi_{\rm ion}$, indicate that dwarf galaxies at high redshift are extraordinarily efficient photon producers. For a sample of star-forming galaxies at $4.8 < z < 7$ with a median stellar mass of $6.3 \times 10^7 M_\odot$, CANUCS observations yield a median efficiency of $\log \xi_{\rm ion} = 25.39 \pm 0.6 \ {\rm Hz\ erg}^{-1}$ alongside very blue UV continuum slopes ($\beta_{1500} = -2.56 \pm 0.23$) [Harshan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.1112H/abstract). Both $\beta_{1500}$ and $\xi_{\rm ion}$ show a marginal anti-correlation with stellar mass, reinforcing the outsized role of the faintest dwarf galaxies in maintaining the cosmic ionizing photon budget.

The elevated $\xi_{\rm ion}$ values and the large scatter in observed emission line equivalent widths are driven primarily by bursty star formation histories. Based on H$\alpha$-to-UV flux ratios, approximately 60% of low-mass galaxies at $4.7 < z < 6.5$ are experiencing bursty star formation histories [Asada et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.52711372A/abstract). These starbursts are frequently triggered by galaxy-galaxy interactions, which comprise roughly 40% of the high-$z$ dwarf sample. CANUCS has identified extreme examples of this early mass assembly, such as a major merger of two ultra-low-mass ($M_* \sim 10^7 M_\odot$) galaxies at $z \sim 5$ connected by a metal-poor star-forming bridge [Asada et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.523L..40A/abstract). Furthermore, these interactions appear to facilitate rapid quenching on timescales of $\lesssim 100$ Myr. An example is the extremely compact transition galaxy MACS0417-z5BBG at $z=5.2$, which has a half-light radius of $r_{\rm hl} \sim 30$ pc, a stellar mass of $4.3 \times 10^7 M_\odot$, and a precipitously declining star formation rate of just $\sim 0.14 M_\odot \ {\rm yr}^{-1}$ [Strait et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...949L..23S/abstract).

The viability of dwarf galaxies as reionization drivers also depends critically on the escape fraction ($f_{\rm esc}$) of their ionizing radiation. CANUCS data provide detailed views of optimal leakers, notably the afore-mentioned CANUCS-A370-z8-LAE system at $z=8.203$ [Willott et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988...26W/abstract). Driven by a high star formation rate density of $50\text{--}100 M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}\,{\rm kpc}^{-2}$ and characterized by a high H$\beta$ equivalent width of $225 \pm 50$ Å, the galaxy exhibits an inferred Ly$\alpha$ escape fraction of $f_{\rm esc} = 0.21 \pm 0.05$ [Willott et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988...26W/abstract). 

Internally, the assembly of these low-mass systems is highly clumpy. Analysis of the [[stellar-mass-function|clump stellar mass function]] (cSMF) reveals a steep power-law slope ($\alpha \sim -2.3$) in galaxies with $M_* < 10^{10} M_\odot$ [Sok et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250925363S/abstract). The flattened cSMF slopes observed in younger, gas-rich clumps suggest that elevated gas fractions in the early Universe allow for the *in situ* formation of massive clumps, though disruptive feedback mechanisms—amplified by the extreme star formation environments—preferentially destroy lower-mass clumps as the galaxy evolves.

## Spatially Resolved Star Formation and Clump Dynamics

By combining deep JWST/NIRCam imaging with magnification from cluster lenses, the CANUCS survey probes the internal, spatially resolved assembly histories of distant galaxies. A cornerstone result of the survey is the observational confirmation of inside-out mass assembly in Milky Way progenitors at $0.3 < z < 5$ [Tan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241207829T/abstract). Resolved mass and star formation rate maps reveal that the inner 2 kpc regions of these galaxies maintain a roughly constant stellar mass of $10^{9.3} - 10^{9.4} M_\odot$ from $z = 5$ down to $z = 2$. Concurrently, the outer regions experience nearly an order of magnitude of growth, increasing from $10^{7.5} M_\odot$ to $10^{8.3} M_\odot$ [Tan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241207829T/abstract). At $z > 3$, this mass accretion is highly chaotic; approximately 50% of Milky Way progenitors at $4 < z < 5$ exhibit disturbed morphologies, and major mergers are prevalent drivers of bursty star formation [Tan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241207829T/abstract), [Asada et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.52711372A/abstract). For instance, CANUCS data captured an ongoing major merger between two ultra-low-mass ($M_* \sim 10^7 M_\odot$) galaxies at $z \sim 5$, showcasing synchronized, interaction-driven starbursts that significantly boost the local ionizing photon budget [Asada et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.523L..40A/abstract).

At cosmic noon, CANUCS leverages JWST/NIRISS grism spectroscopy to construct spatially resolved H-alpha maps, enabling precise [[spatially-resolved-galaxy-analysis|Spatially Resolved Galaxy Analysis]] of localized star formation. Observations of a lensed galaxy pair at $z=0.87$ demonstrate that star-forming clumps exhibit highly variable evolutionary states; within a single blue face-on disk, individual clumps are found simultaneously in bursty, quenching, and equilibrium phases [Estrada-Carpenter et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532..577E/abstract). Across the broader survey at $0.5 < z < 5$, the aggregate clump stellar mass function (cSMF) is modeled as a power law:
$$ \frac{dN}{dM} \propto M^{\alpha} $$
where the overall slope is $\alpha \approx -2$ [Sok et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250925363S/abstract). However, the literature reveals complexity and competing physical drivers in clump demographics: the cSMF slope is shallower ($\alpha \sim -1.6$) in massive galaxies and steeper ($\alpha \sim -2.3$) in low-mass galaxies. This mass-dependent variation reflects an ongoing ambiguity regarding the dominant clump formation mechanisms—whether driven by in-situ disk fragmentation or ex-situ accretion—and suggests that stellar feedback preferentially destroys lower-mass clumps over time [Sok et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250925363S/abstract).

Pushing observation into the first billion years of the Universe, CANUCS provides a unique spectrophotometric view of early-stage galaxy assembly through the "Firefly Sparkle", a highly magnified system at $z = 8.296$ [Mowla et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.636..332M/abstract). Interpreted as a Milky Way analog in its nascent formation phase, the Firefly Sparkle's mass is heavily concentrated within ten compact star clusters, each harboring $10^5 - 10^6 M_\odot$ [Mowla et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.636..332M/abstract), [Rusta et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240706255R/abstract). These unresolved clusters exhibit extreme surface mass densities exceeding $10^3 M_\odot \text{ pc}^{-2}$. Spectroscopic analysis of the central cluster reveals a nebular-dominated spectrum characterized by low metallicity, high electron temperatures, and elevated gas densities, which together hint at a top-heavy initial mass function (IMF) in these extreme, metal-poor environments [Mowla et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.636..332M/abstract).

## Chemical Evolution and Metal-Poor Systems

The CANUCS survey utilizes strong gravitational lensing to probe the chemical evolution of [[background-galaxies|Background galaxies]] during the Epoch of Reionization. By magnifying ultra-low-mass systems ($M_* < 10^8 M_\odot$), the survey uniquely identifies extremely metal-poor galaxies and investigates the relationship between gas-phase metallicity, emission line ratios, and star formation histories in the early universe [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]].

A primary achievement of the survey is the spectroscopic identification of some of the most metal-poor galaxies known at high redshift. Notably, the strongly lensed Lyman-$\alpha$ emitter CANUCS-A370-z8-LAE at $z = 8.203$ exhibits a gas-phase metallicity of $12 + \log(\text{O/H}) = 6.85$, corresponding to roughly $1.4\%$ of the solar value [Willott et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988...26W/abstract). Despite its youth and compact size (half-light radius $r_{\rm hl} = 38^{+3}_{-19}$ pc), its extremely low metallicity is best explained by a chemical evolution model in which infalling pristine gas rapidly dilutes the interstellar medium (ISM) during a starburst phase, rather than representing a purely unevolved system forming its first stars [Willott et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988...26W/abstract).

The survey has also identified compelling Population III candidates within highly magnified ($ \mu \gtrsim 100 $) star-forming complexes. The system Lensed And Pristine 1 (LAP1) at $z = 6.639$ has a remarkably low intrinsic stellar mass of $\lesssim 10^4 M_\odot$ and features extreme equivalent widths ($> 300{-}2000$ Å) for Ly$\alpha$ and Balmer lines, alongside uniquely weak [O III] $\lambda\lambda 4959, 5007$ emission [Vanzella et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A.173V/abstract). Using the $R_{23}$ and $R_3$ metallicity indices, observations constrain its oxygen abundance to $12 + \log(\text{O/H}) \lesssim 6.3$. Mirrored subcomponents of the LAP1 complex completely lack [O III] emission, yielding stringent upper limits of $12 + \log(\text{O/H}) < 6.0$. These properties suggest LAP1 is either an actively star-forming metal-free region hosting Population III stars, or a fluorescing H I gas cloud illuminated by escaping ionizing radiation [Vanzella et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&A...678A.173V/abstract). A similarly extreme, low-metallicity ($Z \sim 0.02 Z_\odot$) star complex, T2c at $z=6.146$, exhibits an exceptional ionizing photon production efficiency of $\log(\xi_{\rm ion}) \gtrsim 25.86$, reinforcing that the formation of very massive O-type stars may be strongly favored in pristine, low-mass environments [Vanzella et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...691A.251V/abstract).

On a broader population level, CANUCS reveals that low-mass dwarf galaxies at $4.8 < z < 7$ are highly efficient producers of ionizing photons [Harshan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.1112H/abstract). For these faint sources, the rest-frame UV magnitude correlates strongly with H$\alpha$ equivalent widths, but not with [O III]+H$\beta$ equivalent widths. This decoupling in emission line ratios indicates that the faintest [[very-young-galaxies|Very Young Galaxies]] combine systematically low gas-phase metallicities with recent, rapid bursts of star formation [Harshan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.1112H/abstract). Conversely, in more massive, interacting systems at similar redshifts—such as the dusty major merger MACS0416-Y1 at $z = 8.312$—spectroscopy reveals a more enriched ISM ($12 + \log(\text{O/H}) = 7.76 \pm 0.03$) and ionization parameters ($\log U = -2.48 \pm 0.03$) that more closely resemble the evolved conditions typical of $z \sim 2$ star-forming galaxies, despite experiencing extreme dust heating from a merger-driven starburst [Harshan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240812310H/abstract).

## Little Red Dots and Environmental Quenching

Recent high-resolution observations by the CANUCS survey have identified a population of compact, high-redshift sources known as [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]]. Observed primarily at $z \sim 5-7$, these objects are characterized by their extreme compactness and characteristic "V"-shaped spectral energy distributions (SEDs). The physical nature of LRDs remains an active subject of debate within the literature; it is currently ambiguous whether their steep blue rest-frame ultraviolet slopes and red rest-frame optical slopes are driven by highly dust-obscured post-starburst stellar populations or by dust-free active galactic nuclei (AGNs) [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...698A.317M/abstract), [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251006408M/abstract).

A key discovery in the CANUCS fields is a highly interacting galaxy group at $z \sim 7$ that contains a pair of LRDs separated by a projected distance of only $3.3$ kpc [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...698A.317M/abstract), [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250117925M/abstract). Both LRDs are relatively massive ($M_\star \sim 10^{10} M_\odot$) and dusty ($A_V > 1$ mag), and they are accompanied by three lower-mass ($M_\star \sim 10^{8-9} M_\odot$) satellite galaxies. While the satellites exhibit SEDs consistent with an ongoing, recent-onset starburst, the SEDs of the central LRD pair exhibit a pronounced Balmer break. This break indicates that their star formation was quenched approximately $\sim 100$ Myr prior to observation [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...698A.317M/abstract). The primary LRD in this pair features a UV excess consistent with a dust-free AGN, while its optical continuum appears to be powered by an obscured post-starburst population. The close physical proximity of the LRDs strongly implies that their rapid quenching was environmentally triggered by their ongoing interaction, providing rare observational evidence for environmental quenching during the Epoch of Reionization [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250117925M/abstract).

Further evidence linking LRD formation and evolution to environmental effects comes from the identification of the "Stingray system", a dense galaxy group spectroscopically confirmed at $z = 5.12$ [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251006408M/abstract). The Stingray system comprises an AGN, a Balmer break galaxy, and a star-forming satellite. The AGN within this system exhibits a mix of observational features: it shares the compact size, blue rest-frame UV slope, and broad H$\alpha$ emission characteristic of canonical LRDs, but it possesses a flatter rest-frame optical slope than is typically observed in these sources. This unique combination of properties suggests that the AGN is a transitional object bridging the gap between normal active galaxies and the LRD phase. Much like the $z \sim 7$ interacting pair, the properties of the Stingray system indicate that the complex SEDs of LRDs—and the rapid stellar and supermassive black hole growth phases they represent—may be catalyzed by local environmental density and major galaxy-galaxy interactions [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251006408M/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Prospects

While the CANUCS survey has significantly advanced our understanding of [[first-light-galaxies|First-light galaxies]], several open questions remain regarding early mass assembly and the sources of cosmic reionization. While CANUCS results have successfully demonstrated that the purported "double-break" galaxies at $z > 7$ are predominantly lower-mass, star-forming systems rather than the massive, evolved galaxies that might challenge the standard $\Lambda$CDM paradigm [Desprez et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.2935D/abstract), reconciling the exact high-redshift galaxy abundance remains an active area of research. Specifically, while CANUCS reveals a steep decline in galaxy space density from $z \sim 8$ to $z \sim 12$, this drop is consistent with standard star-formation efficiency models, with the notable deficit of bright galaxies compared to other survey fields primarily attributed to cosmic variance [Willott et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966...74W/abstract). Furthermore, pushing deeper into the dark ages, the normalization of the luminosity density declines by a factor of $\sim 7$ between $z = 11$ and $z = 15$, indicating that galaxy evolution in this ultra-high-redshift regime is actually *less steep* than at $z < 11$ [Asada et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250703124A/abstract). Exploring the precise physical parameters driving these nuanced evolutionary phases continues to be a central focus of [[cosmic-surveys|Cosmic surveys]].

Accurately charting the $z > 10$ universe requires overcoming significant observational biases. Traditional photometric redshift templates assuming sharp Lyman breaks systematically overestimate redshifts in the Epoch of Reionization due to proximate neutral hydrogen. Recent CANUCS analyses have shown that incorporating an analytical damping wing absorption component into intergalactic medium transmission curves is essential to correct a significant systematic [[spectrophotometric-redshifts|photometric redshift]] bias at $z > 7$ [Asada et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241021543A/abstract), [Asada et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983L...2A/abstract). Furthermore, the inclusion of medium-band imaging is critical. By finely sampling spectral energy distributions (SEDs), medium-band imaging helps minimize contamination from low-$z$ interlopers and maximizes completeness for faint and less blue galaxies [Asada et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250703124A/abstract). 

Another compelling puzzle centers on the nature of [[little-red-dots-jwst|Little Red Dots (JWST)]] (LRDs). Observed at $z \sim 5-7$, these compact objects display complex SEDs that could be interpreted either as dust-obscured post-starburst galaxies or as dust-free active galactic nuclei (AGNs). Discoveries in CANUCS suggest that environmental quenching or merger-triggered bursting plays a significant role in their evolution [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...698A.317M/abstract), [Mérida et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251006408M/abstract). However, the literature remains conflicted on whether the LRD phenomenon is primarily powered by stellar processes or accretion. Robust future spectroscopic follow-up is necessary to identify the specific emission and absorption signatures needed to unambiguously determine their power sources.

Finally, while CANUCS has mapped out the low-mass dwarf galaxies that likely drove cosmic reionization through highly bursty star-formation histories [Asada et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.52711372A/abstract), [Harshan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.1112H/abstract), the absolute contribution of faint AGNs versus star-forming clumps to the total ionizing photon budget is not yet fully constrained. Future prospects for the survey and similar observational campaigns hinge on utilizing highly magnifying foreground clusters to break the $z > 15$ barrier. Theoretical forecasts suggest that targeting massive clusters optimized to fill specific JWST modules provides the highest probability of detecting the universe's earliest galaxies [Vujeva et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023arXiv231015284V/abstract), [Vujeva et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...974...23V/abstract), promising to push the frontiers of [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]] even closer to the Big Bang.
