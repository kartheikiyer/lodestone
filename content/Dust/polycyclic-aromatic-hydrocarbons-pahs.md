**Polycyclic Aromatic Hydrocarbons (PAHs)** are a ubiquitous class of complex organic molecules that represent a major reservoir of carbon in the interstellar medium (ISM). Structurally consisting of fused carbon rings—characteristic of [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]]—decorated with peripheral hydrogen atoms, PAHs bridge the evolutionary gap between small gas-phase molecules and large carbonaceous dust grains.

Historically, PAHs were identified as the primary carriers of the "unidentified infrared bands" (UIBs), a series of strong mid-infrared emission features observed widely across the Milky Way and external galaxies [Cook et al. (1996)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1996Natur.380..227C/abstract), [Chan et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001ApJ...546..273C/abstract). Through advancements in mid-infrared spectroscopy, these features have been definitively mapped to the characteristic vibrational modes of PAH molecules following stochastic heating by ultraviolet (UV) photons [Li et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020NatAs...4..339L/abstract). Prominent emission bands at 3.3, 6.2, 7.7, 8.6, 11.3, and 12.7 $\mu$m correspond to specific structural motions. To interpret these complex spectra and extract physical properties like grain size and ionization state, astrophysicists rely heavily on comprehensive laboratory and theoretical libraries, such as the [[nasa-ames-pah-ir-spectroscopic-database|NASA Ames PAH IR Spectroscopic Database]] [Maragkoudakis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979...90M/abstract). Beyond their purely aromatic core structures, mid-infrared spectroscopy reveals rich chemical diversity in the ISM. The presence of fragile [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]] is traced by sub-features such as the 3.4 $\mu$m aliphatic C-H stretch [Lai et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...905...55L/abstract), and PAHs serve as a significant sink for interstellar deuterium [Yang et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJS..251...12Y/abstract).

Astrophysically, PAHs are critically important for the thermodynamics of the ISM, acting as the dominant agents for gas heating via the photoelectric effect in both the warm and cold neutral phases [Wolfire et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...443..152W/abstract), [Hughes et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...575A..17H/abstract). Observationally, PAH emission provides a highly sensitive, high-resolution proxy for both the star formation rate and the molecular gas surface density. Because of their immense brightness, PAHs can trace these processes across diverse galactic environments, from the disks of local spiral galaxies [Cortzen et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.482.1618C/abstract) and circumgalactic superwinds [Veilleux et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250708098V/abstract) to the high-redshift universe [Lin et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...694A..84L/abstract), [Nanni et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988L...5N/abstract).

Despite their immense utility as robust ISM tracers, the abundance and survivability of PAHs are heavily dependent on their local physical environment. A sharp "PAH deficit" is universally observed in low-metallicity dwarf galaxies, particularly below $12+\log(\text{O/H}) \approx 8$ [Haynes et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...724..215H/abstract), [Hunt et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...712..164H/abstract). Furthermore, small PAH molecules are highly susceptible to destruction by extreme radiation and kinetic feedback; their emission is generally eradicated or heavily altered in the central hundreds of parsecs surrounding Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) [Voit et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992MNRAS.258..841V/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...22Z/abstract). Recent *James Webb Space Telescope* (JWST) detections of PAH emission at the "cosmic dawn" ($z > 6$) have also introduced major tensions into textbook models of dust evolution, necessitating alternative, rapid formation channels to explain their presence before asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars had time to evolve. Resolving these tensions dictates the field's most pressing open questions: whether the low-metallicity PAH deficit is driven by a formation bottleneck or enhanced UV destruction, how these molecules survive expulsion into the circumgalactic medium, and how their lifecycle connects to the formation of larger carbon structures like fullerenes.

## Vibrational Spectroscopy and Physical Properties

Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons emit a prominent suite of mid-infrared features at 3.3, 6.2, 7.7, 8.6, 11.3, and 12.7 $\mu$m. These bands correspond to specific C-C stretching and C-H stretching and bending vibrational modes [Li et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020NatAs...4..339L/abstract). Extracting physical properties from these spectra requires detailed decomposition using template libraries calibrated by laboratory and theoretical data, most notably the [[nasa-ames-pah-ir-spectroscopic-database|NASA Ames PAH IR Spectroscopic Database]] [Maragkoudakis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979...90M/abstract). Moreover, spatial mapping of PAH features in dense photodissociation regions (PDRs) has detected the signatures of PAH dehydrogenation, providing vital observational support for the theoretical framework of H$_2$ formation on the surfaces of PAH molecules [Fleming et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...725..159F/abstract).

### Charge State and Ionization
The relative intensities of PAH emission bands are highly sensitive to the charge state of the molecules. The 6.2 and 7.7 $\mu$m C-C stretching modes are strongly enhanced in ionized PAHs, whereas the 11.3 $\mu$m C-H out-of-plane bending mode is dominated by neutral species. Consequently, band ratios such as $L_{6.2}/L_{11.3}$ and $L_{7.7}/L_{11.3}$ serve as robust diagnostics of the PAH ionization state [Galliano et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009eimw.confE...4G/abstract). In active star-forming regions, the fraction of ionized PAHs generally scales with the star formation surface density [Ujjwal et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...684A..71U/abstract). Additionally, unsupervised machine learning techniques dissecting multiphase gas and dust have demonstrated that the $11.3/7.7\ \mu\text{m}$ PAH ratio can be used to constrain the broader shape of the non-ionizing radiation field [Baron et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...968...24B/abstract). High-resolution JWST measurements of PAH band ratios across the multiphase ISM further confirm this, revealing that H II regions generally host systematically higher ionization fractions and hotter grains compared to the diffuse ISM [Chastenet et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L..12C/abstract).

However, there is ongoing debate in the literature regarding the exact charge state and survivability of PAHs in fully ionized H II regions. While some observations suggest that PAHs are rapidly destroyed by hydrogen-ionizing UV radiation [Egorov et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L..16E/abstract), other studies indicate that neutral PAHs can survive in moderate H II environments, giving rise to an enhanced 11.3 $\mu$m feature [Compiègne et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007A&A...471..205C/abstract). Furthermore, in the central regions of active galactic nuclei (AGN), observational evidence points to altered charge distributions that often favor neutral PAHs over ionized ones, likely due to complex radiative and shock-heating environments [García-Bernete et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...666L...5G/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975L...2Z/abstract).

### Grain Size Distribution
Vibrational spectroscopy also constrains the PAH grain size distribution. The 3.3 $\mu$m C-H stretching mode requires high temperatures for excitation and is thus preferentially emitted by the smallest PAHs, typically containing fewer than 100 carbon atoms [Whitcomb et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250918347W/abstract). Recent mapping with JWST's NIRCam medium bands indicates that between $5\%$ and $65\%$ of the total F335M filter intensity originates directly from the 3.3 $\mu$m feature, which correlates strongly with the 11.3 $\mu$m band to trace the spatial distribution of small grains [Sandstrom et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L...7S/abstract). Comparing the 3.3 $\mu$m band to longer-wavelength features (e.g., 11.3 $\mu$m) provides a proxy for the average PAH size. Near AGNs, small grains are preferentially destroyed by hard X-ray and extreme ultraviolet (EUV) radiation, as well as kinetic shocks, shifting the population toward larger molecules [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...22Z/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975L...2Z/abstract). Conversely, in extremely metal-poor environments where the overall PAH abundance is deficient, the fractional contribution of the 3.3 $\mu$m feature to total PAH luminosity actually increases from $\sim 1\%$ to $\sim 3\%$. This suggests that either the smallest PAHs are uniquely robust against destruction, or that their growth into larger grains is bottlenecked at low metallicities [Whitcomb et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250918347W/abstract), [Tarantino et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251204060T/abstract). To trace the bulk grain size evolution, models and isolated galaxy simulations demonstrate that the ratio of the 8 $\mu$m intensity to the total infrared intensity ($\nu I_\nu(8\,\mu\mathrm{m})/I_{\mathrm{TIR}}$) serves as a remarkably tight indicator of the overall PAH mass fraction ($q_{\mathrm{PAH}}$) [Matsumoto et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A..79M/abstract).

### Aliphatic-to-Aromatic Ratio and Deuteration
Detailed spectroscopy of PAH sub-features reveals the chemical diversity of the grain population. The ratio of the 3.4 $\mu$m emission—originating from [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]] like methyl side groups—to the 3.3 $\mu$m emission from pure [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]] directly measures the aliphatic-to-aromatic ratio. Typical values for $L_{3.4}/L_{3.3}$ range from 0.05 to 0.58, corresponding to an aliphatic carbon fraction of $\sim 0.8\% - 8.3\%$ [Lyu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...986..156L/abstract). This ratio systematically decreases in regions with high star formation rates, indicating that fragile aliphatic side groups are preferentially stripped by intense UV radiation fields, leaving behind the more stable aromatic rings [Lyu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...986..156L/abstract). The 3.4 $\mu$m aliphatic C-H stretch absorption feature in the diffuse ISM also exhibits a spectral structure remarkably similar to the carbonaceous component found in primitive meteorites like Murchison, emphasizing a deep chemical connection between interstellar grains and early solar system materials [Pendleton et al. (1994)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1994ApJ...437..683P/abstract).

Additionally, vibrational spectroscopy identifies PAHs as a significant reservoir for interstellar deuterium. When hydrogen is replaced by deuterium, the reduced mass of the oscillator shifts the 3.3 $\mu$m stretch to $\sim 4.4$ $\mu$m. Using computed intrinsic band strengths of $\langle A_{3.3} \rangle \approx 13.2 \, \mathrm{km \, mol}^{-1}$ and $\langle A_{4.4} \rangle \approx 7.3 \, \mathrm{km \, mol}^{-1}$, observations imply a PAH deuteration fraction of $\sim 2.4\%$, representing a $\sim 1200\times$ enrichment over the primordial D/H abundance [Yang et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJS..251...12Y/abstract). Aliphatic C-D stretching modes near 4.6-4.8 $\mu$m have also been modeled and proposed to account for the 'missing' interstellar deuterium budget not locked in aromatic bonds [Yang et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJS..268...12Y/abstract).

## Lifecycle: Formation, Evolution, and Destruction

The origins and evolutionary lifecycle of interstellar PAHs remain the subject of active debate, centered on whether they are primarily injected by stellar sources or formed *in situ* within the interstellar medium (ISM). Traditionally, the production of PAHs and larger carbonaceous grains was attributed to the cooling outflows of carbon-rich AGB stars [Dwek et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005AIPC..761..103D/abstract), [Paradis et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009AJ....138..196P/abstract). However, the recent detection of PAH emission at $z \approx 6.71$ challenges this paradigm, as AGB stars would not have had sufficient time to evolve in the early universe. This points to alternative rapid enrichment channels, such as Type II supernovae or accelerated ISM grain growth [Lin et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...694A..84L/abstract), [Nanni et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988L...5N/abstract).

In the local universe, chemical evolution models increasingly favor a top-down interstellar origin. In this framework, PAHs are continuously replenished through the shattering of larger carbonaceous grains driven by interstellar turbulence and shocks [Seok et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.439.2186S/abstract), [Rau et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.489.5218R/abstract). Intense interstellar radiation fields then drive the photo-chemical processing of hydrogenated amorphous carbon and [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]], stripping their aliphatic side groups to leave behind stable [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]] [Rau et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.489.5218R/abstract), [Narayanan et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...951..100N/abstract), [Lyu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...986..156L/abstract).

Once formed, PAHs are remarkably resilient in the diffuse ISM, where they are a dominant source of photoelectric heating [Wolfire et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...443..152W/abstract). Spatially resolved mapping indicates that compact PAH emission persists for 10-30 Myr, closely matching molecular gas lifetimes and suggesting that gas clouds quickly provide sufficient shielding for both PAH and CO survival [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250610063K/abstract). They can survive extreme transport mechanisms, evidenced by their detection in galactic superwinds extending up to $\sim 35$ kpc into the circumgalactic medium of galaxies like Makani and M82 [Engelbracht et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ApJ...642L.127E/abstract), [Veilleux et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250708098V/abstract). However, PAH abundances are highly sensitive to the local physical environment and exhibit a sharp deficit in galaxies below a metallicity threshold of $12 + \log(\text{O/H}) \approx 8$ [Haynes et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...724..215H/abstract), though a threshold in specific star formation rate is also a key driver [Cook et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..899C/abstract). The literature remains divided on the exact cause of this deficit; it is actively debated whether it primarily reflects a formation bottleneck or enhanced UV destruction due to inhibited grain growth in metal-poor gas [Tarantino et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251204060T/abstract), [Whitcomb et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250918347W/abstract).

The ultimate removal of PAHs from the ISM is driven by photo-destruction and mechanical processing. In addition to UV and shock processing, cosmic rays serve as a significant destruction agent for PAHs in diffuse clouds, where the nuclear and electronic interactions from ions (H, He, CNO) and electrons process molecules on timescales dependent on PAH size and excitation energy [Micelotta et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011A&A...526A..52M/abstract). In star-forming regions, PAH emission drops sharply within the ionized gas of H II regions. This strong anti-correlation with the local ionization parameter indicates that hydrogen-ionizing UV photons are highly efficient at destroying the molecules [Stephens et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...784..147S/abstract), [Egorov et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L..16E/abstract). Destruction is most extreme in the central hundreds of parsecs of AGN, where hard X-ray/EUV radiation and kinetic shocks systematically eradicate the PAH population [Voit et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992MNRAS.258..841V/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...22Z/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975L...2Z/abstract). These harsh environments preferentially destroy smaller PAH molecules (such as the carriers of the $6.2\,\mu\text{m}$ and $7.7\,\mu\text{m}$ emission features), shifting the surviving population toward larger, neutral species and significantly altering the observed mid-infrared band ratios [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...22Z/abstract), [del Valle-Espinosa et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251011581D/abstract).

## PAHs as Tracers of the Interstellar Medium

As a ubiquitous class of [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]], PAHs play a foundational role in the thermal balance of galaxies and serve as powerful observational proxies for the cold ISM and localized star formation.

### Photoelectric Heating and Thermal Balance
PAHs are primary drivers of photoelectric heating in both the diffuse ISM and dense photodissociation regions (PDRs) [Wolfire et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...443..152W/abstract). Upon absorbing far-ultraviolet (FUV) photons, PAH molecules eject electrons that transfer kinetic energy to the surrounding gas. The efficiency of this process is often traced by the ratio of prominent cooling lines to the total infrared (TIR) continuum. In local spiral galaxies, the photoelectric heating efficiency, measured as $([\mathrm{C~II}]_{158} + [\mathrm{O~I}]_{63})/F_{\mathrm{TIR}}$, is observed to vary between $3.5 \times 10^{-3}$ and $8 \times 10^{-3}$, with PAHs becoming increasingly important for gas heating in the central regions [Hughes et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015A&A...575A..17H/abstract). There is a remarkably tight correlation between the [C II] 158 $\mu$m cooling line and PAH emission; mapping studies of regions like Orion indicate that local deficits in the [C II]/FIR ratio often do not reflect a drop in photoelectric heating efficiency, but can instead be interpreted as an 'FIR excess' arising from embedded sources [Pabst et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...651A.111P/abstract). Furthermore, PAHs significantly influence the cosmic-ray ionization rate ($\zeta$) by controlling the free electron density, which in turn alters the abundance of key interstellar molecules like H$_3^+$ [Shaw et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...908..138S/abstract).

While PAH excitation is strongly associated with FUV fields in star-forming regions, there is ongoing debate regarding their dominant heating source in the diffuse ISM. In some early-type and massive spiral galaxies, the general interstellar radiation field from evolved stellar populations contributes substantially to PAH heating; in M 81, for instance, an estimated 67% of the 8 $\mu$m PAH emission is powered by evolved stars rather than young massive stars [Lu et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...797..129L/abstract).

### Tracers of Molecular Gas and Star Formation
Because PAHs are well mixed with interstellar gas and dust, their mid-infrared emission acts as an excellent, high-resolution tracer of the neutral and molecular gas phases. PAH luminosity, particularly at 6.2, 7.7, and 11.3 $\mu$m, exhibits a tight, linear correlation with carbon monoxide (CO) emission [Cortzen et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.482.1618C/abstract), [Chown et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241005397C/abstract). On galaxy-integrated scales, this CO-PAH correlation yields a universal conversion factor for normal star-forming galaxies, where the molecular gas mass ($M_{\mathrm{H}_2}$) scales with the 6.2 $\mu$m luminosity ($L_{6.2}$) as $M_{\mathrm{H}_2}/L_{6.2} \approx 2.7 \times \alpha_{\mathrm{CO}}$ [Cortzen et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.482.1618C/abstract). High-resolution mapping of the CO(2-1) transition further demonstrates that the power-law relation between CO and PAH emission holds across diverse environments with indices between 0.8 and 1.3 [Chown et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983...64C/abstract), [Chown et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241005397C/abstract). However, the use of CO(2-1) as a molecular gas tracer requires careful calibration of the $r_{21}$ line ratio, which varies with star formation rate and can introduce biases in CO-PAH scaling relations if assumed to be constant [Keenan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..228K/abstract). Utilizing these scaling relations, JWST observations of PAH emission have been used to generate highly sensitive maps of the diffuse ISM at 10-40 pc resolutions. These maps trace cold gas down to surface densities of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{gas}} \sim 2\ M_{\odot}\,\mathrm{pc}^{-2}$, revealing a porous, "Swiss cheese"-like topology in the low-surface-density gas that traditional CO mapping cannot easily resolve [Sandstrom et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L...8S/abstract).

Broadband proxies for PAH emission are also widely utilized for these purposes. For instance, the $12\ \mu\text{m}$ emission captured by the *Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer* (WISE) serves as a robust tracer for the *total* molecular gas budget, rather than strictly the dense gas component, demonstrating tight correlations with various CO isotopes down to sub-kiloparsec scales [Gao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...940..133G/abstract).

Beyond molecular gas mass, specific PAH features are increasingly calibrated to trace ongoing star formation. On macroscopic scales, radial PAH profiles reveal distinct star-formation histories in galaxy bulges, with pseudobulges exhibiting high central star formation rates driven by secular evolution, whereas classical bulges show regular, sharp declines in PAH emission toward the center [Fisher et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ApJ...642L..17F/abstract). On highly localized scales, the $3.3\ \mu\text{m}$ PAH emission serves as a precise marker for early, dust-embedded star clusters. Observations of nearby galaxies indicate that this emission typically fades within approximately $3$ Myr as these clusters emerge from their natal environments and clear surrounding dust [Rodríguez et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983..137R/abstract). Furthermore, sub-kiloparsec calibrations of the $3.3\ \mu\text{m}$ feature as a broader star formation rate (SFR) tracer exhibit a sublinear relationship, which is likely driven by the localized destruction of these smallest PAH molecules in intense ionizing radiation fields near nascent stars [Gregg et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...971..115G/abstract).

### Environmental Dependencies and Breakdown of the Correlation
The utility of PAHs as a gas tracer is fundamentally tied to the local gas-to-dust ratio, which is highly sensitive to metallicity. In galaxies like NGC 2403, the gas-to-dust ratio increases radially in step with the oxygen abundance gradient, directly impacting the calibration of dust-based gas masses [Bendo et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010MNRAS.402.1409B/abstract). 

Furthermore, the CO-PAH correlation can break down on sub-kiloparsec scales in specific physical environments. In the centers and barred regions of some galaxies, observed CO fluxes can exceed PAH-based predictions by more than an order of magnitude [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251114833K/abstract). Rather than reflecting a localized suppression or destruction of PAHs, this discrepancy is often driven by elevated CO emissivity resulting from high velocity dispersions and mechanical shocks along the bar lanes [Kim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251114833K/abstract).

## Environmental Variations: Metallicity and Radiation Fields

The abundance and emission characteristics of PAHs are acutely sensitive to their local physical environment. One of the most prominent environmental trends is the "PAH deficit" observed in low-metallicity environments, particularly in dwarf galaxies. Observations consistently show a sharp decline in PAH emission relative to the total infrared luminosity below a metallicity threshold of $12+\log(\text{O/H}) \approx 8$ [Haynes et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...724..215H/abstract), spanning the broad low-metallicity range of $12+\log(\text{O/H}) \approx 7.4$ to $8.3$ studied in nearby samples [Hunt et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...712..164H/abstract). Additionally, specific star formation rate (e.g., $\log(\text{sSFR}) \sim -10.2$) serves as a critical threshold for PAH suppression [Cook et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..899C/abstract). 

The physical origin of this environmental dependence remains a subject of active debate, with the literature divided between formation bottlenecks and enhanced destruction mechanisms. On the formation side, chemical evolution models suggest that low-metallicity environments lack the requisite dust mass and shattering efficiency to produce PAH molecules from larger carbonaceous grains [Seok et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.439.2186S/abstract). Recent high-resolution observations of extremely metal-poor galaxies (e.g., Sextans A at $\sim 7\% Z_\odot$) reveal compact clumps of neutral PAHs that show no signs of radiation processing, favoring an inhibited grain growth scenario over enhanced destruction [Tarantino et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251204060T/abstract). Paradoxically, while the overall PAH mass drops, the fractional contribution of the $3.3\,\mu\text{m}$ feature—which traces the smallest PAHs ($<100$ carbon atoms)—increases as metallicity declines, potentially rising to $5.6\% - 7.7\%$ of the total PAH luminosity at $10\% Z_\odot$ [Whitcomb et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250918347W/abstract). This suggests that the smallest PAHs may be uniquely robust against inhibited growth effects.

Conversely, PAH suppression is also heavily governed by the hardness and intensity of the interstellar radiation field. In massive star-forming regions and H II regions, the PAH fractional abundance strongly anti-correlates with the ionization parameter [Egorov et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L..16E/abstract). Spectroscopic studies demonstrate that PAH equivalent widths remain roughly constant until a threshold radiation field hardness is reached (e.g., $[\text{Ne III}]/[\text{Ne II}] \approx 1$), above which PAH emission drops precipitously as a power law [Gordon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...682..336G/abstract). 

This destruction is driven primarily by hydrogen-ionizing UV photons, which efficiently eradicate PAH carriers in unshielded environments [Hunt et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...712..164H/abstract), [Egorov et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L..16E/abstract). Intense radiation fields also alter the chemical structure of surviving grains; for instance, UV photons in active star-forming regions are observed to preferentially strip [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]] from the molecules, decreasing the aliphatic-to-[[aromatic-hydrocarbons|aromatic]] emission ratios [Lyu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...986..156L/abstract). Consequently, a similar PAH deficit is observed in galaxies characterized by high "starburstiness" (an excess of specific star formation rate), where compact star-forming regions produce intense UV radiation fields that process and destroy PAHs [Murata et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...566A.136M/abstract), [Cook et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..899C/abstract).

Ultimately, the literature remains somewhat conflicting on whether metallicity or radiation field hardness is the dominant driver of the environmental PAH deficit. In local H II regions, variations in the ionization parameter often correlate better with PAH weakening than metallicity itself [Gordon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...682..336G/abstract), [Egorov et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L..16E/abstract). However, in globally metal-poor dwarf galaxies, the harder ionizing radiation fields typical of low-metallicity stellar populations inextricably link the two effects, making it difficult to fully isolate enhanced UV destruction from the underlying evolutionary formation bottlenecks [Hunt et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...712..164H/abstract).

## PAHs in Active Galactic Nuclei and Extragalactic Halos

### PAH Destruction and Processing by AGN
Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) and supermassive black holes strongly influence the survival and spectral properties of [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]] in their vicinity. Observations consistently show that PAHs are generally eradicated or severely depleted within the central hundreds of parsecs of AGNs [Voit et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992MNRAS.258..841V/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...22Z/abstract). This "PAH deficit" is driven by the harsh nuclear environment, where molecules are destroyed by a combination of hard X-ray and EUV radiation, as well as kinetic AGN feedback in the form of shocks [Voit et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992MNRAS.258..841V/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975L...2Z/abstract). As a result, in Seyfert galaxies and quasars, mid-infrared PAH emission often originates from shielded circumnuclear star-forming rings rather than the immediate accretion environment [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...22Z/abstract), [Lai et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...957L..26L/abstract).

When PAHs do survive near an AGN, their population is heavily processed. Spectroscopic data reveals that small PAHs are preferentially destroyed by the radiation field, shifting the population toward larger, neutral species [García-Bernete et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...666L...5G/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260107990Z/abstract). Consequently, AGN hosts typically exhibit altered mid-infrared band ratios compared to normal star-forming regions, characterized by smaller $6.2/7.7\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ and larger $11.3/7.7\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ intensity ratios [Zhang et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...939...22Z/abstract). High-resolution JWST observations of luminous Seyferts confirm that nuclear PAHs are generally dominated by larger, neutral molecules compared to those found in normal star-forming regions [García-Bernete et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...666L...5G/abstract). Furthermore, the presence of a specific population of very large PAHs ($N_C \gtrsim 200$) has been distinctly identified in low-luminosity AGNs, arising as a consequence of their specific low-excitation conditions and feedback modes [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260107990Z/abstract). Furthermore, fragile [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]] (such as methyl side groups) are easily stripped by the intense nuclear radiation, resulting in a significantly suppressed aliphatic-to-aromatic $3.4/3.3\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ ratio near the AGN [Lai et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...957L..26L/abstract).

Recent studies of low-luminosity AGNs indicate that different feedback modes imprint distinct spectral signatures on the dust. Radiative feedback (severe photoionization) and kinetic feedback (fast shocks with velocities $v_s \sim 100\ \mathrm{km\ s}^{-1}$) process the PAH population differently, suggesting that spatially resolved PAH band ratios could serve as empirical diagnostics for distinguishing AGN feedback mechanisms [Zhang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...975L...2Z/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260107990Z/abstract). To analyze these complex features across large samples, unsupervised graph clustering techniques have been used to classify local ultraluminous infrared galaxies and quasars into distinct evolutionary stages based on the similarity of their PAH emission and silicate absorption features [Pavlou et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023A&C....4500742P/abstract).

### Galactic Winds and the Circumgalactic Medium
Despite their vulnerability to extreme radiation and shocks, PAHs demonstrate remarkable survivability when expelled into the circumgalactic medium (CGM) via starburst-driven galactic winds. Far from being restricted to the dense galactic disk, PAHs have been detected entrained in superwinds extending tens of kiloparsecs into the halo [Engelbracht et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ApJ...642L.127E/abstract), [Veilleux et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250708098V/abstract).

In the prototypical starburst galaxy M82, PAH emission is strongly correlated with the cold molecular phase of the outflow out to at least $3.2\ \mathrm{kpc}$, with the PAH-to-CO scaling relations remaining nearly identical to those found in the galactic disk [Lopez et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251001314L/abstract). At even more extreme scales, JWST observations of the Makani galaxy have detected PAH emission up to $\sim 35\ \mathrm{kpc}$ from the central starburst within its larger 100 kpc-scale wind [Veilleux et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250708098V/abstract). 

While the literature exhibits some tension regarding the exact physical state and lifetime of PAHs in highly ionized outflows, current consensus holds that although the molecules survive the $\sim 10^8$-year journey into the halo, they undergo significant erosion during transport. Flux ratios in the outer halo, such as the $(11.3 + 12.2)/7.7\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ band ratio, indicate a continuous decrease in average PAH size and an increase in the ionization fraction as the distance from the galactic nucleus increases [Veilleux et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250708098V/abstract), [Lopez et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251001314L/abstract).

## Cosmic Evolution and the Early Universe

The detection and characterization of PAHs across cosmic time provides a critical window into the history of dust enrichment and galaxy evolution. Historically, mid-infrared extragalactic surveys were restricted to the local and intermediate-redshift universe. However, observations with the *Spitzer Space Telescope* pushed the detection of the 6.2 $\mu$m and 7.7 $\mu$m PAH emission bands to "cosmic noon", including a Lyman break galaxy at $z=3.01$ [Huang et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...660L..69H/abstract). The redshift frontier for mid-infrared emission was further extended by the detection of the 6.2 $\mu$m feature in the submillimeter galaxy GN20 at $z=4.055$, tracing an intense starburst with a star formation rate of $\sim 1600 \, M_\odot \text{ yr}^{-1}$ [Riechers et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...786...31R/abstract). Additionally, a broad near-infrared continuum excess peaking at 2-5 $\mu$m is frequently observed in high-redshift galaxies; while PAHs contribute to this feature, this excess is primarily attributed to emission from circumstellar disks around massive young stellar objects [Mentuch et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...706.1020M/abstract). Even when direct PAH emission is not detected, their presence can be inferred indirectly in the distant universe. For instance, detailed Cloudy modeling has been used to reproduce the physical conditions in an extremely strong damped Lyman $\alpha$ absorber at $z=2.323$, revealing proxy signatures of PAHs [Shaw et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.1335S/abstract).

The advent of the *James Webb Space Telescope* (JWST) has revolutionized the study of PAHs at high redshift, extending high-resolution sensitivity to rest-frame near-infrared features. Spectroscopic surveys of galaxies at $z \sim 0.2-0.5$ successfully mapped both the 3.3 $\mu$m [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]] and 3.4 $\mu$m [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]] C-H stretching bands, revealing that intense ultraviolet (UV) radiation fields in active star-forming regions preferentially strip aliphatic side groups from PAH molecules [Lyu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...986..156L/abstract). Moving to higher redshifts, spatially resolved JWST and ALMA observations of a $z=1.46$ spiral galaxy show a distinct deficit of 8 $\mu$m PAH emission in the starburst core, indicating the destruction of PAH molecules by intense ionizing radiation—a phenomenon consistent with local starburst analogs [Liu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250509728L/abstract). Furthermore, a JWST MIRI Low Resolution Spectrometer survey of 37 massive star-forming galaxies and AGN at cosmic noon ($z \sim 1-2$) reveals that the 11.3/3.3 $\mu$m PAH ratios are approximately three times higher than those in local luminous infrared galaxies. This suggests that the grains at cosmic noon are systematically larger, potentially due to rapid coagulation in high-density environments [McKinney et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251007365M/abstract).

JWST has also detected the 3.3 $\mu$m PAH feature in a dust-rich galaxy less than 1.5 billion years after the Big Bang, revealing order-of-magnitude spatial variations in PAH equivalent widths that suggest a physical offset between PAHs and large dust grains in early galaxies [Spilker et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Natur.618..708S/abstract). The most profound implications for cosmic evolution arise from the detection of carbonaceous dust at the "cosmic dawn." Observations of the galaxy JADES-GS-z6-0 at $z \approx 6.71$ exhibit a distinct UV extinction bump. Unlike the standard 2175 Å bump pervasive in the Milky Way, the feature in JADES-GS-z6-0 is narrower and peaks at a longer wavelength of $\sim 2263$ Å, which quantum chemical models attribute to an early population of PAH molecules [Lin et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...694A..84L/abstract). 

The confirmed presence of PAHs at $z > 6$ presents a major theoretical tension for classical models of dust evolution. In the established paradigm, carbonaceous dust is primarily injected into the interstellar medium (ISM) by asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars, which require hundreds of millions of years to evolve. The rapid metal and dust enrichment observed in JADES-GS-z6-0 demonstrates that AGB stars alone could not have had time to be the sole progenitors of these early-universe PAHs [Nanni et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988L...5N/abstract). To explain this extremely rapid carbonaceous grain formation, alternative pathways are required. Proposed mechanisms include either highly efficient dust production by Type II supernovae (requiring an extreme metal depletion fraction of $\approx 73\%$) or rapid dust accretion directly within the ISM (requiring $\approx 10\%$ depletion from supernovae) [Nanni et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988L...5N/abstract). These discoveries at the cosmic dawn force a re-evaluation of the formation timescales of large organic molecules and their role in the thermodynamics of the ISM during the universe's first billion years.

## Modeling and Laboratory Astrochemistry

The interpretation of astronomical mid-infrared spectra relies heavily on a triad of laboratory experiments, quantum chemical calculations, and empirical modeling. Because individual [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]] are notoriously difficult to identify in the interstellar medium (ISM) due to spectral blending and complex environmental processing, experimental simulation and theoretical models are required to map specific spectral features to the physical and chemical states of the carriers.

### Laboratory Experiments and Quantum Chemistry
Early laboratory efforts to measure the emission spectra of PAHs under simulated ISM conditions demonstrated that small, neutral PAHs alone cannot reproduce the observed astrophysical bands; instead, a population of PAH cations and larger neutral species is required [Cook et al. (1996)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1996Natur.380..227C/abstract). Specialized experimental setups, such as the PIRENEA cold ion cyclotron resonance cell, have allowed researchers to trap and isolate PAH ions over long timescales, simulating their photodissociation and chemical fragmentation into smaller carbon molecules ($C_3, C_2, CO$) under ultraviolet irradiation and oxygen attack, demonstrating that this mechanism releases small carbon molecules like C$_2$ and C$_3$ and provides a pathway for the formation of small hydrocarbons in photodissociation regions (PDRs) [Joblin et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003sf2a.conf..175J/abstract).

Complementing experimental simulation, quantum chemistry provides access to reaction pathways and molecular properties that are difficult to measure in the lab. For instance, explicitly correlated coupled-cluster theory has been used to calculate highly anisotropic potential energy surfaces and rotational state-to-state cross-sections for PAH precursors colliding with helium [Sogomonyan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250615536S/abstract). Quantum chemical calculations have also elucidated gas-phase pathways demonstrating that fundamental nitrogen-bearing prebiotic aromatic molecules, like pyridine and (iso)quinoline, are synthesized not from PAH processing, but from simple radicals like methylene amidogen (H$_2$CN) and cyanomethyl (H$_2$CCN) [Yang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024NatAs...8..856Y/abstract).

Quantum methods are particularly crucial for studying isotopic variations. Computations of deuterated PAHs show that replacing a hydrogen atom with deuterium shifts the characteristic C-H stretching and bending modes to longer wavelengths. Due to the difference in reduced mass between the C-H and C-D oscillators, the 3.3 $\mu$m C-H stretch shifts to 4.4 $\mu$m by a predictable factor of $\sqrt{13/7}$ [Yang et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJS..251...12Y/abstract). These models yield an intrinsic band strength ratio of $\langle A_{4.4}/A_{3.3} \rangle \approx 0.56$ for neutral deuterated PAHs. However, tensions remain in the literature regarding the exact reservoir of interstellar deuterium: while aromatic C-D units alone appear insufficient to account for the "missing" deuterium in the local ISM, quantum calculations of deuterated [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]] suggest that aliphatic C-D bonds might bridge this gap [Yang et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJS..268...12Y/abstract).

### Spectral Modeling and Databases
To bridge the gap between molecular physics and observational astrophysics, researchers rely extensively on the [[nasa-ames-pah-ir-spectroscopic-database|NASA Ames PAH IR Spectroscopic Database]]. This repository of computed and experimental spectra allows astronomers to model the composite PAH emission of galaxies by fitting observed features to linear combinations of different PAH sizes, charge states, and compositions. However, sensitivities exist within the modeling process. For example, applying a redshift to calculated spectra to account for the anharmonicity of PAH vibrational modes can introduce a $15\%-20\%$ variance in the derived PAH parameters [Maragkoudakis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979...90M/abstract).

On macroscopic scales, the extraction of PAH features from galaxy spectra is frequently performed using spectral decomposition tools like PAHFIT, which separates the PAH subcomponents from thermal dust continuum, atomic/molecular gas lines, and stellar light [Van De Putte et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250705848V/abstract). Similarly, models like DustEM are employed to parameterize the bulk properties of the dust in differing radiation fields, such as inside H II regions [Stephens et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...784..147S/abstract). Machine learning has also advanced template derivation; for instance, algorithms have been utilized to construct specialized "Irradiated" and "Shielded" spectral templates for the $3.2\text{--}3.6\ \mu$m region, successfully tracing the feedback of far-ultraviolet (FUV) fields on the ISM [Schroetter et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...685A..78S/abstract).

Despite the sophistication of these tools, methodological ambiguities persist. Different spectral decomposition codes (such as PAHFIT and CAFE) can produce noticeable variations in the extracted continuum underlying the 11-15 $\mu$m region. When fed into database fitting routines, these underlying methodological discrepancies can drive a $\sim 7\%$ difference in the inferred neutral PAH fraction of a galaxy [Maragkoudakis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979...90M/abstract), highlighting that the derived physical properties of interstellar PAHs remain somewhat sensitive to the chosen modeling architecture.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

Despite significant observational advances, the precise mechanisms governing the lifecycle of [[aromatic-hydrocarbons|Aromatic hydrocarbons]] in galaxies remain actively debated. A major unresolved question is whether the sharp decline in PAH emission below a metallicity threshold of $12+\log(\text{O/H}) \approx 8$ [Haynes et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...724..215H/abstract) is primarily driven by a formation bottleneck—such as inhibited grain growth and shattering—or by enhanced destruction from hard, unshielded UV radiation fields [Cook et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.445..899C/abstract), [Tarantino et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251204060T/abstract). The survival of PAHs in extreme environments presents a related theoretical challenge; while PAHs are broadly observed to be eradicated within the ionized gas of H II regions and the central hundreds of parsecs of active galactic nuclei [Voit et al. (1992)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1992MNRAS.258..841V/abstract), [Egorov et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...944L..16E/abstract), they have recently been detected surviving transport into the circumgalactic medium over distances up to $\sim 35$ kpc in galactic superwinds [Veilleux et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...990...57V/abstract).

The chemical relationship between PAHs, [[aliphatic-hydrocarbons|Aliphatic hydrocarbons]], and fullerenes (such as $\text{C}_{60}$ and $\text{C}_{70}$) represents a shifting paradigm in astrochemistry. Traditional models posited that fullerene formation requires strictly hydrogen-poor environments. However, the simultaneous detection of PAHs and fullerenes in hydrogen-rich planetary nebulae suggests that fullerenes may instead form through the complex photochemical processing of hydrogenated amorphous carbon [García-Hernández et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...724L..39G/abstract), [Bernard-Salas et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013lcdu.confE..32B/abstract). Unraveling this evolutionary pathway is a key frontier, particularly as laboratory and theoretical studies continue to clarify the origins of fundamental prebiotic molecules. For example, recent studies demonstrate that certain prebiotic nitrogen heterocycles such as pyridine and (iso)quinoline are synthesized via gas-phase radical reactions (e.g., from H$_2$CN and H$_2$CCN) rather than from the processing of PAHs or fullerenes [Yang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024NatAs...8..856Y/abstract). Another persistent mystery involves the diffuse interstellar bands (DIBs). Statistical mapping of DIBs has revealed complex correlations between DIB strengths and PAH abundances, suggesting the two classes of organic molecules are physically linked tracers of the interstellar medium [Lan et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.3629L/abstract).

Historically, the broad and complex nature of PAH emission bands precluded detailed kinematic analysis. However, the advent of high-resolution integral field spectroscopy with JWST has established PAH kinematics as a viable new sub-field. Recent studies have successfully employed PCA tomography to extract velocity maps from the $3.3~\mu\text{m}$ PAH feature in luminous infrared galaxies [Donnan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532L..75D/abstract). By isolating the signature of rotation in the second principal component of the emission band, researchers can now trace the bulk motion of the smallest dust grains. Initial results indicate that while PAHs generally follow the rotation of the galactic molecular gas, structural deviations in their kinematic position angles suggest that the dynamical behavior of nano-grains can decouple from that of the bulk gas [Donnan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532L..75D/abstract).

Future space missions will dramatically expand the utility of PAHs as cosmological probes. The upcoming SPHEREx mission will conduct an all-sky spectrophotometric survey between 0.75 and $5~\mu\text{m}$, enabling the measurement of the $3.3~\mu\text{m}$ PAH feature in hundreds of thousands of galaxies out to $z \sim 0.4$ [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...992....3Z/abstract). This unprecedented sample will constrain the nano-grain populations of massive galaxies and potentially resolve discrepancies in far-infrared reddening maps, which are insensitive to PAH abundance variations and thus under-predict reddening in certain regions [Lee et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250811746L/abstract). Looking further ahead, the PRobe far-Infrared Mission for Astrophysics (PRIMA) will utilize its FIRESS instrument for low- and high-resolution spectroscopy to detect rest-frame mid-infrared PAH emission out to the epoch of reionization [Bisigello et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A.125B/abstract), [Yoon et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025JATIS..11c1634Y/abstract). By extending PAH diagnostics to galaxies with $L_{\rm IR} \sim 10^{12} L_{\odot}$ at $z > 6$, PRIMA will map the earliest stages of carbon dust enrichment. Interpreting these vast new datasets will rely heavily on continuously updated theoretical models and laboratory data, such as the [[nasa-ames-pah-ir-spectroscopic-database|NASA Ames PAH IR Spectroscopic Database]], to accurately decompose complex mid-infrared spectra and derive robust physical parameters [Maragkoudakis et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979...90M/abstract).
