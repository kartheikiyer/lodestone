## Article

In extragalactic astrophysics, **galaxy quenching** refers to the [[galaxy-quenching-mechanisms|physical processes]] responsible for the severe suppression or complete cessation of star formation within a galaxy. This phenomenon fundamentally underpins the observed bimodality of the local universe, driving the evolutionary transition of galaxies from the actively [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] that populate the "blue cloud" across the intermediate [[green-valley-galaxies|green valley]] to the passive, [[quiescent-galaxies|quiescent]] "[[red-sequence|Red sequence]]" [Smethurst et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.450..435S/abstract). Quenching is characterized empirically by a drastic decline in a galaxy's specific star formation rate (sSFR), often coupled with a reduction in its cold molecular gas reservoir and overall [[average-star-formation-efficiency|star formation efficiency]]. Rather than a single evolutionary track, quenching encompasses a diverse suite of mechanisms that operate over widely varying timescales, altering both the interstellar medium and the fundamental structure of the host galaxy [Martig et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...707..250M/abstract).

The physical drivers of quenching are broadly categorized into internal and external mechanisms. In massive central galaxies, [[mass-driven-quenching|Mass-driven quenching]] dominates the evolutionary pathway. This process is tightly correlated with structural parameters such as central stellar surface density, velocity dispersion, and bulge mass, strongly implicating active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback as the primary agent that heats or expels gas to halt star formation [Bluck et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441..599B/abstract), [Fang et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...776...63F/abstract). Conversely, the evolution of low-mass and satellite galaxies is primarily governed by [[environmental-quenching|Environmental quenching]]. As these galaxies fall into dense dark matter halos, interactions with the [[intracluster-and-intragroup-phenomena|intracluster or intragroup medium]] strip away their cold gas via ram-pressure or halt cosmological accretion through [[suffocation-astrophysics|Suffocation (astrophysics)]] [Peng et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015Natur.521..192P/abstract). Environmental quenching typically follows a "delayed-then-rapid" timescale [Wetzel et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..336W/abstract), whereas internal quenching can manifest either as slow secular exhaustion or as an abrupt truncation triggered by major mergers and [[compaction-events|Compaction events]] [Schawinski et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.440..889S/abstract). 

This article explores the comprehensive framework of galaxy quenching, detailing the varied evolutionary pathways from the main sequence to absolute quiescence. It examines how galaxies undergo [[structural-transformation|Structural transformation]] alongside star formation cessation, the spatial dynamics distinguishing "[[inside-out-formation|inside-out]]" from "outside-in" quenching, and the lifecycle of infalling galaxies subject to extended [[intracluster-and-intragroup-phenomena|Intracluster and Intragroup Phenomena]]. While classical theoretical models initially suffered from an "over-quenching" problem that predicted too many red low-mass satellites, contemporary research faces profound new tensions at the opposite end of cosmic history. Recent JWST observations have revealed an unexpected abundance of massive [[quiescent-galaxies|Quiescent galaxies]] at $z > 3$, alongside rapid [[mini-quenching-events|Mini-quenching events]] at even higher redshifts, demanding feedback efficiencies that current cosmological simulations struggle to reproduce. Furthermore, critical open questions remain regarding why some retired galaxies retain substantial molecular gas with [[negligible-star-formation|Negligible star formation]], the frequency of [[rejuvenation-episodes|Rejuvenation episodes]], and the statistical prevalence of tidal gas removal versus AGN-driven winds in cementing a galaxy's final transition to the red sequence.

## Galaxy Bimodality and the Transition Framework

The observed distribution of galaxies in color-magnitude and star formation rate versus stellar mass ($M_\star$) parameter spaces exhibits a stark bimodality [Gonçalves et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...759...67G/abstract), [Bluck et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441..599B/abstract). Galaxies broadly separate into actively [[star-forming-galaxies|star-forming]] systems populating the "blue cloud" and [[quiescent-galaxies|quiescent]] systems residing on the "[[red-sequence|Red sequence]]". Between these two populations lies a sparsely populated intermediate region known as the "green valley." This bimodality is fundamentally traced by the specific star formation rate, defined as $\text{sSFR} = \dot{M}_\star / M_\star$. While blue cloud galaxies lie on the star-forming main sequence, quenched galaxies reside significantly below it, typically exhibiting $\text{sSFR} \lesssim 10^{-11} \text{ yr}^{-1}$ [Cen et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...781...38C/abstract), [Hartley et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.522.3138H/abstract). To overcome ambiguities in classifying galaxies strictly by the star-forming main sequence, recent analyses demonstrate that utilizing the star formation rate surface density ($\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$) versus stellar mass diagram is a valuable complementary diagnostic. This approach reveals that, when normalized by their star-forming extent, high-mass galaxies are often as active as low-mass ones [Salim et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...958..183S/abstract).

The relative scarcity of galaxies in the green valley implies that the transition from the blue cloud to the red sequence must occur rapidly compared to the Hubble time [Gonçalves et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...759...67G/abstract). Observational modeling suggests that the [[green-valley-crossing-time|Green Valley Crossing Time]] is typically on the order of $1-2$ Gyr [Trayford et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.460.3925T/abstract), [Bravo et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.522.4481B/abstract). However, some high-resolution hydrodynamical simulations predict even faster transitions of $\approx 400$ Myr driven by the onset of active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback [Blank et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.5296B/abstract). 

Rather than representing a single evolutionary track, the green valley is increasingly viewed as hosting multiple distinct evolutionary pathways. It has been argued that the green valley is a "red herring" when morphology is not accounted for [Schawinski et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.440..889S/abstract). Late-type galaxies with substantial disks may traverse the [[green-valley-galaxies|green valley]] slowly as their cosmic gas supply shuts off, followed by a gradual exhaustion of remaining gas reservoirs over several Gyr. Conversely, early-type galaxies appear to undergo rapid quenching accompanied by a rapid [[structural-transformation|Structural transformation]] from disk to spheroid, often associated with major mergers or the virtually instantaneous destruction of their gas reservoirs [Schawinski et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.440..889S/abstract), [Smethurst et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.450..435S/abstract). 

The spatial progression of quenching provides further physical clues. Spatially resolved integral field spectroscopy reveals that green valley galaxies frequently exhibit "[[inside-out-formation|inside-out]]" quenching, where the suppression of star formation begins in the central bulge regions before propagating to the outer disk [Lian et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.472.4679L/abstract), [Lin et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872...50L/abstract). This is characterized by a central suppression of star formation, though the primary physical driver is a subject of active research. Some studies identify a deficit primarily in the central molecular gas fraction ($f_{\rm gas}$) while preserving similar [[average-star-formation-efficiency|star formation efficiencies]] between the bulge and disk [Lin et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...851...18L/abstract), whereas other observations find the reduction is predominantly driven by a steep decline in the central star formation efficiency (SFE) [Pan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964..120P/abstract). However, there remains tension with some cosmological simulations, which erroneously predict outside-in quenching for green valley galaxies, highlighting ongoing challenges in modeling subgrid feedback [Starkenburg et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...874L..17S/abstract).

While the bimodality paradigm is standard, some literature challenges the strict dichotomy between star-forming and [[dead-galaxies|dead galaxies]]. Observations utilizing far-infrared dust emission suggest that galaxies may instead form a continuous "Galaxy End Sequence." In this framework, the apparent red sequence simply arises because optical colors change very little once the sSFR drops below a critical threshold, rather than representing a fundamentally separate population driven by rapid quenching events [Eales et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3125E/abstract).

## Internal Quenching: AGN Feedback and Mass-Driven Processes

For central galaxies, the cessation of star formation is strongly correlated with intrinsic physical properties, a process often termed [[mass-driven-quenching|Mass-driven quenching]]. Above a transitional mass scale of $M_* \gtrsim 10^{10.5} M_\odot$, the quenched fraction of central galaxies rises sharply [Bluck et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.2559B/abstract), [Contini et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...882..167C/abstract). However, total stellar mass is only a secondary proxy; observational analyses consistently reveal that the central velocity dispersion ($\sigma_c$), bulge mass, and the central stellar surface density within 1 kpc ($\Sigma_1$) are significantly tighter predictors of quiescence [Bluck et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441..599B/abstract), [Zhang et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...911...57Z/abstract). For instance, galaxies are observed to quench when they cross a critical central density threshold that scales with total stellar mass [Fang et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...776...63F/abstract):
$$ \Sigma_1 \propto M_*^{0.64} $$
In addition to internal structure, recent work demonstrates that once systematic biases in abundance matching are corrected, the dark matter halo mass remains the dominant quenching factor for central galaxies, exhibiting a sharp transition threshold near $10^{12.1} M_\odot$ [Zhao et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251216290Z/abstract). 

Because $\sigma_c$ and bulge mass scale tightly with the mass of the central supermassive black hole ($M_{\rm BH}$), these structural correlations are widely interpreted as evidence that active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback governs the transition to [[passive-galaxies|Passive galaxies]] [Bluck et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.499..230B/abstract). Studies mapping elemental abundances provide corroborating evidence: stellar metallicity in passive galaxies is primarily driven by velocity dispersion, forging a strong link between AGN feedback, the prevention of continuous gas inflows, and the resulting chemical evolution driven by starvation [Baker et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.534...30B/abstract). Observations from the Galaxy Zoo project analyzing Type 2 AGN host galaxies exhibit evidence of a rapid drop in star formation rate within the last 2 Gyr, though secular evolution also contributes to the quenching of the broader AGN population [Smethurst et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.2986S/abstract). Interestingly, the prevalence of optical and radio-loud AGN is found to be remarkably similar for both central and satellite galaxies at a fixed stellar mass, suggesting that these common internal feedback processes operate consistently across different environments [Wang et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...860..102W/abstract).

Theoretical models and cosmological simulations increasingly rely on AGN feedback to successfully reproduce the observed red sequence and prevent excess star formation in massive halos [Weinberger et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3291W/abstract), [Davé et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.471.1671D/abstract). This feedback is typically implemented as a dual-mode process. During phases of high accretion relative to the Eddington limit, the AGN operates in a thermal mode (or quasar mode), isotropically heating surrounding gas. At lower accretion rates, the AGN transitions to a kinetic feedback mode (often associated with radio jets), which injects stochastic momentum into the halo [Weinberger et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3291W/abstract). Simulations indicate that kinetic feedback is highly efficient at thermalizing via shocks and providing the distributed heating necessary to offset radiative cooling, thereby maintaining systems as [[quiescent-galaxies|Quiescent galaxies]] [Weinberger et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3291W/abstract), [Quai et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.504.1888Q/abstract).

The energetic output of the AGN preferentially halts star formation in the core, leading to "[[inside-out-formation|inside-out]]" quenching profiles frequently observed in [[transition-galaxies|Transition galaxies]] traversing the green valley [Lin et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872...50L/abstract), [Bluck et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.499..230B/abstract). Spatially resolved spectroscopy demonstrates that central AGN feedback can deplete molecular gas reservoirs and suppress star formation efficiency on kiloparsec scales [Ellison et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.505L..46E/abstract). Consequently, AGN feedback not only suppresses star formation but also shapes the [[structural-transformation|morphological evolution]] of massive galaxies by preventing the re-formation of extended gas disks following mergers [Dubois et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.3948D/abstract).

While the consensus favors AGN feedback as the primary driver of internal quenching, alternative intrinsic mechanisms have been proposed. The growth of a dominant central bulge can stabilize a gas disk against gravitational fragmentation, suppressing star formation without necessitating massive gas outflows—a non-ejective process termed morphological quenching [Martig et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...707..250M/abstract). However, high-resolution simulations using the FIRE-2 model indicate that stellar feedback, magnetic fields, and morphological quenching alone are insufficient to halt cooling flows in massive halos, emphasizing the necessity of AGN feedback for long-term maintenance quenching [Su et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.487.4393S/abstract). Furthermore, observational tensions exist regarding the timing of total gas expulsion. AGNs are frequently observed in gas-rich, star-forming hosts, a fact that initially appears to contradict violent [[negative-feedback|Negative feedback]] models [Ward et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2936W/abstract). Recent simulations clarify that effective AGN feedback can coexist with substantial cold gas reservoirs; its primary role is to gradually deplete central gas and halt continuous cosmological accretion, rather than instantaneously ejecting the entire interstellar medium [Ward et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2936W/abstract), [Michałowski et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964..129M/abstract).

## Environmental Quenching: Satellite and Halo Interactions

As galaxies are accreted into massive dark matter halos and become satellites, they are subjected to external hydrodynamic and gravitational forces that dictate their evolution. While internal, mass-driven mechanisms dominate the cessation of star formation in high-mass central systems, [[environmental-quenching|Environmental quenching]] is the primary driver of passivity for low-mass galaxies ($M_* < 10^{10} M_\odot$) [Bluck et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462.2559B/abstract), [Contini et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...889..156C/abstract). However, the dominance of the environment is strongly mass-dependent. Semi-analytic analysis utilizing the GAEA model suggests that for satellite galaxies above $10^{10} M_\odot$, quenching is primarily driven by the internal self-regulation between star formation and stellar feedback rather than environmental mechanisms [De Lucia et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.482.5041D/abstract).

The evolutionary timeline of [[satellite-quenching|Satellite quenching]] is widely characterized by a "delayed-then-rapid" scenario. Following infall into a host halo, satellite galaxies typically maintain their star formation rates for a delay period of $2-4$ Gyr. After this delay, star formation is rapidly extinguished with an $e$-folding timescale of $<1$ Gyr [Wetzel et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..336W/abstract), [Foltz et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...866..136F/abstract), [Cora et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483.1686C/abstract), [De Daniloff et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250910612D/abstract). 

Two primary physical mechanisms compete to explain this transformation. The first is [[suffocation-astrophysics|Suffocation (astrophysics)]] (often termed strangulation), wherein the satellite's extended hot gas halo is removed or its cosmological gas accretion is halted. The galaxy then slowly consumes its remaining cold gas reservoir. Evidence from stellar metallicity trends strongly supports starvation as a widespread prerequisite for quenching [Peng et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015Natur.521..192P/abstract), [Trussler et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.491.5406T/abstract). However, pure starvation models struggle to explain the highly depleted cold gas fractions and short transition times observed in dense clusters.

The second mechanism is ram-pressure stripping (RPS), the active removal of the cold interstellar medium due to hydrodynamic drag from the intracluster medium. RPS is observationally linked to "outside-in" quenching, where star formation is first suppressed in the galaxy outskirts [Finn et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...862..149F/abstract), [Wang et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.523.6020W/abstract), [Çakır et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260203088C/abstract). Direct spectroscopic confirmation has recently caught ram-pressure stripping in action in a forming galaxy cluster as early as $z=2.5$, demonstrating that RPS was already operating during the peak epoch of cosmic galaxy formation [Xu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250321724X/abstract). Environmental quenching effects are not strictly a low-redshift phenomenon; they have been definitively observed at $z \approx 2$, where the quenching probability is enhanced for low-mass galaxies found in proximity to more massive [[quiescent-galaxies|quenched]] neighbors on scales of $\sim 500$ kpc [Ji et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...862..135J/abstract). 

The literature reflects an active debate regarding the relative efficiency of RPS versus suffocation. While some studies suggest environmental quenching is highly inefficient for isolated dwarf galaxies, requiring [[gradual-gas-stripping|Gradual gas stripping]] or starvation over $>9$ Gyr [Wheeler et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.442.1396W/abstract), others demonstrate that the "impulsiveness" of ram pressure can drive rapid ($<1$ Gyr) quenching in cluster environments [Samuel et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.3849S/abstract). Additionally, merger shocks associated with [[intracluster-and-intragroup-phenomena|Intracluster and Intragroup Phenomena]] have been shown to enhance RPS efficiency [Roberts et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...971..182R/abstract). Recent analysis suggests that initial strangulation followed by active gas ejection via outflows—rather than purely environmental RPS—best reproduces the chemical properties of the local population of quiescent galaxies [Trussler et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.491.5406T/abstract). However, in dense cluster environments, RPS frequently remains an essential component for actively stripping the remaining cold gas [Bluck et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.499..230B/abstract). Satellite kinematics can serve as a diagnostic tool in this debate, as red satellite kinematics are consistent with earlier infall times compared to star-forming satellites, helping distinguish quenching models that require prolonged intracluster processes [Adhikari et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...878....9A/abstract).

Interestingly, internal feedback processes can directly modulate the severity of environmental quenching. Observational evidence reveals an anisotropic distribution of quenched satellites, which are statistically less frequent along the minor axis of their central galaxies. This suggests that energetic AGN outflows propagating along the minor axis can clear out the local circumgalactic medium, thereby reducing the hydrodynamic drag of ram pressure and allowing satellites in these regions to retain their gas for longer periods [Martín-Navarro et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021Natur.594..187M/abstract).

Importantly, [[halo-quenching|Halo quenching]] effects are not confined strictly within the virial radius of the final host halo. Observational measurements of galactic conformity demonstrate that the [[galaxy-environment|Galaxy environment]] suppresses star formation out to $2-3$ virial radii [Wetzel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.439.2687W/abstract), [Hearin et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract), [Hatfield et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.472.3570H/abstract). This extended footprint is heavily populated by "backsplash" galaxies—systems that have passed through the cluster core and exited the virial radius on highly elliptical orbits [Wetzel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.439.2687W/abstract), [Casey et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.520.4715C/abstract). Furthermore, many satellites undergo "pre-processing" in lower-mass groups prior to their accretion into massive clusters, accounting for up to half of the quenched satellite population in massive clusters [Wetzel et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..336W/abstract), [Hough et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.518.2398H/abstract). These phenomena underscore that a satellite's integrated environmental history, rather than its instantaneous local density alone, dictates its quenching trajectory.

## Morphological and Structural Transformation

Galaxy quenching is frequently accompanied by a profound [[structural-transformation|Structural transformation]], as systems evolve from rotation-dominated, star-forming disks into dispersion-supported, quiescent spheroids. The causal relationship and timing between star formation cessation and morphological change remain subjects of active debate. Observations indicate that central structural parameters, such as the inner stellar mass surface density within 1 kpc ($\Sigma_1$), central velocity dispersion, and bulge mass, are among the strongest predictors of a galaxy's quenched status [Fang et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...776...63F/abstract), [Bluck et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.441..599B/abstract). This correlation is commonly attributed to a two-step process wherein major mergers or violent disk instabilities drive gas to the center, rapidly building a dense bulge and triggering active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback that subsequently quenches star formation. Notably, AGN feedback during such major mergers can rapidly remove central gas and trigger the formation of large-scale galactic bars, features that are characteristically found in gas-poor, recently quenched systems [Łokas et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...668L...3L/abstract). The presence of a galactic bar can also be used as a morphological signature for slow star formation quenching, as barred [[green-valley-galaxies|Green Valley galaxies]] exhibit longer transition timescales compared to unbarred systems [Nogueira-Cavalcante et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...630A..88N/abstract).

Alternatively, the presence of a dominant bulge can dynamically stabilize the gas disk against fragmentation—a process known as morphological quenching—halting star formation without necessarily requiring the immediate expulsion of gas [Martig et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...707..250M/abstract). Semi-analytic modeling indicates that black hole quenching better reproduces the passive fraction and morphological distribution of galaxies than halo quenching alone, provided that minor mergers contribute significantly to supermassive black hole growth [Koutsouridou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.516.4194K/abstract). Furthermore, some empirical analyses challenge the compaction-triggered quenching model entirely, suggesting instead that galaxies age at a constant surface density and that the observed density thresholds result merely from ab initio evolutionary rates set by global densities rather than discrete structural events [Abramson et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016arXiv160807577A/abstract).

In dense environments, the morphological evolution of satellite galaxies follows a distinct pathway. While [[environmental-quenching|Environmental quenching]] mechanisms like ram-pressure stripping efficiently remove cold gas and quench star formation, they do not immediately destroy the stellar disk. Consequently, ram-pressure stripped galaxies often transition initially into "red spirals" or lenticulars (S0 galaxies) as their disks slowly fade [Marasco et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.5359M/abstract). Recent phase-space analyses of cluster infallers suggest that the timeline of these transformations is complex; some studies propose that morphological transition from late- to early-type can begin during the "delay" phase prior to rapid star formation quenching, effectively making morphological quenching an additional ingredient in the environmental quenching process [Sampaio et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.509..567S/abstract). Regardless of the exact sequence, the structural metamorphosis from the blue cloud to the [[red-sequence|Red sequence]] requires the redistribution of stellar orbits, a process deeply intertwined with a galaxy's integrated history of mergers, accretion, and environmental interactions.

## Quenching Timescales and Pathways

The transition of galaxies from the star-forming main sequence to the [[red-sequence|Red sequence]] proceeds through a variety of evolutionary pathways, characterized by widely differing timescales. Analytical modeling of massive galaxies quantifies that star formation is lost roughly equally to smooth mass quenching ($\approx 49\%$ per Gyr) and to fast stochastic mechanisms like ram-pressure stripping ($\approx 41\%$ per Gyr) [Kipper et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...647A..32K/abstract). The overall decline in star formation is frequently parametrized by an exponential decay model:
$$ {\rm SFR}(t) = {\rm SFR}_0 \exp\left(-\frac{t}{\tau_{\rm Q}}\right) $$
where $\tau_{\rm Q}$ is the characteristic quenching e-folding time-scale [Lian et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...832...29L/abstract). Observations of transition and post-starburst systems reveal a dichotomy between the slow, secular exhaustion of gas and rapid, dynamically driven truncation.

**Slow Secular Exhaustion**
For many isolated and central galaxies, [[mass-driven-quenching|Mass-driven quenching]] manifests as a slow decline in star formation over several gigayears. This pathway is typically driven by the cessation of cosmological cold gas accretion or the maintenance of a hot halo—a process often referred to as starvation or [[suffocation-astrophysics|Suffocation (astrophysics)]]. Studies of stellar metallicities and ages indicate that the complete transition from star-forming to passive can take $\sim 4$ Gyr for local galaxies with $M_* < 10^{11} M_\odot$ [Peng et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015Natur.521..192P/abstract). For central galaxies in the mass range of $10^{9.5}$ to $10^{11} M_\odot$, quenching e-folding times typically span $1.5-0.5$ Gyr, leading to a total migration time of roughly 4 Gyr from the main sequence to absolute quiescence [Hahn et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...841....6H/abstract). Consequently, these galaxies exhibit a prolonged [[green-valley-crossing-time|Green Valley Crossing Time]], slowly fading in specific star formation rate while largely preserving their late-type, disk-like morphologies [Schawinski et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.440..889S/abstract), [Smethurst et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.450..435S/abstract).

**Delayed-Then-Rapid Quenching**
In dense environments, [[satellite-quenching|Satellite quenching]] is best described by a "delayed-then-rapid" evolutionary track. Upon accretion into a larger host group or cluster, satellite galaxies often maintain their pre-infall star formation rates for a delay period of 2 to 4 Gyr [Wetzel et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..336W/abstract), [Cora et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483.1686C/abstract). Following this delay, external [[environmental-quenching|Environmental quenching]] mechanisms—most notably ram-pressure stripping of the cold interstellar medium—act highly efficiently, truncating star formation with an e-folding time of $\tau_{\rm Q} < 0.8$ Gyr [Wetzel et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..336W/abstract). Observations of group galaxies further reflect this, suggesting a typical total quenching timescale of approximately 4.9 Gyr, which is consistent with slow mechanisms like starvation operating during the delay phase before rapid gas stripping ensues [Zabelle et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...947...17Z/abstract). This rapid gas stripping phase creates a sharp cutoff in star formation, even though the total elapsed time from initial cluster infall to absolute quiescence can span over 5 Gyr.

**Fast-Track Quenching and Post-Starbursts**
A subset of the galaxy population undergoes extremely rapid quenching ($\tau_{\rm Q} < 0.5$ Gyr), bypassing the extended green valley phase entirely [Lian et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...832...29L/abstract), [Socolovsky et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.476.1242S/abstract). This "fast-track" is observationally linked to post-starburst (E+A) galaxies and is frequently accompanied by profound [[structural-transformation|Structural transformation]] into spheroidal morphologies [Schawinski et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.440..889S/abstract), [Suess et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...87S/abstract). The dominant triggers for this rapid cessation are believed to be gas-rich major mergers and extreme [[compaction-events|Compaction events]]. Such violent interactions can tidally eject up to 50% of a galaxy's molecular gas reservoir [Puglisi et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5..319P/abstract), [Spilker et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...936L..11S/abstract) or induce central starbursts that rapidly exhaust the remaining fuel. While rapid, merger-driven quenching accounts for only a small minority (1-5%) of the $z \sim 0$ quenched population [Weigel et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...845..145W/abstract), it appears increasingly vital at higher redshifts. It is worth noting that even the Milky Way itself is thought to have experienced a generalized quenching event approximately 9 Gyr ago at the end of its thick-disk formation phase, during which its star formation rate temporarily dropped by an order of magnitude in less than 2 Gyr [Haywood et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016A&A...589A..66H/abstract).

**High-Redshift Constraints**
Recent JWST observations have uncovered a population of [[high-mass-quiescent-galaxies|massive, fully quenched galaxies]] at $z > 3$, extending up to $z \sim 5$ [de Graaff et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240405683D/abstract), alongside discoveries of low-mass, [[mini-quenched-galaxies|mini-quenched systems]] emerging as early as $z = 7.3$ [Looser et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.629...53L/abstract). The existence of these massive systems requires extreme quenching timescales of $\sim 200-600$ Myr [Weller et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240602664W/abstract). The brevity of these timescales challenges conventional feedback models, suggesting that early massive galaxies either experienced highly efficient AGN-driven outflows, catastrophic gas exhaustion, or rapid [[mini-quenching-events|Mini-quenching events]] to abruptly join the ranks of [[passive-and-quiescent-galaxies|Passive and Quiescent Galaxies]] within the first billion years of the Universe.

## The Role of Gas Reservoirs and the Interstellar Medium

Galaxy quenching is fundamentally governed by the availability and physical state of the interstellar medium (ISM). The transition from the main sequence of [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] to quiescence is driven by two primary factors: a reduction in the cold gas fraction ($f_{\rm gas} \equiv M_{\rm gas}/M_\star$) and a decline in the star formation efficiency (${\rm SFE} \equiv {\rm SFR}/M_{\rm gas}$) of the remaining fuel [Piotrowska et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.492L...6P/abstract), [Dou et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...907..114D/abstract). As galaxies traverse the color-mass diagram to become [[green-valley-galaxies|Green Valley galaxies]], both $f_{\rm gas}$ and SFE typically decrease. Spatially resolved observations reveal that retired galactic regions follow a distinct resolved molecular gas main sequence, offset to lower molecular gas surface densities ($\Sigma_{\rm H_2}$) at a fixed stellar surface density by a factor of roughly five compared to star-forming regions [Ellison et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502L...6E/abstract).

A notable tension in the literature arises because quiescence does not strictly require the complete absence of cold gas. Observations of early-type and recently quenched elliptical galaxies demonstrate that many [[quiescent-galaxies|Quiescent galaxies]] retain substantial atomic (HI) and molecular gas reservoirs, sometimes exhibiting $f_{\rm gas} \ge 17\%$ [Michałowski et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964..129M/abstract), [Deo et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260108027D/abstract). In these systems, the star formation rate declines faster (on a timescale of $\sim 1.8$ Gyr) than the cold gas mass is depleted. Consequently, the transition to a fully retired state must involve a severe suppression of the SFE. The stabilization of this gas against fragmentation—potentially via morphological quenching, non-thermal pressure from magnetic fields and cosmic rays, or elevated turbulence—prevents [[h2-regulated-star-formation|H2-regulated star formation]] even when fuel is abundant [Martig et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...707..250M/abstract), [Tabatabaei et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018NatAs...2...83T/abstract), [Michałowski et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964..129M/abstract).

The literature suggests multiple evolutionary pathways dictate the ISM's fate. In some [[transition-galaxies|Transition galaxies]], quenching is primarily SFE-driven and associated with short decay timescales ($<1$ Gyr), whereas gas-depletion-driven quenching exhibits a broader range of timescales [Lin et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260109225L/abstract). The gas depletion time ($\tau_{\rm dep} = 1/{\rm SFE}$) is highly sensitive to the adopted [[star-formation-rate-tracers|Star formation rate tracers]] and the galaxy's evolutionary stage; older quiescent systems display significantly longer depletion times than recently quenched galaxies [Belli et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...909L..11B/abstract).

Along with cold gas, the dust content of the ISM undergoes secular destruction and removal. The timescale for dust removal in early-type galaxies has been directly measured at $\tau = 2.5 \pm 0.4$ Gyr [Michałowski et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...632A..43M/abstract). This slow exponential decline, alongside proportional drops in molecular and atomic gas, suggests that [[gas-depletion-and-consumption|Gas Depletion and Consumption]] through [[astration|Astration]] or slow starvation governs the ISM evolution in many systems [Michałowski et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964..129M/abstract).

Conversely, extreme events can rapidly eject or heat the ISM, providing robust [[negative-feedback|Negative feedback]]. Observations of high-redshift starbursts and post-starburst systems reveal that up to $50\%$ of a galaxy's molecular gas can be tidally ejected into massive tails during major mergers, abruptly halting star formation [Puglisi et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5..319P/abstract), [Spilker et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...936L..11S/abstract). Similarly, active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback can heat or destroy central molecular gas reservoirs on timescales of $<100$ Myr [Schawinski et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...690.1672S/abstract), [Ellison et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.505L..46E/abstract), driving a rapid, inside-out cessation of star formation.

## Spatial Dynamics: Inside-Out vs. Outside-In Quenching

Spatially resolved observations of star formation radial profiles and color gradients provide critical constraints on the physical mechanisms that halt star formation. By mapping the geometry of quenching, researchers can generally distinguish whether galaxies cease forming stars from their centers outward ("inside-out") or from their peripheries inward ("outside-in"). These spatial dynamics map closely to the dichotomy between internal [[mass-driven-quenching|Mass-driven quenching]] and external [[environmental-quenching|Environmental quenching]].

Inside-out quenching is the dominant pathway for massive central galaxies ($M_* > 10^{10.5} M_\odot$). Integral field spectroscopy surveys, such as MaNGA and CALIFA, reveal that these galaxies undergo a rapid suppression of their central specific star formation rate ($\Sigma_{\rm sSFR}$) while star formation continues in their extended disks [Bluck et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.499..230B/abstract), [Kalinova et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...648A..64K/abstract). This central suppression strongly correlates with central velocity dispersion and black hole mass, implicating active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback and morphological stabilization by a central bulge [Lin et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...872...50L/abstract). Quantitative analyses combining spatially resolved gas measurements indicate that central star formation is primarily suppressed by a sharp drop in star formation efficiency (SFE), whereas the subsequent quenching of the outer disk is driven by a combination of declining SFE and cold gas fractions ($f_{\rm gas}$) [Pan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964..120P/abstract). Evidence for [[inside-out-formation|Inside-out formation]] and subsequent inside-out quenching is not limited to the local Universe; it has been observed out to $z \sim 2$ in protoclusters [Laishram et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251218805L/abstract) and in massive transition galaxies as early as $z = 3.7$ [Schreiber et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021A&A...646A..68S/abstract). 

Conversely, outside-in quenching dominates the evolution of low-mass galaxies ($M_* \leq 10^{10} M_\odot$) and satellite galaxies residing in dense [[galaxy-environment|Galaxy environment]]s [Liu et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...860...60L/abstract). In these systems, interactions with the intracluster or intragroup medium strip the loosely bound gas from the galaxy outskirts. Observations of local cluster satellites show truncated star-forming disks relative to their stellar extents, consistent with ram-pressure stripping driving a steeply declining radial star formation profile [Finn et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...862..149F/abstract), [Bluck et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.499..230B/abstract), [Çakır et al. (2026)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026arXiv260203088C/abstract). Consequently, the cessation of star formation progresses from the outer disk inward, leaving a temporary core of active star formation before the galaxy fully transitions to the red sequence.

Despite this general dichotomy, the literature presents notable complexities. For instance, detailed studies of post-starburst systems reveal a diversity of quenching pathways, including globally synchronous suppression and off-center quenching regions, suggesting that mergers and stochastic events can complicate simple radial gradients [Quai et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.490.2347Q/abstract), [Leung et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250905172L/abstract). Furthermore, there is an active tension between observations and cosmological hydrodynamical simulations regarding spatial quenching dynamics. While observations overwhelmingly find that [[green-valley-galaxies|Green Valley galaxies]] quench from the inside out, simulations such as Illustris and EAGLE have frequently predicted centrally concentrated star formation in these transition objects, erroneously implying outside-in quenching [Starkenburg et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...874L..17S/abstract). Resolving these spatial discrepancies remains a major challenge for theoretical models of sub-grid feedback and gas accretion.

## Preprocessing and the Lifecycle of Infalling Galaxies

The classical picture of [[environmental-quenching|Environmental quenching]] assumes that galaxies are stripped of their gas only upon entering the virial radius of a massive cluster. However, the hierarchical nature of [[galaxy-accretion|Galaxy accretion]] dictates that many galaxies fall into clusters not as isolated field systems, but as members of smaller groups. In these group environments, galaxies undergo a phase of "pre-processing." Studies of satellite orbits indicate that group pre-processing is responsible for up to half of the quenched satellite population observed in present-day massive clusters [Wetzel et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..336W/abstract). Cosmological simulations demonstrate that the delay between initial accretion and quenching is heavily dependent on the halo mass at this first infall, underlining the critical role of group-scale environments in the early stages of the quenching lifecycle [Gu et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020arXiv201004166G/abstract). Furthermore, this pre-processing is highly anisotropic; galaxies infalling along large-scale cosmic filaments exhibit significantly higher quenched fractions than those in isotropic infall regions, even at large distances of $R/R_{200} > 3$ [Salerno et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv221009300S/abstract). Despite the undeniable impact of these prior environments, detailed tracking in the TNG300 simulations suggests that "post-processing" remains the dominant final quenching mechanism for most satellites after they fall into their ultimate host halos [Park et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...954...98P/abstract).

The spatial extent of cluster environmental influence is further expanded by the presence of "backsplash" galaxies—systems that have passed through a cluster's pericenter and temporarily exited the virial radius on highly elliptical orbits. These galaxies experience severe [[intracluster-and-intragroup-phenomena|Intracluster and Intragroup Phenomena]], such as ram-pressure stripping, before rebounding into the cluster outskirts. Consequently, environmental quenching effects can be observed out to $\sim 2.5$ virial radii [Wetzel et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.439.2687W/abstract). Backsplash galaxies can account for up to $40\%$ of the ostensibly central galaxies located just outside of groups and clusters [Wetzel et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.439.2687W/abstract), and simulation data suggest they comprise approximately $65\%$ of the quenched population residing in the immediate circum-cluster environment [Hough et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.518.2398H/abstract). Observational searches in the local Universe have begun to isolate this population, identifying ultra-low-mass quenched systems ($M_* \sim 10^7 M_\odot$) located between $1.0$ and $2.7$ virial radii from host systems like M81, which serve as strong backsplash candidates [Casey et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.520.4715C/abstract).

A closely related manifestation of these extended environmental effects is "galactic conformity," the empirical observation that the star-forming or quenched status of neighboring galaxies is highly correlated with the state of the central galaxy. Quenched central galaxies are preferentially surrounded by quenched neighbors out to scales of $1-5$ Mpc, well beyond the formal virial radius [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract). The physical origin of galactic conformity remains a subject of active debate in the literature. Some authors argue that this "two-halo" conformity represents a smoking gun for halo assembly bias, implying that the co-evolution of galaxies and their dark matter halos fundamentally breaks the assumption that halo mass alone dictates galaxy quenching statistics [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract). Conversely, other studies contend that conformity can be entirely explained as an artifact of the standard halo mass function's dependence on large-scale density, combined with standard [[halo-quenching|Halo quenching]] prescriptions, without requiring explicit assembly bias [Zu et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.476.1637Z/abstract), [Alam et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483.4501A/abstract). Regardless of the underlying dark matter dynamics, the conformity signal underscores that the lifecycle of infalling galaxies is intricately tied to the broader assembly history of their macroscopic environment.

## Historical Development and the 'Over-Quenching' Problem

In early models of [[galaxy-formation-and-assembly|Galaxy Formation and Assembly]], it was commonly assumed that a satellite galaxy's hot gas reservoir was instantaneously and completely stripped upon accretion into a larger host halo. While this mechanism successfully suppressed star formation, detailed comparisons with local galaxy surveys revealed that these semi-analytic models predicted a drastically inflated fraction of faint, red satellite galaxies in clusters [Kimm et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009MNRAS.394.1131K/abstract). This discrepancy became known as the over-quenching problem. To reconcile models with the observed color-magnitude distribution and the [[red-sequence|Red sequence]], the timescale for gas stripping had to be extended. Earlier semi-analytic models implemented a delayed stripping timescale of $\sim 2$ Gyr, which allowed satellite galaxies to continue forming stars after infall and significantly improved agreement with observations. This physical picture was later independently derived and empirically supported by the "delayed-then-rapid" scenario for [[satellite-quenching|Satellite quenching]] [Wetzel et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.432..336W/abstract). More recent data-driven models, such as the UniverseMachine framework, have further refined this picture by demonstrating that galaxy assembly is strongly correlated with dark matter halo assembly, showing that quenched fractions at a fixed halo mass systematically decrease as redshift increases [Behroozi et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.488.3143B/abstract).

The historical development from early semi-analytic prescriptions to full cosmological hydrodynamical simulations introduced more nuanced treatments of gas flows and feedback. Modern simulation suites, such as EAGLE and IllustrisTNG, self-consistently model both [[environmental-quenching|Environmental quenching]] and internal active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback. For instance, earlier models struggled with AGN feedback efficiencies, prompting IllustrisTNG to implement a dual-mode feedback system—a thermal "quasar" mode at high accretion rates and a stochastic kinetic wind mode at low accretion rates—to successfully produce massive [[quiescent-galaxies|Quiescent galaxies]] without expelling too many baryons [Weinberger et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465.3291W/abstract). Similarly, the EAGLE simulation has successfully reproduced the observed $z=0.1$ color-magnitude distribution and the transition timescales of galaxies across the green valley [Trayford et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.460.3925T/abstract). Simulations have continued to refine how feedback affects galaxy populations. For example, the Simba simulation has successfully reproduced the observed color bimodality in the scatter of the stellar-to-halo mass relation, attributing the successful split to the combined effects of jet-mode and X-ray AGN feedback [Cui et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5.1069C/abstract).

Despite these advancements, the over-quenching problem has not been completely eradicated, and new challenges have emerged. Comparisons between observations and state-of-the-art simulations (including EAGLE, IllustrisTNG, and SIMBA) reveal that models still struggle to perfectly reproduce quenching efficiencies at the lowest masses. For example, simulations persistently fail to capture the observed absence of quiescent field galaxies below $M_* \sim 10^9 M_\odot$ [Dickey et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...53D/abstract), and IllustrisTNG tends to excessively quench low-mass companion galaxies ($M_* < 10^{9.5} M_\odot$) compared to photometric surveys [Zheng et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...969..129Z/abstract). Recently, data-driven approaches like those from the SAGA Survey have utilized updated UniverseMachine models (UM-SAGA) to effectively constrain satellite quenching efficiencies for dwarf galaxies down to stellar masses of $10^7 M_\odot$ [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240414500W/abstract). 

Furthermore, a major new tension has arisen at high redshift: recent observations by the James Webb Space Telescope have uncovered a substantial population of [[high-mass-quiescent-galaxies|High-mass quiescent galaxies]] extending up to $z \sim 5$ [de Graaff et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240405683D/abstract). Current simulations, including IllustrisTNG and ASTRID, severely underestimate the comoving number density of these early quiescent systems by up to $\sim 2$ dex [Weller et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240602664W/abstract). This discrepancy indicates that while the over-quenching problem historically dominated the low-mass satellite regime, contemporary models simultaneously suffer from an "under-quenching" problem for massive galaxies in the early Universe.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

The advent of JWST has fundamentally challenged established paradigms of galaxy evolution by uncovering a substantial population of massive [[quiescent-galaxies|Quiescent galaxies]] at $z > 3$ (and up to $z \sim 5$ [de Graaff et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240405683D/abstract)), as well as surprising low-mass systems that temporarily quenched as early as $z = 7.3$ [Looser et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.629...53L/abstract). Current cosmological simulations (such as IllustrisTNG and ASTRID) systematically underestimate the comoving number density of these early quenched systems by up to 2 dex [Weller et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240602664W/abstract). Reconciling this discrepancy requires exceptionally rapid quenching mechanisms capable of halting star formation within $\sim 700$ Myr of the Big Bang, potentially driven by intense [[compaction-events|Compaction events]] or extreme active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback [Looser et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.629...53L/abstract), [Weller et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240602664W/abstract). 

Furthermore, theoretical models predict that primordial galactic ecosystems may experience a highly stochastic star formation history characterized by [[mini-quenching-events|Mini-quenching events]]—rapid, temporary transitions between starbursts and quiescence [Looser et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024Natur.629...53L/abstract). The precise physics governing these early cycles remains unresolved. While supernova feedback appears insufficient to permanently quench early dwarf galaxies [Gelli et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964...76G/abstract), recent work proposes that cosmic rays could serve as a vital feedback agent, providing the necessary pressure to regulate star formation in relatively isolated early-universe halos [Owen et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv221206469O/abstract).

The permanence of quiescence is also increasingly debated. Both high-redshift observations and simulations suggest that quenched galaxies frequently undergo [[rejuvenation-episodes|Rejuvenation episodes]], wherein they accrete new gas or merge with gas-rich companions to temporarily re-enter the population of [[star-forming-galaxies|Star-forming galaxies]] before quenching again [Weller et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240602664W/abstract), [Harrold et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251214599H/abstract). In fact, recent "on-off-cycle" frameworks suggest that the bulk of star-forming galaxies at $z=0$ have not undergone strictly secular evolution, but rather have experienced long-term cycles of quenching and rejuvenation on the Star Formation Main Sequence [Fortun\'e et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250320858F/abstract). This cyclical evolution complicates the traditional dichotomy between positive feedback and [[negative-feedback|Negative feedback]]. For instance, while AGN-driven outflows are the canonical mechanism for massive galaxy quenching, some hydrodynamic models indicate that AGN overpressure can simultaneously trigger rapid starbursts (positive feedback) in a galaxy's dense gas prior to its ultimate expulsion or heating [Bieri et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...812L..36B/abstract). 

A related, major open question concerns the physical state of the interstellar medium in ostensibly "dead" galaxies. Observations reveal that many retired systems retain substantial molecular ($H_2$) and atomic ($HI$) gas reservoirs, yet exhibit anomalously low star formation efficiencies [Ellison et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502L...6E/abstract), [Michałowski et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...964..129M/abstract). Resolving why this cold gas fails to fragment and form stars—whether due to turbulence, magnetic fields, or morphological stabilization against gravitational collapse—remains critical to understanding quenching maintenance. 

Finally, the literature reflects ongoing tension regarding the dominant physical mechanisms for rapid gas removal. While feedback-driven winds are often invoked to clear out cold gas, recent high-redshift observations have demonstrated that tidal forces during major mergers can physically eject $\sim 50\%$ of a galaxy's molecular gas into extended tails, suppressing star formation without the need for ejective AGN winds [Puglisi et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021NatAs...5..319P/abstract), [Spilker et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...936L..11S/abstract). Quantifying the statistical prevalence of tidal gas removal versus feedback-driven outflows as a primary quenching channel will be a major focus for forthcoming large-scale spectroscopic surveys. Another emerging frontier is the study of non-local feedback phenomena, such as the "Dragon Effect." Recent studies propose that powerful AGN-driven outflows from a massive host galaxy can dynamically disrupt and suppress star formation in adjacent satellite galaxies, highlighting the importance of energetic feedback that extends well beyond a single galaxy's interstellar medium [Khoram et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250912308K/abstract).
