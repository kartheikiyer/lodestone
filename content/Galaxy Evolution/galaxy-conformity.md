## Article

In the study of galaxy formation and evolution, **galaxy conformity** refers to the empirical correlation between the physical properties of neighboring galaxies—most notably their star formation rates and colors—that persists even when controlling for the mass of their host dark matter halo. While classical theoretical frameworks, such as standard Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) models, traditionally assume that the virial mass of a dark matter halo ($M_{\rm vir}$) exclusively dictates the statistical properties of its resident galaxies, conformity demonstrates that galactic environments play a persistent, coordinated role in shaping [[galaxy-property-distributions|Galaxy property distributions]] [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract). The primary observational signature of this phenomenon is that quiescent (or passive) central galaxies are preferentially surrounded by quiescent neighbors, whereas actively star-forming centrals tend to host star-forming neighbors. 

The phenomenon is broadly categorized into two spatial regimes based on the scales over which these correlations operate: 1-halo and 2-halo conformity. **1-halo conformity**, or small-scale conformity, describes the correlated co-evolution of a central galaxy and its orbiting satellites within the virial radius of a single dark matter halo [Knobel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...800...24K/abstract). The presence of this local signal implies a shared group-wide quenching efficiency, where localized processes like gas depletion or central feedback affect the entire halo uniformly. **2-halo conformity**, conversely, extends well beyond individual virial radii. First formalized in massive spectroscopic surveys, it links the star formation states of low-mass central galaxies residing in separate, distinct dark matter halos across megaparsec scales ($1-5 \ {\rm Mpc}$) [Kauffmann et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.430.1447K/abstract). 

The discovery of large-scale 2-halo conformity has profound implications for cosmology and the physical mechanics of environmental quenching. Theoretically, it is frequently interpreted as a "smoking gun" for [[anisotropic-galaxy-assembly-bias|halo assembly bias]], suggesting that the broader cosmic web and secondary halo characteristics—such as large-scale tidal forces—can synchronize the gas accretion and subsequent quenching of widely separated galaxies [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract). However, the true physical origin of this extended signal remains a subject of intense debate. A major open question in the field is the extent to which observed 2-halo conformity is a genuine cosmological signature versus an artifact of observational systematics. Resolving these tensions, characterizing the precise mechanisms of gas physics involved, and tracking the evolution of conformity across cosmic time are primary science drivers for next-generation surveys utilizing facilities like DESI, the Roman Space Telescope, and JWST.

## Historical Development and Early Observations

The understanding that galactic properties correlate with their environment dates back to the early 20th century. As early as the 1930s, it was realized that the morphological mix of galaxies is fundamentally tied to local environmental density, a phenomenon formally known as the [[oemler-effect|morphology-density relation]]. According to classical textbook theory, denser environments, such as galaxy clusters, predominantly host early-type (elliptical and lenticular) galaxies, whereas late-type, star-forming spirals are more common in the low-density field. To explain these emerging spatial distributions, early theoretical arguments in the 1980s proposed that biasing in galaxy formation—specifically, the segregation of galaxies from the underlying mass distribution—was a physical necessity to explain the presence of large cosmic voids and superclustering [Dekel et al. (1986)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1986ComAp..11..235D/abstract).

Prior to and alongside SDSS, the 2dF Galaxy Redshift Survey played a foundational role in identifying three-dimensional cluster demographics and establishing early space distributions of galaxy systems [de Propris et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001misk.conf..136D/abstract). The subsequent advent of massive spectroscopic surveys, most notably the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), allowed for robust, quantitative measurements of these environmental trends. Early analyses of SDSS Data Release 5 established that while galaxy luminosity and size are largely independent of environment, color and morphology are strongly tied to the properties of member galaxies within group catalogs [Deng et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007A&A...474..783D/abstract). Furthermore, clustering studies using SDSS DR6 data confirmed that redder galaxies preferentially inhabit dense groups and clusters compared to their bluer counterparts [Deng et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009PASP..121..231D/abstract), though complementary studies of volume-limited samples revealed that a galaxy's morphological type depends even more strongly on its local three-dimensional environment than its color [Deng et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ChJAA...7..639D/abstract). Data from SDSS and visual classification projects, particularly the primary data release of morphological classifications for nearly 900,000 galaxies by Galaxy Zoo [Lintott et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.410..166L/abstract) alongside foundational analyses [Lintott et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.389.1179L/abstract), firmly established that galaxies generally exhibit pronounced color and morphological bimodalities. This divides the broader [[galaxy-property-distributions|Galaxy property distributions]] into a star-forming "blue cloud" and a quiescent "red sequence." Initial models of galaxy formation largely attributed this quenching of star formation to the mass of the host dark matter halo or the immediate local overdensity, assuming that the dark matter halo mass, $M_{\rm vir}$, exclusively governs galaxy occupation statistics.

However, detailed analyses of SDSS group catalogs revealed that the morphology-density relation and halo mass alone could not fully account for the observed clustering of galaxy properties. Studies began to demonstrate that at a fixed halo mass, the properties of satellite galaxies are strongly correlated with those of their central galaxy [Yang et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ApJ...638L..55Y/abstract). For example, early-type satellites are significantly more abundant in groups hosting an early-type central galaxy than in groups with a late-type central [Wang et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...718..762W/abstract). This tendency for neighboring galaxies to mirror the star formation state and morphology of their primary galaxy was formally dubbed "galaxy conformity." 

A watershed moment in the quantification of this effect came with the work of Kauffmann et al. [Kauffmann et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.430.1447K/abstract). Utilizing a volume-limited sample of galaxies at $z < 0.03$ from SDSS Data Release 7, Kauffmann et al. systematically re-examined the phenomenon. They demonstrated that conformity exists across a central galaxy stellar mass range spanning two orders of magnitude. Crucially, they found that for low-mass central galaxies ($M_* < 10^{10} M_\odot$), this conformity signal extends out to physical scales in excess of $4 \ {\rm Mpc}$—well beyond the virial radii of their host dark matter halos. This finding formally bifurcated the phenomenon into "1-halo" and "2-halo" conformity, representing a significant paradigm shift. It demonstrated a clear violation of the standard assumption that environmental effects are strictly local to individual halos, suggesting instead that large-scale cosmic environments play an ongoing role in galaxy quenching.

## 1-Halo Conformity: Central-Satellite Co-evolution

In the framework of galaxy evolution, 1-halo galactic conformity refers to the empirical correlation between the physical properties of a central galaxy and its constituent satellite galaxies within the same dark matter halo. At a fixed halo mass, satellites orbiting a quiescent central galaxy are significantly more likely to be quenched than satellites orbiting a star-forming central [Wang et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...718..762W/abstract), [Knobel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...800...24K/abstract). This phenomenon indicates that the star-formation state of the central galaxy is a critical predictor of the broader [[galaxy-property-distributions|Galaxy property distributions]] within the group. 

Furthermore, conformity manifests differently depending on the distinct environment being studied. Observations show that morphological conformity is generally dominant in multi-member galaxy groups, while emission-class (star-formation) conformity is the dominant correlation seen in isolated galaxy pairs [Robotham et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.431..167R/abstract). Recent photometric studies of isolated galaxy pairs in the S-PLUS survey confirm the presence of galactic conformity, finding that the red pair fraction increases significantly in systems with similar luminosities [Cerdosino et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.528.4993C/abstract). Interestingly, small-scale color conformity observed within massive cluster environments, such as the Virgo Cluster, can be interpreted as a vestige of infallen groups. The conformity signal remains intact in the intermediate regions of the cluster but is gradually lost as the constituent galaxies mix dynamically and move toward the cluster center [Lee et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...823...73L/abstract).

Observational evidence confirms that 1-halo conformity is not exclusively a low-redshift phenomenon. The correlation between central and satellite star-formation states is firmly established at $z \sim 0$ and persists with high statistical significance out to redshifts of $z \sim 2$ [Hartley et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.1613H/abstract), [Kawinwanichakij et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...817....9K/abstract). The presence of this signal over approximately 10 billion years of cosmic history suggests that the physical mechanisms governing central-satellite co-evolution operate relatively independently of the global decline in the cosmic star formation rate.

While conformity was initially identified via broadband colors and star formation rates (SFR), it also extends to galactic structure. However, morphological conformity—such as the tendency for early-type, elliptical satellites to cluster around early-type centrals—manifests more weakly than SFR conformity across most regimes [Otter et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.492.2722O/abstract). This discrepancy implies that star formation properties are susceptible to relatively rapid environmental suppression, whereas gross morphological transformations require longer secular timescales to fully develop, reflecting the fact that galaxy morphology is fundamentally a direct product of interaction histories and prolonged environmental perturbations over cosmic time [Buta et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011arXiv1102.0550B/abstract). Furthermore, small-scale dynamical coherence, in which satellite galaxy rotation aligns with neighbor motion, is often found to accompany color conformity, hinting that shared interaction histories or common gas accretion kinematics play a role [Lee et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...893..154L/abstract).

Physically, the 1-halo conformity signal strongly implies that traditional models of isolated satellite quenching are incomplete; instead, the phenomenon is better described by a shared group quenching efficiency [Knobel et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...800...24K/abstract), [Pahwa et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.470.1298P/abstract). Rather than satellites being quenched solely by post-infall processes (such as ram-pressure stripping) while the central galaxy remains unaffected, both centrals and satellites appear subject to a unified regulatory mechanism. Proposed drivers include preventative feedback—such as active galactic nucleus (AGN) outbursts from the central galaxy—that uniformly heats the intragroup medium and suppresses star formation across the entire halo [Hartley et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.1613H/abstract). However, observational measurements of this phenomenon are highly sensitive to systematic errors in group-finding algorithms, requiring careful calibration (see *Methodological Challenges and Systematic Biases*).

## 2-Halo Conformity and Assembly Bias

While 1-halo conformity describes the local correlation between central and satellite galaxies within a single dark matter halo, "2-halo conformity" refers to the large-scale correlation of properties across separate, distinct halos. Observations indicate that this conformity signal extends well beyond the virial radius of individual halos, typically spanning scales of $r \sim 1-5 \ {\rm Mpc}$ [Kauffmann et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.430.1447K/abstract), [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract) and potentially persisting out to spatial separations of $\sim 40 \ {\rm Mpc}$ [Kerscher et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018A&A...615A.109K/abstract). This long-range correlation is most pronounced for low-mass central galaxies ($M_* < 10^{10} M_\odot$) located in the vicinity of massive groups and clusters ($M_{\rm 200c} \ge 10^{13} h^{-1} M_\odot$), where the low-mass centrals are preferentially quenched compared to their isolated counterparts [Kauffmann et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.430.1447K/abstract), [Lacerna et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.513.2271L/abstract). Studies indicate that in massive haloes ($M_h > 10^{13} M_\odot$) where centrals are fully quenched, the concept of conformity becomes meaningless, and the apparent conformity signal instead arises when these massive systems are mixed observationally with lower-mass haloes ($M_h < 10^{13} M_\odot$) where centrals and satellites exhibit similar quenching excesses [Treyer et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.477.2684T/abstract).

The existence of 2-halo conformity presents a direct challenge to the standard Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) framework, which traditionally assumes that the virial mass of a dark matter halo ($M_{\rm vir}$) exclusively governs its galaxy occupation statistics. Because the star formation state of neighboring central galaxies is correlated even at fixed halo mass, large-scale conformity is frequently interpreted as a "smoking gun" for [[anisotropic-galaxy-assembly-bias|central galaxy assembly bias]]. In this paradigm, secondary halo properties tied to a halo's assembly history or its location within the large-scale environment strongly influence the quenching of its resident galaxies. For instance, empirical "age matching" techniques, which assign galaxy color based on halo age at a fixed stellar mass, successfully reproduce conformity signals by preferentially placing the reddest, quenched galaxies in the oldest halos [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract), [Bray et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.455..185B/abstract). Related to this framework is the concept of "assembly conformity," which demonstrates that fossil groups assemble their halo and stellar mass earlier, and are more stellar-deficient, than normal groups of the exact same mass [Li et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...898...39L/abstract).

At megaparsec scales, the geometric environment of the cosmic web plays a critical role. Red galaxy fractions depend more strongly on local environmental measures than on large-scale geometric environments, but the geometric environment exhibits the largest residual effect in cosmic voids [Bhambhani et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.522.4116B/abstract). The suppression of star formation is strongly correlated with large-scale tidal environments and proximity to cosmic web filaments and nodes [Wang et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.4616W/abstract), [Nandi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250911288N/abstract). Supporting this connection, there is direct evidence for halo assembly bias tied to geometric environments, explicitly showing that low-mass haloes residing in knots (nodes) are older than those of the same mass located in voids [Tojeiro et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.470.3720T/abstract). Theoretical models suggest that pairs of dark matter halos can exhibit strongly correlated mass accretion rates ($\mathrm{d}M_{\rm vir}/\mathrm{d}t$) even when separated by more than 30 times their virial radii. This long-range coherence implies that neighboring halos residing in the same large-scale tidal environment experience synchronized regulation of their growth [Hearin et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.461.2135H/abstract). Modern simulations reveal a direct correlation between the amplitude of measured 2-halo conformity and the overall strength of the assembly bias signal across different galaxy samples [Lacerna et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250503880L/abstract). Further modeling indicates that while internal halo properties like age and concentration can account for $20-30\%$ of the assembly bias signal, broader environmental measures, such as smoothed matter density or tidal anisotropy, can account for the full effect [Xu et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502.3242X/abstract) (see [[anisotropic-galaxy-assembly-bias|Anisotropic galaxy assembly bias]]).

The coordination of quenching across these distances also requires physical mechanisms capable of regulating the cold gas supply. Initial observations of 21-cm emission suggested a significant conformity signal in the neutral hydrogen (HI) mass fractions of galaxies extending out to $5 \ {\rm Mpc}$ [Li et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021RAA....21...32L/abstract). However, when the sample in that study was strictly confined to central galaxies, this 2-halo signal was reduced to a very weak amplitude. The authors concluded that the apparent 5 Mpc correlation was primarily caused by sample selection bias rather than a genuine physical effect [Li et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021RAA....21...32L/abstract). Nonetheless, for high-mass, gas-rich central galaxies, small-scale conformity likely traces ongoing, coherent gas accretion from the cosmic web. Conversely, genuine large-scale conformity observed around low-mass, gas-poor centrals may be a thermodynamic relic of intergalactic gas "pre-heating" at high redshift, which raised the entropy of the intergalactic medium and suppressed subsequent gas infall [Kauffmann et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.430.1447K/abstract). The exact mode of gas removal remains debated, but evidence suggests it is primarily driven by the halting of fresh pristine gas inflows (strangulation) rather than immediate dynamical stripping [Hatfield et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.472.3570H/abstract), supported by the presence of fading discs and ring structures in green valley galaxies [Smith et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.517.4575S/abstract).

Despite these robust theoretical frameworks, the true physical origin of large-scale conformity remains intensely debated. Alternative explanations suggest that much of the observed signal may arise from observational systematics, the presence of ejected "backsplash" galaxies, or natural variations in the halo mass function (see *Methodological Challenges and Systematic Biases*).

## Theoretical Modeling and Simulations

Traditional HOD frameworks assume that quenching statistics can be accurately predicted by halo mass alone. To replicate conformity, theoretical models and simulations must incorporate secondary halo properties—a manifestation of [[anisotropic-galaxy-assembly-bias|halo assembly bias]]—or resolve broader environmental gas-stripping mechanisms.

**Semi-Analytic Models (SAMs)**
Semi-analytic models have been widely used to dissect the physical origins of conformity, though their predictions vary significantly depending on their treatment of satellite trajectories and environmental quenching. Early comparisons using the Guo et al. SAM suggested that conformity-like effects arose primarily from a systematic error where gas-poor satellite galaxies were misclassified as centrals, though the predicted signal was much weaker than observed in survey data [Kauffmann et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.430.1447K/abstract). More recent iterations of models like L-GALAXIES and GALFORM demonstrate that the 2-halo conformity signal is highly sensitive to the treatment of "orphan" or "ejected" backsplash galaxies [Lacerna et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.475.1177L/abstract). Models that explicitly track the ram-pressure stripping of gas reservoirs in group outskirts successfully reproduce conformity out to scales of $5 \text{ Mpc}$ for low- and intermediate-mass centrals [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.1913A/abstract). 

**Hydrodynamical Simulations and Mock Catalogues**
Modern cosmological hydrodynamical simulations naturally produce conformity signals and offer physical frameworks for interpreting them. In the Illustris simulation, a clear conformity signal emerges that can be empirically modeled by combining standard abundance matching with "age matching." In this paradigm, at a fixed stellar mass, the reddest galaxies preferentially inhabit the oldest dark matter halos [Bray et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.455..185B/abstract). 

In IllustrisTNG, studies of low-mass central galaxies reveal that those residing within $\sim 5 \ h^{-1}\text{Mpc}$ of massive groups or clusters are preferentially quenched compared to isolated counterparts [Lacerna et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.513.2271L/abstract). A substantial fraction of this 2-halo signal is attributed to backsplash galaxies that have passed through a massive primary halo [Wang et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.523.1268W/abstract). Other simulations, such as MUFASA, show that strong conformity is a late-time phenomenon ($z \lesssim 1$) that emerges primarily once halos grow above a characteristic quenching mass scale of $\sim 10^{12} \text{ M}_\odot$ [Rafieferantsoa et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.475..955R/abstract). The MillenniumTNG project has further demonstrated that explaining the scale-dependent clustering of star-forming emission-line galaxies (ELGs) out to $r \sim 20 \ h^{-1}\text{Mpc}$ requires incorporating environmental shear rather than intrinsic halo properties alone [Hadzhiyska et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.524.2507H/abstract). To rigorously test these physical connections, multiwavelength mock catalogues of the low-redshift Universe have been developed that seamlessly integrate simulated HI content and 'baryonified' host halo profiles to search for subtle signals of galaxy assembly bias and conformity [Paranjape et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.503.4147P/abstract). However, matching simulations directly to observations remains complex: studies have found that galaxy assembly bias depends strongly on selection criteria and number density, indicating that no single secondary halo property can fully replicate the complex signal predicted by advanced hydrodynamical simulations [García-Moreno et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250406770G/abstract).

**Extended HODs**
To bridge the gap between simulations and observational data, extended HOD frameworks explicitly parameterize conformity. Introducing a "group quenching efficiency," $\rho$, correlates the colors of galaxies within a group with the concentration or formation time of their parent halo, successfully recovering 1-halo conformity [Paranjape et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.454.3030P/abstract), [Pahwa et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.470.1298P/abstract). Alternatively, secondary parameters like smoothed matter density or tidal anisotropy can account for the entirety of the [[anisotropic-galaxy-assembly-bias|Anisotropic galaxy assembly bias]] signal [Xu et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.502.3242X/abstract). However, observational evidence from SDSS-DR18 suggests that the large-scale cosmic web environment has only a weak overall influence on the standard HOD. This indicates that detecting definitive secondary dependencies often requires highly multivariate approaches rather than simple structural metrics [Izzo Dominguez et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250909800I/abstract). Significant advances have been made in this direction; researchers have developed improved HOD prescriptions designed specifically for emission-line galaxies (ELGs) that systematically incorporate conformity and modified radial satellite profiles to more accurately replicate small-scale clustering observations [Reyes-Peraza et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.3877R/abstract).

## Evolution across Cosmic Time

The evolution of galactic conformity over cosmic time provides critical constraints on when and how environmental quenching mechanisms became effective. While the phenomenon is well-established in the local universe, recent observational and theoretical efforts have focused on characterizing its [[butcher-oemler-effect|redshift evolution]] out to the epoch of peak star formation and beyond.

**Intermediate Redshifts ($0.5 < z < 2.5$)**
Observational studies indicate that 1-halo conformity—where the star-formation state of satellite galaxies mirrors that of their central host—is not exclusively a late-time feature of the universe. Near-infrared surveys have demonstrated that the tendency for passive satellites to preferentially orbit passive central galaxies persists to at least $z \sim 2$, with little evidence for a strong redshift-dependent evolution in the strength of the correlation [Hartley et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.1613H/abstract). Measurements of satellite quenching efficiencies out to $z < 2.5$ confirm this picture; the conformity signal remains statistically significant ($\gtrsim 3\sigma$) in the redshift bin $0.6 < z < 1.6$, confirming that the physical mechanisms driving synchronized group quenching have been active for billions of years [Kawinwanichakij et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...817....9K/abstract). Morphological conformity has similarly been detected in group environments out to $z \sim 1.0$ [Hwang et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...700..791H/abstract), [Wang et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...718..762W/abstract).

However, the literature contains conflicting evidence regarding the theoretical mechanisms driving this high-redshift signal. Some models, which attribute large-scale (2-halo) conformity to the tidal environment regulating halo mass accretion rates, predict that conformity should drop to undetectable levels by $z \gtrsim 1$ [Hearin et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.461.2135H/abstract). Additionally, cross-correlation analyses suggest that while environmental quenching is a dominant force by $z \sim 0.5$, it plays a negligible role at $z \sim 2$ [Hatfield et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.472.3570H/abstract). This potential decoupling of group quenching from large-scale density at high redshift complicates physical interpretations of the conformity signal.

**The JWST Era and High-Redshift Quiescent Galaxies ($z > 3$)**
The advent of JWST has pushed the study of environmental effects into the early universe. Recent observations of massive ($M_* \geq 10^{10} M_\odot$) quiescent systems at $3 \leq z_{\text{phot}} < 5$ reveal a breakdown of the classical conformity paradigm. Unlike in the local universe, these early quiescent centrals are frequently surrounded by substantial populations of star-forming neighbors, explicitly contrasting the expected conformity effect [Binh et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251214881B/abstract). The diverse clustering environments of these objects suggest that the earliest episodes of galaxy quenching were likely driven by internal processes rather than group-wide environmental effects. Conversely, extremely large-volume hydrodynamical simulations modeling JWST-like surveys indicate that a footprint-confined large-scale conformity in star-formation efficiency may still manifest at $z \simeq 5$ due to cosmic variance, modulating galaxy properties coherently over scales up to $\sim 50 \text{ cMpc}$ [Lim et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251109618L/abstract).

**Probing Conformity via Lookback Time**
In addition to deep extragalactic surveys, conformity can be traced across cosmic time using the resolved star formation histories of galaxies in the Local Volume. By calculating the evolution of the star formation rate and quenched fraction as a function of lookback time, researchers have reconstructed a 2-halo conformity signal extending out to $1 \text{ Gyr}$ ago. This demonstrates a correlation in the quenched fractions of neighboring galaxies separated by $2\text{--}3 \text{ Mpc}$ [Olsen et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...943...30O/abstract). Interestingly, within our own Local Group, it has been noted that both the Milky Way and M31 appear to be in a 'green valley' transition state, likely undergoing a critical transformation of their global properties before their star formation is fully extinguished [Mutch et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...736...84M/abstract). This novel temporal approach bridges the gap between spatial clustering and evolution, providing an independent metric for testing how environmental correlations assemble over time.

## Methodological Challenges and Systematic Biases

Observational measurements of galactic conformity depend critically on the accurate identification of dark matter halos and the correct assignment of central and satellite designations. Because these halo properties cannot be observed directly, empirical studies rely on group-finding algorithms applied to redshift surveys. These algorithms, alongside physical projection effects, are prone to systematic errors that can artificially generate or amplify conformity signals, leading to active debates in the literature over whether observed correlations are physical or methodological.

**Group Finder Bias and Spurious 1-Halo Conformity**
Group-finding algorithms typically rely on spatial proximity and abundance matching, which introduce correlated errors in group membership, central-satellite designation, and halo mass assignment. These systematic errors tend to artificially homogenize [[galaxy-property-distributions|Galaxy property distributions]] within identified groups [Campbell et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452..444C/abstract). Consequently, group finders exhibit an inherent tendency to introduce weak conformity even when none is present in the underlying galaxy population. When standard group-finding algorithms are applied to mock catalogs explicitly constructed with zero intrinsic conformity, the resulting "observed" groups still exhibit strong 1-halo conformity in their quenched fractions [Calderon et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.480.2031C/abstract). This suggests that many literature claims of 1-halo conformity based on quenched fractions may be spurious signals driven entirely by algorithmic systematics rather than physical "group quenching." To mitigate these effects, statistical frameworks such as the Halo Transition Probability (HTP) have been proposed to quantify and correct mass assignment and classification errors [Campbell et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452..444C/abstract).

**Backsplash Galaxies and the 2-Halo Debate**
At larger scales, 2-halo conformity measurements are particularly vulnerability to the misclassification of "ejected" or "backsplash" galaxies. These are former satellite galaxies that have passed through the virial radius of a massive host—experiencing environmental quenching in the process—and subsequently exited the halo to reside in the surrounding field. Because group finders classify them as isolated centrals, they carry the quenched signature of their former host into the low-density field.

Several studies using hydrodynamical simulations and semi-analytic models argue that the apparent 2-halo conformity of low-mass centrals ($M_* < 10^{10} M_\odot$) near massive groups is almost entirely explained by backsplash and fly-by populations [Wang et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.523.1268W/abstract), [Ayromlou et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.519.1913A/abstract). Removing these ejected satellites from models drastically reduces the measured 2-halo conformity signal [Lacerna et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.475.1177L/abstract). Conversely, other analytical frameworks argue that backsplash galaxies alone are mathematically insufficient to produce robust conformity signals at large projected separations of $1 - 5 \, \mathrm{Mpc}$. According to these models, the persistence of the signal at such distances necessitates a genuine physical correlation driven by large-scale structure [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract).

**Environmental Density and the Halo Mass Function**
Another prominent theoretical alternative argues that the 2-halo conformity signal does not require any new assembly bias physics at all. In this framework, the apparent large-scale correlation is driven entirely by the natural variation of the halo mass function with large-scale environmental density. Because denser regions host proportionally more massive halos—which are inherently more effective at quenching their galaxies—the spatial clustering of these halos indirectly creates a conformity signal. Semi-analytic models and HOD frameworks incorporating this indirect environmental effect, coupled with standard halo mass-driven quenching, can successfully replicate the observed environmental dependence of galaxy colors without explicitly linking galaxy properties to secondary halo characteristics [Zu et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.476.1637Z/abstract), [Sun et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.477.3136S/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

Despite robust observational detections out to scales of several Megaparsecs, a central open question remains whether large-scale conformity is a genuine signature of [[anisotropic-galaxy-assembly-bias|Anisotropic galaxy assembly bias]] and dark matter halo co-evolution [Hearin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.452.1958H/abstract), or an artifact of systematic observational and theoretical effects. To disentangle this unresolved physics, researchers are moving beyond basic halo mass and local density models to identify the specific "hidden variables" that drive conformity. This is often framed in terms of the residual quenching statistic, $\Delta$, which isolates variations in star formation that cannot be attributed to standard environmental metrics [Sin et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.488..234S/abstract). 

To robustly quantify these multivariate dependencies, recent theoretical work has increasingly employed information-theoretic metrics. For example, Mutual Information frameworks have been utilized to extract statistically significant, non-linear correlations between the cosmic web environment and galaxy properties such as color and specific star formation rate [Sarkar et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022JCAP...03..024S/abstract), [Nandi et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250911288N/abstract). Similarly, Mutual Information has proven sensitive to the coupling between galaxy formation efficiency and the assembly time of the host dark matter halo [Camargo et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.tmp..248C/abstract). Looking further into the mechanisms tying local galactic properties to these broader environments, studies show that the internal structure of galaxies, such as the number of spiral arms, actively correlates with star formation efficiency, where a higher number of arms frequently indicates lower overall star formation efficiency [Porter-Temple et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.515.3875P/abstract).

Accurately modeling these environmental effects is also a critical systematic requirement for precision cosmology. Emission line galaxies (ELGs) are the primary tracers of large-scale structure for the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) at $z > 0.8$. Recent analyses of DESI early data reveal that ELGs exhibit a strong conformity effect, likely driven by merger-induced starbursts and tidal interactions between central-satellite pairs [Yuan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.538.1216Y/abstract). Standard Halo Occupation Distribution (HOD) models consistently fail to reproduce the small-scale clustering of ELGs unless conformity is explicitly incorporated into the ELG-halo connection [Gao et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...961...74G/abstract). Similarly, multitracer HOD frameworks applied to eBOSS data ($z \sim 0.8-1.1$) have detected 1-halo conformity at $>3\sigma$ significance while finding that halo mass-driven quenching fails to describe the high quenching efficiency observed in low-density filaments [Alam et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.497..581A/abstract).

Future progress will rely on expanding the observational baseline across broader redshift ranges and lower stellar mass limits. Next-generation facilities such as the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, and JWST will be transformative in this regard. By reconstructing spatially resolved star formation histories for galaxies in the Local Volume and beyond, these observatories will allow direct spatiotemporal tests of conformity within our past light cone [Olsen et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...943...30O/abstract). Furthermore, deep observations of $z > 3$ quiescent galaxies by JWST are already testing whether conformity is a persistent, redshift-invariant feature of galaxy formation, or if the earliest cosmic environments exhibit fundamentally different clustering and quenching paradigms [Binh et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251214881B/abstract).
