**Patchy reionization** describes the highly structured, spatially non-uniform phase transition of the intergalactic medium (IGM) from a neutral to a fully ionized state during the cosmic dawn. Driven by the emergence of the first luminous sources, this epoch is characterized by an "inside-out" topology where discrete, ionized H II bubbles form around overdense regions of space. As the ionizing ultraviolet background builds, these isolated bubbles grow and eventually merge in a complex percolation process, culminating in the completion of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] across the universe [Furlanetto et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...613...16F/abstract), [Iliev et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006MNRAS.369.1625I/abstract). This modern paradigm starkly contrasts with early theoretical models that approximated the transition as a sudden, uniform [[global-reionization|Global reionization]], demonstrating instead that the patchiness is an intrinsic, scale-dependent feature dictated by the underlying clustering of dark matter halos and the filamentary cosmic web [Barkana et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...609..474B/abstract).

The physical progression of patchy reionization is governed by a complex competition between the emission of ionizing photons and their consumption by the IGM. Star-forming galaxies are overwhelmingly favored as the primary ionizing sources, though there is active debate regarding whether faint, low-mass dwarf galaxies ("democratic" models) or massive, UV-bright systems ("oligarchic" models) dominate the photon budget. The earliest generations of metal-free Population III stars may have even triggered an early episode of [[partial-reionization|Partial reionization]] before the bulk of the galaxy population emerged [Benson et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006MNRAS.369.1055B/abstract), [Visbal et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.453.4456V/abstract). As radiation escapes these host halos—a process quantified by the highly uncertain escape fraction, $f_{\rm esc}$—it encounters small-scale dense structures like minihalos and Lyman-limit systems that act as radiation "sinks." The interplay between the ionizing radiation and the IGM also triggers crucial self-regulating feedback loops. Notably, photoheating raises the local gas temperature, elevating the Jeans mass and suppressing subsequent gas accretion and star formation in low-mass halos. 

Observing the morphology and timeline of patchy reionization is a primary frontier in modern cosmology, relying on a diverse array of complementary multi-wavelength probes. The redshifted 21-cm line of neutral hydrogen serves as the premier tool for mapping the bubble size distribution and power spectrum, while the expanding ionization fronts leave indelible secondary imprints on the cosmic microwave background via the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect. At the tail end of the epoch, absorption features in high-redshift quasar spectra, such as the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest and Gunn-Peterson troughs, provide tight constraints on the final overlap phase, strongly favoring a late completion at $z \approx 5.5$ rather than an extended [[delayed-reionization|Delayed reionization]] scenario [Qin et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.506.2390Q/abstract), [Cain et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531.1951C/abstract). However, recent James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) discoveries of numerous early, UV-luminous galaxies have introduced a major observational tension known as the "photon budget crisis." Reconciling the excess ionizing photons produced by these high-redshift galaxies with the late end required by quasar observations remains a central open question, underscoring the need for upcoming 21-cm interferometers like the Square Kilometre Array (SKA) to directly map the topological history of the reionization era.

## Morphology and Topology of Inhomogeneous Ionization

The spatial distribution of ionized hydrogen during the epoch of reionization is characterized by a complex, evolving topology. Rather than proceeding as a uniform phase transition, the process is highly patchy, driven by the clustering of ionizing sources. The consensus in the literature strongly supports an "inside-out" model of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]], wherein high-density regions containing highly biased galaxy overdensities are ionized first, while large-scale cosmic voids remain neutral until the later stages of the epoch [Iliev et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006MNRAS.369.1625I/abstract). This theoretical picture has been increasingly corroborated by observations, such as the ASPIRE survey, which revealed a significant enhancement of intergalactic Ly$\alpha$ transmission around $z > 6.5$ [O III] emitters, confirming that reionization occurs earlier in the vicinity of biased halos [Jin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...93J/abstract). Additionally, the initial spatial distribution of early ionizing sources is modulated by supersonic relative velocities between dark matter and baryons originating at the epoch of recombination. This relative velocity effect can suppress the abundance of the earliest star-forming halos and introduces a significant scale-dependent bias into the morphology of the first ionized structures [Tseliakhovich et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010PhRvD..82h3520T/abstract).

At more local scales, constrained simulations suggest that the reionization of the Local Group depends strongly on source efficiency: efficient sources lead to external reionization by nearby clusters, while inefficient sources result in self-reionization [Iliev et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.413.2093I/abstract). Furthermore, the inhomogeneous reionization time of present-day galaxies varies by mass; for example, the progenitors of Milky Way-sized galaxies typically reionized between $z=8$ and $15$, generally earlier than the global cosmic average [Aubert et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...856L..22A/abstract).

The topology of the ionized intergalactic medium (IGM) transitions through three primary stages: a pre-overlap phase dominated by isolated H II bubbles, an intermediate percolation phase where bubbles rapidly merge, and a final phase dominated by a single, highly-connected ionized region that consumes the remaining neutral islands [Elbers et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.486.1523E/abstract). Advanced topological analyses, such as those utilizing persistent homology, reveal that following the overlap/percolation phase, the topology transitions to a network of neutral filaments and then to isolated patches of neutral hydrogen undergoing outside-in ionization [Elbers et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.486.1523E/abstract). Remarkably, large-scale radiation-hydrodynamics simulations suggest that the single largest percolated bubble can emerge quite early, around $z \approx 9-10$, long before [[global-reionization|Global reionization]] is completed [Jamieson et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241108943J/abstract).

Quantifying the bubble size distribution is critical for predicting 21-cm power spectra and Ly$\alpha$ emitter visibility. Prior to overlap, the characteristic size $R$ of an H II region correlates strongly with the ultraviolet luminosity of its brightest central galaxy. Fits from the ASTRID-ES simulations describe this relationship as:
$$ \log(R) = -0.314 M_{\rm UV} - 2.550 \log(1+z) + 7.408 $$
with a scatter of $\sigma_R < 0.15$ dex [Davies et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.525.2553D/abstract). Even at high redshifts, Poisson fluctuations in the galaxy distribution significantly impact early bubble size distributions, ensuring that a large fraction of the ionized volume already resides in multi-source bubbles early in the process [Barkana et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.391..727B/abstract).

But measuring and predicting the bubble size distribution during the percolation phase remains a subject of active debate. Standard analytic excursion-set models have historically been used to predict H II region sizes, but recent work demonstrates that these models may fail to capture the true scale of percolating structures, underestimating bubble volumes by factors of 10 to 1000 when compared to full radiative transfer simulations [Lin et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.461.3361L/abstract). To rigorously define and measure bubble sizes in simulations, researchers have adopted the watershed algorithm—a technique traditionally used for void-finding in large-scale structure. Application of the watershed algorithm reveals that the true bubble size probability density function (PDF) is narrower, more sharply peaked, and evolves more slowly than analytic predictions suggest, largely because simple analytic models inadequately treat the complex geometry of bubble mergers [Lin et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.461.3361L/abstract).

Finally, the morphology of individual H II regions is influenced by local source clustering. Rather than exhibiting the sharp Strömgren boundaries predicted for isolated sources, the clustered nature of galaxies in overdensities creates smooth boundaries. At the edges of these complexes, the neutral fraction rises gradually towards the global background value over a distance spanning 2 to 5 times the inner radius of the H II region [Wyithe et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.374..960W/abstract).

## Sources of Ionizing Radiation: Stars vs. Quasars

The epoch of cosmic reionization is driven by the buildup of the metagalactic UV background, which transitioned the intergalactic medium from a neutral to an ionized state. The total ionizing emissivity, $\dot{N}_{\rm ion}$, requires a sufficient budget of Lyman-continuum photons escaping their host halos. Establishing the relative contributions of Active Galactic Nuclei (AGNs), star-forming galaxies, and Population III stars remains a central challenge in understanding [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]].

**Active Galactic Nuclei and Quasars**
While quasars dominate the ionizing UV background at lower redshifts ($z < 3$), the AGN luminosity function drops precipitously at $z > 6$. Current consensus indicates that AGNs are sub-dominant during the bulk of the reionization epoch, contributing only $\sim 1-10\%$ of the total ionizing photon budget at $z \approx 6$ [Trebitsch et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.518.3576T/abstract). Furthermore, AGN feedback in early dwarf galaxies does not appear to significantly enhance the leakage of ionizing radiation into the intergalactic medium [Trebitsch et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.478.5607T/abstract). Consequently, star-forming galaxies are overwhelmingly favored as the primary drivers of hydrogen reionization. Note that while galaxies dominate, the spectral shape of the combined radiation field from galaxies and quasars controls the thickness and sharpness of ionization fronts [Kakiichi et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.468.3718K/abstract). Furthermore, quasars may also contribute indirectly by driving galactic winds that boost the stellar ionizing photon escape fraction for several dynamical times [Seiler et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.480L..33S/abstract). Alternatively, miniquasars powered by the intermediate-mass black hole remnants of the first stars could have contributed significantly to reionization, potentially preheating the IGM more uniformly than stellar sources [Madau et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...604..484M/abstract).

**Star-Forming Galaxies and the Escape Fraction**
The galactic contribution to the ionizing budget depends critically on the ionizing efficiency, $\xi_{\rm ion}$, and the escape fraction, $f_{\rm esc}$. The literature is currently divided on which galaxy population provides the bulk of these escaping photons. 

In the traditional "democratic" reionization scenario, faint, low-mass dwarf galaxies ($M_{\rm UV} > -15$) dominate the photon budget. Their shallow gravitational potential wells result in low gas column densities that allow ionizing radiation to easily escape, theoretically yielding high escape fractions [Paardekooper et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.2544P/abstract). Bayesian inference using the MERAXES model further supports this, suggesting high escape fractions of $\sim 18\%$ for low-mass halos ($M < 10^9 M_\odot$) and lower values ($\sim 5\%$) for massive systems [Mutch et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.527.7924M/abstract).

Conversely, recent "oligarchic" models emphasize the role of massive, UV-bright galaxies. In these models, strong, bursty star formation with high surface densities ($\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$) punches optically thin channels through the interstellar medium (ISM), a process that becomes significantly more efficient in brighter galaxies at $z > 6$ [Sharma et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.458L..94S/abstract). This mechanism allows $f_{\rm esc}$ to scale positively with $\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$, potentially enabling less than $5\%$ of massive galaxies to account for $>80\%$ of the reionization budget [Naidu et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...892..109N/abstract). Moreover, the morphological structure of these galaxies—often shaped by bursty supernova feedback—plays a crucial role; dispersion-dominated systems are found to correlate with significantly higher ionizing escape fractions compared to stable, rotation-dominated disks [Bhagwat et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531.3406B/abstract). Reconciling these viewpoints, the THESAN simulations indicate a temporal shift in the photon budget drivers, where low-mass galaxies dominate at $z > 7$, while more massive halos provide the bulk of escaped radiation at lower redshifts [Yeh et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.520.2757Y/abstract). The stellar population models utilized also play a critical role; simulations incorporating binary star evolution show that binaries can increase the escape fraction by a factor of $\sim 3$ and maintain a high ionizing luminosity for longer durations than single-star models, significantly accelerating the reionization timeline [Rosdahl et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.479..994R/abstract). Additionally, the formation of proto-globular clusters may provide a significant sub-dominant contribution to the photon budget, with escape fractions comparable to the overall cluster formation efficiency [Ma et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.504.4062M/abstract). Observationally, spatially resolved Mg II emission has emerged as a promising indirect tracer for the escape fraction of ionizing photons, as it tightly correlates with neutral gas column densities [Chisholm et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.498.2554C/abstract).

**Population III Stars**
The first generation of metal-free stars (Population III) forming in minihalos undoubtedly provided the earliest ionizing photons. While they may have driven an early epoch of [[partial-reionization|Partial reionization]], constraints on the electron scattering optical depth from the cosmic microwave background severely restrict their total contribution. To avoid violating these limits, Pop III star formation efficiencies must generally be low, rendering them a sub-dominant component of the global ionizing budget across cosmic time [Visbal et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.453.4456V/abstract). However, a proposed "flash" scenario suggests that an early generation of supermassive Pop III.1 stars could have driven a rapid, significant ionization event at $z \sim 20-30$, potentially accounting for a substantial fraction ($\tau \sim 0.04$) of the early optical depth before their rapid demise [Tan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250618490T/abstract).

**The JWST "Photon Budget Crisis"**
The exact timing and source distribution of reionization face new tensions following recent observations from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). JWST has revealed an unexpectedly high abundance of UV-bright galaxies at $z > 10$ with high inferred values of $\xi_{\rm ion}$. If these early galaxies possess the non-negligible escape fractions expected of them, they produce an excess of ionizing photons that would complete reionization too early. This introduces a "photon budget crisis" that strongly conflicts with the late-ending reionization inferred from the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest [Muñoz et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240407250M/abstract). Resolving this tension may require a rapid, mass- or redshift-dependent decline in $f_{\rm esc}$ or $\xi_{\rm ion}$ as reionization concludes [Chakraborty et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...07..078C/abstract), though recent work suggests this apparent need could also be an artifact of reionization simulations underestimating the IGM opacity from massive, star-forming halos rather than a true physical decline in emissivity [Cain et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531.1951C/abstract).

## Sinks and the Role of the Small-Scale IGM

The progression of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] represents a competition between the emission of ionizing photons by early galaxies and their subsequent consumption by the intergalactic medium (IGM). This consumption is driven by the local recombination rate, which scales with the square of the gas density. Because the IGM is highly structured, recombinations are greatly enhanced by the small-scale clumping of gas. The role of these dense structures—collectively acting as "sinks" for ionizing radiation—is critical in determining the required ionizing photon budget and regulating the mean free path of radiation.

Prior to local reionization, baryons can collapse into dark matter halos with virial temperatures below the atomic cooling threshold ($T_{\rm vir} < 10^4$ K). When the advancing ionization fronts overtake these dense, neutral structures, the fronts are trapped, transitioning from fast R-type fronts to slower D-type fronts preceded by shock waves [Shapiro et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004MNRAS.348..753S/abstract). This triggers minihalo photoevaporation, a process in which the bound gas is boiled out into the IGM. Because these evaporating minihalos consume vast quantities of ionizing photons, they act as substantial sinks that can increase the required cosmological photon budget by factors of $3$ to $10$ [Haiman et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001ApJ...551..599H/abstract). 

However, the injection of thermal energy into the IGM during reionization raises its temperature to $\sim 10^4$ K, resulting in a dramatic increase in the local gas pressure. This increased pressure suppresses further baryonic accretion into low-mass halos through Jeans-mass filtering [Iliev et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.376..534I/abstract). The resulting pressure smoothing scale, quantified at $\sim 100$ kpc [Rorai et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...775...81R/abstract), [Kulkarni et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...812...30K/abstract), introduces a self-regulating feedback mechanism. It not only prevents new minihalos from forming within ionized bubbles but also smooths the local density field, thereby reducing the IGM recombination rate over time.

As the large-scale neutral islands disappear toward the end of reionization, the ionizing photon mean free path ceases to be limited by the boundaries of ionized bubbles. Instead, it becomes regulated by discrete, optically thick gas reservoirs known as Lyman-limit systems (LLSs) [Madau et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...851...50M/abstract). High-resolution simulations show that LLSs at $z \sim 6$ are predominantly found within the filamentary structures of the cosmic web or in the immediate dense environments surrounding massive galaxies [Fan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...45F/abstract), [Fan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240500100F/abstract). 

The exact severity of these sinks remains an active area of debate in the literature. Recent observations of quasar spectra at $z = 6$ have measured a surprisingly short ionizing photon mean free path of $0.75_{-0.45}^{+0.65}$ proper Mpc [Davies et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918L..35D/abstract). This implies that absorption by residual neutral gas is highly efficient. Translating this to the traditional clumping factor $C \equiv \langle \rho^2 \rangle / \langle \rho \rangle^2$, recent analyses suggest $C \approx 12$ [Davies et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240618186D/abstract), which is in tension with the canonical values of $C \approx 3$ derived from earlier radiation-hydrodynamic simulations. Consequently, models of an absorption-dominated end to reionization require a cumulative output of $\sim 3$ to $6.1_{-2.4}^{+11}$ ionizing photons per baryon to successfully complete the phase transition [Davies et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918L..35D/abstract), [Davies et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240618186D/abstract). This heightened demand on the photon budget exacerbates theoretical tensions regarding whether early galaxy populations can provide sufficient ionizing flux without violating other observational constraints.

## Feedback Loops and Self-Regulation

The progression of patchy reionization is intricately tied to the formation of the first galaxies through a network of feedback mechanisms. As the intergalactic medium (IGM) transitions from neutral to ionized, the energy injected by early stars and galaxies regulates subsequent star formation, leading to a highly coupled, non-linear evolution.

**Radiative Feedback and Photoheating Suppression**
The primary mechanism by which reionization regulates itself is radiative feedback, commonly referred to as photoheating suppression. As the ultraviolet background (UVB) ionizes the IGM, the gas temperature is rapidly elevated to $T \sim 10^4$ K. This heating increases the thermal pressure of the gas, which in turn raises the cosmological Jeans mass—the minimum mass required for a dark matter halo to accrete and retain baryonic gas. Consequently, gas accretion is quenched in low-mass "minihalos" with virial masses below $M \approx 10^8 - 10^9 M_\odot$, and existing gas within these shallow potential wells can be photoevaporated [Haiman et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001ApJ...551..599H/abstract), [Iliev et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.376..534I/abstract), [Ocvirk et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.463.1462O/abstract). In addition to UV photoheating, early minihalos below the atomic cooling threshold are also subjected to negative radiative feedback from infrared photons; the photodetachment of H$^-$ ions by the infrared background limits their cooling capacity and suppresses star formation even prior to local reionization [Cen et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.465L..69C/abstract).

Because low-mass dwarf galaxies are thought to be the dominant sources of ionizing photons, their widespread suppression establishes a framework of self-regulated reionization. As the global volume-averaged ionized fraction increases, a larger proportion of the universe becomes subject to Jeans-mass filtering, inherently throttling the production of new ionizing photons [Iliev et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.376..534I/abstract). Crucially, because the topology of the epoch is governed by [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]], this suppression occurs locally rather than globally. Simulations show that the star formation rates of dwarf galaxies decline sharply immediately following the reionization of their specific local environment, while more massive halos ($M > 10^{10} M_\odot$) possess sufficiently deep potential wells to continue accreting gas unaffected [Dawoodbhoy et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.480.1740D/abstract). Care must be taken in theoretical modeling, as simulations utilizing uniform ultraviolet background models may spuriously heat the IGM as early as $z \sim 15$, potentially overestimating the effects of photoionization feedback on early galaxy formation [Oñorbe et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...837..106O/abstract).

**The Role of Supernova Feedback**
While the theoretical basis for photoheating suppression is robust, its actual impact on the global ionizing photon budget is a subject of active debate. Supernova (SN) feedback is widely recognized as the dominant mechanism regulating star formation across a broad range of halo masses. Several hydrodynamical simulations indicate that SN feedback is so efficient at ejecting gas and quenching star formation in dwarf galaxies that the additional suppression from photoionization is sub-dominant, or entirely masked, on a cosmic scale [Pawlik et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.451.1586P/abstract), [Mutch et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462..250M/abstract), [Wu et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.488..419W/abstract). In contrast, other models suggest that photoheating and SN feedback do not act independently; rather, they can mutually amplify each other's ability to suppress the star formation rate, demonstrating the importance of including them simultaneously [Pawlik et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009MNRAS.396L..46P/abstract).

**Thermal History of the IGM**
The competition and coupling of these feedback loops dictate the thermal history of the IGM. The heat injected by patchy reionization persists long after the epoch concludes, leaving a "thermal memory" that is retained in the IGM temperature-density relation, parameterized as $T = T_0(1+\delta)^{\gamma-1}$ [Davies et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.489..977D/abstract), [Rorai et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...775...81R/abstract). Furthermore, this heat sets a characteristic pressure smoothing scale—the Jeans filtering scale—that suppresses small-scale baryonic fluctuations and dictates the clumpiness of the post-reionization IGM [Kulkarni et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...812...30K/abstract), [Rorai et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...775...81R/abstract). Measuring this thermal fossil record through probes like the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest remains one of the primary methods for constraining the timing and morphology of the feedback loops that governed the reionization era.

## 21-cm Cosmology and Power Spectrum Statistics

The redshifted 21-cm line of neutral hydrogen serves as the premier observational tool for mapping the morphology of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]]. Because the 21-cm signal directly traces the distribution and physical state of the neutral intergalactic medium (IGM), its statistical properties encode the astrophysical characteristics of early ionizing sources and the thermal history of the universe.

**The 21-cm Power Spectrum and Thermal History**
The 21-cm power spectrum is the primary statistic used to quantify IGM fluctuations. The amplitude and shape of the power spectrum are highly sensitive to the mass-dependent escape fraction of ionizing photons and the efficiency of galactic feedback, with varying supernova feedback models altering the power spectrum amplitude by up to $\approx 17\%$ at fixed ionized fractions [Geil et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.462..804G/abstract), [Kim et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.433.2476K/abstract). Generically, the variance of 21-cm fluctuations is predicted to reach a minimum midway through the epoch of reionization, when the global neutral fraction is near $50\%$ and the signal transitions from being dominated by overdense to underdense regions [Wyithe et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.379.1647W/abstract).

The 21-cm signal depends fundamentally on the spin temperature of the hydrogen gas. Before ionization fluctuations dominate the IGM, the signal is driven by variations in the spin temperature, which couples to the kinetic temperature of the gas via the Wouthuysen-Field effect. X-ray heating plays a critical role in this phase; an X-ray contribution to reionization of just $10\%$ can modulate the 21-cm power spectrum by $20-30\%$ across the characteristic scale of H II regions [Warszawski et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009MNRAS.396.1106W/abstract). While many rapid semi-numerical models approximate a uniform spin temperature outside ionized bubbles to simplify parameter inference [Mirocha et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.514.2010M/abstract), local variations in the coupling timing can introduce significant stochasticity into the observed power spectrum [Kaurov et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017arXiv170904353K/abstract).

**Higher-Order Statistics: Bispectrum and Skewness**
Because the topology of reionization is highly non-Gaussian—characterized by the growth and percolation of discrete ionized bubbles—the power spectrum alone suffers from parameter degeneracies, such as the inability to fully untangle source clustering from the overall reionization history. Higher-order statistics, such as the 21-cm bispectrum and skewness, are therefore necessary to break these degeneracies. 

The 21-cm bispectrum is highly sensitive to the ionization topology. As one considers configurations from squeezed to stretched triangles, the bispectrum transitions from negative to positive values. The exact position of this zero-crossing serves as a direct tracer of the size distribution of ionized and neutral regions [Hutter et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.492..653H/abstract). Similarly, the 21-cm skew spectrum (the cross-spectrum of the squared temperature field with itself) isolates non-Gaussianities, exhibiting distinct troughs at $k \sim 0.1 \text{ Mpc}^{-1}$ and peaks at $k \sim 0.4-1.0 \text{ Mpc}^{-1}$ during the mid-to-late stages of reionization [Cook et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.2734C/abstract). Beyond emission statistics, the 21-cm forest provides a means to probe individual minihalos and protogalactic disks in absorption against high-redshift radio sources [Furlanetto et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...579....1F/abstract).

**Cross-Correlations with Galaxies**
The cross-power spectrum between the 21-cm field and high-redshift galaxy populations offers another powerful diagnostic. Early in the reionization process, overdense regions exhibit a positive correlation between 21-cm emission and galaxies due to temperature-galaxy correlations. As reionization progresses and ionized bubbles grow around these overdensities, this correlation transitions to negative [Moriwaki et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.489.2471M/abstract), [Moriwaki et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.3183M/abstract), creating an anticorrelation that persists throughout the main phase of bubble growth [Wyithe et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.375.1034W/abstract). This sign reversal specifically marks the epoch when ionization fluctuations begin to dominate over spin temperature fluctuations, and the average spin temperature formally exceeds that of the cosmic microwave background [Moriwaki et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.3183M/abstract).

**Observational Prospects**
Next-generation low-frequency radio interferometers, notably the Hydrogen Epoch of Reionization Array (HERA) [Montero-Camacho et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.508.1262M/abstract) and the Square Kilometre Array (SKA), are designed to detect these statistical signatures. Forecasts suggest that SKA-Low observations of just $\sim 200$ hours at $z=7.5-8.5$ will be able to differentiate between competing models of the ionizing escape fraction using the 21-cm power spectrum [Seiler et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.487.5739S/abstract), while simultaneously possessing the sensitivity to measure the non-Gaussian features of the bispectrum and skew spectrum [Hutter et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.492..653H/abstract), [Cook et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.2734C/abstract). In addition to statistical measures, matched-filter techniques applied to 21-cm maps may allow for the direct identification of up to $\sim 150$ individual ionized regions in noisy interferometric data from arrays like the Murchison Widefield Array (MWA) [Malloy et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...767...68M/abstract).

## Secondary CMB Anisotropies and the kSZ Effect

The reionization of the intergalactic medium introduces a screen of free electrons that scatter cosmic microwave background (CMB) photons via Thomson scattering. This scattering process both suppresses the primary CMB temperature anisotropies and generates new, secondary anisotropies. The total integrated column density of these free electrons is parameterized by the electron-scattering optical depth, $\tau$. Measurements of large-scale CMB polarization by the Planck satellite yield tight constraints on this value, with recent combined analyses indicating $\tau \approx 0.05$ (e.g., $\tau_e = 0.052_{-0.008}^{+0.009}$ [Nikolić et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526.3170N/abstract) or $\tau = 0.0492^{+0.0014}_{-0.0030}$ [Elbers et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250821069E/abstract)). These low optical depths favor a relatively late and rapid reionization process, disfavoring an extended history with significant early [[partial-reionization|Partial reionization]] by sources such as Population III stars [Visbal et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.453.4456V/abstract).

Beyond suppressing primary anisotropies, the scattering of CMB photons off coherently moving free electrons generates secondary temperature fluctuations known as the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect. On small angular scales ($\ell \gtrsim 3000$), the kSZ effect is a dominant secondary CMB signal. It comprises two components: a homogeneous ("Ostriker-Vishniac") component from the fully ionized post-reionization universe, and a "patchy" component driven by the spatial fluctuations of expanding HII bubbles during [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] [Smith et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017PhRvL.119b1301S/abstract). 

The patchy kSZ power spectrum acts as a redshift-integrated probe of the timing and duration of the epoch of reionization. Recent observations by the South Pole Telescope constrain the patchy kSZ power to $\mathcal{D}_{3000}^{\rm pkSZ} = 1.1_{-0.7}^{+1.1} \mu\mathrm{K}^2$ at $\ell = 3000$ [Nikolić et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526.3170N/abstract). Incorporating this patchy kSZ measurement into Bayesian inference models shifts the posterior distributions away from extended histories and toward later, faster reionization scenarios [Nikolić et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.526.3170N/abstract). 

While the kSZ power spectrum is highly informative, it is subject to parameter degeneracies—most notably between the clustering of ionizing sources and the overall reionization history. To break these degeneracies, higher-order statistics are necessary. Dedicated kSZ higher-order statistics have been developed to separate the kSZ signal from primary CMB anisotropies and foregrounds, enabling future surveys to probe the morphology of both hydrogen and helium reionization [Smith et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017PhRvL.119b1301S/abstract), [Kumar et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250611188K/abstract). Furthermore, spatial variations in the free electron density cause "patchy screening," generating secondary temperature and polarization anisotropies that directly trace the large-scale distribution of ionized gas [Coulton et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240113033C/abstract).

Finally, secondary CMB anisotropies are not exclusively tied to hydrogen reionization. The subsequent epoch of doubly ionized helium (He II $\rightarrow$ He III), which occurred at $z \sim 2.5-3.5$ and was driven by the hard UV spectra of quasars, also proceeded in a highly patchy manner. This patchy helium reionization leaves its own imprint on the CMB via additional optical-depth fluctuations and a distinct kSZ signal, which next-generation, high-resolution CMB surveys aim to isolate [Hotinli et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023PhRvD.107j3517H/abstract), [Çalışkan et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...10..034C/abstract), [Kumar et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250611188K/abstract). The frequency-dependent escape fractions for He I and He II radiation are significantly boosted by X-ray feedback from early AGN or stellar clusters, critically impacting this subsequent helium reionization epoch [Benson et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...770...76B/abstract). Furthermore, recent comparisons between He II effective optical depth measurements and hydrodynamic simulations strongly favor a rapid and late helium reionization completion at $z \approx 2.74$ [Gaikwad et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250304893G/abstract).

## Lyman-Alpha Emitters and Forest Opacity

High-redshift quasar spectra exhibit broad regions of complete Ly$\alpha$ absorption, known as Gunn-Peterson troughs, which serve as a primary probe of the intergalactic medium (IGM) neutral fraction. Observations of the Ly$\alpha$ forest reveal large-scale opacity fluctuations at $z > 5$. To reproduce the longest Gunn-Peterson troughs and the observed variance in the effective optical depth, models require a late end to [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]], with overlap occurring at $z \approx 5.5-5.6$ [Qin et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.506.2390Q/abstract). During this tail end of reionization, the patchy topology of the ionized IGM, combined with spatial fluctuations in the ultraviolet background (UVB), drives the observed variance in Ly$\alpha$ transmission. Constraints from Ly$\alpha$ luminosity functions at $z=6.6-7.3$ imply a rapid increase in the global neutral fraction from near-zero to over $80\%$ during this interval [Morales et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...919..120M/abstract).

The relationship between Ly$\alpha$ transmission and the local galaxy density—the opacity-density relation—provides a critical test for reionization models, though the literature currently presents a complex, and sometimes conflicting, picture. On one hand, deep surveys of biased environments show a significant excess of Ly$\alpha$ transmission around $z > 6.5$ [O III] emitters extending over $25\, h^{-1}{\rm cMpc}$, supporting an "inside-out" topology where reionization occurs earlier around highly clustered sources [Jin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...93J/abstract). Observations of the LAGER-z7OD1 protocluster reveal that ionized bubbles around individual LAEs are approaching overlap at $z \sim 7$, suggesting accelerated reionization in dense environments [Martin et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251013140M/abstract). Conversely, some of the most highly transmissive Ly$\alpha$ forest segments at $z \approx 5.7$ are found to intersect galaxy *underdensities* [Gangolli et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025JCAP...03..069G/abstract), [Zhu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251009568Z/abstract). Radiative transfer models suggest these transmissive voids correspond to regions that were recently reionized; their low gas densities and high residual heating lead to suppressed recombination rates and enhanced Ly$\alpha$ transmission, introducing a degeneracy with the underlying source clustering [Gangolli et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025JCAP...03..069G/abstract). Furthermore, anisotropic escape of ionizing photons along large-scale structures may also contribute to the patchy transmission profile [Zhu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251009568Z/abstract).

Reionization's patchiness also influences the interpretation of quasar proximity zones—regions of enhanced transmission immediately surrounding a quasar. Because quasars are highly biased tracers of large-scale structure, they are often born into pre-existing, galaxy-generated H II regions. Consequently, the sizes of observed proximity zones can appear consistent with a largely ionized IGM even if the global neutral fraction remains as high as $\sim 50\%$ [Lidz et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...670...39L/abstract). As reionization concludes, the mean free path of ionizing photons drops sharply as the last neutral islands disappear. High-resolution simulations show that the mean free path near massive halos is systematically shorter ($\approx 10\%$) than in random sightlines due to the concentration of dense structures and Lyman-limit systems [Fan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...979..150F/abstract). Even after overlap, dense environments can retain significant neutral gas; for example, a massive H I gas reservoir discovered at $z=5.4$ permeating a protocluster demonstrates that high-column density neutral gas can persist well after the global epoch of reionization has ended [Heintz et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240706287H/abstract).

At redshifts where the global IGM is significantly neutral ($z > 6$), Lyman-$\alpha$ emitters (LAEs) become invaluable probes of the local ionization topology. Because the resonant Ly$\alpha$ line is easily scattered by neutral hydrogen, the fraction of UV-selected galaxies exhibiting detectable Ly$\alpha$ emission drops rapidly as the IGM neutral fraction increases. However, models suggest that IGM attenuation alone cannot fully account for the observed rapid drop—altering the fraction by no more than a factor of $\sim 2$—implying that the co-evolution of intrinsic galaxy properties is also required [Mesinger et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.446..566M/abstract). Galaxies that *are* observed as LAEs at $z > 7$ are typically found in overdense regions where multiple sources have carved out large, overlapping ionized bubbles, allowing the Ly$\alpha$ photons to redshift out of resonance before encountering the neutral IGM damping wings [Furlanetto et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006MNRAS.365.1012F/abstract), [Tilvi et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...891L..10T/abstract). Furthermore, the resonant scattering of Ly$\alpha$ photons within the circumgalactic and intergalactic medium creates extended, low-surface-brightness Ly$\alpha$ halos around these star-forming galaxies, serving as an important diagnostic for the local neutral environment [Zheng et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...739...62Z/abstract). Recent JWST observations have resolved damping wings in individual high-$z$ galaxies to estimate local bubble sizes; however, tension exists in the interpretation of these wings. While some studies infer exceptionally large bubbles at $z \sim 7$, hydrodynamical simulations suggest that residual neutral hydrogen within the ionized bubbles saturates the blue-side intergalactic absorption, implying that the data might instead be explained by smaller bubbles paired with intrinsically stronger Ly$\alpha$ emission [Keating et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.532.1646K/abstract).

## Historical Development of Reionization Models

Early cosmological models frequently treated the epoch of reionization using an instantaneous approximation, where the intergalactic medium (IGM) underwent a sudden, [[global-reionization|Global reionization]] at a single redshift [Liu et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016JCAP...02..046L/abstract). However, the discovery of early quasars and temperature anisotropies in the cosmic microwave background (CMB) soon revealed a more complex timeline. The first- and third-year Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) data implied a high electron-scattering optical depth ($\tau_e \approx 0.09 - 0.15$), suggesting an early onset to reionization at $z > 10$. To reconcile this with Ly$\alpha$ forest observations indicating a significant neutral fraction at $z \approx 6$, theorists explored exotic scenarios such as "double reionization" (effectively a form of [[delayed-reionization|Delayed reionization]]). In these models, an initial burst of Population III stars or miniquasars drove a [[partial-reionization|Partial reionization]] of the IGM, followed by a period of recombination and a subsequent secondary reionization phase [Furlanetto et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...613...16F/abstract), [Furlanetto et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005ApJ...634....1F/abstract), [Benson et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006MNRAS.369.1055B/abstract).

Theoretical consensus gradually moved away from the instantaneous approximation and double reionization models toward a single, extended, and highly [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] process. Foundational excursion set models and early $N$-body radiative transfer simulations demonstrated that reionization proceeds with a patchy, "inside-out" topology, where H II regions grow around overdensities and eventually percolate [Furlanetto et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...613...16F/abstract), [Iliev et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006MNRAS.369.1625I/abstract). A critical realization from this period was the profound impact of cosmic variance. Simulations utilizing small volumes and periodic boundary conditions artificially enforced a sharp, sudden end to reionization. Studies demonstrated that the spread in the reionization redshift across different regions of the universe is severely underestimated in small boxes, requiring simulation volumes in excess of $\sim 30 - 100 \text{ Mpc}$ to properly capture the large-scale scatter and the size distribution of ionized bubbles [Barkana et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...609..474B/abstract), [Iliev et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006MNRAS.369.1625I/abstract), [Gnedin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...793...30G/abstract).

The landscape of reionization models shifted again with the resolution of the WMAP vs. Planck discrepancy. Planck CMB constraints revised $\tau_e$ downward to $\sim 0.05-0.06$, which largely eliminated the need for exotic early ionization sources and aligned CMB data with a late, single-epoch reionization driven primarily by standard star-forming galaxies [Mitra et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015MNRAS.454L..76M/abstract), [Doussot et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...870...18D/abstract). 

Consequently, modern modeling efforts have culminated in sophisticated, large-volume radiation-hydrodynamic (RHD) simulations that explicitly track spatial patchiness. Projects such as the Cosmic Reionization On Computers (CROC) suite self-consistently resolve the formation of dwarf galaxies and track the small-scale clumping of the IGM, revealing how local environments dictate the patchy topology and mean free path of ionizing photons [Gnedin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...793...30G/abstract), [Fan et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...963...45F/abstract). Similarly, the THESAN project couples the AREPO-RT radiative transfer solver with the IllustrisTNG galaxy formation model in $95.5 \text{ cMpc}$ volumes to map the expansion histories of ionized bubbles [Kannan et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022MNRAS.511.4005K/abstract), [Neyer et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.531.2943N/abstract). These state-of-the-art simulations show that patchy reionization undergoes distinct stages of topological evolution, culminating in the emergence of a single, massive percolated H II region that dominates the volume as early as $z \sim 9-10$ [Jamieson et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241108943J/abstract). Theoretical studies comparing seminumerical models to these full radiative transfer simulations highlight that certain rapid reionization schemes may suffer from photon non-conservation, which can bias the inferred redshift evolution of the ionized fraction [Hutter et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.477.1549H/abstract). A notable numerical challenge in these advanced radiative transfer simulations is the treatment of radiation propagation; the reduced speed of light approximation, commonly used to lower computational costs, can lead to overestimating the post-reionization neutral fraction unless the chemistry modules are carefully calibrated to correct for it [Ocvirk et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019A&A...626A..77O/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Observational Frontiers

While the paradigm of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] is firmly established, the exact timing of its completion and the precise nature of the driving sources remain subjects of active debate. Recent JWST observations have uncovered an unexpected abundance of UV-bright galaxies at $z > 10$. If these galaxies possess standard ionizing efficiencies, their cumulative output would drive a rapid reionization that completes by $z \sim 8$, introducing a "photon budget crisis" [Muñoz et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240407250M/abstract). This early timeline is in stark tension with Ly$\alpha$ forest opacity measurements and Gunn-Peterson trough observations, which strongly indicate a late end to reionization at $z \sim 5.5-6.0$ [Qin et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.506.2390Q/abstract), [Jin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...93J/abstract).

Resolving this tension requires revisiting assumptions about the escape fraction of ionizing photons, $f_{\rm esc}$. Historically, models favored a "democratic" reionization driven by an unseen population of faint, low-mass dwarf galaxies [Srbinovsky et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010PASA...27..110S/abstract). However, recent analyses suggest that massive, UV-bright "oligarch" galaxies may actually dominate the photon budget. If these oligarchs exhibit *extreme escape fractions*—potentially driven by bursty star formation that punctures the interstellar medium—they could supply $\gtrsim 80\%$ of the ionizing photons [Naidu et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...892..109N/abstract). Conversely, recent measurements indicate that the mean free path of ionizing photons at $z=6$ is surprisingly short, which would elevate the required photon budget to $\sim 6$ photons per baryon and demand population-averaged escape fractions exceeding $20\%$ [Davies et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918L..35D/abstract). Whether $f_{\rm esc}$ scales preferentially with high-mass starbursts or low-mass halos remains a critical unresolved problem.

To map the topology of the intergalactic medium (IGM) directly, the field eagerly anticipates 21-cm interferometers such as *SKA-Low*. By measuring the 21-cm power spectrum and higher-order statistics like the bispectrum, SKA-Low will be capable of breaking the degeneracy between the intrinsic clustering of sources and the growth of ionized bubbles [Hutter et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.492..653H/abstract), [Cook et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.2734C/abstract). SKA-Low tomography will track the merging of these bubbles and the emergence of the single largest percolated region, providing a direct geometric map of the reionization process [Jamieson et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241108943J/abstract). 

To link the IGM topology directly to the sources, future surveys will cross-correlate 21-cm maps with galaxy populations observed by JWST, *Euclid*, and upcoming extremely large telescopes. These observatories will identify the specific galaxies residing within ionized bubbles, testing the "inside-out" geometry where overdense regions reionize before voids [Wyithe et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007MNRAS.375.1034W/abstract), [Zackrisson et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.493..855Z/abstract). 

Additionally, *line intensity mapping* (LIM) of lines such as [O III], Ly$\alpha$, and H$\alpha$ will provide a powerful statistical probe of the faint galaxy populations that cannot be individually resolved [Mas-Ribas et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...950...39M/abstract). Cross-correlating 21-cm data with 3D line intensity mapping (LIM) surveys (such as [O III]) offers a unique dynamical signature: the direct cross-power spectrum transitions from positive to negative as expanding ionized bubbles sweep across the IGM [Moriwaki et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.489.2471M/abstract). Conversely, for 2D near-infrared background (NIRB) surveys, a direct cross-correlation vanishes due to foreground cleaning; instead, one must cross-correlate the *squared* 21-cm signal with the NIRB to recover the same dynamical signature of the evolutionary stages of reionization [Sun et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241021410S/abstract). In addition to 21-cm and LIM techniques, the dispersion measures of high-redshift fast radio bursts (FRBs) provide a potential new probe of reionization patchiness, with mean dispersion values predicted to reach a maximum of $\sim 5900\text{ pc cm}^{-3}$ [Ziegler et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241102699Z/abstract). Beyond direct observations of the IGM and source galaxies, primordial magnetic fields may have been generated on linear scales through vorticity induced by temperature fluctuations, reaching strengths of $\sim 10^{-24}$ G before being amplified during the reionization process [Naoz et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013PhRvL.111e1303N/abstract).
