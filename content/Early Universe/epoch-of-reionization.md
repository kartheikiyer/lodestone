The Epoch of Reionization (EoR) marks the final major [[global-reionization|global phase transition]] of the [[early-universe|Early universe]]. Following cosmological recombination at $z \sim 1000$, the baryonic matter in the universe consisted primarily of cold, neutral hydrogen gas. As the first dark matter halos collapsed to form stars and active galactic nuclei, their [[stellar-ionizing-radiation|Stellar ionizing radiation]] permeated the intergalactic medium (IGM), systematically transforming the neutral gas back into a warm, highly ionized plasma. This period, spanning roughly from redshift $z \approx 20$ to $z \approx 6$, serves as the critical evolutionary link between the highly uniform early universe and the complex, structured cosmos observed today [Fan et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ARA&A..44..415F/abstract). While early X-ray sources such as miniquasars may have contributed to the initial pre-heating of the IGM [Madau et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...604..484M/abstract), a strong consensus indicates that [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ultraviolet photons]] from star-forming galaxies drove the bulk of hydrogen reionization, preceding the much later, quasar-driven epoch of [[he-ii-reionization|He II reionization]].

Because galaxies are highly biased tracers of the underlying dark matter distribution, reionization was fundamentally an [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] process. Rather than occurring uniformly throughout space, ionizing radiation carved out localized, expanding "bubbles" of ionized gas that eventually percolated and overlapped [Razoumov et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...572..695R/abstract). The timeline of this phase transition is broadly anchored by the Thomson scattering optical depth of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and by the [[gunn-peterson-effect|Gunn-Peterson effect]]—saturated Lyman-$\alpha$ absorption troughs seen in the spectra of high-redshift quasars that indicate a rapidly increasing neutral fraction at $z \gtrsim 6$. To directly probe the 3D topology of these expanding bubbles and the thermal state of the IGM, astrophysicists rely on the redshifted 21-cm hyperfine transition of neutral hydrogen. Current and next-generation radio interferometers aim to measure the 21-cm power spectrum to chart the IGM's transition from absorption to emission, mapping the exact [[reionization-history|Reionization history]] of X-ray heating and ultraviolet ionization [Mesinger et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.431..621M/abstract).

Modern research in the EoR is driven by a deep interplay between high-resolution radiation-hydrodynamic simulations and multi-wavelength observations. A major theoretical focus is the back-reaction of [[photoionization-and-radiative-heating|Photoionization and Radiative Heating]] on the galaxy population itself; as the IGM heated, increased thermal pressure suppressed gas accretion in low-mass dark matter halos, thereby regulating [[early-galaxy-formation|Early galaxy formation]] [Qin et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.467.1678Q/abstract). Observationally, the field is currently grappling with a severe "photon budget crisis" triggered by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). JWST has revealed an unexpected overabundance of highly efficient, UV-bright galaxies at $z > 9$. If these sources possessed high values for the [[escape-fraction|Escape fraction]] of ionizing photons, they would have reionized the universe far too early, contradicting established CMB and quasar constraints. Reconciling this tension—whether through a mass-dependent [[photon-escape-fraction|Photon escape fraction]], extreme IGM clumping, or previously underestimated feedback mechanisms—remains the foremost open question in understanding the cosmic dawn.

## Introduction and the Cosmic Phase Transition

Following cosmological recombination, the baryonic matter in the universe existed as a cold, dark, and predominantly neutral gas. As the first collapsed dark matter halos formed stars and active galactic nuclei, their energetic radiation systematically transformed the neutral hydrogen back into a warm, highly ionized plasma. This period represents a critical link between the uniform [[early-universe|Early universe]] and the structured cosmos observed today. Furthermore, alternative cosmological scenarios, such as Warm Dark Matter and sterile neutrinos, can be uniquely constrained by tracing the [[reionization-history|ionization history]] alongside these stellar emission models [Romanello et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021Univ....7..365R/abstract).

The reionization of the IGM was fundamentally a radiation-driven process initiated by the production of Lyman continuum photons with energies exceeding $13.6$ eV. Theoretical models and simulations indicate that this was an [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] process. Rather than occurring uniformly throughout space, [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing radiation]] fronts expanded outward from overdense regions housing the earliest galaxies, creating localized "bubbles" of ionized gas [Razoumov et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...572..695R/abstract). As the abundance of luminous sources grew, these isolated bubbles expanded and eventually percolated, culminating in the complete overlap of ionized regions and the conclusion of the EoR [Lidz et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ASSL..423...23L/abstract).

## Historical Development of Reionization Constraints

The theoretical expectation that the universe must have undergone a phase transition to explain the highly ionized IGM observed locally was confirmed observationally in the early 2000s. The foundational constraints on this timeline were established through two complementary probes: the absorption spectra of the first high-redshift quasars and the polarization of the CMB.

The earliest direct evidence anchoring the end of [[re-ionization|reionization]] came from the spectra of quasars at $z \gtrsim 6$. These spectra exhibited near-complete flux decrements at wavelengths shorter than the rest-frame Lyman-$\alpha$ transition, $(1+z)\lambda_\alpha$, a phenomenon known as the [[gunn-peterson-effect|Gunn-Peterson effect]]. Foundational surveys demonstrated a rapid increase in the volume-averaged neutral hydrogen fraction, $x_{\rm HI}$, from $x_{\rm HI} < 10^{-4}$ at $z \leq 5.5$ to $x_{\rm HI} > 10^{-3}$ at $z \sim 6$ [Fan et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ARA&A..44..415F/abstract). However, interpreting these Gunn-Peterson troughs is subject to a fundamental limitation: because the Lyman-$\alpha$ scattering cross-section is extremely large, even a predominantly ionized IGM with a neutral fraction of $x_{\rm HI} \sim 10^{-4}$ yields an optical depth $\tau \gg 1$. Consequently, the saturated absorption profiles made it difficult to precisely determine the true neutral fraction or the exact onset of [[reionization-history|reionization process]] at higher redshifts.

To constrain the earlier stages of the epoch, astrophysicists turned to the Thomson scattering optical depth ($\tau_e$), an integral constraint measured from the large-scale polarization of the CMB. Free electrons liberated during reionization scatter CMB photons, damping temperature anisotropies and generating a polarization signal. Early data from the Wilkinson Microwave Anisotropy Probe (WMAP) measured a surprisingly high optical depth of $\tau_e = 0.09 \pm 0.03$. This value implied a significant ionization fraction extending to higher redshifts, suggesting that the reionization process began as early as $z \sim 14$ [Fan et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ARA&A..44..415F/abstract), [Shull et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007astro.ph..2323S/abstract). 

The apparent tension between the high CMB optical depth (implying early reionization) and the quasar Gunn-Peterson troughs (indicating a largely neutral universe just above $z \sim 6$) initially sparked intense theoretical debate. Early theoretical work proposed that an initial generation of massive Population III stars or miniquasars could have [[partial-reionization|partially reionized]] and pre-heated the universe as early as $z \sim 15$ [Madau et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...604..484M/abstract). Extending this idea, some literature suggested an exotic, non-monotonic "double reionization" history, involving a subsequent period of recombination followed by a second, final reionization driven by standard star-forming galaxies at $z \sim 6$. However, subsequent theoretical modeling demonstrated that a non-monotonic double reionization scenario is physically implausible given the rapid decline of IGM recombination rates and the nature of radiative feedback [Furlanetto et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005ApJ...634....1F/abstract), [Furlanetto et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...686...25F/abstract). 

This tension was ultimately resolved as models of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]] improved and observational data was refined. Subsequent measurements by the Planck satellite revised the Thomson scattering optical depth downward, alleviating the requirement for an extreme early ionizing photon budget [Madau et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...851...50M/abstract). Together, these historical WMAP/Planck and quasar constraints established the modern consensus: reionization was a single, extended, and spatially patchy process that gradually ionized the universe over hundreds of millions of years, concluding around $z \sim 6$ [Fan et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ARA&A..44..415F/abstract).

## Primary Drivers: Star-Forming Galaxies and Quasars

The identity of the sources that drove hydrogen [[re-ionization|reionization]] is a central question in cosmology. The total cosmic [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing photon budget]] is governed by three primary factors: the number density of early sources, their intrinsic Lyman-continuum efficiency ($\xi_{\rm ion}$), and their [[escape-fraction|Escape fraction]] ($f_{\rm esc}$) of ionizing photons. Cosmological simulations and empirical constraints strongly indicate that [[ultraviolet-galaxies|star-forming galaxies]] were the primary drivers of this phase transition. Active Galactic Nuclei (AGN) and quasars, while capable of producing hard ionizing spectra, were too rare at high redshifts to dominate the photon budget. Current models indicate that AGN accounted for only 1--10% of the ionizing budget at $z=6$ [Trebitsch et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023MNRAS.518.3576T/abstract), though their radiation may have indirectly influenced the topology of ionized bubbles [Seiler et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.480L..33S/abstract) and was responsible for the subsequent [[he-ii-reionization|He II reionization]] at lower redshifts [Paschos et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007arXiv0711.1904P/abstract).

Within the galaxy population, an active debate centers on whether the photon budget was supplied by a numerous population of faint dwarf galaxies ("democratic" reionization) or a smaller number of massive, UV-bright galaxies ("oligarchic" reionization). Traditionally, faint galaxies with absolute UV magnitudes $M_{\rm UV} > -16$, extending down to limits of $M_{\rm UV} \approx -13$, were considered the dominant sources of [[stellar-ionizing-radiation|Stellar ionizing radiation]] [Robertson et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...768...71R/abstract), [Xu et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...833...84X/abstract). Early simulations suggested that Ly-$\alpha$ emission from galaxies at $z \sim 8$ could be detectable with ground-based 8m-class telescopes, highlighting the early potential for identifying reionizing sources [Barton et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004ApJ...604L...1B/abstract). However, a competing oligarchic model proposes that less than 5% of massive galaxies ($M_{\rm UV} < -18$) could account for $>80\%$ of the ionizing budget. This scenario occurs if the [[escape-fractions|escape fraction]] is not a constant across the UV luminosity function but instead scales strongly with star-formation surface density ($\Sigma_{\rm SFR}$) [Naidu et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...892..109N/abstract). The role of heavily dust-obscured, massive starbursts is also an active area of investigation. For instance, the SPT0311-58 system, the most massive infrared luminous system known in the EoR, provides a detailed laboratory for studying molecular gas (CO, [C I], and H$_2$O) at $z = 6.9$ without evidence of AGN activity [Jarugula et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...921...97J/abstract).

Constraining $\xi_{\rm ion}$ and $f_{\rm esc}$ during the reionization epoch remains highly challenging because the opaque IGM precludes direct observation of Lyman-continuum leakage at $z > 6$. Indirect probes of the escape fraction can be achieved by combining rest-frame UV slope measurements with H$\beta$ equivalent widths, allowing for the identification of high-leakage galaxies up to $z \approx 9$ with JWST [Zackrisson et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...777...39Z/abstract). Measurements of lower-redshift analogs indicate that high Lyman-continuum efficiencies (e.g., $\log_{10}(\xi_{\rm ion} / [\rm Hz\,erg^{-1}]) \approx 25.1 - 25.8$) are common in early star-forming galaxies [Bouwens et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...831..176B/abstract), [Jaiswar et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024PASA...41...40J/abstract). Moreover, incorporating binary stars into stellar-population synthesis models can increase the predicted ionizing photon budget by approximately 40% compared to models using only single stars [Liu et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...968...13L/abstract). Recent observations of a short ionizing mean free path indicate that successfully completing reionization may require a population-averaged escape fraction exceeding $20\%$, a dramatic increase over classical estimates [Davies et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918L..35D/abstract). Direct evidence for such physical escape mechanisms has been observed in highly magnified systems like the [[sunburst-arc|Sunburst Arc]], where gravitational lensing reveals ionizing ultraviolet photons actively leaking through narrow, low-density channels in an otherwise optically thick interstellar medium [Rivera-Thorsen et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019Sci...366..738R/abstract). Additionally, the spectroscopic identification of a lensed ultra-faint galaxy ($M_{\rm UV} = -19.6$) at $z = 7.64$ confirms that the sources likely responsible for reionization are accessible through gravitational magnification [Hoag et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017NatAs...1E..91H/abstract).

## The Topology of Inhomogeneous Reionization

The transition from a neutral to an ionized universe was not a spatially uniform process. Instead, it followed a "Swiss-cheese" topology characteristic of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]]. In this paradigm, the first luminous sources carved out localized ionized bubbles (H II regions) within the neutral IGM. As these sources multiplied and grew, their respective bubbles expanded and eventually overlapped, culminating in the complete percolation of ionized gas across the cosmos [Razoumov et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...572..695R/abstract).

The topology of these expanding bubbles is fundamentally tied to the underlying dark matter distribution. Because early star-forming galaxies were highly biased tracers of the dark matter density field, their [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing radiation]] fields overlapped early in the reionization process. This strong clustering allowed sources to collectively produce large ionized regions, potentially reaching scales of $\sim 100$ Mpc prior to the final overlap phase [Cen et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2005astro.ph..7014C/abstract). This topological evolution also carries dynamical consequences; for instance, the drag force between baryons and cold dark matter during reionization induces relative velocities that imprint distinct signatures on the large-scale clustering bias of galaxies [Schmidt et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017PhRvD..96h3533S/abstract). Direct observational evidence for this patchy reionization has been found in systems like the EGS77 galaxy group at $z=7.7$. In this system, three faint galaxies separated by $<0.7$ physical Mpc reside within a shared, overlapping ionized bubble approximately $1.02$ Mpc in radius, demonstrating the localized nature of early bubble formation [Tilvi et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...891L..10T/abstract). On larger scales, the ASPIRE survey has measured enhanced Lyman-$\alpha$ transmission around $z > 6.5$ [O III] emitters extending beyond $25 \, h^{-1} {\rm cMpc}$, confirming that reionization proceeded earlier in biased, overdense environments [Jin et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...93J/abstract).

The patchy nature of reionization dramatically alters the interpretation of quasar proximity zones—regions of enhanced Ly$\alpha$ transmission immediately surrounding high-redshift quasars. Early homogeneous models suggested that the sizes of these zones directly probed the global neutral fraction. However, because quasars typically form in massive halos that are already embedded within large, galaxy-generated ionized bubbles, their proximity zones are largely insensitive to the global neutral fraction and instead trace small-scale IGM structure [Lidz et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...670...39L/abstract). Furthermore, the episodic nature of quasar accretion can leave behind "fossil" ionized bubbles with radii up to $\sim 40$ Mpc. These regions can remain highly ionized for extended periods even after the quasar shuts off, standing out against the broader Swiss-cheese topology of the surrounding IGM [Furlanetto et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...686...25F/abstract). During their active phases, quasars can severely disrupt their local environments; JWST observations of the $z=7.5$ quasar J1007+2115 have discovered a massive, kpc-scale outflow of [O III] gas, suggesting powerful feedback mechanisms were already active in early massive halos [Liu et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...33L/abstract).

The eventual percolation of these ionized bubbles marks the end of reionization. Advanced 3D radiative transfer simulations demonstrate that this percolation is highly complex, contradicting simplistic analytical models which assumed that low-density voids would uniformly ionize before overdense regions [Razoumov et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...572..695R/abstract). Furthermore, the traditional assumption of isotropic bubble growth is increasingly being challenged. Recent cross-correlations of IGM transmission with galaxy positions suggest that [[photon-escape-fraction|ionizing photon escape]]—and thus bubble expansion—is anisotropic, preferentially leaking along large-scale structural filaments rather than forming perfectly spherical fronts [Zhu et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251009568Z/abstract). This intricate, clustered topology dictates the thermal and ionization history of the IGM and forms the primary structural target for upcoming 21-cm tomography experiments aiming to map the reionization epoch.

## Absorption Line Probes and the Lyα Forest

The spectra of high-redshift quasars and gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) provide direct lines of sight through the IGM, serving as powerful tools for charting the timeline and topology of cosmic [[re-ionization|reionization]]. The primary signature of a neutral IGM is the [[gunn-peterson-effect|Gunn-Peterson effect]], wherein ambient neutral hydrogen scatters photons bluer than the local Lyman-$\alpha$ (Ly$\alpha$) transition out of the line of sight. Observations of quasars at $z \sim 6$ demonstrate a rapid increase in the IGM [[cosmic-h-i-density|neutral fraction]], $x_{\rm HI}$, marking the tail end of the reionization process [Fan et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ARA&A..44..415F/abstract). However, because the Ly$\alpha$ scattering cross-section is extremely large, the Gunn-Peterson trough saturates at neutral fractions as low as $x_{\rm HI} \sim 10^{-4}$ to $10^{-3}$. Consequently, simple flux decrements in the Ly$\alpha$ forest cannot uniquely constrain the true neutral fraction in a substantially neutral universe.

To measure higher neutral fractions, observers rely on the Ly$\alpha$ damping wing—the Lorentzian profile of absorption that extends redward of the rest-frame transition. Because the damping wings do not fully saturate, they act as direct ion-counters for the local IGM. GRB afterglows are particularly valuable for this measurement; unlike quasars, they do not typically generate massive, long-lived ionized proximity zones that complicate the local IGM absorption signature. Spectroscopic analyses of the damping wings in high-redshift GRBs, such as GRB 140515A ($z=6.33$) and GRB 210905A ($z \sim 6.3$), constrain the local neutral fraction to $x_{\rm HI} \lesssim 0.12 - 0.15$ [Chornock et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014arXiv1405.7400C/abstract), [Fausey et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240313126F/abstract). These quantitative limits indicate that the IGM was already highly ionized by $z \approx 6.3$, consistent with the consensus that reionization was largely complete by $z \sim 6$. The strong absorption from the Ly$\alpha$ damping wing also systematically affects galaxy observations at high redshift; however, the implementation of new empirical transmission curves that properly account for this neutral hydrogen damping wing absorption has been shown to resolve the systematic overestimation of photometric redshifts for galaxies at $z > 7$ [Asada et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983L...2A/abstract). Additionally, the evolution of the Ly$\alpha$ luminosity function (LF) serves as a sensitive probe of the IGM; current inferences at $z = 6.6$, $7.0$, and $7.3$ indicate a rapid increase in the neutral fraction from approximately $0.08$ to $0.83$ over this interval [Morales et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...919..120M/abstract). Bayesian analysis of Ly$\alpha$ detections and non-detections in Lyman Break Galaxies at $z \sim 7$ similarly suggests a global neutral fraction of approximately $0.59$, confirming that reionization was still actively ongoing 700 Myr after the Big Bang [Mason et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...856....2M/abstract).

While quasars strongly alter their local environments, their proximity zones provide crucial information about the spatial topology of reionization. Radiation from a quasar carves out a highly ionized bubble, allowing transmission spikes to be observed within the generally opaque Gunn-Peterson trough. Because reionization is a fundamentally inhomogeneous (patchy) process driven by clustered star-forming galaxies, quasars are typically born into pre-existing, large-scale ionized regions. Therefore, sightlines through quasar proximity zones often pass entirely through overlapping ionized bubbles, making their physical sizes more sensitive to local small-scale density structures rather than the global volume-averaged neutral fraction [Lidz et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007ApJ...670...39L/abstract).

Absorption probes also constrain the mean free path of [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing photons]], which is regulated by residual neutral gas and [[high-ionization-absorbers|High-ionization absorbers]] at the epoch's conclusion. Recent measurements of stacked quasar spectra at $z \approx 6$ reveal a surprisingly short mean free path of $\lambda_{\rm mfp} = 0.75_{-0.45}^{+0.65}$ proper Mpc [Davies et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918L..35D/abstract). This short $\lambda_{\rm mfp}$ suggests the IGM was highly absorptive, implying a local gas clumping factor as high as $C \approx 12$, significantly larger than the traditionally assumed $C \approx 3$ derived from older simulations [Davies et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240618186D/abstract). To maintain ionization against such a high recombination rate, the universe requires a cumulative output of $\sim 3$ to $6.1$ ionizing photons per baryon by the end of reionization, representing a dramatic increase in the ionizing photon budget over previous estimates [Davies et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918L..35D/abstract), [Davies et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240618186D/abstract).

## The 21-cm Hyperfine Signal and Tomography

The redshifted 21-cm hyperfine transition of neutral hydrogen represents the most direct and information-rich probe of the IGM during the cosmic dark ages and the [[reionization-history|reionization epoch]]. Because the transition emits at a specific rest-frame wavelength, 21-cm observations can theoretically yield a full three-dimensional map—or tomography—of the IGM's density, temperature, and ionization state. 

**Spin Temperature and the Wouthuysen-Field Effect**
The observability of the 21-cm line against the CMB depends on the spin temperature, $T_S$, which governs the relative population of the $n=1$ singlet and triplet hyperfine levels. During the dark ages, adiabatic expansion cools the IGM kinetic temperature ($T_K$) below the CMB temperature ($T_\gamma$). To render the 21-cm line visible in absorption, $T_S$ must couple to the cold $T_K$. This coupling is primarily driven by the Wouthuysen-Field effect, a resonant scattering process facilitated by the background of Lyman-$\alpha$ (and higher-order Lyman series) photons emitted by the first generation of stars and galaxies [Meiksin et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010MNRAS.402.1780M/abstract). As reionization progresses, early X-ray sources—such as miniquasars or X-ray binaries—heat the IGM. Once $T_K$ surpasses $T_\gamma$, the 21-cm signal transitions from absorption into emission [Mesinger et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.431..621M/abstract). 

**The Global 21-cm Signal**
The sky-averaged, or global, 21-cm signal measures the cosmic evolution of $T_S$ and the global neutral fraction. The EDGES experiment reported a deep absorption trough centered at 78 MHz, corresponding to redshift $z \sim 18$ [Takalana et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020Ap&SS.365..188T/abstract). If confirmed, this anomalously strong absorption indicates either that the early IGM was substantially colder than standard adiabatic cooling permits, or that an excess radio background was present. It would also require a highly efficient and rapid onset of star formation in low-mass ($10^8 - 10^{10} M_\odot$) halos [Mirocha et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.483.1980M/abstract). 

**The 21-cm Power Spectrum and the 21-cm Forest**
While the global signal tracks the volume-averaged history, the 21-cm power spectrum captures the topology of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]]. Current-generation interferometers, including PAPER, LOFAR, and HERA, have placed increasingly stringent upper limits on the power spectrum amplitude. For example, PAPER measurements at $z = 8.4$ constrain the IGM kinetic temperature to $> 5-10$ K, ruling out theoretical models featuring extremely inefficient X-ray heating [Pober et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...809...62P/abstract). Theoretical prediction of this power spectrum requires large-scale simulations to capture ionized bubbles; recent innovations like the "Fixed & Paired" numerical technique have suppressed cosmic variance and reduced computational costs for these simulations by a factor of 3.5 [Acharya et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.3793A/abstract). Furthermore, theoretical predictions of this power spectrum must account for underlying cosmology; tensions in cosmological parameters like $H_0$ and $\sigma_8$ can significantly alter the predicted 21-cm power spectrum and UV luminosity functions, necessitating a broader parameter space for accurate inference modeling [Acharya et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241011620A/abstract). In addition to emission tomography, the high-redshift universe can be probed via the "21-cm forest." Analogous to the Ly$\alpha$ forest, this phenomenon relies on high-resolution radio absorption spectra against background sources to probe the thermal state of the IGM and the abundance of gravitationally bound minihalos before the onset of global reionization [Furlanetto et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...579....1F/abstract). Detecting radio-loud quasars at $z > 6.5$ remains a priority for mapping the neutral hydrogen through absorption line studies against the 21-cm forest [Jarvis et al. (2004)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2004mas..conf..133J/abstract).

**Tomography and Multi-Wavelength Synergies**
The primary obstacle to 21-cm tomography is bright galactic and extragalactic foreground emission. Chromatic instrument responses can cause smooth foreground power to spill into the theoretically pristine EoR window of Fourier space, a phenomenon dominated by the baseline-shortening "pitchfork effect" [Lanman et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.494.3712L/abstract). 

To bypass these systematics and break physical degeneracies, the field is rapidly moving toward cross-correlations with other tracers of the [[early-universe|Early universe]]. Stacking 21-cm maps around spectroscopically confirmed Lyman-$\alpha$ emitters (LAEs) can map the sizes of ionized bubbles, as LAEs preferentially reside in ionized zones that are strongly anticorrelated with the 21-cm signal [Chen et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251007374C/abstract). A critical signature of the reionization timeline is the transition from a positive to a negative cross-correlation between the 21-cm field and galaxy density, which occurs when ionization fluctuations finally dominate over spin-temperature fluctuations [Moriwaki et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.530.3183M/abstract). Further synergies include cross-correlating the 21-cm signal with line intensity mapping of [C II] [Gong et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...745...49G/abstract) or matching the squared 21-cm field with near-infrared background (NIRB) fluctuations [Sun et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241021410S/abstract), providing an independent clock for the reionization history.

## The JWST Era and the Photon Budget Crisis

The advent of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has fundamentally challenged established models of [[early-galaxy-formation|Early galaxy formation]]. Early JWST observations have revealed a surprising abundance of UV-bright galaxies at redshifts $z \gtrsim 10$, indicating that star formation in the early universe was significantly more efficient than predicted by standard $\Lambda$CDM models [Chakraborty et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...07..078C/abstract). Furthermore, these early galaxies exhibit remarkably high [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing radiation]] production efficiencies, $\xi_{\rm ion}$ [Muñoz et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240407250M/abstract). The extreme nature of these early sources is highlighted by recent discoveries such as LAP1-B at $z = 6.6$, the most chemically primitive star-forming galaxy identified to date, which possesses an ionizing radiation field consistent with a population of zero-metallicity stars [Nakajima et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250611846N/abstract). However, high-resolution analysis of sources like GS9422 ($z=5.94$) suggests that extreme spectroscopic features can often be explained by obscured Type-2 AGN rather than exotic stellar populations [Tacchella et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025MNRAS.540..851T/abstract). Additionally, JWST has identified extremely compact galaxies at very high redshifts, such as a highly magnified source at $z=9.51$ with a radius of just $\sim 16$ parsecs, displaying strong nebular emission lines and an exceptionally high star-formation rate surface density [Williams et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023Sci...380..416W/abstract). Simulations predict that galaxies detected in NIRCam wide and deep surveys are responsible for producing 40-80% of the total ionizing photon budget throughout the EoR [Yung et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.496.4574Y/abstract). Furthermore, recent tests of alternative cosmologies, such as those featuring an anti-de Sitter vacuum in the dark energy sector, suggest that modifications to the cosmological background alone are insufficient to explain the JWST galaxy excess without also invoking evolving astrophysical properties [Chakraborty et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250902431C/abstract).

When combined with traditional assumptions about the [[escape-fraction|Escape fraction]] of ionizing radiation, the prolific output of these UV-bright galaxies introduces a severe theoretical tension often termed the "photon budget crisis." If the observed population of $M_{\rm UV} < -15$ galaxies at $z \gtrsim 9$ is taken at face value, they alone would produce enough photons to complete reionization too early. A premature end to the [[reionization-history|Reionization history]] strongly conflicts with measurements of the CMB scattering optical depth and observations of the [[gunn-peterson-effect|Gunn-Peterson effect]] in the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest, which robustly anchor the conclusion of hydrogen reionization to $z \sim 6$ [Muñoz et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240407250M/abstract), [Chakraborty et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024JCAP...07..078C/abstract).

The literature currently offers conflicting, yet physically plausible, resolutions to this photon budget tension. One proposed solution involves the suppression of the [[photon-escape-fraction|Photon escape fraction]] ($f_{\rm esc}$). While JWST galaxies are efficient producers of ionizing radiation, their dense interstellar media and bursty star-formation histories may prevent these photons from ever reaching the IGM. Spectroscopic and photometric modeling of galaxies at $4.5 < z < 9.0$ suggests population-averaged escape fractions of only $\langle f_{\rm esc} \rangle \approx 3\%$, which would sufficiently throttle the photon supply to keep the reionization timeline consistent with CMB and quasar data [Papovich et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250508870P/abstract).

Conversely, the crisis may be resolved not by a lack of escaping photons, but by a larger-than-expected IGM sink. As mentioned previously, recent measurements of the ionizing photon mean free path at $z \simeq 6$ reveal surprisingly short distances of $\lambda_{\rm mfp} \approx 0.75$ proper Mpc [Davies et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...918L..35D/abstract). This implies a highly opaque IGM characterized by a large clumping factor ($C \approx 12$), which dramatically increases the requirement for complete ionization to approximately 3 to 6.1 photons per baryon. This absorption-dominated regime would naturally demand the extreme ionizing photon production observed by JWST without forcing an early end to reionization [Davies et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240618186D/abstract).

Finally, the JWST findings are reinvigorating debates over which sources actually dominate the photon budget. While canonical models argue that reionization is driven by a "democratic" population of ultra-faint galaxies, the abundance and high $\xi_{\rm ion}$ of massive UV-bright sources suggest an "oligarchic" model. In such scenarios, a small fraction ($< 5\%$) of massive galaxies with $M_{\rm UV} < -18$ could account for $\gtrsim 80\%$ of the reionization budget due to a strong correlation between star-formation surface density and $f_{\rm esc}$ [Naidu et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...892..109N/abstract). The true resolution to the photon budget tension likely requires a synthesis of these mechanisms, necessitating refined constraints on the halo-mass dependence of $f_{\rm esc}$ and the topology of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]].

## Feedback and the Suppression of Dwarf Galaxies

As the intergalactic medium undergoes [[photoionization-and-radiative-heating|Photoionization and Radiative Heating]] during cosmic reionization, the gas temperature rises to $\sim 10^4$ K. This thermal injection increases the cosmological Jeans mass, exerting a hydrostatic pressure that suppresses the accretion of fresh baryons onto low-mass dark matter halos. Consequently, this radiative feedback mechanism is widely expected to quench star formation in dwarf galaxies, particularly those residing in halos with masses $M < 10^9 M_\odot$ [Qin et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.467.1678Q/abstract). Empirical models matching high-redshift luminosity functions find that [[early-galaxy-formation|star formation efficiency]] peaks at approximately 30% in halos of $10^{11}$ to $10^{12}$ solar masses [Sun et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.460..417S/abstract). 

High-resolution hydrodynamic simulations demonstrate that the hydrostatic suppression of baryonic accretion can reduce the total mass of dwarf galaxy halos by up to a factor of two during the epoch of reionization. When combined with internal baryonic processes such as cooling, star formation, and feedback, the baryonic growth of these high-redshift halos is suppressed by 30% to 60% [Qin et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.467.1678Q/abstract). This back-reaction leaves a lasting structural imprint; semi-analytic models predict that this suppression creates a bimodality in the luminosity function of present-day satellite galaxies, separated by a distinct "valley" forged by the reionization process [Bose et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...863..123B/abstract). JWST observations of the isolated dwarf Leo P reveal a star formation pause immediately following the EoR, illustrating the long-term impact of radiative feedback on local low-mass galaxies [McQuinn et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976...60M/abstract).

However, the exact magnitude of this suppression remains a subject of active debate in the literature. Modern simulation suites highlight that standard ultraviolet background (UVB) models often overestimate the amplitude of the radiation field at high redshifts ($z > 6$), which can spuriously heat the intergalactic medium too early ($z \sim 15$). This premature heating leads to a dramatic overestimation of photoionization feedback, artificially raising the minimum halo mass required for early galaxy formation [Oñorbe et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...837..106O/abstract). Furthermore, some models—such as the Cosmic Reionization On Computers (CROC) simulations—suggest that reionization has surprisingly little effect on the global cosmic star formation history. In these models, the diffuse gas susceptible to photoionization heating lacks the molecular density required to actively form stars in the first place, leaving the core star-forming regions of early galaxies relatively unaffected [Gnedin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...793...30G/abstract), [Zhu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...942...52Z/abstract).

In addition to radiative heating, mechanical feedback from supernovae profoundly influences the evolution of dwarf galaxies. Supernovae expel gas from shallow potential wells, regulating star formation and altering the topology of the surrounding interstellar medium. This mechanical disruption is critically linked to the [[escape-fraction|Escape fraction]] of [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing photons]]. Supernova-driven shocks not only clear low-density channels for stellar radiation to escape but also generate their own highly energetic ionizing photons. The harder radiation spectra emitted from these shocks can exhibit an escape fraction up to 4 to 7 times larger than that of standard stellar photons, potentially contributing an additional $\sim 10\%$ to the global ionizing photon budget [Johnson et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...743..126J/abstract). Ultimately, the dynamic interplay between supernova feedback and star formation dictates the instantaneous [[photon-escape-fraction|Photon escape fraction]], the variations of which are expected to imprint distinct, observable signatures on the large-scale 21-cm power spectrum [Seiler et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.487.5739S/abstract).

## Computational Frontiers: Hydrodynamics and Radiative Transfer

Modeling the EoR requires capturing physics across a staggering dynamic range, coupling the cosmological expansion of the IGM to the sub-parsec scales of [[early-galaxy-formation|Early galaxy formation]] and stellar feedback. To achieve this, modern computational astrophysics employs a dual strategy: highly resolved radiation-hydrodynamics simulations and rapid, large-volume semi-numerical codes.

**Full Radiation-Hydrodynamics**
The gold standard for EoR modeling involves coupling $N$-body dark matter dynamics, baryonic hydrodynamics, and radiative transfer. The Cosmic Reionization on Computers (CROC) project exemplifies this approach, self-consistently tracking star formation and the propagation of [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing photons]] [Gnedin et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...793...30G/abstract). A major focus of such simulations is the "back-reaction" of reionization on galaxy evolution. Photoheating from the ionizing background increases the local Jeans mass, which can suppress gas accretion and reduce the baryonic mass of dwarf galaxy halos (masses $< 10^9 M_\odot$) by up to a factor of two [Qin et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.467.1678Q/abstract), [Zhu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...942...52Z/abstract). Note that the CROC simulations predict gas and stellar mass-metallicity relations that flatten at high masses, suggesting that gas accretion rates may be lower than metal production in early massive galaxies [Noel et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv221016750N/abstract).

Accurate radiative transfer is computationally expensive but crucial for capturing the topology of [[inhomogeneous-reionization|Inhomogeneous reionization]]. Earlier generations of hydrodynamic simulations that relied on spatially uniform ultraviolet background (UVB) models were found to spuriously overheat the IGM at early times ($z \sim 15$) due to an overestimation of the UVB amplitude [Oñorbe et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...837..106O/abstract). Modern self-consistent algorithms resolve this by explicitly tracking ionization fronts. To overcome the computational limits on simulation volume and mitigate cosmic variance, recent advances include "Fixed & Paired" initial conditions—which effectively increase the statistical volume by a factor of $3.5$ at no extra computational cost [Acharya et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.529.3793A/abstract)—and the FLARES (First Light And Reionisation Epoch Simulations) suite, which resimulates targeted overdense regions from a massive parent volume to build representative composite distribution functions [Lovell et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.500.2127L/abstract). The MEGATRON suite uses parsec-scale resolution and non-equilibrium chemistry to reproduce the spectroscopic diversity observed by JWST at $z > 8$ [Katz et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251005201K/abstract).

**Semi-Numerical Approaches**
While full hydrodynamics is essential for capturing non-linear baryonic physics, predicting large-scale observables—such as the 21-cm power spectrum—demands simulation boxes hundreds of megaparsecs across. Here, semi-numerical codes bridge the gap. The widely used code 21CMFAST utilizes perturbation theory and excursion-set formalism to rapidly generate 3D density, velocity, and ionization fields [Park et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484..933P/abstract). 

Recent iterations of 21CMFAST feature physically motivated parametrizations where galaxy star formation rates and the [[photon-escape-fraction|Photon escape fraction]] ($f_{\rm esc}$) scale directly with the host dark matter halo mass, allowing for rapid Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) parameter inference when compared against real observations [Park et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.484..933P/abstract). Other emerging semi-numerical frameworks, such as AMBER, forgo the standard excursion-set approach to instead assign reionization redshifts based on local radiation intensity, further accelerating large-volume mock generation for upcoming 21-cm tomography experiments [Trac et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...927..186T/abstract).

Together, these computational tools allow theorists to translate microscopic assumptions about the [[escape-fraction|Escape fraction]] and stellar feedback into macroscopic predictions for the [[global-reionization|Global reionization]] timeline and the complex topology of the high-redshift universe.

## The Final Transition: Helium II Reionization

While the hydrogen in the IGM was largely ionized by $z \approx 6$, primarily by [[stellar-ionizing-radiation|Stellar ionizing radiation]], the complete ionization of helium required a much harder radiation field. The first electron of helium is ionized at energies similar to hydrogen ($24.6$ eV) and proceeds concurrently. However, the second ionization potential of helium is $54.4$ eV (corresponding to wavelengths $\lambda < 228$ \AA). Typical star-forming galaxies produce negligibly few photons at these extreme energies. Consequently, the universe underwent a second, distinct phase transition—[[he-ii-reionization|He II reionization]]—driven by the emergence of a cosmic population of quasars [Paschos et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007arXiv0711.1904P/abstract), [Faucher-Giguère et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...703.1416F/abstract).

As the quasar luminosity function peaked at lower redshifts, the intensifying extreme-ultraviolet and soft X-ray background began carving out large He III bubbles in the IGM. Radiative transfer simulations indicate that the percolation of these He III regions was essentially complete by $z \approx 2.5$ [Paschos et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007arXiv0711.1904P/abstract). Prior to complete overlap, the [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|ionizing background]] spectrum was severely suppressed immediately above the $54.4$ eV edge due to local absorption. However, higher-energy photons ($>0.5$ keV) were able to permeate the universe more uniformly owing to the rapidly decreasing frequency dependence of the photoionization cross-section [Faucher-Giguère et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...703.1416F/abstract). The highly clustered nature of quasars also led to the formation of enormous ionized zones, some of which may have persisted as "fossil" bubbles long after the central active galactic nucleus (AGN) shut off, subject to rapid local recombination rates in the IGM [Furlanetto et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...686...25F/abstract).

The most profound consequence of this late-stage phase transition was a second era of intergalactic heating. [[photoionization-and-radiative-heating|Photoionization and Radiative Heating]] during He II reionization injected significant kinetic energy into the IGM, raising the gas temperature by a factor of approximately $1.7$ at the mean cosmic density compared to a scenario with only hydrogen reionization [Paschos et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007arXiv0711.1904P/abstract). This [[energy-injection-hypothesis|energy injection]] permanently altered the temperature-density relation of the IGM and broadened the pressure smoothing scale of the intergalactic gas. Properly modeling this heat injection is a crucial physical requirement for interpreting the small-scale structure of the hydrogen Lyman-$\alpha$ forest and avoiding the spurious early heating often seen in simplified uniform-background simulations [Oñorbe et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...837..106O/abstract).

Observationally, the progress of this transition is directly probed via the He II Lyman-$\alpha$ transition (rest-frame $304$ \AA) in the spectra of high-redshift quasars. Theoretical models employing mock sightlines drawn from hydrodynamical simulations at $\bar{z} = 2.5 \pm 0.1$ predict a mean He II Lyman-$\alpha$ transmission of $\bar{F} = 0.304 \pm 0.002$, a synthetic measurement whose extended absorption features and broadening widths are physically consistent with the observed thermal state of the expanding He III gas [Paschos et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2007arXiv0711.1904P/abstract). Furthermore, advanced cosmological techniques such as kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) tomography provide a powerful method for inferring the duration and timing of helium reionization by combining cosmic microwave background measurements with large-scale galaxy surveys [Hotinli et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023PhRvD.107j3517H/abstract). Thus, while hydrogen reionization defines the end of the cosmic dark ages, the later epoch of He II reionization represents the final global phase transition of the IGM, establishing the thermodynamic baseline of the modern universe.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

Despite significant observational and theoretical progress, the detailed timeline, topology, and driving sources of cosmic reionization remain subjects of active debate. A primary barrier to resolving the recently identified photon budget crisis is the poorly constrained [[photon-escape-fraction|Photon escape fraction]] ($f_{\rm esc}$). To definitively calibrate $f_{\rm esc}$ across different galaxy populations and test whether reionization was driven "democratically" or "oligarchically," future missions like the proposed Habitable Worlds Observatory (HWO) aim to use UV integral field spectroscopy to resolve Lyman continuum escape on $10-100$ pc scales in local galaxy analogs [Carr et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250623105C/abstract), [Citro et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250703171C/abstract).

In the radio regime, 21-cm tomography remains the most promising avenue for directly mapping the neutral intergalactic medium. Upcoming and current-generation low-frequency interferometers, including HERA and the Square Kilometre Array (SKA), are designed to measure the 21-cm power spectrum with sufficient sensitivity to differentiate between competing $f_{\rm esc}$ models based on the size and clustering of ionized bubbles [Seiler et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.487.5739S/abstract). However, extracting this faint cosmological signal requires mitigating foreground synchrotron emission that is orders of magnitude brighter [Lanman et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020MNRAS.494.3712L/abstract), [Chapman et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019arXiv190912369C/abstract).

To overcome foreground systematics and break modeling degeneracies, future analyses will heavily rely on multi-wavelength synergies. Line Intensity Mapping (LIM) of fine-structure and molecular transitions (such as [C II] and CO) provides a complementary, low-resolution tracer of the sources driving reionization [Shekhar Murmu et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022arXiv221009612S/abstract). The TIME (Tomographic Ionized-carbon Mapping Experiment) will specifically target [C II] at $6 < z < 9$ to measure star formation during the EoR while simultaneously probing molecular gas at lower redshifts [Sun et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...33S/abstract). Additionally, H-$\alpha$ line intensity mapping is emerging as a promising future probe of both the IGM and early galaxy formation, with large-scale simulations like Cosmic Dawn (CoDa) III demonstrating its utility in mapping the reionization epoch [Lee et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025RNAAS...9...96L/abstract). Cross-correlating 21-cm maps with other cosmological fields—for example, by stacking 21-cm data around spectroscopically confirmed Lyman-$\alpha$ emitters [Chen et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251007374C/abstract), or by cross-correlating the squared 21-cm field with the cosmic near-infrared background (NIRB)—offers a robust method to extract the timing and topology of reionization even in the presence of dominant foregrounds [Sun et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241021410S/abstract). To uniquely identify the onset of the reionization process, recent studies propose leveraging the Pearson cross-correlation coefficient between 21-cm maps and line-intensity maps (such as [O III] or [C II]) as a new boundary condition [Libanore et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250908886L/abstract).

Finally, the thermal history of the [[early-universe|Early universe]] remains an open question. The extent to which early X-ray sources (such as X-ray binaries or miniquasars) pre-heated the intergalactic medium dictates the amplitude of the 21-cm signal. While extreme models lacking early heating are already disfavored by limits on the IGM kinetic temperature ($T_K > 5-10$ K) [Pober et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...809...62P/abstract), determining the exact overlap between the X-ray heating epoch and the onset of [[photoionization-and-ionizing-radiation|UV reionization]] will be a primary objective for the next decade of 21-cm cosmology [Mesinger et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013MNRAS.431..621M/abstract). The thermal Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ) effect from the EoR is predicted to be a sub-dominant but potentially significant contaminant for future high-sensitivity CMB experiments on small angular scales [Iliev et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv241204385I/abstract).
