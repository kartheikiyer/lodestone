## Article

**Spatially Resolved Galaxy Analysis** is a subfield of [[extragalactic-astronomy|Extragalactic astronomy]] dedicated to characterizing the internal physical properties of galaxies on kiloparsec and sub-kiloparsec scales. Historically, observational constraints heavily relied on [[integrated-galaxy-properties|Integrated galaxy properties]], where single-aperture photometry or spectroscopy treated distant galaxies as unresolved point sources. While these global measurements established foundational [[luminosity-functions-and-scaling-relations|Luminosity Functions and Scaling Relations]], they inherently average over complex [[internal-galactic-structure|Internal Galactic Structure]] and mask the presence of heavily dust-obscured regions or underlying [[spatially-resolved-stellar-populations|Spatially resolved stellar populations]]. Modern spatially resolved analysis overcomes these limitations by utilizing high-resolution multi-band imaging and [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] (IFS). By breaking a galaxy's light down into individual spatial resolution elements (pixels or spaxels), astrophysicists can map the localized distribution of stellar mass, gas-phase metallicity, and star formation rates. This approach enables the detailed deconvolution of [[galaxy-subcomponents|Galaxy subcomponents]]—such as bulges, bars, and spiral arms—and transitions the study of [[galaxy-morphological-classification|Galaxy morphological classification]] from qualitative visual assessments to robust, quantitative physical modeling [Conselice et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ARA&A..52..291C/abstract).

A major theoretical triumph of this resolved approach has been the discovery that many [[global-observables-of-galaxies|Global observables of galaxies]] are actually the integrated manifestations of fundamental, localized physical laws [Sánchez et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ARA&A..58...99S/abstract). Spatially resolved surveys have identified tight local counterparts to global scaling relations, most notably the resolved Star Formation Main Sequence (rSFMS) and the resolved Mass-Metallicity Relation (rMZR), both often contextualized within [[luminosity-functions-and-scaling-relations|Luminosity Functions and Scaling Relations]] [Cano-Díaz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...821L..26C/abstract). By mapping [[spatially-resolved-stellar-populations|Spatially resolved stellar populations]] and applying [[traditional-sed-fitting-techniques|Traditional SED fitting techniques]] to individual resolution elements, researchers can directly observe localized evolutionary phenomena, such as the "inside-out" quenching of star-forming disks and the presence of [[negative-metallicity-gradients|Negative metallicity gradients]]. Furthermore, mapping [[galaxy-kinematics|Galaxy kinematics]] through two-dimensional velocity and dispersion profiles allows astronomers to cleanly separate the dynamically cold rotation of stellar disks from the turbulent, high-velocity multiphase outflows driven by [[seyfert-galaxies|active galactic nuclei (AGN)]]. 

This article outlines the methodologies, key findings, and theoretical implications of [[sub-galactic-scale-astrophysics|Sub-galactic Scale Astrophysics]]. It begins by tracing the historical development of morphological quantifiers, such as the [[cas-parameters|CAS parameters]] [Conselice et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003ApJS..147....1C/abstract), and the vital shift toward spatially resolved [[galaxy-mass-to-light-ratio|Galaxy mass-to-light ratio]] mapping. The discussion then explores the internal dynamics of star formation, chemical enrichment, and [[stellar-and-galactic-kinematics|Stellar and Galactic Kinematics]], detailing how internal secular evolution and external environmental impacts shape [[galaxy-structure|Galaxy structure]]. The later sections highlight recent breakthroughs in [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]], where ground-based adaptive optics [Förster Schreiber et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011Msngr.145...39F/abstract) and space-based observatories like JWST are successfully resolving kiloparsec-scale subcomponents at Cosmic Noon and parsec-scale star clusters at the Epoch of Reionization [Abdurro'uf et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251208054A/abstract). Finally, the article previews pressing open questions—such as the contested universality of a resolved Fundamental Metallicity Relation (rFMR) and the exact feedback efficiency of AGN outflows—that will drive the next generation of [[deep-spectroscopic-surveys|Deep spectroscopic surveys]] and extremely large telescopes.

## Historical Development and the Morphological Paradigm

Historically, the study of [[galaxy-structure|Galaxy structure]] was primarily qualitative, relying on the visual Hubble sequence established in the 1920s and 30s. This system categorized galaxies into ellipticals, spirals, and irregulars based on apparent features such as smooth light profiles, spiral arms, and central bulges. While foundational, visual classification is inherently subjective. It also suffers from strong wavelength dependence and surface brightness dimming, making it difficult to apply consistently to faint or high-redshift samples. 

To move beyond qualitative descriptions, the field transitioned toward quantitative [[galaxy-morphological-classification|Galaxy morphological classification]]. This capacity to trace large-scale structure and galaxy evolution fundamentally builds on early wide-field [[spectroscopy-surveys|Spectroscopy surveys]] [Colless et al. (1996)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1997ASSL..212..227C/abstract) and the historical shift toward comparing [[differential-galaxy-counts|Differential galaxy counts]] and spectral energy distributions (SEDs) with evolutionary models to understand [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|high-redshift morphology]] [Bruzual et al. (1988)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1988ASSL..141..161B/abstract). The creation of foundational datasets, such as the 2MASS Large Galaxy Atlas, provided uniform [[panchromatic-surveys|near-infrared (NIR) imaging]] that proved crucial for examining [[internal-galactic-structure|Internal Galactic Structure]] like bars and bulges consistently across all Hubble types [Jarrett et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003AJ....125..525J/abstract). 

As the field advanced, the importance of automated [[morphological-parameter-distributions|Morphological parameter distributions]] in large digital surveys became paramount for disentangling complex [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|galaxy assembly]] histories [Wadadekar et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012arXiv1201.2252W/abstract). Initially, this was dominated by parametric modeling, most notably Sersic fitting, which models the two-dimensional surface brightness profile of a galaxy to extract fundamental parameters such as the effective half-light radius ($R_e$) and the Sérsic index ($n$) (see [[structural-concentration-and-profiles|Structural Concentration and Profiles]] and [[galaxy-shapes|Galaxy shapes]]). Automated pipelines enabled the application of these parametric fits to large galaxy surveys [Häußler et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ASPC..442..155H/abstract). For instance, the [[gama-survey|GAMA survey]] facilitated automated [[galaxy-subcomponents|multi-component structural decomposition]] across nine bands spanning the [[nuv-luminosity-function|near-ultraviolet]] to the NIR [Kelvin et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010AIPC.1240..247K/abstract). Concurrently, the Spitzer Survey of Stellar Structure in Galaxies (S4G) applied Concentration, Gini, and M20 parameters to specifically quantify NIR morphology and resolve [[stellar-distributions|stellar mass structure]] [Holwerda et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...781...12H/abstract). This quantitative approach was instrumental in demonstrating, for instance, that galaxies at [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|high redshift]] are a factor of $2$ to $5$ smaller at a given stellar mass than their local counterparts [Conselice et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ARA&A..52..291C/abstract).

However, symmetric parametric models often fail to capture the complex, disturbed morphologies of merging or highly star-forming systems. This limitation drove the development of model-independent, non-parametric structural quantifiers. The [[cas-parameters|CAS parameters]] define a three-dimensional parameter space that cleanly distinguishes major galaxy classes and traces evolutionary modes, such as recent star formation and major merging activity, out to $z \sim 3$ [Conselice et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003ApJS..147....1C/abstract). Alongside CAS, the Gini-M20 index—measuring the inequality of the flux distribution (Gini) and the second-order moment of the brightest $20\%$ of pixels ($M_{20}$)—became a standard tool for identifying [[morphological-parameter-distributions|structural disturbances]] [Conselice et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ARA&A..52..291C/abstract). Despite their widespread adoption, the literature highlights a notable tension: standard CAS and Gini-M20 metrics can be insensitive to faint, asymmetric outer structures such as tidal tails. This has necessitated the development of newer indices that specifically isolate the [[galaxy-outskirts|Galaxy outskirts]] to better capture subtle dynamical phases [Wen et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...787..130W/abstract). Furthermore, to circumvent the effects of projection and dust, linear brightness profiles have been introduced; these models provide inclination-independent metrics through [[isophotal-analysis|Isophotal analysis]] [Devour et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJS..244....3D/abstract).

The most recent paradigm shift in morphological analysis is the transition from light-based structural measurements to spatially resolved mass mapping. Because galactic light is often dominated by young, luminous stellar populations and modulated by complex dust obscuration, optical morphology does not strictly trace the underlying [[stellar-distributions|Stellar distributions]]. Utilizing pixel-by-pixel [[traditional-sed-fitting-techniques|Traditional SED fitting techniques]], researchers can now map spatial variations in the [[galaxy-mass-to-light-ratio|Galaxy mass-to-light ratio]] ($\Upsilon$) across the extent of a galaxy [Zibetti et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IAUS..262...89Z/abstract), [Lanyon-Foster et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.424.1852L/abstract). These resolved mass maps reveal that galaxies are significantly smoother in mass than at any individual optical or near-infrared wavelength [Zibetti et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IAUS..262...89Z/abstract). Crucially, [[integrated-galaxy-properties|Integrated galaxy properties]] derived from unresolved, single-aperture photometry are biased low by up to $40\%$ compared to the integrated totals of resolved mass maps. This discrepancy arises because highly dust-obscured regions are underrepresented in global colors [Zibetti et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IAUS..262...89Z/abstract). Consequently, spatially resolved mass analysis has superseded traditional light-based classifications as the most robust tracer of true physical structure.

## Methodologies in Resolved Spectro-Photometry

The transition from integrated global measurements to spatially resolved analysis relies on constructing detailed two-dimensional maps of galactic properties. The technical approaches to this challenge generally fall into two broad categories: photometric mapping via high-resolution [[panchromatic-surveys|multi-band imaging]], and combined spectral-spatial mapping via integral-field units. 

*Pixel-by-pixel SED fitting* leverages multi-band photometric data, often utilizing codes like [[pixedfit|piXedfit]], to measure spatially varying colors and apply stellar population synthesis models to individual resolution elements. An invaluable resource for these analyses is the comprehensive atlas of 129 galaxy SEDs, which provides a key baseline for [[monochromatic-sfr-estimators|Monochromatic SFR estimators]] and K-corrections [Brown et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJS..212...18B/abstract). In more specific applications, a multiband atlas of Blue Compact Dwarf (BCD) galaxies employs focused H$\alpha$ and U-band imaging to successfully identify and map localized star-forming knots [Cairós et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001ApJS..136..393C/abstract). Additionally, estimating the total infrared (TIR) luminosity in resolved subregions frequently relies on Spitzer 8 and 24 $\mu$m bands; notably, these resolved estimates are significantly influenced by local [[chemical-abundances|oxygen abundance]] and the intensity of star formation [Boquien et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...713..626B/abstract). This technique corrects severe biases inherent to the [[integrated-light-method|Integrated light method]]. For example, integrated photometry systematically underestimates total stellar mass by up to 40% in local galaxies because bright, young star-forming regions outshine older, dust-obscured populations [Zibetti et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IAUS..262...89Z/abstract). This masking effect is even more extreme at [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|high redshifts]]. In early galaxies, integrated light can yield extremely young inferred [[galaxy-stellar-age|stellar ages]], whereas [[spatially-resolved-stellar-populations|Spatially resolved stellar populations]] uncover underlying older stellar populations, ultimately increasing estimated [[galaxy-mass-to-light-ratio|stellar masses]] [Giménez-Arteaga et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948..126G/abstract). Furthermore, pixel-by-pixel mapping of [[monochromatic-sfr-estimators|specific star formation rates (sSFR)]] provides direct empirical support for [[accelerated-galaxy-evolution|inside-out quenching]] scenarios [Jafariyazani et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...887..204J/abstract).

To capture complex [[galaxy-kinematics|gas kinematics]], [[stellar-and-galactic-kinematics|stellar kinematics]], and [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] simultaneously, [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] (IFS) is routinely employed. Large-scale surveys like the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] and SAMI have produced continuous 2D spectral maps for thousands of galaxies [Croom et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021MNRAS.505..991C/abstract), [Sánchez et al. (2021)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021RMxAA..57....3S/abstract). For extracting broader trends from massive datasets, researchers apply [[non-parametric-methods-in-astrophysics|Principal Component Analysis (PCA)]]. For instance, PCA-based methods were utilized to develop the [[integrated-spectral-type|'eta' parameter]] as a continuous measure of galaxy spectral type [Madgwick et al. (2001)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2001astro.ph.10220M/abstract). IFS is highly effective for the deconvolution of [[galaxy-subcomponents|Galaxy subcomponents]]. By evaluating the line-of-sight velocity dispersion alongside traditional emission-line ratios, researchers can kinematically isolate dynamically cold [[thick-disks|thin disks]] from [[seyfert-galaxies|active galactic nucleus (AGN)]] outflows [Law et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...35L/abstract). Detailed resolved IFS analyses also identify distinct behavioral tracks governed by critical local surface densities; below a threshold of $\log \Sigma_{M_*} \sim 7.5$, local stochastic processes dominate the scatter of the spatially resolved star-forming main sequence [Hall et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...865..154H/abstract).

Handling the massive datasets generated by resolved spectro-photometry requires automated morphological and structural parameterization. Tools like [[galfit|GALFIT]] fit two-dimensional surface brightness profiles to computationally separate bulges, disks, and bars without manual intervention [Häußler et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ASPC..442..155H/abstract). Additionally, non-parametric metrics—such as the concentration, asymmetry, and clumpiness [[cas-parameters|CAS parameters]]—are extracted from resolved maps [Conselice et al. (2003)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2003ApJS..147....1C/abstract). When galaxies are resolved into individual stars, researchers can directly derive localized [[galaxy-stellar-age|luminosity-weighted mean ages]] to reconstruct detailed star-formation histories [Kim et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...753...26K/abstract). The emergence of specialized open-source tools further facilitates [[photometry-and-redshift-estimation|numerical color-magnitude diagram (CMD) fitting]] to infer these histories computationally [Zheng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJS..279...12Z/abstract).

For [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|early-universe observations]], spatial resolution limits are frequently overcome by coupling ground-based spectrographs with [[high-resolution-spectroscopy|adaptive optics]]. Instruments such as VLT/SINFONI have successfully mapped internal kinematics and AGN-driven outflows in $z \sim 2.4$ systems [Fischer et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...875..102F/abstract), [Förster Schreiber et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011Msngr.145...39F/abstract). At the highest spatial resolutions, modern imaging capabilities have achieved significant milestones closer to home, such as the resolution of [[discrete-sources|intricate bow shocks]] around Galactic Center sources, and the discovery of the first spatially resolved X-ray supernova remnant in the [[andromeda-galaxy-and-satellites|Andromeda Galaxy]] [Kong et al. (2002)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...580L.125K/abstract). 

## Spatially Resolved Star Formation and Quenching

Spatially resolved observations using [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] have demonstrated that global [[luminosity-functions-and-scaling-relations|Luminosity Functions and Scaling Relations]] describing star formation are largely the integrated result of kiloparsec-scale physical processes [Sánchez et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ARA&A..58...99S/abstract). By mapping galaxies on a pixel-by-pixel basis, researchers have recovered a tight resolved Star Formation Main Sequence (rSFMS) that correlates the star formation rate surface density with the [[structural-concentration-and-profiles|stellar mass surface density]] [Cano-Díaz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...821L..26C/abstract). Interestingly, the resolved Star Formation Main Sequence exists independently within both the blue star-forming and red quiescent regions of the [[galactic-bimodality|Galactic Bimodality]] [Hemmati et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...797..108H/abstract).

In the local universe, the rSFMS remains consistent regardless of the host galaxy's integrated mass [Cano-Díaz et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...821L..26C/abstract). This fundamental relation persists at higher redshifts; at $z \sim 0.26$, the rSFMS maintains a similar shape, though the average [[chemical-abundances|metallicity]] drops compared to local counterparts [Yao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661A.112Y/abstract). In multi-wavelength resolved studies of specific local systems, images demonstrate that the number of [[discrete-sources|ultraluminous X-ray sources (ULXs)]] strongly correlates with local star formation rate densities [Mineo et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...771..133M/abstract).

The analysis of [[spatially-resolved-stellar-populations|Spatially resolved stellar populations]] provides direct evidence for the [[accelerated-galaxy-evolution|inside-out quenching]] of galactic disks. The radial profiles of [[monochromatic-sfr-estimators|specific star formation rates (sSFR)]] in transitioning galaxies typically show a dramatic increase from the center to the periphery [Jafariyazani et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...887..204J/abstract). The role of the local environment in spatially resolved quenching remains complex. Observations of cluster environments, such as those from [[fornax-cluster-and-galaxy-studies|Fornax Cluster and Galaxy Studies]], demonstrate that cluster galaxies can be up to $50\%$ smaller than field galaxies, supporting an "outside-in" quenching model where [[cluster-substructure|ram pressure]] strips loosely bound gas from the [[galaxy-outskirts|Galaxy outskirts]] [Chamba et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A..28C/abstract).

Beyond bulk evolution, spatially resolved analysis is critical for the environmental characterization of [[discrete-sources|cosmic transients]]. For instance, [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] data demonstrates that superluminous supernovae (SLSNe) can occur in high-metallicity environments [Chen et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...849L...4C/abstract). Similar techniques applied to [[discrete-sources|gamma-ray burst (GRB)]] hosts reveal that localized explosion sites typically possess metallicities representative of the entire host galaxy [Thorp et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018ApJ...856...36T/abstract).

## Chemical Enrichment and Metallicity Gradients

The [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] of a galaxy—traceable through both gas-phase metallicity via [[emission-line-galaxies|Emission-line galaxies]] and stellar populations via absorption line [[lick-indices|Lick indices]]—serve as a fossil record of its chemical enrichment history. Maps of [[spatially-resolved-stellar-populations|Spatially resolved stellar populations]] and ionized gas have demonstrated that local processes govern galactic chemical evolution [Sánchez et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021RMxAA..57....3S/abstract).

### Radial Abundance Gradients
In the local Universe, [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] from programs like the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] has established that [[late-type-galaxies-and-stellar-populations|undisturbed disk galaxies]] characteristically exhibit [[negative-metallicity-gradients|Negative metallicity gradients]]. These gradients extend across both the stellar and gas phases. Early studies of early-type galaxies quantified stellar metallicity gradients that largely account for observed radial [[color-gradients|Color gradients]] [Cimatti et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020igfe.book.....C/abstract). 

For the ionized gas, robust negative [[chemical-abundances|oxygen abundance]] gradients strongly support an "inside-out" growth paradigm [Barrera-Ballesteros et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023RMxAA..59..213B/abstract). However, this structured radial enrichment is easily disrupted during galaxy-galaxy interactions. Spatially resolved studies of [[disturbed-clusters|post-merger galaxies]] indicate that tidally induced inflows drive relatively metal-poor gas into the central regions, flattening the overall radial profile [Thorp et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.482L..55T/abstract).

### The Spatially Resolved Mass-Metallicity Relation (rMZR)
The global relationship between a galaxy's total stellar mass and its integrated metallicity arises from a fundamental local scaling relation known as the spatially resolved mass-metallicity relation (rMZR). At kiloparsec scales, the local stellar mass surface density strongly correlates with the local gas-phase metallicity, a relationship often categorized under [[luminosity-functions-and-scaling-relations|Luminosity Functions and Scaling Relations]] [Barrera-Ballesteros et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020BAAA...61C...1B/abstract). Observations confirm that the rMZR evolves with cosmic time, maintaining its shape but shifting in normalization [Yao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661A.112Y/abstract).

### Tensions in the Resolved Fundamental Metallicity Relation
While the rMZR is well-established, the existence of a spatially resolved Fundamental Metallicity Relation (rFMR)—incorporating the [[monochromatic-sfr-estimators|resolved star formation rate surface density]]—remains a subject of debate. The literature remains ambiguous as to whether the rFMR is a universal physical constraint governing chemical enrichment history or an artifact of local sample selection [Yao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661A.112Y/abstract).

## Internal Kinematics and Mass Distribution

The dynamical state of galaxies is best constrained through spatially resolved [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]], which provides two-dimensional maps of kinematics. Historically, galaxy kinematics were summarized by global [[luminosity-functions-and-scaling-relations|Luminosity Functions and Scaling Relations]] like the [[d-sigma-relation|D-sigma relation]]. However, mapping these properties on a kiloparsec scale reveals a much more complex interplay. [[galaxy-kinematics|Spatially resolved rotation curves]] and [[width-function-astrophysics|H I velocity widths]] provide independent measures of global mass distribution.

### Gas and Stellar Kinematics
Spatially resolved surveys demonstrate that the kinematics of the ionized gas and the collisionless stellar populations often behave differently. Regions ionized by [[seyfert-galaxies|active galactic nuclei]] display significantly broader velocity distributions indicating disturbed gas [Law et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...35L/abstract). On larger scales, [[galaxy-property-distributions|pairwise velocity dispersion (PVD)]] serves as a stringent test for galaxy formation models, with observations from the [[gama-survey|GAMA survey]] highlighting correlations with luminosity [Loveday et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2018MNRAS.474.3435L/abstract).

Reconciling the rotational velocities of the stellar disk with those of the ionized gas requires applying an asymmetric drift correction within the [[stellar-and-galactic-kinematics|Stellar and Galactic Kinematics]] framework [Catalán-Torrecilla et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ApJ...890....5C/abstract). Expanding [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|kinematically resolved analysis]] to the neutral gas phase, surveys utilize kinematic models to derive robust gas-phase [[luminosity-functions-and-scaling-relations|Luminosity Functions and Scaling Relations]] [Deg et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...976..159D/abstract). To fully capture these complex gas motions, astronomers increasingly utilize 3D visualization techniques like [[galpak-3d|GalPaK 3D]] [Koribalski et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015IAUS..309...39K/abstract). The connection between gas content and stellar mass informs major wide-field statistical studies, such as the [[resolve-survey|Resolve survey]] [Kannappan et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008AIPC.1035..163K/abstract).

### Mass-to-Light Ratio Maps and Dust
To trace true underlying structure, observers convert light distributions into [[structural-concentration-and-profiles|stellar mass surface density maps]]. By applying pixel-by-pixel SED fitting, researchers generate spatially resolved [[galaxy-mass-to-light-ratio|Galaxy mass-to-light ratio]] maps [Lanyon-Foster et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012MNRAS.424.1852L/abstract). Resolved maps show that the stellar mass distribution is remarkably smoother than optical light, as optical light is modulated by recent star formation and dust attenuation [Zibetti et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010IAUS..262...89Z/abstract). 

### Dark Matter and Central Black Hole Mass
In early-type galaxies, resolving the stellar velocity dispersion profile is a primary method for probing [[galaxy-cluster-mass-profiles|dark matter fractions]] [Cimatti et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020igfe.book.....C/abstract). However, breaking the mass-anisotropy degeneracy requires measuring higher-order moments of the velocity profile through [[non-parametric-methods-in-astrophysics|Non-parametric methods in astrophysics]] [Cimatti et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020igfe.book.....C/abstract). At the galactic core, central stellar kinematics allow for the independent derivation of supermassive black hole masses, providing a tool to validate mass estimates from [[spectroscopy-surveys|reverberation mapping]] [Gebhardt et al. (2000)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000ApJ...543L...5G/abstract).

## AGN Feedback and Multiphase Outflows

[[seyfert-galaxies|Active galactic nuclei (AGN)]] exert profound influences on their host galaxies through AGN feedback. [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] has become essential for mapping these multiphase outflows. Using data from the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]], researchers demonstrated that the velocity dispersion of ionized gas strongly correlates with diagnostic line ratios [Law et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...915...35L/abstract).

High-resolution imaging and [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|Resolved spectroscopic observations]] reveal that AGN-driven outflows can extend well beyond $10$ kpc [Fischer et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...887..200F/abstract). These winds often produce shocks that manifest as [[x-ray-detected-galaxies|thermal X-ray emission]] [Fischer et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019ApJ...887..200F/abstract). Spatially resolved [[spectroscopy-surveys|near-infrared spectroscopy]] of luminous infrared galaxies has successfully linked supernova-driven outflows to extranuclear emission, separating starburst phenomena from the central AGN [Colina et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...749..116C/abstract). In weakly active systems like [[low-dispersion-galaxies|LINER galaxies]], resolved spectroscopy unravels complex feedback geometries [Bae et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2012ApJ...753...10B/abstract).

Spatially resolved studies using [[extragalactic-astronomical-surveys|Chandra]] have been critical in mapping excitation processes in canonical targets. Despite detailed mapping, the direct impact of AGN feedback on star formation remains a subject of debate. [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]] suggest that weak AGN activity may have a negligible effect on star formation, as seen in lensed systems [Fischer et al. ## Environmental Impacts on Resolved Structure

The evolution of [[galaxy-structure|Galaxy structure]] is tied to the local environment, where gravitational interactions and hydrodynamic processes imprint signatures onto a galaxy's profiles. 

**Gravitational Interactions and Mergers**
Galaxy-galaxy [[disturbed-clusters|mergers]] and tidal encounters physically distort stellar distributions. While major disruptions are visually obvious, quantifying them requires robust [[morphological-parameter-distributions|Morphological parameter distributions]] like the Gini-$M_{20}$ index. Tracing dynamically active [[galaxy-outskirts|Galaxy outskirts]] requires targeted indices to measure asymmetry [Wen et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014ApJ...787..130W/abstract), while disk lopsidedness is quantified through [[isophotal-analysis|Isophotal analysis]] as utilized in the [[carnegie-irvine-galaxy-survey|Carnegie-Irvine Galaxy Survey]] [Li et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJS..197...22L/abstract). Mapping [[spatially-resolved-stellar-populations|Spatially resolved stellar populations]] using the [[manga-survey|MaNGA survey]] reveals that interactions drive centrally peaked [[monochromatic-sfr-estimators|star formation rate (SFR)]] enhancements [Thorp et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2019MNRAS.482L..55T/abstract). Resolved mapping is also used to characterize the environments of [[gravitational-wave-galaxy-catalogue|gravitational wave events]] [Li et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023RAA....23g5007L/abstract).

**Hydrodynamic Stripping and Cluster Environments**
In dense regions, hydrodynamic forces like [[cluster-substructure|ram pressure stripping]] and [[low-redshift-large-scale-structure|cosmic web stripping]] remove gas and truncate star formation. One of the most profound signatures of environmental stripping is the truncation of the galactic disk. Spatially resolved scaling relations demonstrate that cluster galaxies possess radii approximately 50% smaller than field counterparts, reflecting truncation at the [[galaxy-outskirts|Galaxy outskirts]] [Chamba et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A..28C/abstract). However, environmental [[accelerated-galaxy-evolution|quenching]] manifest in conflicting ways; while ram pressure dictates outside-in truncation, other studies note that elevated densities primarily act to suppress nuclear star formation [Welikala et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...677..970W/abstract). 

## Resolved Analysis at High Redshift

At [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|Cosmic Noon]], galaxies are characterized by high gas fractions and profound instabilities [Förster Schreiber et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2020ARA&A..58..661F/abstract). Early [[resolved-spectroscopic-observations|Resolved spectroscopic observations]] revealed that these massive systems are dominated by [[spatially-extended-star-formation|kiloparsec-scale star-forming clumps]] [Förster Schreiber et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011Msngr.145...39F/abstract). To probe scales below the seeing limit, researchers rely on gravitational lensing, often utilizing the [[sloan-giant-arcs-survey|Sloan Giant Arcs Survey]]. 

The ability to trace mass across cosmic time has been improved by [[extragalactic-astronomical-surveys|deep imaging surveys like UltraVISTA]], which play a critical role in determining the [[galaxy-luminosity-function|Galaxy luminosity function]] across redshifts [McCracken et al. (2013)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013Msngr.154...29M/abstract). Within these populations, [[extremely-red-objects|Extremely red objects]] (EROs) and distant red galaxies exhibit strong spatial clustering [Kim et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.410..241K/abstract). In the densest regions, dynamical analyses of high-redshift [[galaxy-cluster-properties-and-scaling-relations|galaxy clusters]] confirm that velocity dispersion remains a robust mass proxy even at $z = 1.2$ [Abdullah et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251209303A/abstract).

JWST [[high-redshift-galaxy-observations|High-Redshift Galaxy Observations]] have pushed into the [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|Epoch of Reionization]]. Observations demonstrate that early assembly is characterized by highly localized, bursty star formation rather than monolithic growth [Giménez-Arteaga et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948..126G/abstract). A critical revelation is the systematic bias in measuring [[integrated-galaxy-properties|Integrated galaxy properties]] at these epochs. [[traditional-sed-fitting-techniques|Traditional SED fitting techniques]] applied to single-aperture photometry often drastically underestimate the true stellar mass by failing to account for older populations hidden behind recent starbursts [Giménez-Arteaga et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...948..126G/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

A primary open question is the universality of spatially resolved [[luminosity-functions-and-scaling-relations|Luminosity Functions and Scaling Relations]] across cosmic time. Determining whether the lack of a high-redshift resolved Fundamental Metallicity Relation is a physical reality remains a priority for [[high-redshift-galaxy-evolution|High-Redshift Galaxy Evolution]] studies [Yao et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...661A.112Y/abstract).

Another tension surrounds the feedback efficiency of [[seyfert-galaxies|active galactic nuclei (AGN)]]. High-resolution [[integral-field-spectroscopy|Integral Field Spectroscopy]] reveals that AGN-driven outflows can extend over kiloparsec scales [Evans et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ASPC..427...97E/abstract), but their direct impact remains ambiguous. While theory suggests quenching, localized enhancements in star formation at the outskirts of radio bubbles suggest a more complex reality [Xu et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...971..165X/abstract).

Resolving these discrepancies requires pushing toward true parsec-scale resolution. Resolving individual populations in galaxies like [[andromeda-galaxy-and-satellites|Andromeda]] with the same fidelity applied to the Milky Way will require the next generation of ELTs. Future surveys like the [[local-volume-and-nearby-galaxy-studies|Local Volume and Nearby Galaxy Studies]] and next-generation [[deep-spectroscopic-surveys|Deep spectroscopic surveys]] aim to bridge the gap between parsec-scale internal processes and [[global-observables-of-galaxies|Global observables of galaxies]], continuously connecting star formation to the circumgalactic medium [Fahrion et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv251214281F/abstract).
