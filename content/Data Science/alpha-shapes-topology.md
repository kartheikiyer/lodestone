In the context of [[topological-data-analysis-in-astronomy|Topological data analysis in astronomy]], **alpha shapes** provide a rigorous geometric framework for characterizing the multi-scale morphology of the cosmic web. Rather than relying on fixed-scale smoothing techniques like traditional [[isodensity-analysis|Isodensity Analysis]], an alpha shape is constructed as a tunable subset of the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]] applied directly to discrete point distributions, such as galaxy catalogs or $N$-body simulation particles [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]]. By restricting the Delaunay simplices to only those with a circumradius smaller than a specified threshold parameter, $\alpha$, this method generates a family of piecewise-linear geometric objects that naturally adapt to the local density of the data. This discrete construction enables astrophysicists to explicitly preserve the hierarchical, multiscale nature of the universe's large-scale structure without the artificial suppression of small-scale features common to continuous density fields.

The true analytical power of alpha shapes emerges when the refinement parameter $\alpha$ is systematically increased from zero to infinity, generating an ordered sequence of nested subcomplexes known as a filtration. This filtration provides the mathematical foundation for applying [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] to cosmological datasets [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]]. Within this framework, the topological invariants of the cosmic web are quantified using Betti numbers ($\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2$), which independently count the number of connected components (clusters), one-dimensional tunnels (filaments), and enclosed cavities (voids). By utilizing [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] to track the creation and destruction (or "persistence" and lifetime) of these topological features over the $\alpha$-filtration across all spatial scales, algorithms such as [[disperse|DISPERSE]] can successfully isolate robust, physically significant structures from random shot noise. This offers a vastly richer morphological description than early aggregate metrics like the genus statistic or [[partial-minkowski-functionals|Partial Minkowski Functionals]].

Alpha shape topology has evolved into an essential tool for constraining fundamental physics and cosmology. The multi-scale nature of persistent Betti numbers provides higher-order structural information capable of distinguishing primordial non-Gaussianity from the amplitude of matter fluctuations, testing modified gravity theories, and tracing the complex, non-linear ionization stages of the Epoch of Reionization. However, the application of these discrete topological methods to modern observational surveys presents ongoing challenges. Current research is actively investigating "topological bias"—the phenomenon where the multiscale topology of luminous galaxies systematically diverges from that of their underlying dark matter halos—as well as the sensitivity of these networks to variable sampling densities and survey selection functions. Resolving these biasing effects and theoretical limitations remains a critical open question as the field prepares to map the ultimate coherent extent of the cosmic web using massive data volumes from next-generation observatories like Euclid, DESI, and the Vera Rubin Observatory.

## Mathematical Foundations of Alpha Shapes

In the topological analysis of discrete point distributions, such as galaxy catalogues or dark matter particles in $N$-body simulations, the continuous underlying density field is not known a priori. To mathematically define the topology of such a discrete set, one typically relies on the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]] (or Delaunay triangulation). The Delaunay triangulation provides a natural, parameter-free geometric construction that partitions space into a set of space-filling simplices (tetrahedra in three dimensions) based on the mutual proximity of the points. Its dual structure, the [[poisson-voronoi-tessellation|Poisson Voronoi Tessellation]], provides a versatile mathematical model for the stochastic geometry and [[cellular-universe|Cellular universe]] of the cosmic foam [[bibcode-2002astro-ph-6427v|bibcode:2002astro.ph..6427V]].

Alpha shapes are a family of piecewise-linear geometric objects derived from this discrete set. An alpha shape is strictly defined as a subset of the Delaunay triangulation, governed by a single length scale, $\alpha$ [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]]. Given a discrete set of points $S$, the associated alpha complex $K_\alpha$ is constructed by restricting the simplices of the Delaunay triangulation to those whose circumradii $R$ satisfy $R \le \alpha$. As $\alpha$ varies, it acts as a tunable parameter that defines a sequence of valid simplicial complexes from the discrete point distribution [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]].

A fundamental mathematical property of the alpha shape complex is its homotopy equivalence to the union of spherical balls centered on the data points. If one places a solid sphere of radius $\alpha$ around each point in the discrete distribution, the union of these spheres forms a continuous spatial volume, commonly referred to as the sublevel set of the distance field. The discrete alpha complex $K_\alpha$ is homotopy equivalent to this continuous space [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]], [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]]. Consequently, the alpha shape and the continuous union of balls share the exact same topological invariants. These invariants are typically quantified by the Betti numbers ($\beta_k$), which formalize the number of connected components ($\beta_0$), one-dimensional tunnels or loops ($\beta_1$), and enclosed voids ($\beta_2$) [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]].

Because alpha shapes are directly derived from the Delaunay triangulation, they are natively scale-adaptive. This avoids the artificial suppression of small-scale topological information that often plagues continuous density fields smoothed with fixed-scale Gaussian kernels. Furthermore, as the tunable parameter $\alpha$ is monotonically increased from zero to infinity, the corresponding alpha complexes grow from a totally disconnected set of individual points to the full, convex Delaunay triangulation of the entire point set. This ordered sequence of nested subsets, where $K_{\alpha_1} \subseteq K_{\alpha_2}$ for $\alpha_1 \le \alpha_2$, constitutes a filtration [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]].

The filtration of alpha shapes provides the rigorous mathematical foundation required for [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]]. By evaluating the filtration across all possible values of $\alpha$, astrophysicists can track the creation (birth) and destruction (death) of topological features as a function of scale. This allows for a comprehensive, multiscale characterization of the cosmic web that successfully isolates robust physical structures from random shot noise [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]].

## Historical Evolution: From Genus to Persistent Homology

The historical evolution of topological analysis in cosmology is characterized by a paradigm shift from global, single-scale continuous measurements to discrete, multi-scale frameworks driven by [[topological-data-analysis-in-astronomy|Topological data analysis in astronomy]].

In the late 1980s and 1990s, early non-linear clustering analysis heavily relied on statistical solutions and geometric models such as the Voronoi kinematic model and the [[minimal-spanning-tree|Minimal spanning tree]] (MST) to approximate structure formation [[bibcode-1995phr-262-1s|bibcode:1995PhR...262....1S]]. Alongside these, the primary continuous tools for quantifying the topology of the large-scale structure were the genus statistic and Minkowski functionals. Early topological studies of the CfA Redshift Survey first identified a spongelike topology on large linear scales and filamentary coherence on small non-linear scales [[bibcode-1994apj-420-525v|bibcode:1994ApJ...420..525V]]. Formulated for continuous density fields, the genus statistic computes the Euler-Poincaré characteristic of isodensity surfaces. For a Gaussian random field representing random-phase initial conditions, the genus per unit volume as a function of the density threshold $\nu$ follows a universal theoretical curve:
$$ g_s(\nu) \propto (1 - \nu^2)e^{-\nu^2/2} $$
[[bibcode-1987apj-319-1g|bibcode:1987ApJ...319....1G]], [[bibcode-1989apj-340-625g|bibcode:1989ApJ...340..625G]]. Early topology analyses, such as those applied to the IRAS Point Source Catalogue Redshift Survey (PSCz), provided critical observational evidence supporting these random-phase initial conditions on scales of $10\text{--}32 \ h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$ [[bibcode-1998mnras-297-777c|bibcode:1998MNRAS.297..777C]]. While the genus statistic proved to be a robust cosmic standard ruler—largely insensitive to mild redshift-space distortions and monotonic galaxy biasing [[bibcode-2010apj-715l-185p|bibcode:2010ApJ...715L.185P]]—it fundamentally relied on mapping the discrete galaxy distribution to a continuous field via fixed-scale Gaussian smoothing. This fixed-scale approach inherently suppresses hierarchical, small-scale topological information and introduces a dependence on the chosen smoothing radius [[bibcode-2011mnras-416-2494p|bibcode:2011MNRAS.416.2494P]].

To bypass the limitations of Gaussian smoothing, researchers increasingly adopted parameter-free, adaptive density estimators built on the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]], such as the Delaunay Tessellation Field Estimator (DTFE). Studies demonstrated that DTFE preserves the multi-scale, hierarchical nature of the cosmic web significantly better than fixed-scale kernels, allowing for the extraction of a larger amount of topological information from both simulations and observational surveys [[bibcode-2010apj-722-812z|bibcode:2010ApJ...722..812Z]], [[bibcode-2011mnras-416-2494p|bibcode:2011MNRAS.416.2494P]].

The use of Delaunay triangulations naturally paved the way for the adoption of alpha shapes. Rather than condensing the topology of a smoothed field into a single aggregated Euler characteristic, alpha shapes enabled the explicit calculation of individual Betti numbers directly from the discrete spatial distribution of galaxies [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]]. By operating as a subset of the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]] governed by a scale parameter $\alpha$, alpha shapes allowed cosmologists to independently quantify the number of components, tunnels, and voids [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]]. This disaggregation provided a substantially richer morphological description of the cosmic web than early genus curves.

Ultimately, the parameter $\alpha$ serves as a filtration variable, providing the mathematical foundation for applying [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] to cosmology [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]]. By simultaneously tracking the "birth" and "death" of topological features across an ordered sequence of nested simplicial complexes, persistent homology completely removes the need for an arbitrary spatial smoothing scale. This multi-scale approach allows for the rigorous separation of robust physical structures from sampling noise [[bibcode-2017mnras-465-4281p|bibcode:2017MNRAS.465.4281P]]. Today, the legacy of early genus statistics has evolved into sophisticated, discrete algorithms, exemplified by software like [[disperse|DISPERSE]], which uses discrete Morse-Smale complexes and persistence to extract the cosmic web directly from noisy datasets [[bibcode-2013ascl-soft02015s|bibcode:2013ascl.soft02015S]]. 

While the field generally agrees on the superiority of these discrete topological methods over Gaussian smoothing, the literature remains somewhat divided on the optimal statistical summaries for persistence data. Current methodologies variously favor Betti curves as a function of the alpha parameter [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]] versus persistence fields and diagrams that track feature lifetimes [[bibcode-2019mnras-486-1523e|bibcode:2019MNRAS.486.1523E]]. Regardless of the chosen summary, the transition from the genus statistic to alpha shapes and persistent homology represents a fundamental upgrade in the precision and capabilities of topological cosmology.

## Characterizing the Multi-scale Cosmic Web

The cosmic web is a complex, hierarchical network of discrete structures. Early characterizations of these distributions using unnormalized pair-counts identified characteristic scales of approximately $60 \ {\rm Mpc}$ for typical supercluster separations and up to $130 \ {\rm Mpc}$ for large structures in deep surveys [[bibcode-1992a-a-257-1m|bibcode:1992A&A...257....1M]]. To understand the transition from linear to non-linear regimes, the comparative use of [[percolation-analysis|Percolation analysis]] and genus curves helped delineate the emergence of phase correlations and large-scale connectedness [[bibcode-1997apj-476l-1s|bibcode:1997ApJ...476L...1S]]. Early percolation studies of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) Data Release One successfully identified a transition from isolated individual structures to a fully interconnected network at volume filling factors of approximately $0.5\text{--}0.6$ [[bibcode-2005mnras-357-1068p|bibcode:2005MNRAS.357.1068P]]. Traditional topological studies often characterized this network using the genus statistic applied to a density field smoothed with a fixed Gaussian kernel (see [[isodensity-analysis|Isodensity Analysis]]). However, fixed-scale smoothing inherently suppresses small-scale hierarchical features and can blur the boundaries of the cosmic web [[bibcode-2010apj-722-812z|bibcode:2010ApJ...722..812Z]]. Alpha shapes, constructed as a parameter-dependent subset of the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]], provide a scale-adaptive alternative that natively preserves the discrete, multi-scale topology of the galaxy and dark matter distributions [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]], [[bibcode-2011mnras-416-2494p|bibcode:2011MNRAS.416.2494P]].

By varying the scale parameter $\alpha$—which dictates the maximum allowed circumradius for the simplices included in the complex—one generates a nested sequence of subcomplexes known as a filtration. This $\alpha$-filtration allows for the rigorous morphological classification of the cosmic web via Betti numbers ($\beta_k$). In three-dimensional space, Betti numbers decompose the topology into distinct structural elements—isolated components, independent tunnels, and enclosed cavities—offering a much richer description than the Euler characteristic alone [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]], [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]]. Furthermore, by examining the effects of density perturbations across various scales, researchers established that fine cosmic web structures are typically created by small-scale perturbations ($\le 8 \ h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$), whereas larger filaments arise directly from intermediate scales ($8\text{--}32 \ h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$) [[bibcode-2011a-a-531a-149s|bibcode:2011A&A...531A.149S]]. Extending structural analysis beyond basic clustering is essential; complementary network analysis metrics such as transitivity and diameter can discriminate between topologically different distributions (e.g., cosmological simulations versus Lévy walks) that share identical two-point correlation functions [[bibcode-2016mnras-459-2690h|bibcode:2016MNRAS.459.2690H]]. 

Complementary to the discrete $\alpha$-filtration, other metrics like the 'Local Dimension' statistic use power-law fits to local galaxy number counts to directly distinguish between structural environments, quantifying filaments ($D \sim 1$), sheets ($D \sim 2$), and clusters ($D \sim 3$) [[bibcode-2009mnras-394l-66s|bibcode:2009MNRAS.394L..66S]]. Furthermore, hybrid geometric approaches applying Shapefinders and [[partial-minkowski-functionals|Partial Minkowski Functionals]] to datasets like the SDSS DR12 have demonstrated that large superclusters tend to be highly filamentary structures characterized by non-zero genus values [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-4712b|bibcode:2023MNRAS.521.4712B]]. By tracking Betti numbers as a function of $\alpha$, researchers extract a complete topological summary of the matter distribution across all spatial scales simultaneously [[bibcode-2017mnras-465-4281p|bibcode:2017MNRAS.465.4281P]].

The evaluation of alpha shapes is fundamentally intertwined with [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]]. As the filtration parameter $\alpha$ increases, topological features are born (e.g., when a set of points connects to form a loop) and subsequently die (e.g., when a void is completely triangulated and filled). The "persistence" of these features—the range of $\alpha$ over which they exist—enables the separation of robust physical structures from sampling noise and Poisson fluctuations [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]]. Algorithms such as [[disperse|DISPERSE]] leverage these discrete Morse-Smale complexes to extract the persistent topological skeleton of the universe, identifying the most statistically significant nodes, filaments, and voids within noisy datasets [[bibcode-2013ascl-soft02015s|bibcode:2013ascl.soft02015S]].

Astrophysically, the application of alpha shapes has revealed significant insights into the environmental dependence of structure formation. Betti curve analyses have highlighted significant topological discrepancies between dark matter halos and observable galaxies, as well as distinct topological preferences between star-forming and quiescent galaxy populations—features strongly modulated by feedback mechanisms [[bibcode-2023mnras-523-5738o|bibcode:2023MNRAS.523.5738O]]. Moreover, this topological framework has been extended to map the high-redshift intergalactic medium; using non-parametric 3D reconstructions of the Lyman-alpha forest from BOSS data, alpha shapes and persistent homology enable direct topological characterization of the continuous cosmic web [[bibcode-2014mnras-440-2599c|bibcode:2014MNRAS.440.2599C]]. Because they capture higher-order correlations across all scales, alpha shapes remain a foundational tool in [[topological-data-analysis-in-astronomy|Topological data analysis in astronomy]].

## Topological Invariants: Betti Numbers and Persistence

In the context of [[topological-data-analysis-in-astronomy|Topological data analysis in astronomy]], the topological characteristics of the cosmic web can be rigorously quantified using Betti numbers. In three-dimensional space, the Betti numbers $\beta_k$ count the number of independent $k$-dimensional topological holes. Specifically, $\beta_0$ corresponds to the number of connected components (isolated clusters or nodes), $\beta_1$ counts the number of non-contractible loops or tunnels (which trace the filamentary network), and $\beta_2$ represents the number of enclosed voids [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]]. 

Traditional topological analyses of large-scale structure have heavily relied on the Euler characteristic, $\chi = \beta_0 - \beta_1 + \beta_2$, or the related genus statistic. While the genus provides a useful, condensed topological summary at a specific density threshold—and can be rigorously expanded by decomposing the statistic into orthogonal polynomial sequences to separately delineate the effects of galaxy bias, non-linear evolution, and non-Gaussianity [[bibcode-2012apj-751-40j|bibcode:2012ApJ...751...40J]]—it inherently conflates distinct morphological features. By tracking the Betti numbers individually, researchers can break the degeneracies between clusters, filaments, and voids, providing a vastly richer structural description [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]]. In fact, understanding the primordial origin of these distinct structural elements can be mathematically linked to the conditional eigenvalue distributions of the initial shear field, which provide a rigorous geometric framework for identifying the original seeds of halos and voids [[bibcode-2012mnras-421-296r|bibcode:2012MNRAS.421..296R]].

The computation of Betti numbers on discrete point sets, such as galaxy catalogs or $N$-body simulation particles, is naturally facilitated by alpha shapes. By varying the radius threshold parameter $\alpha$, one generates a sequence of nested simplicial complexes derived from the underlying [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]]. This sequence, known as a filtration, tracks the evolution of topological features across all spatial scales simultaneously without the loss of small-scale information inherent to fixed-scale Gaussian smoothing. 

Within this filtration, [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] measures the "lifetime" of each topological feature. A feature is "born" at a threshold $\alpha_{\rm birth}$ and "dies" (e.g., when a void is completely filled or a loop is closed) at $\alpha_{\rm death}$. The robustness of a feature is given by its persistence, $|\alpha_{\rm death} - \alpha_{\rm birth}|$. This framework effectively separates robust, physically significant structures of the cosmic web (high persistence) from shot noise and small-scale sampling artifacts (low persistence) [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]], [[bibcode-2017mnras-465-4281p|bibcode:2017MNRAS.465.4281P]]. Publicly available software such as [[disperse|DISPERSE]] leverages these persistence principles via discrete Morse-Smale complexes to extract the robust cosmic skeleton [[bibcode-2013ascl-soft02015s|bibcode:2013ascl.soft02015S]].

The multiscale nature of persistent Betti numbers has proven to be a powerful tool for cosmological parameter estimation. Persistence statistics evaluated on scales of $\mathcal{O}(10) \ h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$ can break the degeneracy between the amplitude of matter fluctuations $\sigma_8$ and primordial non-Gaussianity. Studies using persistence diagrams on $40 \ (h^{-1}{\rm Gpc})^3$ simulated volumes have demonstrated the capacity to detect local non-Gaussianity $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc} = 10$ at a $97.5\%$ confidence level [[bibcode-2021jcap-04-061b|bibcode:2021JCAP...04..061B]]. Persistent homology has also been successfully applied to the Epoch of Reionization to map the changing topology of the neutral hydrogen and HII bubble networks [[bibcode-2019mnras-486-1523e|bibcode:2019MNRAS.486.1523E]].

Despite its theoretical robustness, applying persistent homology to observed galaxy distributions requires careful handling of "topological bias"—the phenomenon where the topology of the luminous galaxy distribution systematically differs from that of the underlying dark matter [[bibcode-2024mnras-529-4325b|bibcode:2024MNRAS.529.4325B]], [[bibcode-2023mnras-523-5738o|bibcode:2023MNRAS.523.5738O]]. Currently, the literature remains divided on the optimal statistical summary for persistence data. Some authors advocate for one-dimensional Betti curves—plotting $\beta_k$ as a function of $\alpha$—due to their straightforward interpretability and ease of comparison between different tracers [[bibcode-2023mnras-523-5738o|bibcode:2023MNRAS.523.5738O]], [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]]. Conversely, others favor the full two-dimensional persistence diagrams or "persistence fields" to maximize the extracted information regarding feature lifetimes and the hierarchical merging of structures [[bibcode-2019mnras-486-1523e|bibcode:2019MNRAS.486.1523E]], [[bibcode-2021jcap-04-061b|bibcode:2021JCAP...04..061B]].

## Computational Frameworks and Algorithms

To apply [[topological-data-analysis-in-astronomy|Topological data analysis in astronomy]] to discrete point sets, such as galaxy catalogs or dark matter N-body particles, the spatial data must first be mapped into a robust geometric and continuous representation. A foundational framework for this task is the Delaunay Tessellation Field Estimator (DTFE). DTFE leverages the local geometry of the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]] and its dual [[poisson-voronoi-tessellation|Poisson Voronoi Tessellation]] (Voronoi diagram) to reconstruct a piece-wise continuous density field. Unlike traditional methods that use a fixed-scale Gaussian smoothing kernel—which tend to suppress multi-scale topological information—DTFE is inherently scale-adaptive and parameter-free. This adaptability allows it to preserve the hierarchical, multi-scale nature of the cosmic web with superior fidelity [[bibcode-2010apj-722-812z|bibcode:2010ApJ...722..812Z]]. Comparative studies indicate that while higher-order interpolation schemes, such as the Natural Neighbour Field Estimator (NNFE) or Kriging, can produce visually smoother density fields, DTFE performs quantitatively better at recovering topological features like voids and is computationally less demanding [[bibcode-2011mnras-416-2494p|bibcode:2011MNRAS.416.2494P]]. These discrete tessellations are also deeply connected to physical structure formation theories, such as the adhesion approximation, a dynamical model where infinitesimal viscosity leads to space-filling tessellations (composed of vertices, edges, and faces) naturally associated with clusters, filaments, and voids [[bibcode-2009arxiv0912-4520s|bibcode:2009arXiv0912.4520S]].

With a geometric structure established, the topology of the point distribution can be directly analyzed using alpha shapes. An alpha shape is a specific subset of the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]], defined by a scale parameter $\alpha$. As $\alpha$ increases, the alpha shape incorporates more simplices (vertices, edges, triangles, and tetrahedra), generating a nested sequence of simplicial complexes known as a filtration [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]], [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]]. Because these simplicial complexes are homotopy equivalent to the sublevel sets of the distance field, they provide an exact computational framework for calculating Betti numbers ($\beta_0$, $\beta_1$, and $\beta_2$) as a function of scale. This method translates the discrete galaxy distribution into a complete topological characterization without requiring artificial grid interpolations [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]].

The extraction of robust physical features from the resulting simplicial complexes is often complicated by shot noise and sampling artifacts. To address this, algorithms grounded in discrete Morse theory and [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] are employed. The foremost software framework for this task in astrophysics is [[disperse|DISPERSE]] (Discrete Persistent Structures Extractor). [[disperse|DISPERSE]] computes the discrete Morse-Smale complex of the density field—often defined directly on the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]]—to identify critical points corresponding to local maxima (knots), saddles (filaments and walls), and minima (voids) [[bibcode-2013ascl-soft02015s|bibcode:2013ascl.soft02015S]]. By evaluating the "persistence" of these topological features, defined as the scale interval over which a structural feature survives within the filtration, [[disperse|DISPERSE]] systematically separates physically meaningful cosmological structures from noise. This allows for the automated extraction of the cosmic web's skeleton and the calculation of persistent Betti numbers, which provide higher-order constraints on cosmological parameters such as primordial non-Gaussianity [[bibcode-2017mnras-465-4281p|bibcode:2017MNRAS.465.4281P]].

While persistent homology via [[disperse|DISPERSE]] is widely used, alternative graph-theoretical and continuous frameworks offer complementary approaches to structural classification. The T-ReX approach, for example, utilizes graph theory and Gaussian mixtures to retrieve underlying [[galaxy-filament-finding-algorithm|Galaxy Filament-Finding algorithm]] (filamentary patterns) directly from noisy 2D or 3D point distributions [[bibcode-2020a-a-637a-18b|bibcode:2020A&A...637A..18B]]. Similarly, the MulGuisin (MGS) algorithm functions as a topological network finder that uses local density rankings to define galaxy clusters and their interconnecting networks [[bibcode-2023arxiv230103278j|bibcode:2023arXiv230103278J]]. Studies emphasize that among various network construction algorithms, simple spatial proximity often remains the most effective strategy for correlating the physical characteristics of galaxies [[bibcode-2016arxiv160403236c|bibcode:2016arXiv160403236C]]. Furthermore, applying [[percolation-analysis|Percolation analysis]] (percolation theory) to these cosmological graphs has successfully identified critical valency thresholds at which robust, extended filamentary networks emerge [[bibcode-2023arxiv231020184s|bibcode:2023arXiv231020184S]]. In parallel, continuous Hessian-based cosmic web identifiers like the T-Web explicitly classify the cosmic web into voids, sheets, filaments, and knots by evaluating the Hessian of the gravitational potential on a grid and counting the number of eigenvalues above a specific threshold [[bibcode-2009mnras-396-1815f|bibcode:2009MNRAS.396.1815F]]. These thresholding techniques have advanced through the derivation of universal, physically motivated limits; these thresholds explicitly normalize for non-linear structural evolution to preserve cosmic volume fractions across different redshifts [[bibcode-2025a-a-696a-142o|bibcode:2025A&A...696A.142O]]. To bypass grids entirely, Lagrangian methods of cosmic web classification have also been developed, employing smoothed particle hydrodynamics (SPH) techniques to identify dynamic structures directly from discrete particle or halo distributions [[bibcode-2016mnras-458-1517f|bibcode:2016MNRAS.458.1517F]]. To extract individual, discrete filaments from continuous Hessian density fields, modern thinning algorithms such as the COWS (COsmic Web Skeleton) method have been successfully developed [[bibcode-2022mnras-514-470p|bibcode:2022MNRAS.514..470P]].

## Constraining Fundamental Physics and Cosmology

The multiscale topological characterization of the cosmic web using alpha shapes provides a rigorous framework for constraining cosmological parameters and testing fundamental physics. Because Betti numbers ($\beta_0, \beta_1, \beta_2$) and their persistence track higher-order spatial correlations across all scales simultaneously, they capture non-linear structural information that escapes standard two-point statistics, such as the power spectrum [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]], [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]].

**Primordial Non-Gaussianity**
One of the most promising applications of alpha shape topology is constraining primordial non-Gaussianity, parameterized by $f_{\rm NL}$. Traditional topological measures, such as the genus statistic and Minkowski functionals, have long been known to deviate from Gaussian predictions in the weakly non-linear regime ($\sigma_0 < 0.1$) due to primordial quadratic non-linearities [[bibcode-2008mnras-385-1613h|bibcode:2008MNRAS.385.1613H]]. However, [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] applied to alpha shape filtrations allows for a more comprehensive separation of robust physical features from topological noise. Recent theoretical pipelines utilizing persistence have demonstrated the ability to detect local non-Gaussianity of $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc} = 10$ at a $97.5\%$ confidence level within survey volumes of $\sim 40~({\rm Gpc}/h)^3$ [[bibcode-2021jcap-04-061b|bibcode:2021JCAP...04..061B]]. Crucially, this topological approach relies primarily on structure at intermediate scales ($\mathcal{O}(10)$ Mpc/$h$) rather than ultra-large-wavelength modes. By evaluating the multiscale topology of halos, optimal template methods can successfully resolve the traditional parameter degeneracy between $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc}$ and the amplitude of matter fluctuations, $\sigma_8$ [[bibcode-2021jcap-04-061b|bibcode:2021JCAP...04..061B]].

**Modified Gravity and Dark Energy**
Alpha shape topology also offers a geometric test for modified gravity and dynamical dark energy. In standard $\Lambda$CDM cosmology governed by General Relativity, the linear growth of density fluctuations is scale-independent, meaning that topological invariants per comoving volume are largely conserved as the universe expands homologously. In contrast, modified gravity theories—such as $f(R)$ gravity and the Dvali-Gabadadze-Porrati (DGP) braneworld model—predict scale-dependent growth rates even in the linear regime [[bibcode-2012apj-747-48w|bibcode:2012ApJ...747...48W]]. This scale dependence forces the amplitude and shift of multiscale topological curves to evolve over time. By tracking the hierarchical evolution of alpha shape Betti numbers across different redshifts, observers can theoretically isolate these distinct topological signatures to constrain deviations from standard gravity [[bibcode-2011lncs-6970-60v|bibcode:2011LNCS.6970...60V]], [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]].

**Massive Neutrinos**
Finally, topological methods are highly sensitive to the free-streaming effects of massive neutrinos, which suppress the growth of structure on small scales and delay overall large-scale structure formation. Recent high-resolution simulations have shown that massive neutrinos distinctly reshape the cosmic web by decreasing the total volume occupied by both cosmic voids and clusters, subsequently narrowing the volume-weighted density distribution [[bibcode-2025arxiv251216517s|bibcode:2025arXiv251216517S]]. Graph-theoretical representations of the cosmic web derived from discrete point sets, such as the [[minimal-spanning-tree|Minimal spanning tree]], reveal that approximately $70\%$ of network edges reside within cosmic filaments [[bibcode-2007mnras-375-337c|bibcode:2007MNRAS.375..337C]], [[bibcode-2025arxiv251216517s|bibcode:2025arXiv251216517S]]. Because filamentary connectivity and thickness are highly responsive to the presence of hot dark matter, the scale-dependent lengths of these edges can help disentangle the specific structural dampening of massive neutrinos from small-scale baryonic feedback effects [[bibcode-2025arxiv251216517s|bibcode:2025arXiv251216517S]].

**Baryonic Feedback**
Topological metrics are also powerful probes of astrophysics beyond dark matter. Recent high-resolution simulations reveal that feedback from stars and active galactic nuclei (AGNs) leaves distinct topological imprints on the large-scale geometry of gas temperature, pressure, and density fields, reshaping baryonic structures on scales extending up to tens of megaparsecs [[bibcode-2024arxiv240604430s|bibcode:2024arXiv240604430S]].

## Applications in Galaxy Surveys and Reionization

The application of alpha shapes to observational cosmology is primarily driven by the need to quantify the multiscale topology of discrete point sets, such as those produced by the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) and the 2dF Galaxy Redshift Survey. In earlier topological measurements, the 2dFGRS genus analysis confirmed the absence of significant genus asymmetries on scales of $8\text{--}16 \ h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$, a result strongly consistent with standard inflationary predictions [[bibcode-2007mnras-375-128j|bibcode:2007MNRAS.375..128J]]. Because alpha shapes are derived directly from the [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]] of the data, they provide a parameter-free, scale-adaptive filtration that is ideally suited for the highly non-linear, hierarchical clustering of the cosmic web [[bibcode-2010arxiv1006-2765v|bibcode:2010arXiv1006.2765V]], [[bibcode-2013arxiv1306-3640v|bibcode:2013arXiv1306.3640V]]. In the context of [[topological-data-analysis-in-astronomy|Topological data analysis in astronomy]], applying [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] to SDSS data has successfully traced the time-evolution of large-scale structure, demonstrating a clear departure from a uniform topology as the Universe expands and density perturbations collapse at lower redshifts [[bibcode-2017adspr-60-722k|bibcode:2017AdSpR..60..722K]].

A critical finding from the application of alpha shapes to galaxy distributions is the presence of "topological bias." Earlier studies of the SDSS DR7 main galaxy sample had already indicated this effect, showing that the galaxy clustering topology—as measured by the genus curve—varies significantly with luminosity, morphology, and color, often differing fundamentally from underlying dark matter predictions [[bibcode-2010apjs-190-181c|bibcode:2010ApJS..190..181C]]. Specifically, the luminosity dependence of the genus shift in SDSS reveals that brighter galaxies exhibit a cluster-dominated "meatball" topology, whereas fainter galaxies trace a void-dominated "bubble" topology [[bibcode-2005apj-633-11p|bibcode:2005ApJ...633...11P]]. These observations align closely with hydrodynamic simulations predicting that earlier-forming galaxies (typically ellipticals) tend toward a meatball topology, while later-forming galaxies (spirals) display a broadly spongelike geometry [[bibcode-1996apj-465-499g|bibcode:1996ApJ...465..499G]]. Analyses of Betti curves—which track the topological Betti numbers $\beta_0$, $\beta_1$, and $\beta_2$ as a function of the filtration scale $\alpha$—similarly reveal that the multiscale topology traced by galaxies differs significantly from the underlying dark matter halo distribution [[bibcode-2023mnras-523-5738o|bibcode:2023MNRAS.523.5738O]]. Halo masses scale linearly with topological signatures, indicating that galaxies of different masses and star-formation histories preferentially populate distinct structural environments, such as isolated nodes or extended filaments [[bibcode-2024mnras-529-4325b|bibcode:2024MNRAS.529.4325B]]. Beyond pure spatial clustering, extending this framework with network analysis has proven valuable; for example, applying network centrality measures (such as degree, closeness, and betweenness) to surveys like COSMOS allows researchers to formally define distinct topological environments like dense kernels, interconnecting backbones, and isolated fractures [[bibcode-2015mnras-450-1999h|bibcode:2015MNRAS.450.1999H]]. Further network analysis in the COSMOS field has demonstrated that local clustering coefficient distributions allow for the clear discrimination between singlets, dumbbells, and fully connected cliques [[bibcode-2018mnras-477-4738d|bibcode:2018MNRAS.477.4738D]]. Furthermore, while classical topological summaries like the genus curve are known to be robust against mild redshift-space distortions when using density ranking [[bibcode-2010apj-715l-185p|bibcode:2010ApJ...715L.185P]], the precise multiscale connectivity quantified by alpha shapes and Minkowski functionals is sensitive to the observer's line of sight. Precision topological measurements in modern surveys must carefully account for these redshift-space distortions, though parameterizing results by volume-filling fractions can mitigate their impact [[bibcode-2021mnras-508-3771l|bibcode:2021MNRAS.508.3771L]].

Beyond late-time galaxy surveys, alpha shapes and persistent homology are increasingly applied to map the topology of the Epoch of Reionization. During this era, the neutral intergalactic medium is ionized by the first generations of stars and galaxies, creating a complex, evolving network of H II bubbles. Traditional two-point statistics struggle to fully capture this highly non-Gaussian bubble network. By summarizing persistence data into "persistence fields," researchers have identified distinct topological stages of reionization common to all bubble scenarios. Following an initial pre-overlap and overlap stage, the topology enters a "filament stage" dominated by neutral filaments, followed by a final "patch stage" where enclosed patches of neutral hydrogen undergo outside-in ionization [[bibcode-2019mnras-486-1523e|bibcode:2019MNRAS.486.1523E]].

These topological insights are crucial for interpreting the redshifted 21-cm signal from neutral hydrogen. The cross-correlation between the 21-cm signal and early galaxies, specifically Lyman Alpha Emitters (LAEs), serves as a direct probe of reionization topology. LAE angular correlation functions serve as a complementary probe for reionization, effectively constraining both the ionized fraction and the ionizing photon escape fractions [[bibcode-2015mnras-450-4025h|bibcode:2015MNRAS.450.4025H]]. High-density regions ionized early in an "inside-out" reionization scenario produce a strong spatial anti-correlation between LAEs and 21-cm emission on scales $\lesssim 10$ comoving Mpc [[bibcode-2017apj-836-176h|bibcode:2017ApJ...836..176H]], [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-1664h|bibcode:2023MNRAS.525.1664H]]. Topological analysis using persistent homology allows theoreticians to quantify the size distribution and fractal-like connectivity of these ionized regions [[bibcode-2019mnras-486-1523e|bibcode:2019MNRAS.486.1523E]], offering a stringent, mathematically rigorous framework to distinguish competing reionization models in upcoming radio interferometry data.

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While alpha shapes and [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] provide a mathematically rigorous framework for quantifying the multiscale connectivity of the cosmic web, several theoretical and observational challenges remain. As [[topological-data-analysis-in-astronomy|Topological data analysis in astronomy]] moves toward precision cosmology, researchers must resolve ongoing tensions regarding how discrete tracers map onto the underlying continuous density field, while simultaneously preparing for the data volumes of next-generation observatories.

A primary unresolved issue is the presence and calibration of topological bias. Halo masses exhibit a linear scaling relationship with their multiscale topological signatures, meaning that galaxies of different masses, star-formation rates, and morphological types trace fundamentally different geometric environments [[bibcode-2024mnras-529-4325b|bibcode:2024MNRAS.529.4325B]], [[bibcode-2023mnras-523-5738o|bibcode:2023MNRAS.523.5738O]]. Unlike standard two-point clustering bias, this environmental structure bias complicates the direct use of observed voids and filaments as pristine cosmological probes, requiring new models to map the topology of luminous tracers to the dark matter scaffolding. Encouragingly, recent high-resolution cold dark matter simulations indicate that stellar shapes in dwarf galaxies robustly trace the shapes of their underlying dark matter halos, providing a much-needed direct morphological link between observable luminous structures and the invisible dark matter web [[bibcode-2025apj-986-138k|bibcode:2025ApJ...986..138K]].

Observational systematics, particularly sampling density and shot noise, pose another major hurdle. Because alpha shapes are constructed from a [[delaunay-tessellation|Delaunay tessellation]] of discrete points, the resulting topological summaries (as well as continuum metrics like [[percolation-analysis|Percolation analysis]]) are highly sensitive to the number density of the observed tracers [[bibcode-2024arxiv240702574r|bibcode:2024arXiv240702574R]]. Although [[persistent-homology|Persistent homology]] algorithms like [[disperse|DISPERSE]] mitigate this by thresholding topological features based on their persistence—thereby separating robust physical structures from spurious noise artifacts [[bibcode-2013ascl-soft02015s|bibcode:2013ascl.soft02015S]]—the residual dependence on numerical resolution and variable survey selection functions can limit the discriminative power of standalone topological tests [[bibcode-2024arxiv240702574r|bibcode:2024arXiv240702574R]]. 

There are also outstanding questions regarding the extent to which alpha shape topology can break fundamental cosmological degeneracies. For instance, while Betti numbers can capture higher-order correlations sensitive to primordial non-Gaussianity ($f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc}$), cleanly resolving the degeneracy between $f_{\rm NL}^{\rm loc}$ and the amplitude of matter fluctuations ($\sigma_8$) using persistent homology remains an active area of investigation [[bibcode-2021jcap-04-061b|bibcode:2021JCAP...04..061B]]. Similarly, while massive neutrinos are known to delay structure formation and alter the geometry of the cosmic web—detectable via variations in [[minimal-spanning-tree|Minimal spanning tree]] edge lengths [[bibcode-2025arxiv251216517s|bibcode:2025arXiv251216517S]]—their precise imprint on the multiscale Betti numbers in 3D redshift space is not yet fully characterized.

Future directions in the field are heavily tied to the advent of Stage-IV surveys. Data from Euclid, the Vera Rubin Observatory, and the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) will offer unprecedented statistical power. These surveys are expected to probe the maximum statistically significant extent of coherent cosmic structures, which current SDSS analyses suggest may extend up to $\sim 130$ Mpc [[bibcode-2023mnras-519-3227s|bibcode:2023MNRAS.519.3227S]]. Capitalizing on these dense, large-volume datasets will require the topological community to strictly quantify topological bias and sampling effects [[bibcode-2024mnras-529-4325b|bibcode:2024MNRAS.529.4325B]], ultimately allowing alpha shape topologies to serve as robust, independent constraints on dark energy, modified gravity, and the initial conditions of the Universe.
