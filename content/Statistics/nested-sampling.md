## Article

**Nested sampling** is a computational algorithm widely used in [[statistical-methods-in-astrophysics|Statistical methods in astrophysics]] to perform robust Bayesian inference. While traditional Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms focus primarily on [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] by generating samples from [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]], nested sampling was specifically designed to simultaneously compute the marginal likelihood, or Bayesian evidence. It achieves this by transforming the notoriously difficult multi-dimensional integral of the likelihood over the parameter space into a tractable one-dimensional integral over the prior mass [[bibcode-2009bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2009bmc..book.....H]], [[bibcode-2014bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2014bmc..book.....H]]. This dual capability to both sample the posterior and calculate the evidence makes it an exceptionally powerful tool for complex astrophysical problems where evaluating competing theoretical models is just as important as constraining their underlying parameters.

In practice, nested sampling is systematically preferred over standard [[ensemble-mcmc-sampling|Ensemble MCMC sampling]] techniques when rigorous [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] is the primary scientific objective. Because the algorithm maintains an active ensemble of "live points" that steadily contract around regions of highest likelihood, it is inherently robust when navigating the highly degenerate, multi-modal parameter spaces frequently encountered in astronomy. Today, nested sampling is a cornerstone technique across diverse domains, from isolating complex star formation histories in galaxy spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting to constraining dark energy and massive neutrinos using joint datasets from large-scale structure surveys and the cosmic microwave background. 

This article explores the theoretical foundations of nested sampling and its modern evolution, alongside its practical implementations and known limitations. It examines the performance variations among popular software packages—such as MultiNest and PolyChord—when scaling to high-dimensional cosmological spaces. Finally, the article details the pressing computational challenges posed by upcoming Stage IV surveys like Euclid and the LSST, previews recent breakthroughs that integrate neural networks to accelerate sampling, and discusses the growing role of ultra-fast simulation-based models for large-scale [[physical-parameter-estimation|Physical parameter estimation]].

## Introduction to Nested Sampling in Astrophysics

In astrophysics, [[statistical-inference-and-data-methods|Bayesian inference]] relies on the evaluation of the posterior distribution to constrain physical models given observational data. While standard Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithms are optimized for [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]], they do not naturally compute the normalization constant of the posterior, known as the marginal likelihood or Bayesian evidence. Nested sampling was specifically developed to address this limitation. It is a computational algorithm designed to compute the multidimensional integral for evidence estimation, 
$$ \mathcal{Z} = \int \mathcal{L}(\theta) \pi(\theta) d\theta $$
where $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ is the likelihood and $\pi(\theta)$ is the prior. By transforming this high-dimensional integral into a one-dimensional integral over prior volume, nested sampling simultaneously evaluates the marginal likelihood and generates weighted samples from the [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]] [[bibcode-2009bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2009bmc..book.....H]], [[bibcode-2014bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2014bmc..book.....H]].

The ability to explicitly calculate the Bayesian evidence makes nested sampling a superior choice for [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] in astrophysics, such as evaluating competing cosmological frameworks or distinguishing between complex star formation histories in galaxy spectra [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]]. Furthermore, nested sampling is highly robust when navigating degenerate or multi-modal parameter spaces—a common challenge in identifying gravitational wave emissions from binary black holes [[bibcode-2019arxiv190900029j|bibcode: 2019arXiv190900029J]] and in active galactic nucleus (AGN) lag recovery [[bibcode-2025arxiv250509832m|bibcode: 2025arXiv250509832M]].

Several variations of nested sampling are widely used in the astronomical community, but their performance can vary significantly depending on the dimensionality and complexity of the problem. MultiNest, which utilizes an ellipsoidal bounding strategy to isolate regions of high likelihood, has historically been a standard tool for both evidence estimation and parameter estimation [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]]. However, the literature reveals tensions regarding its accuracy in high-dimensional cosmological inference, where sliced nested sampling implementations like PolyChord are often recommended as a more robust alternative [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-1184l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.521.1184L]].

As astrophysics transitions to Stage IV surveys containing billions of objects, traditional posterior sampling methods face severe computational bottlenecks. To mitigate this, recent advancements have focused on augmenting nested sampling with machine learning, boosting the efficiency of importance nested sampling to outperform traditional packages while maintaining high accuracy in demanding applications such as exoplanet detection, cosmology, and galaxy spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]].

## Historical Development and Algorithm Evolution

The transition from standard Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques to nested sampling represents a major shift in the computational landscape of astrophysical [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] and [[model-comparison|Model comparison]]. Historically, evaluating statistical uncertainties and optimizing data extraction relied on analytical or empirical techniques; for instance, early applications of bootstrap resampling were used to estimate non-Poissonian sampling errors in cluster correlation functions [[bibcode-1986mnras-223p-21l|bibcode: 1986MNRAS.223P..21L]], while early efforts to optimally weight survey data utilized Karhunen-Loève eigenmode expansions to estimate power spectra in complex survey geometries [[bibcode-1996apj-465-34v|bibcode: 1996ApJ...465...34V]]. Early developments in computational signal detection, such as the use of Metropolis-Hastings and simulated annealing for localizing gravitational wave signals in planned LISA data, also highlighted the need for efficient algorithms capable of navigating multi-modal parameter spaces [[bibcode-2007phrvd-75b1301c|bibcode: 2007PhRvD..75b1301C]]. Building on the need for rigorous probabilistic modeling, John Skilling originally formalized the foundational nested sampling algorithm to address the difficulty of calculating the Bayesian evidence (the marginal likelihood), a multidimensional integral that is notoriously challenging to evaluate using traditional MCMC methods [[bibcode-2009bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2009bmc..book.....H]], [[bibcode-2014bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2014bmc..book.....H]]. By transforming the multidimensional evidence integral into a one-dimensional integral over the prior volume, Skilling’s framework provided a robust method for simultaneous evidence estimation and generation of [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]]. Theoretically, nested sampling shares foundational links with other recursive estimators found in physics, such as biased sampling and the density of states algorithm [[bibcode-2013arxiv1301-6450c|bibcode: 2013arXiv1301.6450C]].

As models in cosmology and astrophysics grew in complexity, numerical implementations of Skilling's algorithm evolved to efficiently navigate highly multimodal and degenerate parameter spaces. One of the most widely adopted early implementations was MultiNest, which employs an ellipsoidal bounding strategy to isolate high-likelihood regions. MultiNest became a standard tool for a variety of astrophysical applications, including the inference of complex star formation histories in galaxy SED fitting [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]]. 

However, as the dimensionality of cosmological models expanded, tensions emerged regarding algorithmic reliability. Sliced nested sampling approaches, such as PolyChord, were developed to provide a superior balance of speed and robustness for high-dimensional posterior and evidence estimation in precision cosmology, mitigating the biases sometimes introduced by ellipsoidal bounds in complex spaces [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-1184l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.521.1184L]]. Alternative approaches have also sought to merge the benefits of different sampling paradigms, such as the Geometric Nested Sampler, which adapts the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm to efficiently sample within the geometric constraints of nested prior volumes [[bibcode-2019arxiv190900029j|bibcode: 2019arXiv190900029J]]. Parallel to MCMC and nested sampling, other stochastic approaches like Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) emerged as valuable alternatives for localizing regions of [[maximum-likelihood-estimation|maximum likelihood]], particularly in the multi-dimensional calibration of semi-analytic models of galaxy formation [[bibcode-2015apj-801-139r|bibcode: 2015ApJ...801..139R]].

More recently, algorithm evolution has shifted toward importance nested sampling (INS). Unlike vanilla nested sampling, which relies strictly on a rejection-based paradigm, INS utilizes all likelihood evaluations to estimate the posterior and evidence, significantly improving statistical efficiency. A critical requirement for INS is the generation of proposal distributions that closely mimic the target posterior. Modern variants achieve this by boosting INS with deep learning, utilizing neural network regression to dynamically map the parameter space [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]].

## Comparison with Traditional Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)

While both nested sampling and Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) methods aim to characterize [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]], their fundamental mathematical objectives differ. Traditional MCMC methods, such as the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm, are explicitly designed to construct a Markov chain whose equilibrium distribution is the posterior. A foundational milestone in this area was CosmoMC, a Markov chain Monte Carlo framework for cosmological [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] that notably popularized the use of importance sampling for accuracy correction without the need to rerun full sampling chains [[bibcode-2002phrvd-66j3511l|bibcode: 2002PhRvD..66j3511L]]. While standard MCMC methods are highly efficient for parameter estimation, they are notoriously inefficient at calculating the Bayesian evidence, because they spend relatively little time exploring the low-likelihood prior volume. Nested sampling, conversely, evaluates this evidence integral directly, yielding posterior samples strictly as a byproduct [[bibcode-2009bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2009bmc..book.....H]], [[bibcode-2014bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2014bmc..book.....H]]. Thus, nested sampling is systematically preferred when [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] is the primary scientific objective [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-1184l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.521.1184L]].

Standard MCMC algorithms frequently struggle with multimodal posteriors, as transitioning between isolated high-likelihood peaks requires traversing low-probability valleys, often leading to poor convergence. Nested sampling natively handles multimodality by maintaining an ensemble of live points that can populate and track isolated modes simultaneously [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]], [[bibcode-2019arxiv190900029j|bibcode: 2019arXiv190900029J]]. However, MCMC variants such as Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC) significantly outperform nested sampling in high-dimensional spaces. By leveraging the analytical gradient of the log-posterior to simulate particle trajectories, HMC scales exceptionally well with dimensionality. Consequently, HMC remains the dominant choice for field-level inference, such as recovering three-dimensional cosmic density and velocity fields involving massive numbers of parameters [[bibcode-2019a-a-625a-64j|bibcode: 2019A&A...625A..64J]]. Nested sampling, by contrast, generally becomes computationally intractable beyond $\mathcal{O}(10^2)$ parameters without strict structural assumptions.

In practice, the choice between MCMC and nested sampling—as well as the specific algorithmic implementation—can materially impact the derived cosmological constraints, particularly in high-dimensional parameter spaces where bounding approximations may break down [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-1184l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.521.1184L]]. 

Both standard MCMC and vanilla nested sampling are fundamentally rejection-based, meaning a large fraction of likelihood evaluations are discarded, inherently limiting their sampling efficiency. Recent algorithmic advancements have sought to bridge this gap by abandoning pure rejection sampling in favor of deep learning-boosted importance sampling, allowing all likelihood evaluations to contribute to the final integrals [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]]. 

Nevertheless, for next-generation Stage IV surveys, the computational cost of drawing dense stochastic samples remains a prohibitive bottleneck for *both* MCMC and nested sampling. This scaling wall is driving the astrophysics community to explore much faster alternatives, such as variational inference or simulation-based inference, to replace conventional sampling chains entirely [[bibcode-2022pasa-39-1g|bibcode: 2022PASA...39....1G]], [[bibcode-2022apj-938-11h|bibcode: 2022ApJ...938...11H]].

## Model Selection and Evidence-Based Inference

In Bayesian [[model-comparison|Model comparison]], the central quantitative metric is the Bayesian evidence (or marginal likelihood), defined as the integral of the likelihood $\mathcal{L}(\theta)$ over the prior distribution $\pi(\theta)$ for a given model $M$:
$$Z = \int \mathcal{L}(\theta) \pi(\theta) d\theta$$
Standard Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques sample from the posterior but do not naturally evaluate this integral, making them poorly suited for direct evidence-based model selection. Nested sampling was explicitly designed to compute $Z$ by transforming the multi-dimensional integral over the parameter space into a one-dimensional integral over the prior volume [[bibcode-2009bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2009bmc..book.....H]], [[bibcode-2014bmc-book-h|bibcode: 2014bmc..book.....H]]. As a result, nested sampling and its algorithmic variants are consistently preferred over standard MCMC when comparing cosmological models or navigating complex parameter spaces [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]], [[bibcode-2019arxiv190900029j|bibcode: 2019arXiv190900029J]], [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]].

Nested sampling provides a robust framework for [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] in multi-modal topographies where traditional samplers might become trapped in local optima. It has been successfully deployed across diverse astrophysical domains, including evaluating quenching mechanisms in complex galaxy star formation histories via the BAGPIPES code [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]], characterizing gravitational wave emissions from binary black holes [[bibcode-2019arxiv190900029j|bibcode: 2019arXiv190900029J]]—where there is a growing use of galaxy catalogs to reweight and improve localization in parameter estimation [[bibcode-2025apj-979-102m|bibcode: 2025ApJ...979..102M]]—and identifying robust temporal lags in the reverberation mapping of active galactic nuclei [[bibcode-2025arxiv250509832m|bibcode: 2025arXiv250509832M]]. By thoroughly exploring isolated modes in the parameter space, nested sampling effectively charts the full volume of the [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]], enabling rigorous comparisons between competing physical models.

Despite its theoretical advantages, practical implementations of nested sampling exhibit variations in performance and accuracy, particularly in high-dimensional settings. For extremely high-dimensional cosmological inference where robust error bars and precise evidence are critical, the literature indicates that sliced nested sampling is currently recommended as a safer alternative to ellipsoidal bounding, despite its higher computational cost [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-1184l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.521.1184L]].

To address the computational bottlenecks associated with evidence-based inference, recent advancements have focused on augmenting the algorithm with machine learning techniques to construct efficient proposal distributions. This allows for all likelihood evaluations to be utilized for both posterior and evidence estimation, often yielding order-of-magnitude speedups in realistic applications [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]].

## Applications in Stellar Population Synthesis and SED Fitting

Inferring the physical properties of galaxies—such as stellar mass, metallicity, dust attenuation, and star formation history (SFH)—from photometric and spectroscopic observations is a central challenge in extragalactic astrophysics. Because these parameters are highly correlated and subject to outshining effects (where bright, young stars obscure the emission of older populations), spectral energy distribution (SED) fitting is a highly degenerate inverse problem [[bibcode-2025arxiv250604336i|bibcode: 2025arXiv250604336I]]. To robustly characterize these uncertainties, modern SED fitting codes have largely transitioned to full Bayesian [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]], requiring sophisticated algorithms to explore moderate- to high-dimensional parameter spaces [[bibcode-2021apjs-254-22j|bibcode: 2021ApJS..254...22J]]. Early efforts such as the CIGALEMC code pioneered this transition from simple grid-based [[maximum-likelihood-estimation|Maximum likelihood estimation]] to stochastic MCMC sampling, paving the way for current nested sampling implementations [[bibcode-2011apj-740-22s|bibcode: 2011ApJ...740...22S]].

Nested sampling algorithms are particularly advantageous in this regime because they can efficiently navigate multimodal [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]] and explicitly compute the Bayesian evidence, enabling rigorous [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] (e.g., comparing different functional forms for SFHs). Two prominent examples of inference codes utilizing these methods are Prospector and BAGPIPES. Prospector combines flexible spectroscopic calibration models with parametric and non-parametric SFHs, utilizing algorithms like [[ensemble-mcmc-sampling|Ensemble MCMC sampling]] to fit highly correlated properties across UV to IR wavelengths [[bibcode-2019ascl-soft05025j|bibcode: 2019ascl.soft05025J]]. Similarly, the BAGPIPES code relies on nested sampling to fit arbitrary combinations of spectroscopic and photometric data [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]]. By properly sampling the full posterior, BAGPIPES has successfully recovered complex SFHs, allowing researchers to distinguish between rapid quasar-mode ($<1$ Gyr) and slower jet-mode active galactic nucleus (AGN) quenching mechanisms at high redshift [[bibcode-2018mnras-480-4379c|bibcode: 2018MNRAS.480.4379C]]. In regimes characterized by low mass or low star formation rates, stochastic sampling of the initial mass function becomes critical; Monte Carlo codes like SLUG explicitly stochastically light up galaxies to accurately capture discrete stellar population fluctuations [[bibcode-2012apj-745-145d|bibcode: 2012ApJ...745..145D]]. Another modern Bayesian SED fitting package, piXedfit, is specifically designed for resolved sources and notably utilizes random dense sampling of the parameter space as a fast alternative to MCMC for spatially resolved applications [[bibcode-2021apjs-254-15a|bibcode: 2021ApJS..254...15A]].

Despite the statistical robustness of nested sampling, the computational cost of generating complex stellar population synthesis models within the sampling loop is immense. Standard nested sampling or MCMC applied to high-dimensional SED models can require $\gtrsim 10-100$ CPU hours per galaxy [[bibcode-2022apj-938-11h|bibcode: 2022ApJ...938...11H]]. This per-object cost is a major bottleneck for the billions of targets expected from upcoming Stage IV surveys. Consequently, algorithmic efficiency is an area of active development, with importance nested sampling augmented by neural networks demonstrating significant speedups in SED fitting benchmarks [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]].

Furthermore, even with perfect sampling, systematic uncertainties remain a dominant source of error; for example, spatially resolved (pixel-based) SED fitting yields systematic differences of $\sim 0.2$ dex in stellar mass estimates compared to integrated SED fitting, driven heavily by SFH priors [[bibcode-2025arxiv250604336i|bibcode: 2025arXiv250604336I]]. To circumvent the computational constraints of traditional nested sampling entirely, researchers are increasingly exploring simulation-based inference (SBI) and amortized neural posterior estimation, which can map photometric observables directly to posteriors in a fraction of a second once trained [[bibcode-2022apj-938-11h|bibcode: 2022ApJ...938...11H]]. For example, the Synference framework serves as a modern SBI benchmark, achieving a 1700x speedup over traditional nested sampling for 14-band photometry while maintaining accurate posterior calibration [[bibcode-2025arxiv251110640h|bibcode: 2025arXiv251110640H]]. Alternatively, researchers are accelerating traditional MCMC sampling without bypassing it entirely by using neural network emulators evaluated on GPUs [[bibcode-2024apj-975-145t|bibcode: 2024ApJ...975..145T]].

## Cosmological Parameter Estimation and Large-Scale Structure

Nested sampling has become a cornerstone of [[physical-parameter-estimation|Physical parameter estimation]] in modern cosmology. Constraining the standard $\Lambda$CDM model—and testing theoretical extensions involving massive neutrinos, dynamical dark energy, or modified gravity—requires navigating complex, highly correlated parameter spaces. Unlike standard Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) techniques, nested sampling simultaneously performs robust [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] and calculates the Bayesian evidence (marginal likelihood). This dual capability is essential for rigorous [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] when evaluating competing cosmological models against observational data.

In the analysis of large-scale structure, nested sampling is frequently applied to joint constraints from galaxy clustering, weak lensing (cosmic shear), and cosmic microwave background (CMB) anisotropies. The construction of robust likelihoods for these measurements depends on foundational statistical methods; for instance, the Landy-Szalay estimator remains essential for minimizing variance and bias when computing galaxy-galaxy pair counts [[bibcode-1993apj-412-64l|bibcode: 1993ApJ...412...64L]]. Beyond standard two-point statistics, measures of the cosmic web such as percolation analysis provide complementary geometric information, though these statistics remain highly sensitive to survey sampling density [[bibcode-2024apj-974-126r|bibcode: 2024ApJ...974..126R]]. Furthermore, to maximize the constraining power of large-area galaxy surveys, researchers increasingly rely on Bayesian experimental design to determine optimal sparse sampling strategies [[bibcode-2013mnras-433-3523p|bibcode: 2013MNRAS.433.3523P]]. Additionally, multi-tracer methodologies that combine ratios of spectroscopic and photometric samples are being introduced to directly cancel sampling variance in upcoming Euclid analyses [[bibcode-2024arxiv240412157e|bibcode: 2024arXiv240412157E]].

For example, nested sampling frameworks are utilized to break the degeneracy between geometric (Alcock-Paczynski) and dynamic (redshift-space) distortions by combining galaxy survey data with the baryon acoustic oscillation (BAO) sound horizon scale, $r_s \approx 150\ \mathrm{Mpc}$, calibrated from the CMB [[bibcode-2012mnras-424-2h|bibcode: 2012MNRAS.424....2H]]. Furthermore, extracting cosmological information from non-linear scales ($r \lesssim 10\ h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$) requires marginalizing over complex nuisance parameters associated with the galaxy-halo connection and baryonic feedback. This extraction must account for physical and geometrical biases; for instance, the observer's location within the cosmic web can induce significant systematic effects in bulk flow measurements [[bibcode-2018phrvd-97j3519h|bibcode: 2018PhRvD..97j3519H]]. Approaches such as Cosmological Evidence Modelling map the marginalized Bayesian evidence across the cosmological parameter space by repeatedly populating simulations with galaxies to build a physically motivated surrogate model, effectively circumventing the need for full stochastic sampling of all nuisance parameters simultaneously [[bibcode-2019mnras-490-1870l|bibcode: 2019MNRAS.490.1870L]].

Evaluating the likelihood typically requires robust covariance matrices. To address the impact of non-Gaussian errors in sample covariance, which can significantly alter parameter uncertainties for Stage IV surveys [[bibcode-2016mnras-458-4462b|bibcode: 2016MNRAS.458.4462B]], researchers are increasingly employing analytic covariance matrices that reduce computational costs and avoid the sampling noise associated with large mock catalog suites [[bibcode-2020phrvd-102l3521w|bibcode: 2020PhRvD..102l3521W]].

The extreme precision demanded by current and upcoming weak lensing and clustering surveys has exposed critical performance differences between various nested sampling implementations. During the validation of the Dark Energy Survey (DES) Year 3 analyses, the widely used MultiNest algorithm was found to report inconsistent evidence estimates and artificially narrow [[credible-interval|Credible interval]]s when applied to the full 3x2pt (galaxy clustering plus weak lensing) likelihood [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-1184l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.521.1184L]]. For highly correlated, high-dimensional cosmological parameter spaces, sliced nested sampling implementations (such as PolyChord) have been demonstrated to provide a significantly more robust, albeit computationally heavier, alternative [[bibcode-2023mnras-521-1184l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.521.1184L]].

As datasets grow to encompass billions of galaxies in Stage IV surveys (e.g., Euclid, DESI), the computational cost of traditional nested sampling becomes a severe bottleneck, often requiring $\mathcal{O}(10^6)$ likelihood evaluations. To overcome this limitation, the field is actively developing scalable algorithms that reduce the number of likelihood evaluations required without sacrificing the strict accuracy demanded by high-dimensional cosmological and photometric inference.

## Computational Challenges and High-Dimensional Scaling

Nested sampling provides a highly robust framework for [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] and effectively navigates complex, multi-modal [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]]. However, its performance severely degrades in high-dimensional parameter spaces. As the dimensionality $D$ of a model grows, the volume of the prior space increases exponentially, making the fundamental task of drawing samples from within the constrained likelihood contour computationally expensive. 

These algorithmic limitations are exacerbated by the sheer scale of modern astronomical datasets. Next-generation Stage IV cosmological surveys, such as those conducted by Euclid and DESI, require the analysis of billions of sources. Traditional "per-object" posterior sampling can require $>10-100$ CPU hours per galaxy [[bibcode-2022apj-938-11h|bibcode: 2022ApJ...938...11H]]. At this scale, the computational cost of running standard nested sampling chains for every object becomes strictly infeasible without massive parallelization or fundamental algorithmic innovation.

To address this dimensionality bottleneck, various algorithmic implementations of nested sampling have been developed, balancing speed against the risk of biased cosmological constraints. For exceptionally high-dimensional challenges—such as joint cosmic shear and galaxy clustering analyses involving upwards of 150 parameters—researchers are increasingly relying on GPU parallelization and differentiable emulators to achieve $\mathcal{O}(10^3)$ speedups, reducing inference times from years to days [[bibcode-2023ojap-6e-20p|bibcode: 2023OJAp....6E..20P]]. For high-dimensional imaging problems, such as sparse Bayesian mass-mapping, algorithms like trans-dimensional MCMC are employed to exploit wavelet-space sparsity, allowing the sampler to dynamically adjust the number of active parameters [[bibcode-2023ojap-6e-45m|bibcode: 2023OJAp....6E..45M]]. In adjacent fields such as line intensity mapping, robust regridding frameworks utilizing Monte-Carlo sampling particles have been developed to accurately compute Fourier-space statistics by suppressing aliased power in Cartesian grids [[bibcode-2024mnras-528-5586c|bibcode: 2024MNRAS.528.5586C]].

In domains where the computational cost of nested sampling remains prohibitive, the field of [[scientific-data-analysis|Scientific data analysis]] is actively exploring alternative inference frameworks. For example, Approximate Bayesian Computation (ABC) has gained traction as a likelihood-free alternative for constraining halo occupation distribution parameters when functional forms of the likelihood are unknown or interactable to compute [[bibcode-2017mnras-469-2791h|bibcode: 2017MNRAS.469.2791H]]. Variational inference and Simulation-Based Inference (SBI), such as Amortized Neural Posterior Estimation (ANPE), can yield accurate posteriors with up to a $10,000\times$ increase in speed by training neural networks to approximate the posterior directly [[bibcode-2022apj-938-11h|bibcode: 2022ApJ...938...11H]], [[bibcode-2022pasa-39-1g|bibcode: 2022PASA...39....1G]]. While these methods generally sacrifice the direct evidence calculation native to nested sampling, they provide a necessary, highly scalable pathway for [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] in the era of billion-object surveys. Other alternatives to MCMC and nested sampling have also emerged for specific high-dimensional problems. For instance, the Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation (INLA) has proven to be a highly computationally efficient alternative for modeling autocorrelated spatial structures in integral field spectroscopy (IFU) data [[bibcode-2019mnras-482-3880g|bibcode: 2019MNRAS.482.3880G]]. Similarly, optimization-based approaches like the EL_2O method (expectation optimization of $L_2$ distance) provide faster alternatives to traditional sampling for high-dimensional galaxy clustering analyses [[bibcode-2019arxiv190104454s|bibcode: 2019arXiv190104454S]].

## Recent Advances: Machine Learning and GPU Acceleration

Traditional [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] and [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] using standard nested sampling algorithms routinely require $\mathcal{O}(10^6)$ likelihood evaluations. To overcome these bottlenecks, there has been a rapid paradigm shift toward integrating machine learning, neural emulators, and GPU acceleration into Bayesian sampling frameworks.

A major algorithmic breakthrough is the direct integration of deep learning into the proposal generation step of nested sampling. The NAUTILUS algorithm achieves this by utilizing neural network regression to map the active contour of the nested sampling run, producing highly efficient proposal distributions that closely mimic the true [[posterior-distributions|Posterior distributions]] [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]]. By combining deep learning with importance nested sampling, NAUTILUS is able to utilize all likelihood evaluations (unlike strict rejection-based samplers) and demonstrates an order-of-magnitude speedup over traditional codes like MultiNest and Dynesty, all while maintaining high accuracy in complex spaces such as galaxy SED fitting [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]]. Alongside improvements to nested sampling, complementary approaches such as neural net assisted MCMC have been developed to accelerate joint 3x2pt analyses, providing another scalable pathway for Stage IV cosmological inference [[bibcode-2020mnras-491-2655m|bibcode: 2020MNRAS.491.2655M]].

Beyond algorithmic sampling improvements, the evaluation of the likelihood function itself has been drastically accelerated using differentiable neural emulators. Emulators interpolate high-dimensional parameter spaces to replace expensive physical forward models. To optimize the training of these emulators while minimizing the need for expensive simulations, techniques like Bayesian optimization for Gaussian process emulators have been successfully applied in contexts such as Lyman-alpha forest analysis [[bibcode-2019jcap-02-031r|bibcode: 2019JCAP...02..031R]]. Furthermore, amortized neural posterior estimation allows researchers to bypass traditional sampling entirely once a network is trained. Approaches like the SEDFLOW algorithm utilize neural posterior estimation to reduce the inference time for a 12-parameter SED model to $\sim 1$ second per galaxy, fully recovering the posteriors without the need to run a fresh [[ensemble-mcmc-sampling|Ensemble MCMC sampling]] or nested sampling chain for every target [[bibcode-2022apj-938-11h|bibcode: 2022ApJ...938...11H]].

Beyond imaging and spectral fitting, Bayesian Gaussian process regression is increasingly used to infer variability power spectra of active galaxies from irregularly sampled time series [[bibcode-2025arxiv250105886l|bibcode: 2025arXiv250105886L]]. These approaches reflect the broader emergence of Bayesian functional data analysis for characterizing entire populations of continuous functions, such as light curves and spectra [[bibcode-2024arxiv240814466l|bibcode: 2024arXiv240814466L]].

Simultaneously, hardware acceleration via GPUs and automatic differentiation libraries (e.g., JAX, PyTorch) has dramatically expanded the dimensionality limits of cosmological inference. Codes such as CosmoPower-JAX employ differentiable emulators evaluated on GPUs to perform high-dimensional parameter estimation. In a joint cosmic shear and galaxy clustering analysis featuring 157 parameters, this GPU acceleration yielded fully converged posteriors in just 3 days—an $\mathcal{O}(10^3)$ speedup over the estimated 6 years required by standard samplers [[bibcode-2023ojap-6e-20p|bibcode: 2023OJAp....6E..20P]]. Similarly, SwiftCl has emerged as a powerful JAX-accelerated pipeline providing differentiable angular power spectra beyond the Limber approximation, enabling extremely fast gradient-based sampling tailored for Stage IV surveys [[bibcode-2025arxiv250522718r|bibcode: 2025arXiv250522718R]]. GPU-accelerated frameworks applied to galaxy population models achieve processing rates of $\sim 15$ GPU-seconds per galaxy while marginalizing over complex stellar population synthesis parameters [[bibcode-2024apj-975-145t|bibcode: 2024ApJ...975..145T]]. Likewise, tools like ASTROPHOT utilize PyTorch to perform GPU-accelerated forward modeling and simultaneous fitting of overlapping astronomical sources across images [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-6377s|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.6377S]], while packages such as pysersic provide JAX-accelerated Bayesian structural modeling of galaxies [[bibcode-2023joss-8-5703p|bibcode: 2023JOSS....8.5703P]].

Beyond accelerating inference loops, machine learning has also advanced the generation of simulated data crucial for validating these pipelines. For example, deep diffusion models are now being used for generating highly realistic radio galaxy images with high-speed conditioned sampling, expanding the capabilities of mock astronomical datasets [[bibcode-2024a-a-691a-360v|bibcode: 2024A&A...691A.360V]].

Despite these transformative speedups, the literature notes important caveats regarding simulation-based inference and emulators. These methods require training sets that perfectly match the observed data characteristics. Out-of-distribution measurement errors or missing data can bias the resulting posteriors, though recent methodological extensions like SBI++ attempt to analytically marginalize over these observational discrepancies without requiring network retraining [[bibcode-2023apj-952l-10w|bibcode: 2023ApJ...952L..10W]].

## Open Questions and Future Directions

Upcoming Stage IV surveys, such as Euclid and the Vera C. Rubin Observatory's Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST), will observe billions of galaxies, presenting an unprecedented data volume challenge. Running an independent nested sampling chain for every galaxy in a Stage IV survey is widely considered computationally infeasible, prompting an urgent need for algorithmic innovation. 

As cosmological models grow more complex, tensions regarding the reliability of different nested sampling implementations in high-dimensional spaces have also emerged. Resolving these discrepancies and establishing benchmark standards for high-dimensional [[model-comparison|Model comparison]] remains an active area of research.

To address these scalability and accuracy limitations, future directions heavily feature the integration of machine learning. Neural network-assisted algorithms utilize deep learning to boost importance nested sampling, outperforming traditional samplers by orders of magnitude in both speed and accuracy for high-dimensional likelihoods [[bibcode-2023mnras-525-3181l|bibcode: 2023MNRAS.525.3181L]]. Additionally, coupling nested sampling with differentiable cosmological emulators has been shown to accelerate [[parameter-estimation|Parameter estimation]] in joint models by massive factors [[bibcode-2023ojap-6e-20p|bibcode: 2023OJAp....6E..20P]].

There is also an ongoing paradigm shift toward alternative inference frameworks, such as amortized neural posterior estimation (ANPE) and simulation-based inference (SBI) [[bibcode-2022apj-938-11h|bibcode: 2022ApJ...938...11H]], [[bibcode-2023apj-952l-10w|bibcode: 2023ApJ...952L..10W]]. These techniques train deep neural networks on large sets of simulations to yield near-instantaneous posteriors for new observations. A major open question is whether nested sampling will remain the primary workhorse for direct cosmological inference, or if it will be relegated to validating these extremely fast [[fitting-algorithms|Fitting algorithms]] and generating the baseline training data for SBI models.

Finally, extending Bayesian field-level inference—where the entire 3D primordial density field and cosmological parameters are jointly inferred—remains a formidable theoretical and computational challenge. While frameworks utilizing Hamiltonian Monte Carlo have successfully reconstructed initial conditions and bulk flows [[bibcode-2019a-a-625a-64j|bibcode: 2019A&A...625A..64J]], achieving converged small-scale virial mass estimates in field-level inference frameworks like BORG (which rely on N-body forward modeling) remains computationally demanding [[bibcode-2024mnras-527-1244s|bibcode: 2024MNRAS.527.1244S]]. To tackle these massive $\mathcal{O}(10^6)$-dimensional field-level inference problems, researchers are benchmarking advanced alternatives like Microcanonical Langevin Monte Carlo (MCLMC), which shows promise as a potentially superior sampler compared to traditional adjusted samplers [[bibcode-2025arxiv250420130s|bibcode: 2025arXiv250420130S]].
