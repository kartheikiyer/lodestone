## Article

The **[Ne II] 12.81 μm line** is a prominent mid-infrared [[fine-structure-emission-lines|fine-structure emission line]] originating from singly ionized neon (Ne$^+$). As a forbidden transition excited primarily by electron collisions, it serves as a crucial cooling line and a reliable tracer of intermediate-density ionized gas within [[h-ii-regions|H II regions]] and the broader interstellar medium [Tommasin et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...676..836T/abstract). Because the formation of Ne$^+$ requires primary [[stellar-ionizing-radiation|Stellar ionizing radiation]] produce enough energy to strip neutral neon, its presence is fundamentally tied to the radiation produced by young, massive stars. Unlike optical [[balmer-lines|Balmer lines]] traditionally used as [[star-formation-tracers|Star formation tracers]], mid-infrared [[neon-emission-lines|Neon emission lines]] are largely impervious to dust attenuation. This relative immunity to extinction allows [Ne II] to probe deeply embedded environments—such as heavily obscured starbursts and ultraluminous infrared galaxies—that are otherwise hidden at shorter wavelengths [Spoon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1223S/abstract).

In the broader context of [[mid-infrared-astronomy|Mid-Infrared Astronomy]], [Ne II] emission is a foundational tool for characterizing the physical state and energetic drivers of galaxies. Its luminosity correlates tightly with the intrinsic star formation rate (SFR), providing a nearly extinction-free alternative to optical diagnostics. Furthermore, the combination of [Ne II] with higher-ionization species forms powerful [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|Diagnostic emission line ratios]]. By comparing the flux of [Ne II] to lines like [Ne III] 15.56 μm or [O IV] 25.89 μm, astrophysicists can reliably distinguish the soft radiation fields of star-forming regions from the hard, non-thermal spectra of active galactic nuclei (AGN). This provides a robust alternative to heavily reddened [[optical-emission-line-diagnostics|Optical Emission-Line Diagnostics]] such as the [[bpt-diagram|BPT diagram]]. Additionally, when paired with sulfur fine-structure lines, the total ionized neon flux serves as a critical proxy for alpha-element [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]], enabling the measurement of gas-phase metallicities and radial abundance gradients across galactic disks [Giveon et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...566..880G/abstract).

This article explores the diverse physical applications of [Ne II] emission, highlighting how high-resolution [[emission-line-spectroscopy|Emission Line Spectroscopy]] has uncovered complex gas kinematics hidden behind cosmic dust. While textbook models historically assign forbidden lines strictly to lower-density [[narrow-line-regions-and-seyferts|Narrow-Line Regions and Seyferts]], [Ne II] profiles frequently exhibit broad, blue-shifted wings that trace high-velocity, multi-phase outflows and galactic winds. The article also details recent breakthroughs facilitated by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), which has extended the detection of neon lines to high redshifts, providing direct probes of early-universe metallicity and [[high-ionization-emission-lines|High-ionization emission lines]]. Finally, the discussion addresses several critical open questions in the field, including the persistent failure of current stellar atmosphere models to reproduce high-excitation line ratios in low-metallicity dwarf galaxies [Simpson et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...444..721S/abstract), uncertainties surrounding the universality of the neon-to-sulfur abundance ratio, and the ambiguous physical mechanisms driving the mid-infrared narrow-line Baldwin effect and the [[c-ii-158-m-line|[C II] 158 μm line]] deficit in extreme starbursts.

## Atomic Physics and Spectral Characteristics

**Excitation and Critical Density**
The 12.81 μm transition occurs within the ground-state [[fine-structure-emission-lines|fine-structure levels]] of the Ne$^+$ ion. Because it is a forbidden line, it relies on collisional [[excitation-mechanisms|Excitation mechanisms]] driven by free electrons in the ionized gas. The emergent line luminosity is governed by the electron density ($n_e$) and temperature of the environment, although fine-structure lines at mid-infrared wavelengths are significantly less sensitive to temperature fluctuations than optical diagnostics. The physical behavior of the [Ne II] line is heavily influenced by its critical density—the density threshold where collisional de-excitation begins to dominate over radiative decay, allowing the line to probe specific regimes of the density-ionization parameter space. Observations indicate that in typical starbursts and AGN, the bulk of the [Ne II] emission originates from intermediate- to high-ionization gas with electron densities ranging from $n_e \sim 10^2$ to $10^4 \text{ cm}^{-3}$ [Tommasin et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...676..836T/abstract), while densities in the diffuse warm ionized medium are significantly lower. Indeed, integrated galaxy spectra frequently display enhanced [S II]/H$\alpha$ and [N II]/H$\alpha$ ratios relative to individual [[h-ii-regions|H II regions]], pointing to a substantial contribution from a diffuse ionized medium [Lehnert et al. (1994)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1994ApJ...426L..27L/abstract). In low-density gas, long recombination times can lead to time-dependent ionization effects, allowing highly ionized neon species to persist longer than predicted by strict equilibrium models [McCallum et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024MNRAS.535.2889M/abstract). Recent advancements in modeling such ionized environments also include the development of a physically motivated "charge-exchange method," which calculates [[oxygen-spectral-lines|[O I]]]/H$\alpha$ emissivity as a function of metallicity and ionization fraction to accurately derive electron temperatures within H II regions when traditional auroral lines are unavailable [Kreckel et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...667A..16K/abstract).

**Ionization Potential and Hardness of the Radiation Field**
The formation of Ne$^+$ requires primary ionizing photons with enough energy to strip neutral neon. Furthermore, the persistence of the [Ne II] line is highly sensitive to the hardness of the local radiation field. When photon energies exceed $\sim 41$ eV, Ne$^+$ is further ionized to Ne$^{++}$, generating the [Ne III] 15.6 μm line. Consequently, the combination of these successive ionization states forms powerful [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|Diagnostic emission line ratios]], such as [Ne III] 15.6 μm / [Ne II] 12.8 μm, which robustly measure the hardness of the ionizing continuum [Tommasin et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...676..836T/abstract). Historically, standard local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) stellar atmosphere models have struggled to correctly predict the emergent flux for energies $> 41$ eV, frequently underestimating the high-excitation neon ratios observed in low-metallicity star-forming regions [Simpson et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...444..721S/abstract). Beyond typical star-forming regions, investigations into the ground-state fine-structure configurations of high-ionization neon coronal lines have even raised the possibility of population inversions, which could theoretically lead to laser gain in the extremely dense plasmas surrounding active galactic nuclei .

**Dust Attenuation and Kinematic Tracing**
A major advantage of the [Ne II] 12.81 μm transition over optical features like the [[balmer-lines|Balmer lines]] is its relative immunity to dust attenuation. Mid-infrared [[neon-emission-lines|Neon emission lines]] are largely unaffected by typical extinction, making them superior diagnostics for measuring chemical abundances and gas densities in obscured systems [Spinoglio et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...941...46S/abstract). However, in the most deeply buried ultraluminous infrared galaxies (ULIRGs) and AGN, even the mid-infrared continuum and fine-structure lines can suffer attenuation by factors of 10 or more due to extreme dust columns (e.g., $A_V \approx 10-112$) [Spoon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1223S/abstract), [Pérez-Beaupuits et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011A&A...533A..56P/abstract). 

Despite extreme obscuration, high-resolution [[emission-line-spectroscopy|Emission Line Spectroscopy]] of [Ne II] can reveal complex gas kinematics hidden at shorter wavelengths. Because the 12.81 μm emission penetrates thick dust, the [Ne II] line frequently uncovers stratified outflows and highly disturbed gas, featuring broad components and blue-shifted wings with velocities reaching thousands of $\text{km s}^{-1}$ that trace the optically obscured bases of nuclear outflows [Spoon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1223S/abstract), [Goold et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966..204G/abstract).

## Tracer of Obscured Star Formation

The [Ne II] $12.81\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ fine-structure line is a robust and widely used diagnostic of the star formation rate (SFR), particularly in highly obscured environments. Young, massive stars ($M \gtrsim 10 M_\odot$) with lifetimes $\lesssim 10^7$ yr produce intense [[stellar-ionizing-radiation|Stellar ionizing radiation]] in the form of Lyman continuum photons. This radiation photoionizes the surrounding interstellar medium, giving rise to [[h-ii-regions|H II regions]]. While this ionizing flux is traditionally measured using optical [[balmer-lines|Balmer lines]] like H$\alpha$, optical recombination lines are highly susceptible to dust attenuation, which leads to systematic underestimates of the SFR in dusty galaxies.

[[mid-infrared-astronomy|Mid-Infrared Astronomy]] provides a spectroscopic window that largely circumvents this limitation. Because dust extinction at $\lambda \sim 12.8\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ is significantly lower than at optical wavelengths, the [Ne II] line serves as a nearly extinction-free probe of the ionized gas. Observations of both local starbursts and high-redshift galaxies demonstrate that [Ne II] luminosity correlates tightly with other SFR indicators, such as the total infrared continuum luminosity and dust-corrected H$\alpha$ emission [Wild et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.410.1593W/abstract).

Because neon can exist in higher ionization states within star-forming regions, [Ne II] is frequently combined with the [Ne III] $15.56\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ line to account for the hardness of the radiation field [Giveon et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...566..880G/abstract). Comparing mid-infrared fine-structure emission to far-infrared cooling lines—such as the [[c-ii-158-m-line|[C II] 158 μm line]]—helps isolate the fraction of [C II] emission originating from the ionized gas phase. For instance, observations utilizing the [N II] 205 μm line (which originates strictly from ionized gas) have shown that a substantial fraction of global [C II] emission (often $35\%$--$90\%$) can arise from ionized gas rather than from neutral photodissociation regions [Röllig et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016A&A...591A..33R/abstract). Further extending these far-infrared studies to the early universe, ALMA observations of [N II] 205 μm at $z = 5\text{--}6$ show that 'typical' high-redshift [[emission-line-galaxies|star-forming galaxies]] exhibit extremely low [N II]/[C II] ratios, likely as a result of low metallicity and dust abundance in the early universe [Pavesi et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJ...832..151P/abstract). Additionally, the [Ne II] line is frequently utilized alongside other mid-infrared features to probe dust processing. For example, the observed correlation between elevated [[fe-ii-emission-lines|[Fe II] 26 μm]] / [Ne II] 12.8 μm ratios and diminished polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon (PAH) emission strength suggests that supernova activity may be a primary mechanism for PAH destruction in low-metallicity starbursts [O'Halloran et al. (2005)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006ApJ...641..795O/abstract). Furthermore, a strong correlation has been observed between the [[o-iii-88-m-line|[O III] 88 μm line]] and 24 μm continuum emission, providing a powerful predictive tool for estimating far-infrared line fluxes in high-redshift galaxies [Lambert-Huyghe et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...666A.112L/abstract).

Despite its broad utility, there remains an active debate in the literature regarding whether mid-infrared neon lines are truly "extinction-free" in the most extreme astrophysical environments. While [Ne II] accurately traces the intrinsic ionizing power in typical star-forming galaxies, high-resolution [[emission-line-spectroscopy|Emission Line Spectroscopy]] of deeply buried nuclei and ULIRGs reveals that [Ne II] can still be significantly attenuated by dust. In systems with extreme column densities, massive dust columns can attenuate mid-infrared emission lines by factors of 10 or more, with visual extinctions reaching $A_V = 10-50$ in the ULIRG IRAS F00183-7111 [Spoon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1223S/abstract) and $A_V \approx 55-112$ in the obscured nucleus of NGC 4945 [Pérez-Beaupuits et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011A&A...533A..56P/abstract). Consequently, while [Ne II] remains a premier tracer of obscured star formation, its absolute calibration in the most deeply embedded, compact starbursts must cautiously account for residual mid-infrared dust attenuation.

## Mid-Infrared AGN and Starburst Diagnostics

Mid-infrared fine-structure lines provide a robust method for distinguishing between thermal excitation driven by star formation and non-thermal excitation from an AGN. While [[optical-emission-line-diagnostics|Optical Emission-Line Diagnostics]] (such as the [[bpt-diagram|BPT diagram]]) are highly sensitive to dust attenuation, mid-infrared diagnostics are significantly more transparent to dust, enabling the classification of heavily obscured galaxies [Fernández-Ontiveros et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJS..226...19F/abstract), [Wild et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011MNRAS.410.1593W/abstract). The primary mid-infrared [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|Diagnostic emission line ratios]] leverage the flux of [Ne II] $12.81\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ alongside higher ionization species, namely [Ne III] $15.56\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, [O IV] $25.89\ \mu\mathrm{m}$, and [Ne V] $14.32\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ or $24.32\ \mu\mathrm{m}$.

Because [Ne II] and [Ne III] are readily produced in the softer radiation fields of [[h-ii-regions|H II regions]], the [Ne III]/[Ne II] ratio serves as a sensitive tracer of the hardness of the stellar ionizing radiation field [Dale et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1821D/abstract). In contrast, the production of Ne$^{4+}$ (ionization potential $97\ \mathrm{eV}$) and O$^{3+}$ ($55\ \mathrm{eV}$) requires a much harder radiation field, typically found within the [[narrow-line-regions-and-seyferts|narrow-line region]] (NLR) of an AGN. While extremely hot Wolf-Rayet stars can theoretically create Ne$^{4+}$, extensive modeling and observations of optically classified starbursts such as NGC 3621 suggest that robust [Ne V] emission is almost exclusively an indicator of AGN activity [Abel et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...678..686A/abstract). 

By plotting high-ionization ratios such as [O IV]/[Ne III] or [O IV]/[S III] against the [Ne III]/[Ne II] ratio, researchers can construct diagnostic diagrams that clearly separate AGN from pure starbursts and low-metallicity dwarf galaxies [Weaver et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...716.1151W/abstract), [Hao et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...704.1159H/abstract). Theoretical demarcation lines in these diagrams are often derived using two-zone photoionization models that combine a matter-bounded component (maximizing [Ne III] and [O IV]) with a radiation-bounded component (maximizing [Ne II] emission) [Meléndez et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014MNRAS.443.1358M/abstract). The magnitude of the high-ionization ratios correlates strongly with overall AGN dominance, scaling linearly with bolometric AGN indicators such as intrinsic $2{-}10\ \mathrm{keV}$ or $14{-}195\ \mathrm{keV}$ X-ray luminosity [Tommasin et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...676..836T/abstract), [Spinoglio et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...941...46S/abstract). Modeling of AGN further suggests that Seyfert 1 galaxies exhibit higher excitation spectra and fewer radiation-bounded clouds than Seyfert 2 galaxies, likely due to differences in the size of the narrow-line region or the alignment of the obscuring torus [Schmitt et al. (1998)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998ApJ...506..647S/abstract). These tools are constantly evolving; recently, researchers have developed robust [Ne V]-selected AGN catalogs and introduced alternative diagnostic diagrams—such as plotting [Ne III]/[O II] against [O III]/[O I]—to successfully classify intermediate-redshift galaxies up to $z \approx 1$ [Feuillet et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...962..104F/abstract). Furthermore, recent JWST-based photoionization modeling highlights that highly ionized species such as [Ar V] 7.90 μm and [Ne VI] 7.65 μm provide superior prescriptions for diagnosing the ionization parameter and peak energy of the AGN ionizing spectrum [Zhang et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250801439Z/abstract).

Despite their utility, these mid-infrared diagnostics face several ambiguities and active tensions in the literature:

*   **Extinction in extreme environments:** As previously noted, highly ionized mid-infrared lines such as [Ne V] and [O IV] can be attenuated by factors of 12 to 160 in the most obscured AGN, complicating their use as direct tracers of intrinsic AGN power [Pérez-Beaupuits et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011A&A...533A..56P/abstract).
*   **High-ionization lines without an AGN:** There is an ongoing debate regarding whether high-excitation species like [O IV] and [Ne V] exclusively trace AGN activity. Evidence suggests that fast radiative shocks propagating through dense interstellar media ($n \sim 10^6\ \mathrm{cm}^{-3}$) or extremely young, low-metallicity massive starbursts can produce ionizing radiation hard enough ($> 54.9\ \mathrm{eV}$) to generate these lines without an accreting black hole [Thuan et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...689..897T/abstract), [Hao et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...704.1159H/abstract). This is further supported by studies of [[low-ionization-emission-regions|low-ionization emission-line regions (LIERs)]] in quiescent galaxies, where temperature measurements suggest that interstellar shocks are the dominant ionization source over weak AGN activity [Lee et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240807952L/abstract).
*   **Discrepancies in stellar atmosphere models:** Current local thermodynamic equilibrium (LTE) stellar atmosphere models consistently fail to reproduce specific high-excitation mid-infrared line ratios in starbursts (such as [O IV]/[O III]). These theoretical models appear to significantly underestimate the emergent flux for energies $>41\ \mathrm{eV}$ when compared to empirical observations [Simpson et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...444..721S/abstract), [Fernández-Ontiveros et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJS..226...19F/abstract).

## Probing Metallicity and Chemical Abundance Gradients

Mid-infrared neon fine-structure lines provide a powerful, extinction-resilient tool for deriving [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] in dusty environments where traditional optical diagnostics fail. Because neon and sulfur are both primary elements produced via alpha-chain reactions during stellar carbon and oxygen burning, their nucleosynthetic yields depend very little on the underlying stellar metallicity. Consequently, the Ne/S abundance ratio is expected to remain relatively constant across different galactic environments. By summing the dominant ionization states in [[h-ii-regions|H II regions]], the flux ratio $([\text{Ne II}]\ 12.8\ \mu\text{m} + [\text{Ne III}]\ 15.6\ \mu\text{m}) / ([\text{S III}]\ 18.7\ \mu\text{m} + [\text{S IV}]\ 10.5\ \mu\text{m})$ serves as a robust proxy for the Ne/S ratio and an excellent metallicity tracer for heavily obscured objects [Fernández-Ontiveros et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJS..226...19F/abstract). Observational studies utilizing this ratio have consistently measured Ne/S values ranging from $\sim 11$ to $16$ in local group galaxies such as M33 and NGC 6822 [Rubin et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.387...45R/abstract), [Rubin et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.459.1875R/abstract). The literature consensus indicates that this universal Ne/S ratio is significantly higher than the canonical solar Ne/S value of $\sim 6.5$.

The Ne/H ratio, derived from [Ne II] and [Ne III] luminosities, is also a reliable tracer of radial abundance gradients across galactic disks. Observations of Galactic H II regions have mapped a steady decline in metallicity from the inner Galaxy ($\sim 2 Z_\odot$) to the outer disk ($\sim 1 Z_\odot$), accompanied by a steep rise in the $[\text{Ne III}]/[\text{Ne II}]$ excitation ratio [Giveon et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...566..880G/abstract). Similar gradients are observed in nearby spirals; for example, the Ne/H ratio in M33 declines at a rate of $d\log(\text{Ne/H})/dR_G = -0.058 \pm 0.014$ dex kpc$^{-1}$ [Rubin et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.387...45R/abstract). The [Ne III]/[Ne II] ratio can also enhance other abundance estimates; for instance, while the [N III]/[O III] ratio is a physically robust probe of the N/O abundance ratio, the accuracy of this diagnostic is significantly improved when using the [Ne III]/[Ne II] [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|ratio]] to correct for the hardness of the local ionizing radiation field [Peng et al. (2020)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2021ApJ...908..166P/abstract). These principles also extend to localized sources. Detailed modeling of the Wolf-Rayet nebula N76 in the Small Magellanic Cloud has successfully utilized [Ne III] and [Ne V] emission to tightly constrain the physical conditions and the profound impact of a single luminous ionization source on the surrounding ISM [Tarantino et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...969..101T/abstract).

The application of neon lines to metallicity diagnostics highlights a critical coupling between gas-phase metallicity and the ionization parameter. Empirical [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|Diagnostic emission line ratios]] often diverge from the predictions of theoretical models that assume a constant ionization parameter. Studies of star-forming galaxies indicate that the average ionization parameter systematically increases by $\sim 0.7$ dex as metallicity decreases from $2 Z_\odot$ to $0.05 Z_\odot$ [Nagao et al. (2006)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2006A&A...459...85N/abstract). This metallicity-dependent excitation must be accounted for when calibrating optical indicators like the [[r23-line-ratio|R23 line ratio]] against mid-infrared neon diagnostics. 

Finally, neon emission lines have become increasingly vital for anchoring [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] in the early universe. Early JWST observations of galaxies at $z > 7$ using [Ne III] and optical oxygen lines reveal that the Ne/O abundance pattern exhibits no significant redshift evolution, confirming that the relative production of these alpha-elements was already established within the first few hundred million years after the Big Bang [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...940L..23A/abstract). Moreover, high-redshift scaling relations utilizing neon lines suggest that while the ISM excitation conditions evolve significantly from $z \sim 0$ to $z \sim 3$, the fundamental mass-metallicity relationship experiences little to no significant evolution between $z \sim 3$ and $z \sim 6.5$ [Shapley et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...950L...1S/abstract). Expanding into the distant universe, the AURORA survey has established robust empirical metallicity calibrations for galaxies up to $z \sim 10$ using direct-method oxygen abundances, providing critical baselines for high-redshift observations [Sanders et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250810099S/abstract).

## Kinematics and Multi-phase Outflows

The 12.81 $\mu$m [Ne II] fine-structure transition is a powerful tool for tracing the gas kinematics of ionized regions in dusty environments. While textbook models historically assign forbidden lines strictly to lower-density [[narrow-line-regions-and-seyferts|Narrow-Line Regions and Seyferts]], high-resolution spectroscopy in [[mid-infrared-astronomy|Mid-Infrared Astronomy]] demonstrates that [Ne II] frequently traces high-velocity gas and turbulent outflows. Because the mid-infrared continuum suffers significantly less dust attenuation than optical wavelengths, [Ne II] line profiles can reveal the dynamics of deeply embedded regions, including the heavily obscured bases of galactic winds and nuclear outflows that are completely invisible in the optical [[o-iii-5007-line|[O III] 5007 Å line]] [Spoon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1223S/abstract).

In ULIRGs and AGN, [Ne II] emission often exhibits complex, multi-component line profiles. Notably, in the ULIRG IRAS F00183-7111, [Ne II] and [Ne III] lines trace highly disturbed gas spanning velocities from $-3500$ to $+3000$ km s$^{-1}$. The pronounced blue-shifts and broad blue wings in these profiles trace the partial disruption of the obscuring medium around a buried AGN [Spoon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1223S/abstract). Recent JWST/MIRI observations of low-luminosity AGN, such as the Sombrero galaxy and NGC 1052, reveal that emission-line widths increase with ionization potential, yet the lower-ionization lines still display significant blue wings extending beyond $1000$ km s$^{-1}$, consistent with stratified outflows aligned with radio jets [Goold et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966..204G/abstract). In more extreme nearby systems like NGC 1365, recent JWST/MIRI observations of the [Ne V] 14.3 $\mu$m line have unveiled an obscured northwest counter-cone of the nuclear outflow that is completely invisible in traditional optical diagnostics [Ceci et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250708077C/abstract). Similarly, in the broad absorption line quasar Mrk 231, [Ne II] emission exhibits blueshifted wings reaching $-400$ km s$^{-1}$, tracing large-scale non-circular motions and outflows within a massive rotating disk [Alonso Herrero et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...690A..95A/abstract).

The kinematics of the [Ne II] line are crucial for separating the rotating host galaxy disk from biconical outflows. In edge-on systems like the Seyfert 2 galaxy NGC 7172, low-excitation lines (ionization potentials $< 25$ eV) such as [Ne II] primarily trace the rotating disk emission, whereas [[high-ionization-emission-lines|High-ionization emission lines]] (e.g., [Ne V], [Ne VI]) trace the perpendicularly expanding biconical ionized gas outflow [Hermosa Muñoz et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...690A.350H/abstract). Observations of outflows at higher redshifts also highlight complex morphologies; for instance, spatial mapping of non-resonant Fe II* emission in $z \sim 1.3$ galactic winds has traced bi-conical outflows aligned with the minor kinematic axis [Finley et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017A&A...605A.118F/abstract). Despite these distinct morphological separations, there remains an active debate in the literature over whether the high-velocity turbulent outflows observed in many such systems are driven primarily by AGN radiation pressure or by nuclear starburst-driven shocks. 

On smaller physical scales, [Ne II] is utilized to map the local dynamical state of the interstellar medium and [[h-ii-regions|H II regions]]. High-spectral-resolution observations (down to $\sim 5$ km s$^{-1}$) of the dwarf starburst galaxy He 2-10 have mapped the three-dimensional kinematics of individual star-forming clumps, revealing how ionized gas flows through ambient molecular material [Beck et al. (2015)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ApJ...814...16B/abstract). In the starburst galaxy NGC 253, high-resolution imaging reveals a double-peaked, arclike [Ne II] emission region centered on the nucleus, likely representing gas driven by dynamical resonances in a barred potential [Böker et al. (1998)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998ApJ...498L.115B/abstract). Within our own Galaxy, high-resolution kinematic mapping of the [Ne II] $12.8\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ line in the Galactic Center H II regions H1 and H2 has revealed complex radial velocity distributions that are highly consistent with bow-shock and pressure-driven models [Dong et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017MNRAS.470..561D/abstract). Furthermore, [Ne II] is a proven tracer of complex gas streams in the Galactic Center, where it has been used to model the kinematic structure of the Minispiral and the inner edge of the circum-nuclear disk [Vollmer et al. (1999)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2000NewA....4..581V/abstract).

## Historical Development: From ISO to Spitzer

The observation of mid-infrared [[neon-emission-lines|Neon emission lines]], such as the [Ne II] $12.81~\mu\text{m}$ fine-structure transition, has historically been challenged by telluric absorption from Earth's atmosphere. The advent of space-based infrared observatories fundamentally transformed the study of these features, transitioning them from isolated detections to foundational tools for diagnosing the interstellar medium in heavily obscured environments. 

The launch of the Infrared Space Observatory (ISO) in 1995 marked a major milestone in mid-infrared spectroscopy. Utilizing the Short-Wavelength Spectrometer (ISO/SWS), researchers were able to systematically measure [Ne II] alongside higher-ionization lines like the [Ne III] $15.6~\mu\text{m}$ transition. ISO/SWS enabled comprehensive surveys of Galactic and extragalactic [[h-ii-regions|H II regions]], detecting [Ne II] across a wide range of galactocentric radii and establishing its utility as a tracer of gas-phase metallicity and stellar ionizing radiation [Giveon et al. (2012)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002ApJ...566..880G/abstract). ISO also mapped [Ne II] emission in the nuclear regions of nearby starbursts. For example, $1.4''$ resolution mapping of the NGC 253 nucleus revealed a double-peaked arclike [Ne II] emission structure, successfully tracking the heavily obscured starburst activity at the galaxy's center [Böker et al. (1998)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1998ApJ...498L.115B/abstract). 

While ISO demonstrated the diagnostic potential of [Ne II], the Spitzer Space Telescope, launched in 2003, revolutionized the field with its unprecedented sensitivity and mapping capabilities. The Spitzer Infrared Spectrograph (Spitzer/IRS) allowed for extensive, high-resolution spectroscopic surveys of both local and distant galaxies. A cornerstone of this era was the SINGS survey (Spitzer Infrared Nearby Galaxies Survey), which obtained high-resolution spectra for 155 nuclear and extragalactic regions [Dale et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1821D/abstract). SINGS demonstrated that [Ne II] is an excellent, extinction-insensitive tracer of star formation in many [[emission-line-galaxies|Emission-line galaxies]]. Spitzer mapping of the prototypical starburst M82 showed that the [Ne III]/[Ne II] ratio is generally low ($\sim 0.18$) across the galactic plane, but increases significantly with distance from the plane due to decreasing gas density [Beirão et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...676..304B/abstract). Furthermore, it showed that the $[\text{Ne III}]~15.6~\mu\text{m} / [\text{Ne II}]~12.8~\mu\text{m}$ ratio serves as a robust gauge of the radiation field hardness, which is strongly modulated by metallicity in star-forming regions [Dale et al. (2019)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...693.1821D/abstract).

Spitzer/IRS also vastly expanded the sample of emission-line galaxies and AGN observed in [[mid-infrared-astronomy|Mid-Infrared Astronomy]]. Dedicated surveys of Seyfert galaxies utilized [Ne II] to distinguish between starburst and AGN activity. Because [Ne II] is predominantly excited by the softer UV radiation of young massive stars, while AGN produce harder spectra capable of creating higher-ionization states, ratios such as $[\text{O IV}]~25.9~\mu\text{m} / [\text{Ne II}]~12.8~\mu\text{m}$ and $[\text{Ne III}]~15.6~\mu\text{m} / [\text{Ne II}]~12.8~\mu\text{m}$ emerged as premier [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|Diagnostic emission line ratios]] [Tommasin et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...676..836T/abstract), [Weaver et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2010ApJ...716.1151W/abstract). In these diagnostic diagrams, AGN consistently populate higher-excitation branches than pure starbursts, allowing researchers to uncover obscured accretion in dusty galaxies. 

Additionally, Spitzer's statistical power led to the discovery of new scaling relations. Notably, observations revealed a mid-infrared narrow-line Baldwin effect, characterized by a strong anti-correlation between the equivalent width of the [Ne II] line and the underlying mid-infrared continuum luminosity in AGN [Keremedjiev et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...690.1105K/abstract), [Hönig et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008A&A...485L..21H/abstract). Additionally, Spitzer studies of M101 H II regions highlighted that the equivalent widths of aromatic (PAH) features correlate more strongly with the [Ne III]/[Ne II] ionization index than with metallicity, indicating significant dust grain processing in hard radiation fields [Gordon et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...682..336G/abstract). The literature remains ambiguous regarding the physical origin of the Baldwin phenomenon; it is currently unclear whether this mid-infrared effect in narrow lines is driven by the same physical mechanisms as the traditional broad-line Baldwin effect observed in the ultraviolet, as they do not exhibit the same dependence on Eddington ratios [Keremedjiev et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...690.1105K/abstract).

## High-Redshift Observations and Recent Advances

The advent of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has fundamentally transformed the study of [[redshifted-emission-lines-and-signals|Redshifted emission lines and signals]], extending the detection of neon fine-structure and forbidden lines into the era of cosmic dawn. High-resolution [[emission-line-spectroscopy|Emission Line Spectroscopy]] in the infrared is now providing unprecedented probes of interstellar medium (ISM) conditions, chemical enrichment, and galaxy evolution in the early universe.

Using the JWST NIRSpec instrument, rest-frame optical and ultraviolet neon lines (e.g., [Ne III] $\lambda3869$, [Ne V] $\lambda3426$) are now routinely observed at extreme redshifts. Early galaxies ($z > 5$) characteristically exhibit higher ionization parameters and lower metallicities than typical local galaxies, strongly resembling rare local analogs with high excitation [Schaerer et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022A&A...665L...4S/abstract). For instance, JWST JADES observations of galaxies at $z \sim 5.5\text{--}9.5$ probe extreme ISM conditions, with [O III]/[O II] ratios frequently exceeding 10 . To interpret these high-redshift observations, astronomers often rely on [[emission-line-galaxies|extreme emission-line galaxies]] in the lower-redshift universe, such as the recently discovered EELG1002 at $z \sim 0.8$, which boasts a record-breaking [O III]+H$\beta$ equivalent width of $\sim 3700$ Å and serves as a vital analog for early universe conditions [Khostovan et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...994...34K/abstract). JWST has enabled the first direct measurements of Ne/O abundances at $z > 7$ using the [Ne III] $\lambda3869$ line, revealing no significant evolution in this $\alpha$-element ratio out to these epochs [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...940L..23A/abstract). Moreover, [Ne III] has been tentatively detected in GS-z12 at $z=12.5$, representing one of the most distant metal detections to date [D'Eugenio et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A&A...689A.152D/abstract).

Despite these rapid advances, tensions remain regarding the evolution of the mass-metallicity relation at $z > 3$. Even prior to JWST, ground-based programs like the MOSDEF survey made pioneering use of the [Ne III]/[O II] ratio to estimate the nebular oxygen abundance in galaxies at $z > 4$, uncovering an evolution toward lower metallicity at a fixed stellar mass [Shapley et al. (2017)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...846L..30S/abstract). While some modern empirical calibrations indicate that the relationship between [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|Diagnostic emission line ratios]] and stellar mass is invariant beyond $z \sim 3$, the [Ne III]/[O II] ratio appears to be uniquely elevated at a fixed stellar mass for $4.0 \leq z < 6.5$ compared to $z \sim 3.3$ [Shapley et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...950L...1S/abstract). Further metallicity-dependent ionization modeling is required to fully interpret these [[high-ionization-emission-lines|High-ionization emission lines]] at high redshift. For instance, observations with JWST/NIRSpec in the CEERS survey suggest that specific star formation rate (sSFR), rather than absolute SFR, is the primary driver of the physical conditions governing [Ne III]/[O II] and [O III]/H$\beta$ ratios in high-redshift galaxies [Gupta et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024RNAAS...8..266G/abstract).

Neon lines have also proven crucial for separating starbursts from active galactic nuclei (AGN) in the early universe. Because conventional optical diagnostics can be biased by severe dust attenuation or fail to capture highly ionized gas, the detection of extreme [[high-ionization-emission-lines|High-ionization emission lines]] can uncover hidden accretion. For example, JWST NIRSpec observed a high-equivalent-width ($11 \pm 2$ Å) [Ne V] $\lambda3427$ line in the compact $z=5.59$ galaxy GN42437. The strength of this line cannot be reproduced by massive stars or fast radiative shocks alone, providing strong evidence for a narrow-line intermediate-mass black hole ($\log(M_{\rm BH}/M_\odot) \sim 5-7$) [Chisholm et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240218643C/abstract). Furthermore, far-infrared diagnostics have proven invaluable at high redshift; the first detections of the [N II] 122 μm line in high-$z$ systems like the Cloverleaf quasar have demonstrated the utility of [N II] and [O III] lines for constraining the temperatures of ionizing stars [Ferkinhoff et al. (2011)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011ApJ...740L..29F/abstract).

At intermediate and lower redshifts, observations with JWST/MIRI have mapped rest-frame mid-infrared [[atomic-fine-structure-lines|Atomic fine-structure lines]] with unprecedented spatial resolution. For instance, MIRI Medium-Resolution Spectroscopy (MRS) of the infrared-luminous galaxy FLS1 at $z \sim 0.54$ resolved [Ne II] $12.81\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ and [Ne III] $15.55\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ emission, providing a detailed kinematic map of the ISM halfway back to the peak epoch of star formation [Young et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2023ApJ...958L...5Y/abstract). In the local universe, JWST/MIRI has revolutionized the search for obscured, low-luminosity AGN by spatially resolving [Ne V] $14.32\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ and [O IV] emission. In objects such as NGC 1052 and the Sombrero Galaxy, mid-infrared fine-structure line widths increase systematically with ionization potential (reaching $>1000\ \mathrm{km\ s^{-1}}$ for species requiring $>50\ \mathrm{eV}$ to ionize), indicating highly stratified, jet-driven outflows that were previously hidden by dust [Goold et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966..204G/abstract).

## Open Questions and Future Directions

While the fine-structure lines of [Ne II] $12.81\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ and [Ne III] $15.56\ \mu\mathrm{m}$ serve as robust [[diagnostic-emission-line-ratios|Diagnostic emission line ratios]], several theoretical and observational tensions regarding their emission remain unresolved. To address these multifaceted issues, recent projects such as the Fine-structure Line Atlas for Multi-wavelength Extragalactic Study (FLAMES) offer a comprehensive effort to cross-validate infrared fine-structure lines against optical counterparts, which will be instrumental in assessing AGN contributions to far-infrared cooling lines [Peng et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025arXiv250710702P/abstract).

A primary theoretical challenge involves reconciling observed mid-infrared line ratios with predictions from stellar atmosphere models. Observations of low-metallicity galaxies, such as blue compact dwarfs, often reveal an extremely hard radiation field. However, standard stellar evolution models struggle to reproduce specific high-excitation ratios, particularly those sensitive to energies $> 54.9\ \mathrm{eV}$ (e.g., [O IV]/[O III] and [Ne III]/[O III]). This discrepancy suggests that current models may significantly underestimate the emergent ionizing flux in these regimes [Simpson et al. (1995)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1995ApJ...444..721S/abstract), [Fernández-Ontiveros et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJS..226...19F/abstract), [Hao et al. (2009)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...704.1159H/abstract).

A related open question in [[chemical-abundances|Chemical Abundances]] concerns the universality of the neon-to-sulfur abundance ratio. Because both neon and sulfur are primary $\alpha$-elements produced during stellar carbon and oxygen burning, their ratio is expected to be relatively insensitive to metallicity. While some studies derive a near-universal Ne/S ratio of $\sim 11-16$ across different metallicities—significantly higher than the canonical solar value of $\sim 6.5$ [Rubin et al. (2018)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008MNRAS.387...45R/abstract), [Rubin et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016MNRAS.459.1875R/abstract)—resolving this apparent "neon deficit" relative to canonical solar abundances is critical for accurately using the $([\mathrm{Ne\ III}] + [\mathrm{Ne\ II}]) / ([\mathrm{S\ IV}] + [\mathrm{S\ III}])$ ratio as an extinction-free metallicity tracer in obscured systems [Fernández-Ontiveros et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJS..226...19F/abstract).

The physical origin of the [[c-ii-158-m-line|[C II] 158 μm line]] deficit observed in ultraluminous infrared galaxies is another area of active debate. Future work must determine the precise mechanisms by which high-intensity star formation fields and obscured active galactic nuclei (AGN) drive this phenomenon [González-Alfonso et al. (2007)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2008ApJ...675..303G/abstract), [De Looze et al. (2014)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014A&A...568A..62D/abstract). In the context of the early universe, simulation-based studies suggest that the unusually high [O III] 88 μm / [C II] 158 μm ratios observed in galaxies at $z > 6$ are fundamentally tied to lower carbon-to-oxygen (C/O) abundance ratios compared to solar values [Nyhagen et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A&A...702A.260N/abstract). Similarly, the physical driver behind the mid-infrared Baldwin effect—a strong anti-correlation between the equivalent width of narrow mid-IR lines like [Ne II] and the underlying continuum luminosity—remains ambiguous. Unlike the broad-line Baldwin effect seen in the [[agn-broad-line-region|AGN Broad Line Region]], the narrow-line mid-IR effect does not exhibit a clear dependence on the Eddington ratio, suggesting a potentially different physical mechanism related to dust reprocessing or the specific geometry of [[narrow-line-regions-and-seyferts|Narrow-Line Regions and Seyferts]] [Keremedjiev et al. (2008)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2009ApJ...690.1105K/abstract).

**Future Directions**
The advent of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has inaugurated a new era of high-resolution [[mid-infrared-astronomy|Mid-Infrared Astronomy]]. JWST's spatial resolution allows for the decoupling of AGN and star-formation signatures on parsec scales, revealing hidden, low-luminosity AGN and characterizing radiation field hardness in the early universe [Goold et al. (2023)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024ApJ...966..204G/abstract), [Hernandez et al. (2025)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983..154H/abstract). Furthermore, JWST is pushing the detection of neon emission out to redshifts $z > 4$, yielding the first direct metallicity and ionization parameter measurements for galaxies in the Epoch of Reionization [Arellano-Córdova et al. (2022)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2022ApJ...940L..23A/abstract), [Chisholm et al. (2024)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024arXiv240218643C/abstract).

To expand these detailed studies across large cosmological volumes, the community looks toward future facilities. Proposed space-based missions, such as the Space Infrared telescope for Cosmology and Astrophysics (SPICA), would provide the necessary sensitivity for deep spectroscopic surveys in the mid- and far-infrared. Such observatories will be essential to track the evolution of the mass-metallicity relation and interstellar medium conditions during the peak of cosmic star formation, circumventing the severe biases introduced by dust attenuation in optical [[emission-line-spectroscopy|Emission Line Spectroscopy]] [Nagao et al. (2010)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2011A&A...526A.149N/abstract), [Fernández-Ontiveros et al. (2016)](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2016ApJS..226...19F/abstract).
